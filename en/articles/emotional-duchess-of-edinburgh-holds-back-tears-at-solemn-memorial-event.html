Emotional Duchess of Edinburgh holds back tears at solemn military memorial event

Sophie inherited her position as Colonel-in-Chief of the Corps of the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers from her beloved father-in-law, Prince Philip

By Isaac Bickerstaff · October 1, 2026

As Rivals returns, Cotswold fever hits a new high – and with Hollywood blockbusters behind her and Broadway ahead, Hayley Atwell is ruffling feathers in Rutshire as the former Mrs Rupert Campbell-Black

An emotional Duchess of Edinburgh struggled to hold back tears during a poignant military engagement on Thursday. Sophie, 61, was at the Royal British Legion National Remembrance Gardens in Staffordshire to mark a day of Commemoration for the Corps of the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers, for whom she serves as Colonel-in-Chief.

The diligent royal took a leaf out of the Princess of Wales’ style playbook for the engagement, pairing a military-inspired blazer from Alexander McQueen with a navy Emilia Wickstead dress in one of Catherine’s favourite prints – the Prince of Wales check. The Duchess of Edinburgh completed her ensemble with her Giulia Barela duranta earrings, her Loewe watch, a Sophie Habsburg bag and ivory suede boots. Pinned to her lapel was the Regimental Brooch of the Corps.

Sophie inherited her Colonel-in-Chief role from her father-in-law, Prince Philip, in 2022. The late Duke of Edinburgh held the position for over 50 years.

It’s been a characteristically busy season for the hard-working Duchess of Edinburgh, nicknamed ‘Super Sophie’ by her fans. The King’s sister-in-law flew out to Zambia for an official tour, which saw her advocate for some of her most treasured causes, including gender equality, education, climate, and working to end preventable blindness.

Almost as soon as she returned to Britain, Sophie was busy representing her charities at home, promptly attending a reception to celebrate the 80th anniversary of the Royal Mencap Society.

The Duchess of Edinburgh, who inherited patronage of the Society following the death of former Royal Patron, the Queen Mother, in 2002, wore one of her favourite outfits for the occasion. A vision in florals, the Duchess re-wore the Suzannah London ‘grandad’ dress, previously seen when she supported King Charles at the King’s Award for Enterprise ceremony in Windsor Castle last year. Sophie completed her look on Wednesday with her Strathberry mosaic nano bag and her trusted espadrilles from Penelope Chilvers.

In a conversation with guest of honour Geoffrey Leer, 80, the Duchess of Edinburgh spoke of why she is so proud to support the charity. ‘What makes Mencap special is meeting lovely people like you. Things have changed a lot during your lifetime for people with a learning disability, which is good. They have a voice and opinions, and that’s being listened to,’ Sophie said.

‘The next challenge is to have more opportunities for people in workplaces. People with a learning disability are life enhancers. A lot more people are taking interest in giving people with a learning disability opportunities, but there’s a long way to travel.’

In addition to the military obligations she carries with such pride, the Duchess of Edinburgh has long been a champion and advocate for those living with disabilities. This July, Sophie reached out to Chloe, a 17-year-old girl with cerebral palsy, to wish her luck on a cycling charity ride from Land’s End to John O’Groats to raise money for PALS and Treloar’s, two charities that support children with disabilities. In a break from the traditional forms of royal communications, the Duchess sent Chloe a video message, in which she sent her ‘warmest wishes’ and reflected on her own experience cycling 450 miles from Holyroodhouse in Edinburgh to Buckingham Palace in 2016.

Last week, Sophie was in The Hague to attend the Women, Peace and Security Forum, no doubt drawing on her years of experience working to end the weaponisation of sexual violence during conflicts. The Duchess met with Women Peace Builders and thanked them for committing to such essential work.
