Edit Schools Guide 2027: The best public schools in the South East

The 2027 Edit Schools Guide reveals the best public schools in the South East

By Edit · September 29, 2026

Park Road, Abingdon OX14 1DE

Website abingdon.org.uk Head Michael Windsor, since 2016; previously head of Reading Blue Coat School Pupils 1,270. Day: 1,128 boys, 50 girls; boarding: 142 boys. Ages 4–18 Term Fees Day: £9,500; boarding: £19,375 Bursaries 7% of pupils, up to 100% of day fees Alumni Actor Tom Hollander, all five members of Radiohead, musical theatre composer Toby Marlow

A boys-only education at Abingdon is officially a thing of the past: this September, it admits its first-ever cohort of girls into Year 7 and the Sixth Form, bringing an almost 800-year era to a close. Still, the current students are delighted, while their parents have welcomed the change with open arms, assured that it will only serve to ‘further enhance the school’. (As one parent says: ‘I only wish that Abingdon were co-ed when our daughter was younger, so that she could have had the chance to flourish here like her brothers have.’) A new uniform and streamlined games kit have been introduced for all the students, to help create a cohesive identity – with the unmistakable ‘Abingdon pink’ front and centre – while the addition of extra netball, tennis and basketball courts ensure all incoming students are well catered for. The start of the 2026/27 academic year also marks the formation of the Abingdon Schools Group (uniting with both Abingdon Prep and The Manor Prep). Though fast-evolving, the school remains unchanged at its core: families can expect the same high academic standards, impressive breadth of opportunities through the much-loved ‘other half’ programme of co-curriculars, and a nurturing, supportive environment that yields excellent academic results. Last year, 72 per cent of students attained grades 9–8 at GCSE, 72 per cent A*–A at A-level, and an impressive 89 per cent of leavers headed to their first-choice universities, the vast majority of them Russell Group.

Haywards Heath RH17 6SQ

Website ardingly.com Head Alastair Tighe, since 2026; previously head of Wells Cathedral School Pupils 1,058. Day: 395 boys, 325 girls; boarding: 172 boys, 166 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £11,460; boarding: £17,547 Bursaries 8% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Private Eye editor Ian Hislop, former US ambassador Sir David Manning, Chelsea footballer Shim Mheuka

At Ardingly, new head Alastair Tighe has been bequeathed an enviable inheritance. Although two-thirds of its Upper Sixth take A-levels, his has become the top-ranked boarding school in the UK for International Baccalaureate results, averaging a rather giddy 40.1; an improvement on the previous year’s already strong 38.9. In addition, he can justly take pride in its World Ready initiative, which covers such life skills as employability, enterprise and social education; and in its trips offering, which includes such destinations as New York, Iceland and Kenya. But while most of the students here hail from the Home Counties, they’re joined by an International Sixth Form, meaning that 20 per cent of the overall intake is from overseas (a quota that has recently included fully-funded refugee children from Afghanistan and Ukraine). More than 25 sports are played at the school, from basketball and golf to martial arts; and Ardingly has lately announced new partnerships with Sussex County Cricket Club and the Jofra Archer Institute for Cricket in Barbados. In 2025, the school held its first-ever Giving Day – with activities including dragon boat racing on the nearby reservoir and zip-lining from the chapel tower – to raise some £190,000 for bursaries (and a brand-new watersports activity centre). However, while that sounds rather exceptional, the pupils at Ardingly are not so different from their peers elsewhere: they have a seemingly universal love for the kitchen’s chicken goujons.

Church Road, Steep, Petersfield GU32 2DG

Website bedales.org.uk

Head Will Goldsmith, since 2021; previously director of teaching and learning at Latymer Upper School Pupils 722. Day: 203 boys, 192 girls; boarding: 135 boys, 192 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £14,717; boarding: £18,999 Bursaries 12% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni The Earl of Snowdon, actors Minnie Driver and Daniel Day-Lewis

‘I enjoy the non-hierarchical nature of the school,’ says one student, who believes the mixed-age dorms not only create a sense of mutual respect but also enable the passing down of such traditions as early morning walks up to the Poet’s Stone. These egalitarian relation-ships also pertain to students and teachers, who are addressed by their first names. ‘By removing such arbitrary barriers as uniforms and formal titles, Bedales fosters an atmosphere of mutual trust in which students are not afraid to fail, and therefore are not afraid to innovate,’ explains a parent. And talking of innovation: Japanese has recently been introduced as a Bedales Assessed Course (at GCSE level); a new Bedales ‘vodcast’ shows Sixth-formers the ropes of audio production and presenting; and the Rock Studio is undergoing a renovation. But then, all the arts thrive here, as does an openness to ‘alternative’ education: students cook from scratch in the Bakehouse, learn blacksmithing and weaving, and walk their own livestock at the annual Bedales Agricultural Show. Head Will Goldsmith is described by one student as an ‘attribute to ensuring great development within pupils’, and that assessment is supported by an anecdote shared by one parent. Shortly after starting at the school, their son was sitting all by himself. ‘So Will asked to join him and they spent the next 40 minutes or so discussing what our son called “life, the universe, and everything in between!”'

De Parys Avenue, Bedford MK40 2TU

Website bedfordschool.org.uk Head Michael Sloan, since 2026; previously head of King’s College Taunton Pupils 992 boys: 799 day, 193 boarding Ages 7–18 Term Fees Day: £10,306; boarding: £17,744 Bursaries 12% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni England and Northampton Saints rugby player Fraser Dingwall, co-creator of the NHS Sir George Godber, former England cricketer Sir Alastair Cook

Bedford takes Movember extremely seriously. Last year, its youngsters raised more than £45,000 for the men’s health charity; and every year they release a song in support. But altruism is just one aspect of the busy life at this thriving establishment for all-rounders. For example, Third-formers must take up at least two extracurriculars (and they appear to continue them as they get older, even when it’s no longer mandatory). So when they are not stargazing in the on-site planetarium and observatory – Bedford is the only school in the country to have one – the boys might be found playing on the new golf simulator or fine-tuning business ideas. Talking of which, entrepreneurship is part of the curriculum from Year 10. As part of the ‘Ready to Lead’ initiative, students can attend management courses. With computer science now prioritised, they enter various cybersecurity competitions. And with new head Michael Sloan joining, more change is afoot. The Fives courts are being developed; a new Sixth Form Centre is in the works; a new organ is being fitted in the stunning chapel; and Upper School staff will now teach Years 7 and 8 students, thus giving them a smoother transition into the senior school. Otherwise, the shooting facilities are – ahem – top-of-the-range; there are 57 rugby teams from ages seven to 18; and the opening in April of Bedford School Mohali – India’s first British school for girls (boarding and day) – is the first of a family of international outposts for Bedford.

Cranbrook, Kent TN17 4AA

Website benenden.school Head Rachel Bailey, since 2024; previously head of Royal Masonic School for Girls Pupils 520 girls: 50 day, 470 boarding Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £15,400; boarding: £21,300 Bursaries 7% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Former head of MI5 Baroness Manningham-Buller, The Princess Royal, humanitarian Sue Ryder

Benenden is highly committed to its mission: providing students with ‘A Complete Education’. This means great emphasis is placed on building a dynamic learning environment that stretches far beyond the curriculum. (Year 9s, for example, are now offered courses in data science, natural sciences, sustainability and business.) At the same time, the wonderful Benenden’s Electives Programme introduces the children to a range of 70 different topics, from sign language and investing to journalism and meteorology. And STEM is steaming ahead: one Sixth-former recently ranked among the top four biologists of their age group in the UK and earned a place atthe International Biology Olympiad in the Philippines, while a student sister-act was selected as finalists at the Fourth European Nuclear Competition for Secondary Schools in Bologna. Cultural perspectives are also broadened by an exchange programme that sees students swap with their counterparts in Chile, Canada, Australia and Benenden’s International School in Guangzhou. In other developments, the children’s author and illustrator Grace Easton has been made artist inresidence, while Olympian Robin Francis has been appointed director of swimming; the latter with great timing, given the upcoming revamp of the sports complex, which will include improvements to the pool, modernised changing facilities and a reconfigured gym. Because the school has a wide catchment area, there is an efficient network of buses that run to nearby towns and train stations – and of course the termly pick-ups and drop-offs at airports.

Overton House, 131 High Street, Berkhamsted HP4 2BB

Website berkhamsted.com Head Martin Walker, since 2026; previously vice principal Pupils 2,010. Day: 1,035 boys, 887 girls; boarding: 51 boys, 37 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £12,400; boarding: £18,853 Bursaries Variable % of pupils, on application Alumni Sailor Sir Robin Knox-Johnston, DJ Roman Kemp, golfer Alice Hewson

The big news at Berkhamsted is that Martin Walker has taken over as principal of the whole group, having previously served as head of The Sixth and vice principal. He has already appointed a psychologist, brought in a dedicated Oxbridge consultant and made the school smartphone-free; and all agree he is precisely the right person not only to lead this highly regarded institution into the future but to build on its stellar reputation. Initially, parents gravitate towards Berkhamsted for its ‘all-round educational experience’, ‘amazing facilities’ and ‘high standard of sports’, not to mention its diamond structure – whereby the genders split after prep school, coming back to co-ed for A-levels – and its sprawling campus, before delightedly discovering first-hand just how ‘non-woke and grown-up’ it is, and how ‘lacking in ego’. ‘While the school aims to get the best out of each pupil, that doesn’t come at a cost,’ one parent tells us. ‘The students are well cared-for and guided to achieve their results – and praised when they do.’ It’s an approach that clearly works: 75.5 per cent of last year’s GCSEs grades were 9–7, with 26 per cent of all grades being 9s while A-levels were equally strong, with 57.2 per cent of grades marked A*–A and 68 per cent of leavers heading to Russell Group universities. For those looking further afield, there’s a head of international university applications, who last year ensured that every student was offered a place, whether at Barnard College in the US or Sophia University in Tokyo.

Bradfield, Berkshire RG7 6AU

Website bradfieldcollege.org.uk Head Jeremy Quartermain, since 2025; previously Head of Rossall School Pupils 809. Day: 108 boys, 58 girls; boarding: 350 boys, 293 girlsAges 13–18 Term Fees Day: £12,400; boarding: £20,250 Bursaries 12% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Novelist Louis de Bernières, England cricketers Gus Atkinson and Lauren Bell

‘For me, it was really important that both my son and daughter got to grow up alongside the opposite sex, not thinking they were anything special!’ says one parent, for whom Bradfield’s co-ed status was a major factor in her choice and who now robustly praises the school’s facilities and teachers, and the various life lessons it imparts: ‘My 14-year-old daughter finds the boys really annoying – but I’m glad she’s realising this now, rather than while hanging around at the gates of some boys’ school thinking the students there are faultless young gods…’ Demystifying the opposite gender is just one facet of the broad education offered at this beautiful, warm red-brick enclave surrounded by lush, green playing fields. The vibrant music depart-ment boasts vocal teachers whose specialisms range from West End show tunes to pop and jazz – one has even worked with Alicia Keys and Take That – while lucky actors get to tour their Greek plays to Athens every three years. Sport is famously extremely strong: few get the better of the girls’ hockey team or nimble football squad. Thanks to a unique boarding structure, all Year 9s go into Faulkner’s House for a year before moving into their senior houses for the remainder of their time. While Bradfield is primarily full boarding, there’s enough flexibility that pupils can return from their weekends at home on Monday mornings. And responding to demand, a new dedicated day house is opening.

BN2 0AL

It is no secret that teenagers greatly need their slumbers, but Brighton College is perhaps alone in turning this requirement into an educational pursuit, holding sessions on sleep science to ensure its charges are up to speed on how it affects health and academic performance. Wellbeing is also fostered with seminars on the benefits of cold-water immersion (swimming in the sea); pupils might further connect with nature through an upcoming expedition to Antarctica, made possible by an alumnus in celebration of Old Brightonian Sir Vivian Fuchs, who led the first successful land crossing of the Antarctic continent in 1957–58. This sense of curiosity is also fostered by an impressive range of talks and seminars, with pupils in every year group enjoying masterclasses on such diverse topics as stargazing and magical realism. All of this intellectual stimulation has clearly had an impressive impact: following a batch of exam results in 2025 that broke the school’s record, an impressive 45 leavers were offered places at Oxbridge. Pupils are still relishing the new Richard Cairns Building, with its 400-seat theatre, a purpose-built computing and digital media department, Sixth Form study and Global Futures centre. Next up, the prep will be turned into two boarding houses and a brand-new school built to replace it. ‘Brighton College is unique in that it offers an academic setting, but also puts a huge emphasis on kindness and the individual,’ says one parent.

Harestone Valley Road, Caterham, Surrey CR3 6YA

Website caterhamschool.co.ukHead* Ceri Jones, since 2015; previously second master of Tonbridge School Pupils 1,029. Day: 435 boys, 425 girls; boarding: 99 boys, 70 girls Ages 4–18*Term Fees Day: £9,665; boarding: £20,170 Bursaries 20% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Actor Chuk Iwuji, Team GB hockey captain Zach Wallace, SpaceX rocket engineer Alia Ardon

Caterham head Ceri Jones describes the school as being ‘relentlessly optimistic about what we can achieve.’ The proof is in the pudding, with the headline development is its launch of a new GCSE-stage curriculum – focusing on entrepreneurial, problem solving and digital skills – called the Edge Certificate. Though the faculty was hoping about 20 pupils would sign up, the number has been closer to 100; and little wonder in a climate that more than ever rewards stu-dents for something beyond top grades. Elsewhere, the school is leading the way in AI, with its teach-ing and learning ‘RileyBot’ now available in 25 others across the UK. (The unconvinced should know that it has in-built safeguarding, offering age-appropriate answers and flagging issues to tutors, parents and teachers.) But, maintaining a healthy balance, a rewilding project is also being undertaken across the East Surrey campus in partnership with the pioneering Knepp Estate; and with the regeneration of a 180-acre wood, as well as ponds and wildflower meadows popping up everywhere, there’s plenty here to remind students there’s more to life than the tech revolution. Most Caterham families are from Surrey, Kent and Sussex – the towns of Reigate, Oxted and Sevenoaks are particular hot spots – but a steady stream of reverse commuters are also taking the 22-minute train ride from Clapham Junction and hopping onto the five-minute shuttle that runs to and from the school. Clearly, it’s well worth the journey.

Godalming, Surrey GU7 2DX

Website charterhouse.org.uk Head Dr Alex Peterken, since 2018; previously head of Cheltenham College Pupils 1,030. Day: 78 boys, 49 girls; boarding: 492 boys, 411 girls Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £16,369; boarding: £21,015 Bursaries 8.2% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Composers Ralph Vaughan Williams and Rachel Portman, former chancellor of the exchequer Jeremy Hunt

It says something about a school’s rich provision of opportunities that the range is deemed so extensive that, according to one mother, ‘It’s impossible to do everything.’ But, she goes on to say: ‘It means each child can find what suits them best.’ With around 50 nationalities onthe roll, just under a third of its students come from abroad while the rest are typically based within a 45-minute drive. ‘We have never met an unpleasant Carthusian,’ says one parent when asked why they chose this historic school. Head Dr Peterken, described by staff as ‘a passionate educator’ and by pupils as a ‘nice guy [who] cares a lot’, believes parents want a lot more than the ‘recounting of endless statistics about grades; they want institutional openness and skills that are not just academic’. Yet the fact that 85 per cent of leavers go on to Russell Group universities – most popular are Durham, Exeter and UCL – and a good number to US colleges, including Ivy Leaguers, speaks volumes. Founded by the Jacobean businessman Thomas Sutton in 1611, the school’s present Godalming campus, replete with spired Gothic buildings, dates back to 1872. Set in 250 green acres, its facilities include a nine-hole course and 16 football pitches. In 2025, Charterhouse played host to the largest Fives tournament in its history; and thanks to donations from parents and alumni, it was able to resurrect one of the original courts, giving it nine now in total.

Horsham, West Sussex, RH13 0LJ

Website christs-hospital.org.uk Head Matthew Judd, since 2025; previously head of Leighton Park School Pupils 860. Day: 30 boys, 30 girls; boarding: 400 boys, 400 girls Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £11,151; boarding: £16,480 Bursaries 80% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Poet SamuelTaylor Coleridge, England rugby player Joe Launchbury, actress Susannah Fielding

Established to educate poor and orphaned children 474 years ago, Christ’s Hospital has never forgotten the philanthropic intent with which it was founded. (In 2023, the then head Simon Reid, formerly of Gordonstoun, leapt into the lake fully clothed on a chill November day, as he promised he would if the school raised £300,000 for an endowment.) Since then, the school’s ambition has only grown – it now spends some £23 million per annum on bursaries, while only 20 per cent of its pupils pay full fees. And what a school these students gain access to: a stunning 1,000-seat chapel, sailing expeditions in the Aegean and a marching band so esteemed that it played at Brighton & Hove Albion’s stadium in March, ahead of a Premier League game against Nottingham Forest. Christ’s Hospital sent all 10 of its offer holders to Oxford and Cambridge last year, while the Sixth-form art trip to New York took in the Whitney, the Museum of Modern Art and Guggenheim. Historical links are cherished here, too, with one pupil saying they ‘love the way we merge tradition with modernity’. Charles II set up its mathematical school to boost skills in the Royal Navy, while the famous uniform – barely changed since it was worn by Samuel Taylor Coleridge and known as ‘Housey’ in school slang – gets any student sporting it free admission to the Tower of London. Pupils praise ‘passionate’ new head Matthew Judd for ‘responding personally and actively to any issues’.

Horseshoe Lane, Cranleigh, Surrey GU6 8QQ

Website cranleigh.org Head Samantha Price, since 2024; previously head of BenendenPupils 957. Day: 252 boys, 167 girls; boarding: 320 boys, 218 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £11,605; boarding: £20,180 Bursaries 7.5% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Former editor in chief of the Guardian Alan Rusbridger, fashion designer Bunmu Olaye, England cricketer Ollie Pope

Cranleigh’s first female head has brought a sense of warmth and ambition to the school; and it’s gone down a treat with parents, who describe Samantha Price as a ‘breath of fresh air’. Certainly, there are plenty of changes afoot. Cranleigh now operates as one entity across two sites, meaning the senior school and the prep – under the leadership of new head Will Newman – are intertwined and provide children with continuity from the ages of three to 18. And because three-quarters of them are boarders, there’s been a concerted drive to push up the frequency of their activities. (Hence termly trips to London’s West End.) What’s more, two extra boarding houses are in the pipeline, while the new Day+ scheme offers sleepover flexibility and the luxury of choice. Elsewhere, following the unveiling of a new hockey Astroturf, plans are in the pipeline for a performing arts centre, while the new Sixth Form Centre has gone down a treat. Modelled on the shared office spaces that are ubiquitous in the modern working world, it surely provides a brilliant space to conceive business ideas for the Cran Do entrepreneurship awards. Academically, the latest GCSE and A-level results were the best for several years, with 86 per cent of leavers going on to Russell Group or equivalent universities. As one parent says: ‘My three children have completely different interests and abilities and each one has found a place to shine and develop those interests at Cranleigh.’

Banbury Road, Oxford OX2 7PL

Website doverbroecks.com Head Christopher Bromham, since 2026; previously principal at International College Spain ICS Pupils 615. Day: 232 boys, 143 girls; boarding: 103 boys, 123 girls Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £12,160; boarding: £22,065 Bursaries 15% of pupils, up to 50% Alumni Actors Rupert Friend and Phia Saban, former secretary of state for defence Ben Wallace

D’Overbroeck’s was founded in 1977 by Malcolm van Biervliet, a modern languages teacher whoset out to create a more relaxed and enabling alternative to traditional independent schools. What began in his home as a Sixth-form tutorial college has retained its warmth and individuality: students wear their own clothes in the Sixth Form and call teachers by their first names.‘I love the brilliant teaching and academic support,’ says one. ‘I also appreciate the diversity of thecommunity… [which] creates a place where I feel included, encour-aged and able to be myself.’ With 50 per cent of students hailing from overseas, D’Overbroeck’s has a global perspective which will be enhanced by the new principal Christopher Bromham (who has spent more than 20 years in high-performing day and boarding establishments across the UK, Korea, Dubai and Spain). Summerhill House, a new boarders’ residence, is set to open; and courses on criminology, business and entrepreneurship, and creative media have come onstream. The music department is notably busy, with its Concordia concert bringing together the school as well as local primaries and the parent and staff choir. And the foreign trips sound incredible, with recent highlights included an expedition to Tanzania, where students ascended Mount Meru before working on community development projects.

Thatcham, Berkshire RG18 9JJ

Website downehouse.net Head Emma McKendrick, since 1997; previously head of Royal High School Bath Pupils 538 girls: 40 day, 498 boarding Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £11,621; boarding: £21,076 Bursaries 14% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Broadcaster Clare Balding, comedian Miranda Hart, actress Geraldine James

Downe House strives to give its girls an education that meets the demands of an interconnected world. And so its Global Schools Exchange offers the opportunity to study at one of 17 partners overseas (in such fantastic locations as South Africa, New Zealand, Brazil and India) while its Global Internship programme sees Sixth-formers gaining experience with venture capitalists in Zambia, engineers in Thailand and vets in Australia. The newly launched Exploring Europe programme is also enticing, with lucky students visiting four diverse cities – Krakow, Seville,Rome and Berlin – over four terms, to gain a comprehensive understanding of each city through a bespoke curriculum covering geography, history, politics, literature, art, classics and religion. There’s plenty happening back on campus, too: one of the boarding houses has undergone a major refurbishment to accommodate the increasing number of day pupils; and the new performing arts studio, designed as a slick setting for small-scale theatre, film and dance productions, boasts a mini-auditorium. The girls have their own personalised curricula and tutors from Day One, and Downe is committed to the continual professional development of the faculty. A Centre for Learning and Research equips its teachers with cutting-edge findings; and the school also hosts both an annual Discover Teaching event and an Academic Conference featuring distinguished speakers. All of this is helmed by the much-admired, longtime head Emma McKendrick. ‘Our three girls have threatened to riot if she ever leaves,’ says one parent.

Old Wish Road, Eastbourne, East Sussex BN21 4JX

Website eastbourne-college.co.uk Head Tom Lawson, since 2016; previously deputy head of Christ’s Hospital Pupils 600. Day: 190 boys, 160 girls; boarding: 140 boys, 110 girls Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £12,705; boarding: £19,277 Bursaries 18% of pupils, up to 5% Alumni Kent cricketer Tawanda Muyeye, comedian Suzy Eddie Izzard, MP Alison Griffiths

A breath of fresh sea air blows through Eastbourne College, which prides itself on shaping ‘authentic all-rounders’ on its part-red-brick, part-contemporary campus. Here, the focus is on health and happi-ness: when asked to name what they like best about school, pupils bring up ‘community’ again and again: ‘It’s like a mini-family,’ says one. Among top-notch facilities, the newest is a state-of-the-art golf-swing room which – like the radar tracking system and the putting green in the works – plenty of parents are eager to try. A recent ISI review praised Eastbourne’s music, art, drama and design as ‘signifi-cant’ strengths, and one student’s thought-provoking installation on AI and gendered marketing earned a place in a London exhibition. Eastbourne’s first-ever Giving Day raised more than £340,000. As for academics, results are on the up, with a third of GCSEs graded 9–8 and 75 per cent of A-levels A*–B.

College Road, Epsom, Surrey KT17 4JQ

When we asked parents and pupils what they considered Epsom’s greatest strengths to be, the answers varied wildly – but in the best possible way. Some cited the ‘sense of belonging’ and ‘superb’ pastoral care; some its academic rigour and results. Others highlighted the ‘breadth’ of what’s on offer, as well as the ‘world-class’ music, arts and sports facilities. ‘You can genuinely do everything here,’ one pupil tells us. ‘I’m in the first team for hockey, I take art as a serious subject, and I am on track for strong A-levels… Most schools make you choose, but Epsom doesn’t.’ Parents appreciate that, while ‘many schools talk about developing the whole child, Epsom actually delivers it’. Another ‘brilliant’ thing? The strict ‘no phones during the day’ policy, which students genuinely appreciate. ‘It’s actually quite freeing. Less drama, fewer distractions,’ one tells us. ‘And it forces you to spend time with people face-to-face,’ says another.

Windsor, Berkshire SL4 6DW

Website etoncollege.com Head Simon Henderson, since 2015; previously head of Bradfield College Pupils 1,345 boarding boys Ages 13–18 Term Fees £21,892 Bursaries 18% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Novelist George Orwell, actor Dominic West, The Prince of Wales

Since 1440, when Henry VI made provision for the free education of 70 ‘poor boys’, Eton has embraced its identity as an all-male boarding school. Guided by its philosophy of ‘Honouring Tradition, Inspiring Change’, it still strives to improve the life chances of a huge swathe of bright young men: approximately £10 million per annum is spent on financial aid (and the development team is working hard to increase this figure); the Orwell Award pays for extraordinarily talented Sixth-formers from diverse backgrounds to benefit from the college’s care; and impressive local outreach statistics reveal that, last year, about 170 boys undertook 5,000-plus hours of community service. It’s amazing they have time. With four applicants vying for each place, parents from around the world are drawn to the extraordinary range of activities on offer. ‘Facilities are available to do any weird and wonderful thing you’re into,’ attests one. Thus, the various societies attract speakers of the highest calibre, be they politicians, presidents, CEOs or scientists, and the Farrer Theatre is a haven for budding thespians and stage crew (known as ‘Keepers’); a 17-strong student band lately accompanied a rip-roaring production of West Side Story, and artists display their works in the light-filled Drawing Schools. What’s more, athletic types will surely have to be prised out of the awesome new Jo Colman Centre, which boasts eight squash courts, a climbing wall and all sorts of training appurtenances. Simon Henderson will be stepping down at the end of the 2026/27 academic year, with St Edward’s School’s Alastair Chirnside set to take over.

Haileybury, Hertford, Hertfordshire SG13 7NU

Website haileybury.comHead* Eugene du Toit, since 2024; previously head of Wellington School Pupils 924. Day: 194 boys, 184 girls; boarding: 279 boys, 267 girls Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £14,345; boarding: £19,820 Bursaries 10% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Former prime minister Clement Attlee, filmmaker Christopher Nolan, Formula One driver Oscar Piastri*

With its neo-classical architecture and beautiful quadrangle, there is a Harry Potter vibe at Haileybury, where works have been completed in the library and chapel, and the recently unveiled outdoor learning centre does what it says on the tin while going all out as a hub for adventure and wellbeing. As for the world-class Sci-Tech facility, it certainly seems to help students innovate: one of their VEX robotics teams has represented the UK at the VEX World Championship in Dallas. The CubeSat programme aims to position Haileybury as the first secondary school in Europeto launch a mini-satellite into space. And a new programme for leadership and enterprise instils in pupils the skills needed for future career success, giving them expe-rience in real-world work settings. But the school doesn’t rest on its laurels. It’s currently conducting a thorough review of its curriculum, as well as its reporting and assess-ment models; and, collaborating with a leading academic on its adoption of technology, it has just appointed a director of digital innovation and enterprise. All this brainwork is balanced by strong sporting performances, dazzling theatrical productions that range from Legally Blonde to Richard III, and a sophisticated musical out-put from a roster of choirs and ensembles. This dynamic and ambitious culture is overseen by head Eugene du Toit, described by one student as ‘genuinely one of the most approachable and effective school leaders I have met’.
