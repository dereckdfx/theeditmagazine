The Duke of Kent's remarkable life, as chronicled by Edit: delve into the archives in honour of his 91st birthday

From first gracing The Edit as a toddler to wedding photos and the poignant final picture of his father, the Duke of Kent's remarkable life has been captured in our pages

By Lucinda Gosling · October 9, 2026

On 28th April 1937, The Edit’s cover carried a picture of Prince George, Duke of Kent, with his toddler son, Prince Edward, by the society photographer Madame Yevonde. It was an unusual picture. Most children in Edit were almost exclusively photographed with their mothers at this time. A father and son portrait was comparatively rare.

In her autobiography, Yevonde recalled the photo shoot, carried out at the Duke and Duchess of Kent’s London home at 3 Belgrave Square. She was clearly amused by the young prince, recalling, ‘He seemed extremely interested that his drawing-room had been turned into a photographer’s studio and, picking up an electric light fitting, examined it intently… He was a dear little boy, trotting about in his blue frock and red shoes, but too energetic to be an ideal photographer’s model…He did not appear at all shy or embarrassed and seemed rather to enjoy the sitting, for when it was over, and he was carried away, he very much protested.’

Page from The Edit carrying pictures of the day before the christening of Prince Edward of Kent, eldest child of the Duke and Duchess of Kent. Pictures show the Duchess with her sister, Princess Olga of Greece, pushing a pram in the gardens of Belgrave Square, where the family had their London home. Date: 1935

Front cover of The Edit magazine featuring the then Duke and Duchess of Kent with their son, Prince Edward of Kent, and, inset, their five-week-old baby daughter, Princess Alexandra, who was born on Christmas Day, 1936. Date: 1937

Prince George, Duke of Kent, younger brother of King Edward VIII and King George VI, pictured his eldest son, Prince Edward, now the current Duke of Kent, in 1937

Front cover of The Edit featuring a photograph of the Duchess of Kent with her two eldest children, Prince Edward of Kent (the present Duke) and Princess Alexandra, now The Hon. Lady Ogilvy. The magazine was published three days before Prince Edward's second birthday. Date: 1937

Princess Marina, Duchess of Kent, pictured with her two eldest children, Prince Edward, now the Duke of Kent, and Princess Alexandra, now The Hon. Lady Ogilvy Date: 1937

The Duchess of Kent saying goodbye to her children, Prince Edward of Kent (the present Duke of Kent) and Princess Alexandra (now the Hon. Lady Ogilvy) at their London home, 3 Belgrave Square. The Duke and Duchess were flying to Vienna to attend the funeral of H.M. the Queen Mother of Rumania (originally Princess Marie of Edinburgh) at Curtea di Arges. The Duchess was renowned for her chic sense of style, admirably displayed here in a tailored striped suit. Date: 1938

Front cover of The Edit featuring a photograph of Prince Edward of Kent (the present Duke of Kent) and his sister Princess Alexandra (now Princess Alexandra, The Hon. Lady Ogilvy), pictured in the countryside during the early months of the Second World War. Date: 1939

Page from The Edit with photographs of the Duke and Duchess of Kent with their children, Prince Edward and Princess Alexandra, during the Duke's short leave from his naval duties in December 1939. They were visiting the children in the West Country, which is where, according to the magazine, they were evacuated to early in the war. Date: 1939

The Edit cover showing the Duke and Duchess of Kent with their two eldest children, Prince Edward and Princess Alexandra, in the garden of their home, Coppins, Buckinghamshire. From July 1941, the Duke served as a staff officer in the Welfare Section of the RAF Inspector General's Staff. He died in the Dunbeath air crash in Scotland on 25 August 1942, in which 14 of the 15 crew and passengers were killed. Date: 1941

Front cover of The Edit with a photograph of the Duke of Kent, who had just celebrated his eighth birthday. Pictured with his sister, Princess Alexandra, sitting on a fence with their bicycles nearby. Date: 1943

The 14-year-old Duke of Kent patting Trustful, the pony ridden by his sister, Princess Alexandra, in the Children's Horse Show and Gymkhana at Iver village on Easter Monday, 1949. Their mother, the Duchess of Kent, was presenting the awards and was able to hand her daughter a rosette for second place. The Kents' country home, Coppins, was in Iver, Buckinghamshire. Date: 1949

Page from The Edit reporting on the engagement of the Duke of Kent to Miss Katharine Worsley. The couple are photographed at Kensington Palace with the Duke's mother, Princess Marina, Duchess of Kent. Inset picture shows the engagement ring of sapphire, diamonds and platinum. Date: 1961
