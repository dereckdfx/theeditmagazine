‘My father was quite critical’: Zara Tindall reflects on her father Mark Phillips' tough-love approach to parenting

The Olympic medalist opened up about training sessions with her ‘tough’ father, Mark Phillips

By Isaac Bickerstaff · October 8, 2026

Zara Tindall had a bucolic upbringing at Gatcombe Park with her parents, Princess Anne and Mark Phillips, and her brother, Peter Phillips. In addition to all the trappings of a royal childhood – from official photographs to Christmases spent at Sandringham – she was also training to become one of the nation’s most successful equestrians. But she revealed this week that neither parent mollycoddled her, nor did they offer a leg-up in the riding world, despite both being decorated athletes in the sport.

Indeed, Zara has said that her mother and father informed her that she would have to prove her own mettle should she want a career in eventing. She also revealed that her Olympic champion father was near impossible to impress – and that she endeavoured ‘not to be quite that bad’ with her own children.

Speaking on the Talk Horse podcast, Zara reflected on her journey to her horse riding success, one that eventually saw her crowned World Champion, win a silver medal at the London Olympics, and, this year, win the British Open. After leaving Gordonstoun, she decided to see whether she to see whether she might be able to turn her passion for eventing into a career.

‘I think my dad and my mum were like, “But you have to do it properly. And this is how it's going to work. And you're not getting any help, and you need to produce results",’ Zara said.

The Princess Royal and Mark Phillips divorced in 1992, when Zara was 11, but her father remained a hard taskmaster in her training. He was, she said, a great deal stricter than her mother when it came to the sport.

‘Mum's very relaxed,’ she recalled. ‘She's like, “Just, you know, just do what you do,” whereas my father's like, “More of this, more of that”.’

The King’s niece added: ‘My father was quite critical... Very rarely you would get a, “Yeah, that was good”, out of him. So it was always striving to try and get that comment. He was a tough coach.’

The attitude delivered results. In 2006, Zara topped the podium at the World Equestrian Games, riding Toytown to a stunning victory in Aachen. She was named BBC Sports Personality of the Year, then went on to win silver at the 2012 Olympics, where the medal was presented to her by her mother.

Despite this success, Zara is endeavouring to be less tough on her daughters, who have both expressed an interest in following in their mother’s footsteps. She has been taking Mia, 12, and eight-year-old Lena jumping with their cousins, Savannah and Isla Phillips, though it sounds like her eldest is not entirely keen to heed her mother’s advice.

‘I said something to Mia, she just rolled her eyes. I literally said the same thing to the other guys; they didn't roll their eyes at me,’ Zara recounted: ‘I think I was probably like that with my mother and father too.’

But Mia is certainly putting in the hard work. Last month, she took the reins to compete in the Blenheim Horse Trials. With proud mother Zara watching on from the sidelines, Mia rode her horse, Bunny Two Twirls, at the Heythrop Pony Club Blenheim Palace Show Jumping competition. The young Tindall already appeared to be a seasoned competitor, wearing a smart traditional riding uniform and boots, with her blonde hair tied up beneath her helmet.

And young Mia’s love of equestrianism goes beyond simply competing. While Zara Tindall was also busy at the Horse Trials, her daughter was spotted grooming her mother’s horse, Classicals Euro Star, between events. The Olympic silver medalist also coached her daughter through some jumps, no doubt offering up the years of competition practice which saw her claim a stunning victory at the British Open Championships in August – though any eye rolls were not photographed…
