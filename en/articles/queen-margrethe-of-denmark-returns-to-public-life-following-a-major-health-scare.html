Queen Margrethe of Denmark, 86, returns to public life alongside her son, King Frederik, following a major health scare

The former monarch, who was hospitalised in September, joined King Frederik, Queen Mary and Princess Benedikte at Fredensborg Church

By Isaac Bickerstaff · October 8, 2026

Cayetano Martínez de Irujo, 4th Duke of Arjona, was supported by his aristocratic family as he exchanged vows with Bárbara Mirjan on 7th October 2025

Queen Margrethe of Denmark has returned to public duties following a major health scare in September. The former monarch, 86, was rushed to hospital on 6 September and was forced to miss a number of engagements as she recovered. Royal watchers will be relieved, therefore, to see her cut a characteristically elegant figure at a concert commemorating the 300th anniversary of Fredensborg Church on Wednesday.

Queen Margrethe joined her sister, Princess Benedikte, her son, King Frederik X, and his wife, Queen Mary, in a lavish box to watch the event, which included works by Bach, Hasse and Purcell. Princess Benedikte, 82, has been standing in for her sister during a number of engagements, including the opening of parliament on Tuesday.

Known for her playful and stylish approach to regal dressing, Queen Margrethe donned an electric-blue ensemble, completing her outfit with some gold earrings and an artistic brooch seeming to bear her initial. Queen Margrethe has a deeply personal connection to the church, playing a key role in the building’s restorations during the 1970s. The then-monarch, who is known for her love of illustration and costume design, helped mastermind decorative decisions and crafted three liturgical vestments, which were embroidered by Princess Benedikte and her mother, Queen Ingrid.

Last Saturday, she was awarded the ‘Prize of Honour’ by ALT Magazine to mark the publication’s 80th anniversary. These outings come after she spent a month recuperating from mild dehydration and a blood clot in her hip. The Danish royal palace announced that Queen Margrethe had been hospitalised on 6 September, describing her as ‘in good spirits and, given the circumstances, doing well.’ Sadly, it meant that she was unable to attend the funeral of her friend, King Harald of Norway, on 9 September.

The news was of great concern, especially considering Queen Margrethe’s two previous hospitalisations this year. In May, she was admitted after suffering from severe chest pain. A few days later, she was readmitted after doctors discovered a blood clot in her hip region. On both occasions, Queen Margrethe was discharged after a few days.

The hugely popular Queen Margrethe reigned for 52 years and is credited with modernising the Danish monarchy. She has, in recent years, suffered a number of health battles, including a major back surgery in 2023.

She is adored by the public, who branded her the ‘ashtray queen’ thanks to her smoking habit, and the royal families of Europe, many of whom know her as ‘Aunt Daisy’. As a nod to this sweet nickname – used by her third cousin once removed, King Felipe of Spain – she can often be spotted wearing brooches and earrings in the shape of the flower.

In 2022, Margrethe made headlines around the world when she made the explosive decision to strip four of her grandchildren – the offspring of her younger son – of their royal titles, almost immediately demoting them from Princes and a Princess to Counts and a Countess.

But Margrethe, who celebrated her 86th birthday on 16 April, has always been an unconventional monarch.

The first Danish queen by birth (the laws of male-preference primogeniture were overturned by her father), Margrethe has also worked as a scenographer, a costume designer, and an illustrator of works by J. R. R. Tolkien.

Margrethe was the first of three daughters born to Crown Prince Frederick and Crown Princess Ingrid of Denmark (later King Frederick IX and Queen Ingrid). Her sisters are Queen Anne-Marie of Greece, wife of Constantine II, the last King of Greece, and Princess Benedikte of Denmark. Margrethe was also a distant cousin of Queen Elizabeth II, and the pair shared a close relationship.

In addition to her role as a constitutional monarch, Margrethe has also pursued a career of sorts in the art world. In 2021, it was announced that she was a set designer for the fantasy film, Ehrengard, while she previously worked on the costumes for the stage shows of The Nutcracker and The Snow Queen. She even illustrated the Danish editions of Lord of the Rings, with author J.R.R. Tolkien impressed by her style. Considering her love of the arts, it’s no surprise she is also known for eccentric dress sense, frequently wearing brightly coloured and printed ensembles.

No doubt royal watchers hope to see her return to this passion as soon as she is able.
