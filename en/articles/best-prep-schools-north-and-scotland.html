Edit Schools Guide 2027: The best prep schools in the North and Scotland

The 2027 Edit Schools Guide reveals the best prep schools in the North and Scotland

By Edit · September 29, 2026

Crieff, Perthshire PH7 4EX

Website ardvreckschool.co.uk Head Ali Kinge Pupils 132 day and boarding: 71 boys, 61 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £8,815; boarding: £12,645

The Auld Alliance is alive and well at Ardvreck where – thanks to a word-of-mouth recommendation in chic Paris 10 years ago – a steady stream of French children brings cosmopolitan charm to this little Highland school. Nonetheless, most pupils here remain proudly Scottish, and this unflashy and feisty establishment is all about the outdoors. Snowsuits and wellies abound, and every year the whole school, including parents and staff, completes the demanding 11-mile Ard Challenge hike. ‘Not all class-rooms have walls’ are watchwords, as followed by head Ali Kinge, who started as a part-time music teacher at Ardvreck 20 years ago. (‘She’s fun, fair and approachable,’ says one parent.) In 2022, Kinge launched the Scottish Adventure School, offering thrilling out-of-term trips for state-school pupils, too. ‘Ardvreck is full of adventure,’ says one happy pupil. ‘We crawl through mud and hide in bushes.’

Newton-le-Willows, Bedale, North Yorkshire DL8 1TF

Website aysgarthschool.com Head Jonathon Anderson Pupils 232 day and boarding: 162 boys, 70 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £11,250; boarding: £14,640

‘Aysgarth may have a traditional reputation, but in our experience, it is far from old-fashioned or narrow,’ declares one parent, citing the brilliant head, the superb food and the fact that playing instruments – even the bagpipes – ‘is considered cool’. When they’re not preparing for the future in the new IT suite and STEM space, its children can get grubby while charging around the extensive grounds, ‘a landscape of adventure’, with secret tunnels in the walled gardens. More energy can be burnt off in the 60 clubs, which span camp craft to cooking. The school recently became co-ed, and the girls’ provision is thriving. With all the boarding places taken, capacity is yet again being extended to meet the demand.

Dunbar, East Lothian EH42 1NN

Website belhavenhill.com Head Neil Tomlin Pupils 147 day and boarding: 87 boys, 59 girlsAges 4–13 Term Fees Day: £11,100; boarding: £13,600

Idyllic coastal vistas, 20 acres of parkland, a hearty outdoor lifestyle: pupils and parents love ‘Bel-heaven’. The children here enjoy an annual Belhaven Bay Day on the beach as well as the Three Rivers Run, when all year groups ‘romp’ across it and through three tidal streams. Since more than 60 per cent of Scotland’s prep-aged boarders reside here, there’s an abundance of fun. But all this jollity translates into top results, with 26 scholarships won last year to such destinations as Oundle and Fettes. Despite there being no formal school motto, outgoing head Oliver Langton asserts that ‘Jump In’ would certainly get his vote; so the thermally sensitive will be cheered to know that funding is nearly complete for a new roof over the swimming pool. Shiver no more!

Gamekeeper’s Road, Edinburgh EH4 6HU

Website cargilfield.com Head Colin MacIntosh Pupils 290 day and boarding: 151 boys, 139 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £8,995; boarding: £192 per week

Not only has Cargilfield been busy restoring the grave of its founding head, it’s also welcomed a new head in Colin MacIntosh, who inherits a school that prides itself on its close-knit and nurturing ethos. ‘From the moment I walked in the door, it felt like a family,’ says one parent. (And they continue: ‘I send my children there every day, confident that I am doing the very best I can for them.’) The only standalone prep school in Edinburgh offers plenty of special moments, from enjoying books around the library’s open fire to relishing the great outdoors at the annual Beach Day. The Band of Pipes and Drums is great fun, as is the spring concert celebrating Scottish music. Little wonder the kilt is part of the uniform.

Cundall, York YO61 2RW Website cundallmanorschool.com Head Simon Weale Pupils 300 day and boarding: 160 boys, 140 girls Ages 2–16 Term Fees Day: £9,250; boarding: £11,240

Parents praise Cundall Manor’s ‘work-hard-play-hard philosophy’, its ‘approachable’ and ‘passionate’ head Simon Weale, and its ‘strong sense of “free-range” learning’. Certainly, days spent climbing trees and sledging are part of the tradition here; and the annual go-kart race sees pupils tumbling down the hills in handmade chariots to the wild cheers of their peers. Performers also revel in theatrical challenges – pulling off Oliver With A Twist in a week was a real feat – while musicians play a whole host of events, from carol concerts to Speech Days. Small class sizes, a zero phone policy and regular mixing between the year groups all contribute to a warm, collaborative atmosphere.

Newton, Stocksfield, Northumberland NE43 7TP

Website mowdenhall.org.uk Head Phil Sturt Pupils 212 day and boarding: 120 boys, 92 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £8,750; boarding: £10,750

Drive up the long track to Mowden Hall and you’ll be observed by friendly sheep peering over the fences; and despite being just 15 miles from Newcastle, the school emanates a sense of rural calm. The boys and girls (generally, but not exclusively, drawn from across the North and Scotland) are given free rein to explore any budding interest, be it golf, the dissection club or simply getting muddy and splashing in puddles. In 2026, Mowden Hall is the largest it’s ever been, its 200-strong pupil body now able to start from two years old at The Nest nursery – launched last September – and leave after Year 8, when pupils head to destinations as varied as Sedbergh in the North or to Eton and Radley in the South.

Baldersby Park, Topcliffe, North Yorkshire YO7 3BZ

Website queenmarys.org Head Fe Beadnell Pupils 221 day and boarding: 219 girls, 2 boysAges 3–16 Term Fees Day: £10,417; boarding: £14,358

Since September 2025, Fe Beadnell has been head of this school housed in a Grade I-listed mansion in the rolling Yorkshire Dales. She took on an establishment well known for its great strengths in riding – girls can bring their own horses to the soon-to-expand equestrian centre, and the school team are Grassroots and County champions – as well as music. (A new auditorium is on the cards.) But that’s not all it has to offer: the children revel in ski trips to Les Deux Alpes, a whole-school inter-house fashion show every March, and Christmas carols in Ripon Cathedral; and parents note how happy their daughters are that they can stay on until 16. ‘Queen Mary’s has a magic that is hard to replicate,’ says one.

Abbey Park, Melrose, Roxburghshire TD6 9LN

Website stmarysmelrose.org.uk Head William Harvey Pupils 154 day and boarding: 74 boys, 80 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £8,383; four-night weekly boarding: +£1,140

‘Kind’ is a word that comes up again and again when insiders talk about St Mary’s Melrose, both in the school’s own description of its ideal pupil and in the students’ assessments of the head William Harvey. Sport is central here, as attested by last year’s unbeaten hockey and rugby seasons, its Rugby 7s titles at U13 and U11 levels, and the new all-weather cricket nets that are on the cards. For the last 10 years, St Mary’s has run Bang Goes The Borders, a much-loved free science festival aimed at families and attracting up to 1,000 participants; and a new 5HiVe facility has been equipped with 40 iPads, VR headsets, a 3D printer and a green screen.

Terrington, York YO60 6PR

Website terringtonhall.com Head Huw Thomas Pupils 202 day: 101 boys, 101 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £7,887; boarding: £44 per night

A pleasing 101 boys and 101 girls attend this small rural school in the beautiful Howardian Hills of North Yorkshire and Terrington Hall has the reputation of being a close-knit community whose parents look forward to friendly champagne receptions, and whose children enjoy inter-year lunches alongside their teachers. The latter seems a recipe for outgoing pupils; our researcher started off being shown around by two Year 7s but picked up a handful more along the way, ‘all eager to show me their favourite parts’. As for head Huw Thomas, he is ‘the school’s best asset’, according to one parent. (Another tells us he is ‘sensible, enthusiastic, calm and astute’.) Interestingly, he chooses to serve match teas himself; but given the high esteem in which the refreshments are held (‘as good for the parents as they are for the children’, one mother says), that may be a politically astute move. One pupil we spoke to had only two requests: ‘More art, please, and more sausage rolls!’
