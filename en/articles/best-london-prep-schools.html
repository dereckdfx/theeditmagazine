Edit Schools Guide 2027: The best prep schools in London

The 2027 Edit Schools Guide reveals the best prep schools in London

By Tori Cadogan · September 29, 2026

60 Bassett Road W10 6JP

Website bassetths.org.uk Head Pádraic Fahy Pupils 110 day: 54 boys, 56 girls Ages 2–11 Term Fees £9,537

Bassett House was founded nearly 80 years ago in a family residence and still takes pride in its cosy home-from-home feel. This doesn’t come at the cost of dynamism, though: from the annual art exhi-bition to the popular ballroom dancing club, there’s plenty to keep the children both occupied and stretched. Musicians compete in Battle of the Bands competitions, riders trot off to the local stables for lessons, and the acting contingent recently delivered an energetic performance of Roald Dahl’s Revolting Rhymes. The nursery now accepts two year olds, who expand their language skills in French and Spanish lessons, wind down with yoga and connect with nature in the area for outdoor learning.

10–13 Prince’s Gardens SW7 1ND

Website brightoncollegeprep kensington.co.uk Head Lois Gaffney Pupils 335 day: 162 boys, 173 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees £12,489

Perhaps it is only natural that a school located in the heart of South Kensington’s museum district,with landmarks such as Imperial College and Hyde Park as neighbours, would turn out pupils who have been inspired to think big, with an emphasis on confidence, curiosity and kindness as corevalues. To spark a youthful spirit of enterprise, this prep offers a two-year Mini MBA programme, which includes practical projects with input from industry leaders, while chess is an integral part of a BCPS education and is taught as a curriculum subject from Reception onwards in partnership with a chess academy. Last year’s leavers received a total of 75 offers from 39 top senior schools. Little wonder this relatively new establishment, opened in 2023, has seen remarkable growth (from 120 to 335 places in the past two years). September sees the opening of bespoke new premises for Years 6 to 8, which will feature full Apple integration, an entrepreneurship lab, a sports science programme, a fitness suite and more. Watch this space.

68–74 Nightingale Lane SW12 8NR

Website broomwood.com Heads Michael Hodge (principal and boys), Louisa McCafferty (girls), Caron Mackay (pre-prep) Pupils 524 day: 279 boys, 245 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees £10,512

Broomwood is well known for its distinctive approach to education – girls and boys share classes until Year 3, when they move to single-gender zones – guided by the belief that this offers the best of both worlds. Its new facilities include Astroturf pitches, libraries, adventure playgrounds and drama halls popping up across the four sites, all of which are in walking distance of each other. Another plus for parents is the length of the days. Home-work and activities take place at school, meaning that the family abode is a place of relaxation rather than a chore-fuelled wrestling ground. The clever cohort of recent leavers scored an impressive 39 scholarships to senior schools.

Luxemburg Gardens W6 7EA Website butehouse.co.uk Head Sian Bradshaw Pupils 325 day girls Ages 4–11 Term Fees £10,560

It’s not easy to enrol at Bute House. Access is granted to the nursery by means of a random 400-name ballot for just over 20 places; and 7+ assessments lead to 40 girls being selected from 200 applicants. Those lucky enough to gain admission can enjoy a fantastic range of facilities – including a large playing field and huge swimming pool – while the new Safe Space, which is dedicated to wellbeing, offers meditation and yoga alongside one-on-one couns-elling. As for artistic endeavours, the high note of the calendar is the Christmas musical celebration atSt Paul’s Church in Hammersmith; and theatre-lovers have plenty of opportunities to perform and learn the ropes backstage. Of the culture, says one pupil, ‘I love it.’ And she concludes: ‘The ethos is “Every girl runs her own best race.”’

47 Connaught Square W2 2HL

Website connaughthouseschool.co.uk Head Victoria Hampton Pupils 67 day: 31 boys, 36 girls Ages 4–11 Term Fees £9,382

There are just 67 pupils at this family-owned little prep near Hyde Park, so life here is understandably close-knit. (Says our insider parent of the high levels of individual attention: ‘It is as close as it gets to homeschooling, yet within a formal environment.’) Based in a Grade II- listed Georgian building, the school also takes full advantage of its location through sports in the park and trips to the British Museum and the British Library. And with one teacher observing that poetry has ‘a special place in the curriculum’ – as evidenced by the spring term recitation competitions – the latter is clearly rubbing off. Families have also responded keenly to raise the profile of Reading for Pleasure, both at home and in the classroom. In other developments, the science programme has been revamped; and an enrichment Carnival Day in February excitingly combined hands-on STEM activities with Winter Olympics-themed sports.

38–42 Alleyn Park SE21 7AA

Website dulwichprepsenior.org.uk Head Louise Davidson Pupils 808 day and boarding: 789 boys, 23 girls Ages 2–16 Term Fees Day: £10,085; weekly boarding: £13,385; flexi-boarding: £75 per night

At Dulwich Prep & Senior school, ‘love’ is the first of its eight core values, and this one shapes a culture in which every type of student is welcomed and encouraged to flourish. To that end, the facilities are impressively broad, from the large sports hall with climbing wall and cricket nets to the refurbished swimming pool, the bright art studio and the design technology workshop equipped with laser cutters and 3D printers. Music is clearly in the ascendant, with more than 550 individual lessons given every week. Academic support for children is framed as ‘skills and strategies for learning’, a practice that builds confidence, independence and resilience. One parent says the ‘highly empathetic and tailored approach’ is one of the school’s greatest strengths. In a popular move, the school now caters to Years 9, 10 and 11 (their numbers limited so students can receive a tailored journey beyond the prep years).

58 Clapham Common North Side SW4 9RU

Website eatonhouseschools.com Head David Wingfield (boys), Claire Fildes (girls) Pupils 607 day: 401 boys, 206 girls Ages 4–13 (boys), 4–11 (girls) Term Fees £10,500 (boys), £10,220 (girls)

Parents who are uncertain about whether to choose a single-sex or co-ed prep-school education for their offspring would do well to consider Eaton House The Manor. With separate boys’ and girls’ premises on the same site, it offers both. In a wonderful setting, too: opposite Clapham Common, which is used for both sport and play. A visitor discovered from the Latin teacher that the children here are ‘really all like Labradors’ (kind, loyal, energetic and voracious of appetite: there are certainly worse attributes). And luckily for them, according to one pupil, the food is delicious, with Friday’s fish and chips being a treat that is eagerly looked forward to.

55–57 Eccleston Square SW1V 1PH

Website eatonsquareschools.com Head Patricia Watt Pupils 223 day: 123 boys, 100 girls Ages 2–11 Term Fees £11,495

Eaton Square has introduced an initiative called Square Roots: a weekly stay-and-play day for children prior to their joining. This helps them become familiar with a family atmosphere and ethos described by one parent as ‘deeply nurturing… combined with strong academic foundations’ (which have been strengthened by new Quest assessments: a suite of progress-tracking tools that result in highly individual reports, informing next steps). Sports facilities range from the Queen Elizabeth Sports Centre in Victoria to the Royal Parks, while swimming is the feather in thecap. The annual transformation of Eaton Square into a winter wonderland remains a cherished tradition.

Trott Street SW11 3DS

Website lecoledebattersea.co.uk Head Cecilia Beylefeld Principal Laurent Balerdi Pupils 260 day: 132 boys, 128 girls Ages 3–11 Term Fees £7,089

L’École de Battersea has a proudly independent attitude towards its curriculum and spirit. And parents love the non-corporate, family-focused feel of this small bilingual school, replete with ‘Franglophile quirks that give it personality’ (such as the striped aprons worn by pre-prep pupils). With Wednesday afternoons free, as in France, pupils flit effortlessly between languages – and excel at fencing, which head Cecilia Beylefeld has taken it upon herself to learn. Each class is ledby two teachers – one English, one French – so children are exposed to the culture and learning styles of both countries. But the aim is not just fluency. Rather, l’École’s bilingual teaching seeks to form minds that can adapt effortlessly to different worlds. ‘It combines the best of the French and English educational systems in a seamless blend,’ concludes one parent.

218–220 Lambeth Road SE1 7JY

Website fairleyhouse.org.uk Interim head Marc Broughton Pupils 75 day: 44 boys, 31 girlsAges 6–13 Term Fees £16,756

Asked to describe its typical pupil, Fairley House replies: ‘There really is no such thing as an ideal student here.’ Which is a refreshing and honest answer from an institution that specialises in maximising the potential and happiness of pupils with dyslexia and other specific learning difficulties (SpLDs). Homework is done at school, so it is supported. Established on the back of a charity event compered by Billy Connolly in 1982, Fairley House has grown ever since, and planning permission has now been granted for a bespoke new senior school in Lambeth. Highlights have included trips to Tate Britain and the Royal Academy – to see the Summer Exhibition – as well as putting on productions of Oliver Twist and The Lion King, and extracurricular activities flourish too. As the school motto declares: ‘All of a sudden, nothing is impossible’.

20 Penywern Road SW5 9SU; 19 Brechin Place SW7 4QB

Website falknerhouse.co.uk Head Eleanor Dixon (boys), Flavia Rogers (girls) Pupils 349 day: 163 boys, 186 girls Ages 3–11 Term Fees £10,033 (boys); £9,940 (girls)

Everything at family-owned Falkner House is child-centred; even the delightfully bright and airy buildings themselves, which are filled with pupils’ artwork. This is on display in the prep’s virtual exhibition space, too, while their creators are regularly taken into London to visit physical galleries. The emphasis on pastoral care is reflected in the weekly school council meetings as well as in the warm sense of familiarity, which means that all staff, whether they work in the kitchen or the classroom, know the pupils by name. ‘They genuinely care about them,’ notes one mother, who was also attracted by the down-to-earth attitudes of her prospective peers: no ‘hysterical PTA’; no ‘flash families’ or ones ‘falling over to outdo each other’. Instead, there’s a caring, grounded community of teachers, parents and children.

15 Manresa Road SW3 6NB

Website francishollandprep.org.uk Head Suzy Dixon Pupils 253 day girls Ages 4–11 Term Fees £10,995

Unsurprisingly, demand is high for this all-girls prep housed in the historic former Chelsea Library, freshly restored to include ‘some of the most beautiful spaces in education’, according to a recent visitor. There are four applicants per place at 4+ entry; student numbers are steadily increasing; and those who make the cut couldn’t be happier. One fun accolade was winning an RHS Gold Award in last year’s Chelsea in Bloom competition for a floral display made from recycled uniform. Pupils particularly love the school’s diversity, ‘the way that everybody is very kind and welcoming’, the ‘unmatched’ level of support – and head Suzy Dixon. (‘She has always put a smile on my face,’ one student tells us.) Though the news of her impending retirement came as something of a blow, the parent community can’t wait to see what fresh energy her replacement Laura Rodgers will bring when she joins in January 2027 from Glendower.

200 Greyhound Road W14 9SD

Website fulhamprep.co.uk Head Thomas Lewis Pupils 325 day: 200 boys, 125 girls Ages 1–13 Term Fees £9,180

‘Charming’, ‘warm’, filled with ‘obviously happy, delightful’ pupils: these are a few of the ways recent visitors have described Fulham Prep. Now established for 30 years, the school is best-known among local families for offering a rounded education that gives equal weight to academic excellence, the arts and sports. All pupils have weekly PE, games and swimming lessons; nearly half learn an instrument; and most get involved in drama productions. There’s a rich co-curricular programme that encompasses everything from girls’ football to the entrepreneurial Fulham Traders club; and a new nursery means children can join at the age of one, stay all the way to Year 8 and then continue their learning journeys at top boarding schools.

Turks Row SW3 4TW

Website gardenhouseschool.co.uk Head Richard Lock (boys), Emma Studd (girls), Venetia Banbury (Early Years) Pupils 398 day: 178 boys, 220 girls Ages 3–11 Term Fees £12,202

Music plays over a tannoy as pupils arrive every morning. That sets the tone for a place overwhelmingly endowed with positivity. The grass is green on both sides here, with boys and girls separated for studies but brought together for assemblies, meals, clubs – and birthday parties (much appreciated by parents). Last year marked 75 years since the school’s founding, but all seem prouder of the fact that they also celebrated 25 years of their Kindness Code (Garden Housewas the first school to actively put thoughtfulness at its centre). The motto – Not for Oneself But for All – is a key element of the code and ‘underpins all we do’, say the heads.

86–87 Queen’s Gate SW7 5JX

Website glendowerprep.org Head Claire Boyd Pupils 270 day girls Ages 4–11 Term Fees £10,975

‘What I love most is that we are all in one community, no matter who you are,’ reflects one pupil. Another believes this sense of camaraderie is fostered by teachers ‘who always have time to let us talk to them’. But alongside all the good vibrations, there’s a focus on preparing the girls for the rigours of the outside world. The new Glen X: Future Skills programme provides training in algorithmic and digital literacy, financial education and climate awareness, while those in Year 5 now pursue Independent Project Qualifications, diving into topics in depth. Following an upgrade of the library, plans are afoot to overhaul the science labs and art studios, and to build a space where older girls can prepare for life at senior school.

23 Crossfield Road NW3 4NU

Website hallschool.co.uk Head Henry Keighley-Elstub Pupils 470 day boys Ages 4–13 Term Fees £10,710

The Hall certainly produces strong results. From their chosen destinations, the recent cohort of leavers scored an impressive total of 15 scholarships – seven academic, five for music, one for design and technology – and two sports awards. The approach to developing well-rounded pupils is clear: ‘While the school is ambitious and selective, once a boy joins, the focus is firmly on understanding him as an individual and helping him reach his potential,’ says one parent. Sport is played enthusiastically on the playing fields in Finchley (and on tours to such far-flung cricket hot spots as Sri Lanka) while actors enjoy workshops with West End dancers and puppeteers. The jazz band plays at the iconic Soho Pizza Express and pottery is popular. As one boy says, ‘We have so many opportunities to find our talents and what we love.’

Hearnville Road SW12 8RS

Website hornsbyhouse.org.uk Head Edward Rees Pupils 433 day: 226 boys, 207 girls Ages 4–11 Term Fees £8,486

Sharing hot chocolate and cookies with the head is the reward for children who win prizes for embodying the Hornsby values of ‘head, heart and spirit’. This is a friendly, tight community, praised by one mother for having a ‘great network and social life among children and parents’ (aided by most families living within walking distance). As for its head, Edward Rees is said to go above and beyond, with the parents of an eight-year-old praising his involvement in their onward-school discussions, despite being years from a final decision. (‘He took the time to read my son’s reports, speak to his teachers and get to know us as a family,’ they say.) Physical prowess is taken seriously; and a record number of sports scholarships were awarded to 2025 leavers, who headed to the likes of JAGS, Alleyn’s and Woldingham.

596 Fulham Road SW6 5PA

Website kensingtonprep.gdst.net Head Rachel Floyd Pupils 300 day girls Ages 4–11Term Fees £9,183

‘We love seeing smiley, happy KPS girls whizzing along Fulham Road with their trombones, gym bags, LAMDA scripts and much more,’ says the parent of two Kensington Prep pupils. It’s clear the girls are confident and grounded, focused and enquiring; and you only have to watch their sports, eavesdrop on their classes or listen to one of their accomplished choirs and ensembles to realise this school is special. Music is a particular strength, and the arrival of new head Rachel Floyd – an English lit and music graduate – has lifted the standard of recent performances and concerts even higher. What’s more, KP holds a Gilt Primary Science Quality Mark; philosophy is taught from Year 1; and for a central London school, its facilities, including vast playing fields and dedicated art and drama studios, are exceptional.

67 Pont Street SW1X 0BD

Website knightsbridgeschool.com Head Adam Anstey Pupils 309 day: 161 boys, 148 girls Ages 3–16 Term Fees £11,650

Parents choose Knightsbridge School, which has six applicants for every place, on account of its ‘focus on encouraging children to discover and develop their unique talents’, and the fact that its pupils seem ‘happy and free to express themselves’. And the busy roster of 70 extracurricular activities per week – from coding to a journalism club run by a former national news broadcaster, no less – lends itself to plenty of self-expression. Elsewhere, the new KS Plus programme challenges pupils to extend themselves beyond the curriculum, while the Entrepreneur Competition sees finalists selling their products (which must be sustainable) to the community. Clever pupils scoop up awards in national maths and essay competitions and prove the school motto of ‘Work Hard, Play Hard’ to be very apt indeed.

149 Battersea Park Road SW8 4BX

Website newtonprepschool.co.uk Head Graeme McCafferty Pupils 641 day: 346 boys, 295 girls Ages 4–13 Term Fees £10,608

‘Unpretentious’ Newton Prep is loved by South London parents, of whom one calls it ‘a shining light in preparatory education’. Another using less poetic language describes it as ‘academically rigorous without being a sausage factory’. Games are played on-site on the all-weather pitch and in three sports halls, and older pupils get to choose which they play. Meanwhile, a new Stage Sessions series provides a platform for budding thesps to flex their skills; Year 6s exhibit their art every year; and the much-loved tradition of the red-carpeted N’Oscars sees pupil-made films awarded prizes in different categories, with Year 8s acting as paparazzi and security.

162–166 Holland Park Avenue W11 4UH

Website norlandplace.com Head Patrick Mattar Pupils 120 day: 50 boys, 70 girls Ages 7–11 Term Fees £9,574

Norland Place describes its ideal pupil as a ‘polite and interested’ child ‘with stories’, and claims its charges not only ‘have amazing stories’, but ‘are interested in the world around them’. There are ample initiatives intended to stoke creativity and curiosity here. The head of drama personally pens all the plays and musicals (some of which are held in Notting Hill’s wonderful Coronet Theatre) while budding musicians jam in the rock band or spin discs in the new DJ and production club. Artists can paint and draw in a new studio; and as part of a broader development designed to support growing numbers, a music room is also in the works. What would pupils change about their school as it turns 150 years old? ‘I wish,’ muses one, ‘that we could sleep here once a week.’

95 Lancaster Road W11 1QQ

Website nottinghillprep.com Head Maxine Scully Pupils 358 day: 180 boys, 178 girls Ages 4–13 Term Fees £10,496

Notting Hill Prep describes itself as a place where children can celebrate marvellous mistakes and think creatively outside the box. At this Thinking School (an accreditation, not a figure of speech), the curriculum is designed to inspire original thought and deep reflection – and fittingly, the motto is ‘To Think, To Thrive’. Mixed sports are now firmly embedded, while music and drama provide plenty of opportunities for pupils to shine. Every year, the children enter a charity art competition that raises money for the Institute of Child Health at Great Ormond Street, while many extracurriculars are also concerned with creativity, be it in jewellery making, textile design or film club.

Priory Avenue, W4 1TX

Website orchardhs.org.uk Head Henrietta Adams Pupils 331 day: 143 boys, 188 girls Ages 3–11 Term Fees £9,427

‘The teachers teach so well,’ says one joy-filled pupil at this non-selective Chiswick co-ed. ‘Everyone is so nice; and if I ever get into a disagreement with my friends, it will be resolved in a flash. That’s how great my friends are!’ Working closely with such local cultural organisations as the Tabard Theatre and Chiswick Book Festival, Orchard House preaches the power of the written word – it’s integrated Reading Scholar and Reading Explorer programmes into its curriculum – while its Innovation Hub allows children to explore STEM in a variety of brilliant ways, from Lego Spike challenges to experimentation with podcasting and 3D printing. Recognising that learning continues outside school, its next push is to host more talks for parents, to show how high-performance habits can be nurtured at home.

80 Clapham Common North Side SW4 9SD

Website parkgate-school.co.ukHead Malcolm McKinlayPupils 160 day: 81 boys, 79 girls Ages 2½–11 Term Fees £8,969

Parkgate House prides itself on combining the bespoke approach of a small school with the opportunities of a bigger one, and the strategy is clearly paying off. The educational visits programme has been expanded, with trips to the House of Lords and the Royal Opera House (where, incidentally, one of its music scholars was chosen to perform in the Children’s Chorus in the 2024/25 production of Carmen). And pupils have achieved strong results in such difficult-sounding contests as the Leaders Award Primary Engineers competition and the European Championship Geography Bee. ‘Our head Mr McKinlay is just the nicest because he is helpful, caring and supportive,’ reports one pupil, who also has high praise for the food (pasta and meatballs, chicken schnitzel), the uniform and the weekly riding lessons.

18 Pembridge Square W2 4EH

Website pembridgehall.co.uk Head Sophie Banks Pupils 426 day girls Ages 4–11 Term Fees £11,290

‘They say you will know that you’ve found the right school for your child when you step through the door, and this was 100 per cent the case for us with Pembridge Hall,’ says one mother, who felt that the homely atmosphere was conveyed even by the ‘gorgeous teal carpet on the stairs’ (a step up from the ‘drab grey carpet squares’ often associated with educational establishments). Here, there’s a new Friendology curriculum to support friendship; the swimming and netball squads are raising the bar; and an investors’ club is thriving. Up next? A drive to embed financial literacy in the whole curriculum, from Reception to Year 6. Indeed, an innovative drive to instil an entrepreneurial mindset through the Thinking, Enterprise and Design weeks permeates school life. A traditional girls’ prep with a forward-looking outlook, Pembridge is surely shaping the Girl Bosses of the future.

75 Putney Hill SW15 3NT

Website prospecths.org.uk Head Kelly Gray Pupils 269 day: 130 boys, 139 girls Ages 3–11 Term Fees £9,295

Prospect House attributes the impressive 123 scholarships its leavers have scored in the past five years to its tailored learning approach, with teachers identifying students’ strengths and areas of growth, then carving bespoke pathways across STEAM and sports. The new Year 6 classroom is set to improve the tutelage further – and is being followed by a new library and a sports studio – while the introduction of VR goggles has opened up endless possibilities for ‘field trips’. There’s plenty of fun, too; last year’s inaugural staff panto proved so popular with parents that a second date was added. A friendly, nurturing ethos underpins everything; for example, each week, children who have displayed particular kindness, respect and/or resilience are nominated by staff members to have hot chocolate with the head. Served with both whipped cream and marsh-mallows, it’s a firm favourite.

16 Ravenscourt Avenue W6 0SL

Website rpps.co.uk Head Tim Smith Pupils 422 day: 218 boys, 204 girls Ages 4–11 Term Fees £9,048

Incoming head Tim Smith inherits a thriving, happy school which strives to combine both academic achievement and warmhearted fun. Indeed, parents think it’s moving ‘from strength to strength’ and praise its ability to deftly manage school life to combine traits such as academic ambition with kindness; structure with creativity; tradition with modern thinking. Surprisingly, their children are in wholehearted agreement (although one thinks the uniform should dispense with ties, quite reasonably pointing out that ‘grown-ups don’t wear them that much’.) With the green spaces of Ravenscourt Park on the doorstep, there are plenty of opportunities for sports; thus, pickleball was recently introduced, and the prep was alone in entering a mixed gender team into the Rosslyn Park Sevens.

32 Belsize Lane NW3 5AE

Website stchristophers.londonHead Mark MaddocksPupils 254 day girls Ages 4–11 Term Fees £8,793

Founded in 1883 by two sisters in their father’s drawing room, north London’s St Christopher’s prides itself on pupils who are curious, open to challenges and have a zest for fun. Cups are awarded for creative endeavour – even in maths, science and PE – while every new child is given a notebook (fondly known as a Jewel Book), in which they paste pieces of work so that they have a unique record of their learning across the years when they leave. Meanwhile, St Christopher’s has curated its own metacognition programme – inspired by Harvard’s – focused on helping girls become more reflective learners. And a new bespoke pastoral initiative aims to help them understand and manage their own wellbeing.

Earsby Street W14 8SH

Website stjamesprep.co.uk Head Hilary Wyatt Pupils 120 day: 62 boys, 58 girls Ages 0–11 Term Fees £8,615

From its quiet corner of west Kensington, St James radiates a strong sense of holistic wellness. Here, daily meditation practice and Sanskrit lessons are complemented by cookery classes and silk-printing sessions by forest school activities.Fully co-ed, with a new nursery offering – which from September will take babies from nine months old – the school is extending its sporting provision by making use of the facilities at the senior school site in Ashford, Surrey, where there is a climbing wall, basketball and tennis courts, a lake and playing fields. ‘It’s difficult to articulate the inherent magic,’ says one parent. ‘You need to feel it to understand it… In a world where everything happens so fast, the thoughtful, collaborative sharing of ideas here is so transparent and refreshing.’

Lonsdale Road SW13 9JT

Website stpaulsschool.org.uk Head Oliver Snowball Pupils 498 day boys Ages 7–13 Term Fees £10,221

Walking around St Paul’s Prep’s impressive new buildings, you feel the boys here really have been given childhood’s golden ticket. With their dark-green exteriors and abundance of glass, the classrooms and library enclose a beautiful central space known as ‘the heart’, while beyond spread 45 acres of grounds which contain a forest school, seating circles and a wonderful adventure playground. The boys share the seniors’ dining room, their incredible engineering space – housing robotics and the famous Firefly racing cars – and their enthusiasm for sport. (They field 40 rugby teams.) In drama, they’re encouraged to take creative risks; and almost half learn a musical instrument. One parent praises a ‘strong sense of inclusivity’ and the ‘encouragement and celebration of diverse talents’. Overall, this is a dynamic, innovative school steered by the popular Mr Snowball.

6 Wetherby Place SW7 4NE

Website stpschool.co.uk Head Alexander Thomas Pupils 79 day boys Ages 4–13 Term Fees £10,260

Small in numbers, this Catholic prep in Kensington defines itself as down-to-earth and a huge amount of fun, and parents agree: ‘Don’t expect to see frightened, obedient boys,’ advises one mother. ‘Rather, they’re confident and joyful.’ A pilgrimage to Rome is undertaken every five years, accompanied by brilliant food; the Conkers Cup is hotly contested for; and reading is incentivised. (The children ‘bank’ the total word counts of books they have finished and been quizzedon; and having been rewarded in assemblies, a handful are now word ‘multimillionaires’.) Families will be delighted to know that the school is opening its first ever Year 2 class in September, bridging the gap from Year 1 to its traditional entry point of Year 3, so boys can now start aged four and leave at 13.

68 Cadogan Square SW1X 0EA

Website sussexhouseschool.co.uk Head Nicholas Kaye Pupils 184 day boys Ages 8–13 Term Fees £11,820

Traditional yet original, Sussex House places enormous value on a whole education, balancing strong academics with myriad musical, artistic and sporting opportunities. And the upshot? Pupils bag numerous scholarships to senior schools, and a huge proportion head to Eton. But to return to the ‘extras’: running to 266 pages, its annual magazine The Cadogan is an incredible achievement; in sport, fencing is strong (last year’s captain of fencing was ranked No 1 on the U14 European fencing circuit); and more than 90 per cent of the boys have instrument or singing lessons. Clubs embrace debating, chess and architecture. For the latter, long-serving head Nicholas Kaye takes the boys on walks around Chelsea to admire its beautiful buildings.

28–40 Battersea High Street SW11 3JB

Website thomas-s.co.uk Head Rupert Hawkins Pupils 545 day: 305 boys, 240 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees £11,053

‘Be Kind’ is literally written in stone above the door here – and it’s not an empty phrase. This lively co-ed, which encourages children to aim high while looking out for one another, takes its lead from head Rupert Hawkins. And the energy he inspires spills out of the classroom and into the clubs, which range from chess, coding and fencing to the eco-minded ‘plogging’ group who jog around the streets picking up litter. Drama is strong – last year’s Spamalot featured some brilliant stage combat – and the prep’s dancing team won The Great Big Dance Off 2025. But pastoral care is front and centre, too; as evidenced by such thoughtful touches as a virtual worry box, available on pupils’ iPads, ensuring they always have a way to seek help.λλ

Broomwood Road SW11 6JZ

Website thomas-s.co.uk Head Jessica Mair Pupils 650 day: 333 boys, 317 girls Ages 4–13 Term Fees £10,306

The ‘attitude of awareness’ that Thomas’s Clapham claims to inculcate in its charges may sound a lofty ideal, but it translates into confident, cheerful children encouraged to think for themselves. And according to one parent, among the school’s greatest strengths ‘is the way Thomas’s children are encouraged to communicate confidently and respectfully with adults, while still being allowed the freedom to be children’. The atmosphere is lively: think Friday playground discos and three sculpted dairy cows improbably perched on the roof of the art block. Music thrives here, with around 350 lessons each week. And outdoor adventures are a big draw: Year 7 pupils head to Sussex to sleep in hammocks under the stars and cook on open fires, while Years 5 and 6 take to the slopes in Austria.

49 Hugon Road SW6 3ES

Website thomas-s.co.ukHead Annette Dobson Pupils 388 day: 205 boys, 183 girls Ages 4–11 Term Fees £10,578

‘The culture feels traditional in the sense that manners and kindness are emphasised, but the approach is forward-looking and modern,’ explains a parent. Indeed, the school motto (‘Be Kind, Be Thomas’s’) seems to permeate everything here, from its links to local foodbanksto the parent volunteer reading programme. In the sporting arena, cricket and football are played by both boys and girls, while budding artists regularly exhibit at RCA Young Art. A thriving drama scene sees children excel on stage and behind the scenes, while musicians have access to five choirs, 11 weekly ensembles and a packed calendar of performances during the year. ‘Our school is perfect,’ says one pupil, whose only suggestion would be to extend playtimes.
