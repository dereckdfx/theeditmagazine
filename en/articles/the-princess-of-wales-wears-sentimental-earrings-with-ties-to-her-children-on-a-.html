The Princess of Wales wears sentimental earrings on surprise visit to Northern Ireland

Catherine lit up a rainy day in Belfast with a pair of Cartier earrings that have fast become a staple in her royal jewellery box

By Isaac Bickerstaff · October 9, 2026

Once a glittering society retreat and later the scene of audacious wartime espionage, Trent Park is ready for its big reveal. The Marquess of Cholmondeley gives Edit a tour

It was a rainy day in Belfast as Prince William and the Princess of Wales made a surprise visit to Northern Ireland, where the royal couple spent the day on a series of engagements with Homewards UK.

Fortunately, Catherine brought a dose of sunshine to the occasion, donning an elegant ensemble of a ruffled white blouse and tartan skirt (thought to be from Emilia Wickstead), brought together by a statement belt and a navy blue long coat.

The future queen carried an umbrella to stay dry as she and the Prince of Wales met with organisations and young people to help provide belonging and opportunities to those at risk of homelessness. On their visit, the couple celebrated the success of an employment pilot supporting young people in finding and keeping jobs, launched by Prince William’s charity Homewards. Their Royal Highnesses learnt how a focus on sustainable employment and the fostering of strong social relationships assists young people to create stability, independence and a sense of purpose.

The Princess of Wales is in her element when working with young people, having dedicated much of her royal career to championing the importance of early years education and advocating for social intelligence. And while her own children were busy with school today – Prince George having just started at Eton College, while Princess Charlotte and Prince Louis remain studying at Lambrook School – Catherine found a way to keep them close, even across the Irish Sea.

How? A subtle, yet powerful, choice of jewellery. In addition to her characteristic ring stack, the Princess of Wales wore her Cartier ‘Trinity’ earrings. Crafted from three interweaving rings, the earrings honour ‘the magic number’ three: how better to make a subtle nod towards her three beloved children?

The iconic collection by the French label celebrated its 100th anniversary in 2024 and has a legion of famous fans: Grace Kelly loved her classic ring, as did Romy Schneider; actress Jacqueline Bisset, meanwhile, was often seen in a chunky bracelet from the range. Most poignantly perhaps, the choice could be seen as a nod to Catherine's mother-in-law, Princess Diana. The late Princess of Wales was fond of the ‘Trinity’ collection and owned a classic ring, which she wore on her little finger.

Stories abound about the origins of the design. Some point to its ancient Celtic roots, while others claim that the interlocking bands are descended from Russian wedding rings. Whatever the truth behind the fables, the legends persist – and the marriage of the three metals, combining different entities into one, has picked up its own symbolism. Indeed, those three hoops have come to represent endless meanings: past, present and future, for instance, as well as love, loyalty and friendship. Either of these interpretations would feel particularly apt for the Princess of Wales.

He’s the grandson of Grace Kelly, the eldest son of Prince Albert II of Monaco, and for 20 years he has kept his life private. But what happened when Alexandre Grimaldi went public?

Since making her debut in the earrings in January 2025, the Princess of Wales has turned to the Trinity pieces time and time again. The timing of that first appearance sparked speculation that it might have been a sentimental gift from Prince William to mark her birthday. On that occasion, the setting was particularly poignant: the Royal Marsden Hospital for her first solo engagement since she was diagnosed with cancer. The earrings were seen on a number of occasions that summer, including an outing in support of the Royal Foundation Centre for Early Childhood in June, and a hospital outing in July. Later that month came Wimbledon, when Catherine wore the earrings twice, for the men's and ladies' finals.

More recently, the Princess wore the earrings to drop off Prince George for his first day at Eton College in September, a poignant morning that saw the future heir follow in the footsteps of his father and Prince Harry. It would be understandable that Catherine wanted to honour Princess Diana's legacy on such a milestone occasion for the late royal's grandson.

Another recent staple for the Princess of Wales? Laura Lombardi’s ‘Portrait’ necklace. The gold chain has been in the Princess’s collection since 2022, when she debuted it during her state visit to Boston with Prince William. She also wore the chain during a meeting with textile manufacturers in Leeds in 2023 and an outing at the V&A East Storehouse in 2025.

But in recent weeks, the Princess of Wales has worn the necklace twice in quick succession. She was spotted wearing the statement gold chain at Eton and on an outing with the Forwards Trust in Southend-on-Sea, an engagement that also saw her don her Trinity earrings.

More than almost any royal before her, Catherine has made it clear that her work will not come at the cost of providing a normal upbringing for her children. Through her smart, subtle, and sentimental choice of jewels, she has shown time and time again that she can keep George, Charlotte, and Louis close to her heart while she is busy supporting the Crown.
