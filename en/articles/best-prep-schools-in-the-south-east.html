Edit Schools Guide 2027: The best prep schools in the South East

The 2027 Edit Schools Guide reveals the best prep schools in the South East

By Edit · September 29, 2026

Lombard Street, Shackleford, Godalming, Surrey GU8 6AS

Website aldro.org Head Chris Carlier Pupils 202 day and boarding: 129 boys, 73 girls Ages 7–13 Term Fees Day: £10,350; boarding: £15,750

‘The moment we walked through the school gates, we were sold,’ says one parent. ‘We could immediately envisage our son becoming the most confident, spirited and auth-entic version of himself.’ Such vitality and conviction are fostered by the resolute have-a-go culture that underpins life here. Pupils reach the school’s very own island by traversing a rope bridge across a lake; sport is played every day of the week but one; and achievements are rewarded with badges that are proudly sewn onto pupils’ sweaters. Recently, the art department’s two-day exhibition was a hit, while the Chapel Choir performed at both a Parisian church and Disneyland.Plus, the new wellbeing hub opened, a place for counselling as well as just taking a quieter moment. Most families are local, but the busy boarding house offers the Aldro experience to those whose families live further afield.

Lewes Road, East Grinstead, West Sussex RH19 3PD

Website brambletye.co.uk Head William Brooks Pupils 283 day and boarding: 154 boys, 129 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £10,825; boarding: £13,670

West Sussex provides the backdrop for the ‘wholesome, hands-on’ childhood offered by Brambletye, where ‘work hard, play hard, be kind’ are the core values. The great outdoors is considered a living classroom, with children encouraged to get outside and pursue carp fishing, ecology, paddleboarding and quiet reflection around the rhododendron-ringed lake. The school says it is ‘deliberately swimming against the tide’ when it comes to the use of technology – which means prioritising the child over the screen, rather than raising little Luddites – and this approach is paying off. Last year, a record 38 pupils gained scholarships to such top destinations as Brighton College, Harrow and Uppingham.

**London Road, Handcross, Haywards Heath RH17 6HF **

Website brightoncollegeprephand cross.co.uk Head Jonnie Besley Pupils 433 day and boarding: 205 boys, 238 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £10,399; boarding: £13,573

Set in 52 acres of parkland full of giant redwood trees that were planted by the Royal Canadian Air Force in the 1940s, this school has a reputation for excellent pastoral care. ‘Time to Talk’ clubs encourage pupils to share any difficulties (‘This really makes me feel heard,’ says one) and there’s an abundance of animals, including dogs, goats, chickens, bees, birds and a tortoise. Leadership and life skills are valued, with an hour per week from Years 3 to 8 dedicated to problem-solving and critical thinking. Students study Spanish and Mandarin from Reception onwards. Gently selective, the prep is part of the Brighton College group, and almost half its leavers go on to the senior school.

Chesterton, Bicester, Oxfordshire OX26 1UY

Website bruernabbey.org Head Hilary Phillips Pupils 145 day and boarding: 130 boys, 15 girls Ages 7–13 Term Fees Day: £12,655; boarding: £16,098

In a manor house with more than 30 working fireplaces, Bruern Abbey feels like a relaxed home for its neurodiverse pupils, who thrive at this caring school. The emphasis is on outdoorsy fun – as well as hard work – and all needs are catered for. (Pupils can listen to classes through headphones, or take archery instead of a second language.) The school chef, who has worked at Michelin-starred restaurants, conjures up sushi nights and Thanksgiving feasts; the boys have access to the senior school’s new teaching kitchen and parents can attend candlelit formal dinners at which the children pour the wine. But change is in the air, with the arrival of new head Hilary Phillips and girls this September.

Crown Lane, Farnham Royal, Buckinghamshire SL2 3SL

Website caldicott.com Head Jeremy Banks Pupils 255 day and boarding boys Ages 7–13 Term Fees Day: £14,354; boarding: £17,880

Under a self-proclaimed ‘radical traditionalist’ head Jeremy Banks, Caldicott is praised by parents for its ‘ability to balance tradition while navigating the evolving world and future’. That means a healthy dose of sports and outdoor fun, but also an inclusive philosophy; rigorous academics but strictly no homework; plus weekly drama lessons, productions, recitals, concerts and countless opportunities for boys to find their own ‘thing’. Parents are impressed by such initiatives as Caldicott’s recent Digital Wellbeing Conference and note its teachers’ ability to inspire a love of their subject. It’s a formula that works: last year’s cohort of leavers won the second highest number of senior-school scholarships since the school’s founding in 1904.

Headley, Newbury, Berkshire RG19 8LD

Website cheamschool.co.uk Head Nick Milbank Pupils 321 day and boarding: 186 boys, 135 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £11,055; flexi boarding: £0-75

‘Cheam has a talent for making school fun and happy,’ an enthusiastic parent tells us. All pupils sit the CE exam here – its academic rigour remains popular – and in 2025, 30 per cent gained scholarships or exhibitions, with the cohort’s head boy and girl moving respectively on to Radley and Marlborough. The toolkit at this prep also includes celebrating all the students’ accomplishments; for example, through celebration assemblies and the Cheam Diploma. Managed on an online platform, the qualification recognises acts of kindness and of the five pillars – resilience, respect, responsibility, resourcefulness and reflectiveness – and forms part of their application to senior schools.

Brewer Street, Oxford OX1 1QW

Website cccs.org.uk Head Richard Murray Pupils 129 day and boarding: 116 boys, 13 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £9,370; boarding (choristers only): £5,870

Founded by Henry VIII as a school for the younger choristers of Oxford’s Christ Church, nearly500 years later CCCS endures as a pinnacle of excellence for budding musicians, as well as those seeking a small city prep with a homely atmosphere. And on visiting, our researcher found caring pupils from a range of faiths, and a staff who manage to accommodate everyone beautifully while providing a traditional education. There is a bias towards music here, of course; but the recent completion of the new multi-use pavilion has benefitted not only the sports department but all aspects of the outdoor learning curriculum, while its kitchen facilities mean the delicious match teas can now be prepared on site.

Cothill, Oxfordshire OX13 6JL

Website cothill.org.uk Head George May Pupils 160 day and boarding: 152 boys, 8 girls Ages 7–13 Term Fees Day: £9,630; boarding: £14,590

Newly co-ed Cothill only has eight girls to its 150-odd boys, but things already seem to be going swimmingly – the only complaint we could coax out of one young lady being about her new uniform: ‘I wish the culottes had pockets!’ Over the decades, Cothill has become renowned for nurturing well-rounded, confident, and resilient children, blending traditional values with a forward-looking approach. Cothill’s motto, Dum spiro spero ('While I breathe, I hope’), deeply influences the school’s ethos and approach to education. It reflects a philosophy of strength, optimism and perseverance, encouraging children to face challenges with courage and maintain a positive outlook. A wholesome and happy blend of the new and the familiar, then, with one happy parent claiming that ‘energy hums through all aspects of school life’.

Buchan Hill, Pease Pottage, West Sussex RH11 9AU

Website cottesmoreschool.com Head Tom Rogerson Pupils 133 day and boarding: 83 boys, 50 girls Ages 8–13 Term Fees Day: £9,422; boarding: £13,779

‘The school invests in good, old-fashioned fun,’ says one parent, explaining that pupils enjoy childhoods protected from the demands of the modern world. And certainly such skills as letter writing – with fountain pens, no less – and sus-taining conversation over lunch are cherished. (Rambling outdoors, getting knees dirty and climbing trees are encouraged, too.) But donot be fooled: the perspective here is very forward-looking. Helming the world’s first online Esports championships for prep school-aged competitors, Cottesmore also holds its own annual climate change and sustainability week, boasts such initiatives as an Innovation Day and is busy undertaking an ambitious rewilding project.

Danehill, Haywards Heath, West Sussex RH17 7HT

Website cumnor.co.uk Head Fergus Llewellyn Pupils 360 day and boarding: 202 boys, 158 girls Ages 4–13 Term Fees Day: £9,800; flexi-boarding: £11,578

Cumnor House’s numbers are the highest they’ve been in a decade, and parents describe the school as ‘absolutely idyllic’, ‘top-notch’ and led by an ‘outstanding’ head. The Shakespeare play performed in the wonderful garden theatre is an annual highlight; as is Race the Sun, when – armed with maps and Mars bars – groups of pupils navigate the South Downs by night. Attention to pastoral care has seen the introduction of a new pastoral leadership team and an emotional literacy support assistant (great for supporting students with low self-esteem). And capacityfor the popular pre-prep is being expanded and the boarding acc-ommodation spruced up. As foracademics, despite being non-selective, an impressive 60 per cent of recent leavers won scholarships to prestigious senior schools.

Church Lane, Bury, Pulborough, West Sussex RH20 1PB

Website dorsethouseschool.com Head Sarah Sutherland-Booth Pupils 127 day and boarding: 70 boys, 57 girls Age 4–13 Term Fees Day: £9,130; boarding: an additional £163 for four nights, per week

The year 2025 saw two major changes at Dorset House: a merger with Lancing College, and the arrival of the school’s first female head, Sarah Sutherland-Booth. Both have gone down a treat: the former bringing with it access to fantastic facilities and specialist teachers; the latter an adorable puppy named Luna (and much more besides). Importantly though, parents say Dorset House is the same ‘lovely, nurturing, small prep school’ that it has always been, with an ‘amazing sense of community and family feel’, and pupils who are practically ‘fizzing with joy’. As one explains: ‘Not only is every child seen, but they are truly known – the consequence of which is that they are equipped with confidence to be truly themselves, and an innate sense of belonging that remains with them, dare I say, forever.’

Aldenham Village, Hertfordshire WD25 8NL

Website edgegrove.com Head Richard Stanley Pupils 310 day: 208 boys, 102 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees £8,826

None other than the American plutocrat JP Morgan donated to Edge Grove the 48 Hertfordshire acres on which it now stands. Most pupils live within 10 miles or so, and philanthropy and altruism are still values that the school espouses; through Harvest Festival food drives, for example, and its commitment to a wildflower corridor initiative, which saw pupils plant abundant seeds to promote eco-diversity. Sport is comprehensive here – both boys and girls play everything from hockey and rugby to table tennis and basketball – and the prep’s High Performance Academy identifies pupils with sports scholarship potential, coaching them one-on-one. Not to overshadow that there is also impressive work achieved in the classrooms, of course; and a newly opened learning support centre, The Hub, now provides a dedicated space for SEND pupils.

Woolhampton, Reading, Berkshire RG7 5TD

Website elstreeschool.org.uk Head Sid Inglis Pupils 310 day and boarding: 180 boys, 130 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £11,000; weekly boarding: £13,935

Elstree has a host of lovely traditions, such as writing postcards to parents at the start of each term and an all-years, fiendishly complicated team game called Convoy played at its end. Pupils can roam 150 acres of space, climb the affectionately named Wiggly Wag Tree and explore the recently unveiled adventure playground; a brand new art centre is sparking creative inspiration; and the new all-weather pitch is allowing even more sport. These endeavours are overseen by popular head Sid Inglis, who is described by one pupil as a ‘thoughtful person who never gives up on his school’.

Red Rice, Andover, Hampshire SP11 7PW

Website farleighschool.com Head Fr Simon Everson Pupils 430 day and boarding: 210 boys, 220 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £10,970; boarding: £13,990

After one day’s immersion in this Hampshire prep, our researcher concluded: ‘If I lived in the area,I wouldn’t hesitate to send my children to Farleigh. It’s the perfect blend of everything you could hope for from a school.’ It’s a sentiment keenly shared by parents who report ‘immediately falling in love’ with the ‘fairytale-like grounds’ and ‘well-rounded, grounded, confident and genuinely happy’ pupils. That the latter should be so is down to a number of factors. There’s the ‘big, wonderful family’ feel, the ‘excellent’ pastoral care and an ‘impressive breadth’ of opportunities that ensure every curiosity is covered. Then there are the Catholic values, which are ‘moral foundations for life, not something exclusionary’. And last but not least, a head so inspiring that parents and pupils alike ‘hope he will live forever’!

Byfleet Road, Cobham, Surrey KT11 1DR

Website feltonfleet.co.uk Head Shelley Lance Pupils 420 day and boarding: 230 boys, 190 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £8,937; flexi-boarding: £10,599

The academic year at Feltonfleet begins with a special ‘knighting’ ceremony – complete with a real sword – to officially welcome new boarders, aka Feltonfleet Knights, to the ranks. But this is not the school’s only idiosyncrasy, as demonstrated by its very own cinematic awards, Les Césars, given in recognition of pupils’ work on French film projects as part of the language curriculum. A recent visitor found the place family-orientated and warm, adding that ‘nothing seemed to be rushed or crowded’ and that everything was ‘underpinned by rigorous planning’ for academic success. Facilities include a climbing wall, an indoor swimming pool and two rifle ranges (and this year the team proudly retained its supported sling shooting silverware). Thanks to its woodlands, there is lots of outdoor play, too.

Brandeston Hall, Brandeston, Suffolk IP13 7AH

Website framlinghamcollege.co.uk Head Simon Roche Pupils 175 day and boarding: 100 boys, 75 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £12,246; boarding: £17,480

A visitor to this rural Suffolk prep reported how pulling into the drive instantly evoked the feeling of ‘a childhood unplugged’. With acres of rolling grassland, and pupils cheerfully changing into wellies to play outside, there’s a real ‘sense that fresh air and freedom are part of everyday life’. Screens are avoided until Year 7, with the focus being on outdoor activities including adventure camps, ‘colour runs’ – when white-topped participants are showered with vibrant powders – and plenty of sport. (Hockey is particularly strong: the U13 team won the 2026 IAPS National Finals.) Leading the charge is ‘dynamic’ head Simon Roche, who last year ran to Framlingham from Lord’s Cricket Ground in London, to raise funds for a new pavilion. It is now under construction.

Woodhall Park, Watton-at-Stone, Hertfordshire SG14 3NG

Website heathmount.org Head Chris Gillam Pupils 501 day and boarding: 286 boys, 215 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £9,695; flexi-boarding: £11,990

Highfield Lane, Liphook, Hampshire GU30 7LQ

Website highfieldschool.com Head Suzannah Cryer Pupils 418 day and boarding: 229 boys, 189 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £10,990; boarding: £14,090

As a truly independent, family-owned prep, Highfield puts its energy into understanding the aspirations of every single child. The expansive grounds lend themselves to childhood pursuits – climbing trees and kicking footballs – while the Year 4 classrooms have been redesigned with spaces for small-group support. For the first time, flexi-boarding is being offered (with no minimum number of nights). The school is run by head Suzannah Cryer, and the way she leads assemblies is commended by one pupil: ‘She had me smashing pomegranates on the sports hall floor when we returned to school after the Christmas holiday; apparently it was a custom in ancient Greece to bring good fortune and happiness in the coming year!’

Barrow Lane, Langton Green, Tunbridge Wells, Kent TN3 0EB

Website holmewoodhouse.co.uk Head Ruth O’Sullivan Pupils 439 day and boarding: 234 boys, 205 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £9,750; boarding: £13,500

Head Ruth O’Sullivan is praised for nurturing an atmosphere of ‘kindness, support and growth’ at this leafy prep. (Notable mentions also for ‘brilliant, funny, unique’ chemistry teacher Mr O’Riordan and ‘everyone’s favourite’ languages teacher Mr Steele.) There’s a fabulous boarding house, and its ad hoc flexi-option is much appreciated by local parents. The perennially popular rifle shooting operates on its own range and has produced a GB champion. But no Holmewood review would be complete without acknowledging the excellent food, whose Bulla ginger cake and katsu curry get top marks. ‘It’s the first thing my children talk about on the way home!’ says one mother.

Winkfield Row, Nr Ascot, Berkshire RG42 6LU

Website lambrookschool.co.uk Head Jonathan Perry Pupils 630 day and boarding: 318 boys, 312 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £11,274; weekly boarding: £54.60 per night

‘It’s somewhere I feel genuinely supported,’ says one student. ‘There’s always something to get stuck into.’ Along with a nine-hole golf course, expanses of woodland and countless sports pitches, the school also boasts an orchard and beehives – and even its very own farm, complete with chickens and pigs. Pupils play sport at local and national levels (with some representing their country in tennis, squash and equestrianism) while plans are underway not only for a new Future Learning and Wellbeing Centre but for an expansion of the bursary programme. One parent reports that ‘this ethos has rubbed off on [adults], where there is a thriving parent community participating in a wide range of social and charitable activities’.

Hemel Hempstead, Hertfordshire HP1 1TL

Website lockerspark.co.uk Head Gavin Taylor Pupils 164 day and boarding boys Ages 4–13 Term Fees Day: £9,025; boarding: £13,480

On their beautiful 23-acre campus, Lockers Park boys are very much encouraged to be boys; hence they get their knees dirty, build camps, and trade in conkers. While the night-time Dark Tower event is a hit – they chase each other round the grounds, torches in hand – boys also express their artistic sides in such contests as Strictly Lockers and Lockers Got Talent. Then there’s the sports offering, which goes beyond the ones you would expect. (The current intake includes silver and gold medallists in the British Schools Alpine Championships.) And a big change has been the formalisation of a long-term relationship with Harrow, whose ‘family’ it has now joined. So while Lockers Park will remain purposefully small and values-led, it will share in Harrow’s educational, academic and pastoral expertise.

Wokingham, Berkshire RG40 3AB

Website ludgrove.net Head Simon Barber Pupils 186 boarding boys Ages 8–13Term Fees £14,477

‘Genuinely one in a million’ is how parents describe head Simon Barber. ‘He functions as a father figure to the boys. He truly understands them, cares deeply for each one, and knows them all individually.’ Barber presides over a dynamic environment that is, essentially, boy heaven: sport is played five afternoons a week and extracurriculars span mountain biking, outdoor skills (in which they obtain knife certificates in the school woods) and farm club, which involves caring for a flock of chickens and three pigs. Some 370 music lessons are taught every week; the theatre offers a brilliant performance space; birthday ice-cream cakes are provided to share with friends; and in a tradition as enchanting as Barber himself, new boys’ names are painted on the dining-hall boards in their first two weeks, making them feel part of Ludgrove’s history straight away.

Flanchford Road, Leigh, Reigate, Surrey RH2 8RE

Website moonhallschoolreigate.co.uk Head Michelle CattersonPupils 194 day: 100 boys, 94 girls Ages 7–16 Term Fees Day: £11,811

‘Dyslexia is a difference to be understood, not a barrier to success.’ That has been the guiding principle of this specialist prep ever since its founding in 1985, and the reason students travel from far and wide to attend Moon Hall today. Structured literacy, explicit instruction, small classes and intelligent use of assistive technology form the backbone of this school’s approach. And for many, it has proved to be utterly transformative. Head Michelle Catterson says she’s worked hard to ‘raise academic standards’, while making sure to always ‘focus on the individual and celebrate their strengths’. Students especially love the family feeling and the teachers’ desire to help, and everyone looks forward to Friday afternoon activities that range from den-building and ultimate Frisbee to debating and chess clubs.

Moulsford-on-Thames, Oxfordshire OX10 9HR

Website moulsford.com Head Ben Beardmore-Gray Pupils 330 day and boarding: 300 boys, 30 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £8,385; boarding: £10,600

For all its traditions (real tennis, for one), this Thameside prep is embracing change. Last year’s move into co-education continues apace in lower age groups – there are now Year 3 girls who will be leavers by 2031 – while AI’s appearance in the classroom comes with emphasis on both its responsible use and its future role in the workplace. Socialising continues beyond the school gates, as most pupils are local. Still, they’re far from provincial; and a recent Old Mole ended up as Eton’s President of Pop. Head ‘BBG’ is uncannily adept at matching the right children with the right destinations, but never forgets their time here should be joyous. So there is plenty of messing about on the river – and one teacher even makes his daily commute by kayak.

Savile Road, Oxford OX1 3UA

Website newcollegeschool.org Head Dr Matthew Jenkinson Pupils 146 day boys Ages 4–13 Term Fees Day: £9,545

Tucked into the centre of Oxford and dating back to 1379, this tiny school (with just one class per year) has a legendary musical reputation. For good reason. In recent years, alumni have topped the charts, and won Oscars, Baftas and Grammy nominations. Shakespeare is performed in New College Chapel, pre-prep boys take the lead in the annual panto and pupils are regular entrants into the Young Art Oxford competition at the Ashmolean Museum. Following a multimillion-pound redevelopment, a set of classrooms has joined a brand-new auditorium and creative learning centre. And up next is an extension of SHTEAM, a week-long inter-disciplinary festival celebrating the humanities, with arts and STEM. Fascinating themes so far have included ‘The World of Water’ and ‘Our Green Planet’.

Windsor Road, Ascot, Berkshire SL5 7LH

Website papplewick.org.uk Head Nina Kingsmill Moore Pupils 200 day and boarding boysAges 6–13 Term Fees Day £11,970; boarding: £15,744

Boys at Papplewick exude a palpable sense of happiness. Much joy’s to be had in the famed herpetology club, whose members look after such reptiles as bearded dragons and geckos. (Last year, an escapee snake travelled to an away match in an unsuspecting teacher’s Wellington boot!) Hearty singing takes place every day in the chapel and about 70 per cent of the pupils learn instruments. There’s a vibrant art festival in the Lent term and, on the playing fields, its football teams are on a serious winning streak. After 22 years of successfully leading the school, head Tom Bunbury has retired, handing the baton to Nina Kingsmill Moore, previously head of Wetherby and Glendower preps.

Inner Close, Winchester, Hampshire SO23 9LT

Website thepilgrims-school.co.uk Head Tim Butcher Pupils 220 day and boarding boys Ages 4–13 Term Fees Day: £10,250; boarding: £13,000; choristers: £6,500

Recent leavers took a constellation of scholarships with them, boasting four in music alone. Being the only school in the UK with two professional choirs – it educates the choristers of Winchester Cathedral and the quiristers of Winchester College – the musical provision is exceptional, offering everything from classical repertoire to rock. Still, there’s plenty else to do. A merger with Winchester has led to fantastic enrichment opportunities, and a raft of clubs keeps the pupils busy. (Particularly popular is British young pilots, who learn to fly light aircraft and take to the skies from Popham airfield.) Next up? A trailblazing ‘super curriculum’, which will encompass museum visits and talks from visiting College teachers.

Kings Worthy, Winchester, Hampshire SO21 1AN

Website princesmeadschool.org.uk Head Adam King Pupils 217 day: 117 boys, 100 girls Ages 2–11 Term Fees £8,005

Designed by the same architect as the Royal Opera House, Prince’s Mead is an idyllic spot for a prep education. And since the forest school is a much-used, much-loved part of life here – and one shared with the local community – it has just been fitted with a canopy, offering welcome protection during colder cook-ups around the firepit. In other news, nearly £9,000 was recently raised for two charities chosen by pupils; football is thriving; new 3D printers are inspiring creative minds; and the annual fireworks are an unmissable spec-tacle. ‘We didn’t want a school based on the old-fashioned model of formidable teachers,’ says one parent. ‘Instead, we were looking for warm, approachable staff who help children grow in confidence.’

Pangbourne, Berkshire RG8 8QA

Website standrewsberkshire.co.uk Head Ed Graham Pupils 325 day and boarding: 151 boys, 174 girls Ages 2½–13 Term Fees Day: £9,460; boarding: £180 for four nights

You know St Andrew’s is going to be fun the moment you pass a sign displaying the riddle of the day set by head Ed Graham. And in that spirit, there’s a big focus on forest school, which takes place ‘whatever the weather’. (Pupils spend more than two hours a day outside.) Elsewhere, the new performing arts centre is the perfect stage for budding actors; and the recently opened Think Tank is a calm space for collaboration. The skills-based Pre-senior Baccalaureate programme has been extended so that it now runs from Nursery to Year 8. And a new Design and Innovation course will encourage pupils to use modern tools and emerging technologies to bring ideas to life.

Windsor, Berkshire SL4 1QF

Website stgwindsor.org Head Emma Károlyi Pupils 292 day and boarding: 167 boys, 125 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £8,980; weekly boarding: £11,162

Founded in 1348 to educate the choristers of St George’s Chapel, this school boasts a truly iconic setting at the foot of Windsor Castle. Guided by its motto – ‘Real World Ready’ – it’s the only standalone prep in the UK to deliver the IB Primary Years programme. As part of its pastoral provision, it recently launched the wellbeing hub, which offers students, staff and parents free access to 2,000 science-backed resources. Students display artwork within the castle; and music, of course, is strong, with passions fostered by gala performances in the chapel and sessions in the recording suite. Recently, the school has announced a merger with St George’s Ascot to create an all-through co-ed school from 2028.

Carswell Manor, Faringdon, Oxfordshire SN7 8PT

Website st-hughs.co.uk Head David Griffiths Pupils 308 day and boarding: 165 boys, 143 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £9,990; boarding: £12,378

Parents are delighted that, with the new senior school’s opening, their children can continue at St Hugh’s until they are 16 years old. ‘The atmosphere is relaxed, inclusive and genuinely happy. Children are encouraged to enjoy learning, be themselves and support each other,’ says one. (And to see the world: musicians perform in Athens and Rome, and the annual Year 7 trip to Normandy is always much anticipated.) Given St Hugh’s small size, its sports teams exceed all expectations, boasting national medallists in high jump, clay pigeon shooting and golf. And then there’s thefantastically quirky game Kingdom of the Swan, in which houses battle to get their hands round the neck of a giant model bird.

Priest Hill, Old Windsor, Berkshire SL4 2JN

Website sjbwindsor.uk Head Andrew De Silva Pupils 180 day and boarding: 160 boys, 20 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees Day: £9,720; boarding: £14,880

St John’s Beaumont is the archetypal British prep, with a bit of Hogwarts thrown in (and for good measure, some touches of Just William and Malory Towers). From weekly forest school and regular cross-country runs through its woodlands to memorable performances in its outdoor theatre, the stunning grounds offer it all. Its partnership with Brighton College has led to cultural exchanges with students from Rome, Madrid and Lisbon; and its language teaching has recently been expanded. But – along with incoming head Andrew De Silva – there’s much more in progress. Namely, a heap of building works which will deliver new dorms, as well as a new library,dining hall and STEAM centre.

Water Lane, Hawkhurst, Kent TN18 5DJ

Website saintronans.co.uk Head Joe Lewis Pupils 359 day and boarding: 194 boys, 165 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £9,816; weekly boarding: £11,864

‘The school has a great sense of humour,’ says one parent, who recalls how staff dress up on World Book Day and recently joined in an ‘X-Factor’ competition. Another describes it as ‘alive, fun and with a high degree of excellence’. The introduction of weekly boarding has widened the catchment area, with families attracted by the holistic, countryside-prep vibes – and by the new train service from Charing Cross. Days here are screen-free; instead, time is spent at the forest school, beach school and farm; and a conservation pro-gramme instils in pupils a deep connection with nature. In other news, two horses have been added to the farm’s mix and a wonderful nursery building has opened.

Ditton Road, Surbiton, Surrey KT6 6RL

Website shrewsburyhouse.net Head Joanna Hubbard Pupils 313 day boys Ages 4–13 Term Fees £9,420

‘I would like more than 24 hours in a day because there’s so much I want to do,’ says one enthusiastic pupil. ‘We have brilliant lessons, clubs and trips, and I want to try everything!’ Choir tours to Paris, geography jaunts to Iceland and ski trips in Italy certainly sound unmissable, while societies cover everything from robotics and cookery to the student-led Maths 4s, who organise challenges and problem-solving activities. Becoming part of the St Paul’s Schools Group has created not only opportunities for professional development but also for collaborating and sharing expertise. In the classroom, an Innovation Programme marries creativity with technical thinking. And a dedicated new Younger Years building means boys can now join at Reception – and have more time to experience all that the school has to offer.

Mayfield Road, Oxford OX2 7EN

Website summerfields.com Head John Floyd Pupils 305 day and boarding boys Ages 4–13 Term Fees Day: £11,520; boarding: £16,530

Set in 70 acres of parkland – yet only 1.5 miles from the centre of Oxford – Summer Fields has something for everyone. As one parent remarks, ‘The teachers do a marvellous job at spotting and tapping into the boys’ individual talents and potential. They really help nurture them. And where better to learn than Oxford?!’ Following the school motto – mens sana in corpore sano, which translates as ‘A Healthy Mind in a Healthy Body’ – sport is played every day. Musicians perform in Magdalen College Chapel and the ferocious Battle of the Bands. (A recent in-house competition was themed around songs from the university days of the then-head, including Beatles covers.) Looking to the future, his successor John Floyd will oversee plans for a new Year 8 boarding house – and possibly a nursery.

An outdoor kitchen and pizza oven, which should enliven many a summer’s evening.
