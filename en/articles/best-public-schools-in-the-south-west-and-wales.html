Edit Schools Guide 2027: The best public schools in the South West and Wales

The 2027 Edit Schools Guide reveals the best public schools in the South West and Wales

By Edit · September 29, 2026

Westbury Road, Westbury-on-Trym, Bristol BS9 3BA

Website badmintonschool.co.uk Head Jessica Miles, since 2023; previously head of Monmouth School for Girls Pupils 526 girls: 345 day, 181 boarding Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £8,142; boarding: £16,565 Bursaries 16% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Former prime minister of India Indira Gandhi, novelist Dame Iris Murdoch, actress Rosamund Pike

A small school that prides itself on its global reputation, Badminton has just rolled out a suite of new core values: curiosity, courtesy, aspiration and courage. Courtesy is exemplified by an end-of-term tradition called Pump Handles, whereby pupils shake hands with all the teachers, looking them in the eye. Aspiration and courage are demonstrated on the playing fields: cricket is thriving and, despite the name of the school having nothing to do with the sport, the badminton team was recently crowned county champions, showing the climbers and swimmers they are not alonein collecting silverware. Curiosity is fostered in classes whose small sizes (13 up to GCSEs, eight in the Sixth Form) ensure everyone is nurtured and stretched. And Badminton makes the most of its proximity to Bristol, enjoying outreach pro-grammes run by the university and workshops at the famous Old Vic Theatre School. A £2 million refurbishment of boarding houses is being followed up with an install-ation of UPV insulating panels on the swimming-pool roof. Pupils admire head Jessica Miles’s ability to ‘provide an honest atmosphere’ while ‘maintaining professional boundaries’. Parents appreciate her regular video updates. ‘I don’t think I would change anything, except maybe make school hours longer,’ said no student, ever. Except for one who goes to Badminton!

Blundell’s Road, Tiverton, Devon EX16 4DN

Website blundells.org Head Gareth Pearson, since 2026; previously head of Christ College Brecon Pupils 813. Day: 308 boys, 219 girls; boarding: 155 boys, 131 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £11,676; boarding: £19,080. Bursaries 15% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Racing driver Ben Collins, Nobel prize-winner Archibald Hill, England cricketer Dom Bess

New head Gareth Pearson inherits a school that’s ambitious, aspirational – and grounded. ‘The children always look fresh-faced and smiling, dashing around campus, caked in mud from sporting activities,’ says a parent, who chose the school on ‘gut feeling’ and has no regrets. For, rather than hothousing, the focus is on nurturing a broad range of abilities and strengths; and pupils love ‘the opportunities available to all and the encouraging culture’. Not least in sport: rugby, hockey and netball are firm favourites; golf and basketball are growing in popularity; and a steady stream of cricketers head off to the Darren Lehman academy in Australia, while the girls’ team has a full fixtures list. Elsewhere, the thespians staged a West End-style performance of Les Misérables, and the music depart-ment has hit a particular crescendo of late. An ongoing partnership with the inspiring London Chamber Orchestra has been supplemented by new collaborations with the Bath Philharmonic and Exeter University Chapel Choir, who sing Evensong in the school chapel. Following 18 months of hard work, a new AI project is being implemented, with AI classes for pupils from Year 9 on (and training to staff, too). The first phase of a £4 million investment in the sports facilities has delivered a top-of-the-range golf simulator. Next up are a new cricket pavilion and swimming pool.

Blandford Forum, Dorset, DT11 0PX

Website bryanston.co.uk Head Richard Jones, since 2022; previously acting head Pupils 783. Day: 72 boys, 70 girls; boarding: 344 boys, 297 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £13,555; boarding: £19,750 Bursaries 16% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Designers Sir Terence, Jasper and Sebastian Conran, adventurer Ben Fogle, actress Emilia Fox

‘I respect the individuality and freedom that the school gives you. It’s up to you on what you get out of your time here,’ says one pupil. This might well be down to the Bryanston Method, which matches pupils with tutors they meet weekly and encourages them to spend as much time outside the classroom as inside, independently undertaking their own research into assignments. The result is a distinctive approach to education, with an emphasis on creativity: Re-imagine, the week-long Bryanston Art and Design Exhibition at the Royal Watercolour Society Gallery, was a pinnacle of the school calendar, while the auction of a bronze sculpture by Dame Barbara Hepworth – donated to the school in the 1970s – raised funds for the Bryanston Art Bursary Fund. Meanwhile, the lights are rarely down in the Coade Hall theatre, and a partnership with the London-based conservatoire Trinity Laban will fine-tune Sixth-form musicians’ skills, and give them both regular access to world-class string professors and expert guidance on technique for auditions. As for the academics, not only do senior pupils benefit from a diverse programme – A-levels, BTECs and the IB are all on offer – but they are also prepared for post-school life by the Bryanston Founders’ Forum, which was rolled out recently to inspire Bryanston’s next generation of entrepreneurs.

Canford Magna, Wimborne, Dorset BH21 3AD

Website canford.com Head Chris Wheeler, since 2025; previously principal of Monkton Combe Pupils 650. Day: 159 boys, 108 girls; boarding: 213 boys, 170 girls Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £14,315; boarding: £19,073 Bursaries 14% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni BBC presenter and actor Ore Oduba, Childs Farm founder Joanna Jensen, rugby player Will Butt

Exciting new developments at Canford, the decidedly discreet co-ed nestled in 250 acres of parkland (which includes the school’s own arboretum) beside the River Stour. The facilities have long matched the splendid setting, but now they’re even better: a new ‘college’ for the Sixth Form, com-prising a hub of social and study spaces, plus a training kitchen, has been open since February and new residential accommodation has come onstream; the latter being a particularly savvy move, when you consider that 95 per cent of pupils choose to stay here for Sixth Form. When asked what they like most about it, many pupils bring up the environment itself – ‘I love the amazing grounds and architecture,’ says one – while parents praise the regime. ‘Its greatest asset is how balanced it is,’ says one, who has two children here, each with different strengths, both thriving. ‘Academics are taken seriously, but so are music, sport, creativity and character. There’s a real sense that success comes in many forms, and children are encouraged to be aspirational. The facilities are superb, but it’s the people who make Canford special.’ This quality translates into academic success; about a quarter of of A-level grades have been A*s over the last three years, while 70 per cent of GCSEs have been graded 9–7.

Milford Hill, Salisbury, Wiltshire SP1 2RA

Website godolphin.org Head Chris Eades, since 2026; previously housemaster at Tonbridge; head of senior school, Jenny Price, since 2023 Pupils 450. Day: 75 boys, 375 girls; boarding: 10 boys, 50 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £8,350; boarding: £13,900 Bursaries Variable % of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Dame Jilly Cooper, entrepreneur Deborah Meaden, novelist Minette Walters

It may have just updated its name, but it has been 300 years since Elizabeth Godolphin founded this ‘gem’ of a senior school, as one parent describes it, based on the virtues and curriculum she felt were vital for the education of young ladies: dancing, reading, writing and Christianity. And from its perch on the outskirts of Salisbury, it still cleaves to its faith with strong links with the city’s cathedral. Prayers are conducted throughout the week; there’s weekly chapel in the Main Hall; and new girls are welcomed with an iconic blue pinny and a hymn book. Our researcher found relaxed relations between staff and pupils, with fore- and nicknames used, and the same attitude is reflected in the leavers’ popular annual show: written by the students, it features gentle parodies of the teachers and is watched by the whole school. They can rise to the occasion, too; and every year, the Upper Sixth celebrates the founder at a service of commemoration in Westminster Abbey. (Less formal excursions include religious studies trips to India and a long-standing exchange with a grammar school in Melbourne, Australia.) In the academic arena, places are regularly won at Oxbridge as well as at such overseas destinations as IE Madrid and Tufts in Massachusetts. The landmark tricentennial will be marked with a series of special events honouring its heritage, but the establishment is also looking forward to a new era. Namely, this year’s integration of Chafyn Grove prep. The merger creates the city’s only co-ed that takes children from the ages of three to 18, and their shared campus will make the most of each other’s facilities.

Bath Road, Cheltenham, Gloucestershire GL53 7LD

Website cheltenhamcollege.org Head Chris Townsend, since 2026; previously head of Felsted School Pupils 701. Day: 81 boys, 75 girls; boarding: 287 boys, 258 girls Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £15,880; boarding: £21,062 Bursaries 11.6% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Actor Jack Davenport, eventing Olympic gold medallist Tom McEwen, style director Olly Paton

‘I really love all the opportunities that are available,’ says one student; and there certainly seem to be more than enough to keep the days jam-packed. With 180 teams playing 20 sports, pupils can try their hands at rackets, riding and clay-pigeon shooting; and both the girls and boys U16 hockey team reached the National Plate finals, affording them the opportunity to play at Olympic Park in Lee Valley. What’s more, thriving community outreach efforts have seen a whole school Mud Run raise funds for Scotty’s Little Soldiers – a charity close to its heart, with a number of military families in the community – as well as each house supporting a charity of its choosing. Student-led societies span architecture to GreenUp (to grow plants around the school); baking is perennially popular; and a recent dramaturgical highlight saw a performance of Agatha Christie’s The Unexpected Guest. Life inside the classroom is just as dramatic, and a popular change has allowed students to drop one subject at GCSE and instead opt for an elective in such fields as history of art, astronomy and entrepreneurship. And the College is very proud of six pupils who received offers from Oxford and Cambridge across an amazing array of subjects – medicine, veterinary medicine, classics, history of art and engineering. Meanwhile, there are aspirations to add a cookery school, Sixth Form Centre and padel courts to the offering, and international schools are being opened in Kuala Lumpur and Italy.

Bayshill Road, Cheltenham, Gloucestershire GL50 3EP

Website cheltladiescollege.org Head Eve Jardine-Young, since 2011; previously director of studies at Blundell’s Pupils 819 girls: 190 day, 629 boarding Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £14,460; boarding: £22,740 Bursaries 8.5% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Scientist Dame Mary Archer, actress Dame Kristin Scott Thomas, chief of MI6 Blaise Metreweli

After a consultation with pupils, holding ‘visioning’ workshops with the design team and a lengthy build time, CLC’s much-anticipated Sixth Form Centre is opening this year. Inside the refurbished and much-extended Victorian building is up-to-the-minute facilities, including a debating chamber, study pods, a café and even a virtual reality suite. These social and aca-demic spaces comprise a hub for CLC’s A-level and IB programmes, enhancing its world-class teaching. (The school’s average grade for IB was a very strong 38.9, with more than half scoring 40 or higher, while 74 per cent achieved A*–A at A-level and 88 per cent 9–7 at GCSE.) Not that CLC hopes for children who conform to a type, explains one parent. Instead, ‘diversity of intellect, expression, style, ethnicity and religion are positively embraced’. The atmosphere is one of respect and non-judgement; kindness is demonstrated and encouraged. Means-tested bursaries known as Beale Awards – after the pioneering second-ever principal Dorothea Beale, who devoted 48 years to the college – offer fully or partially funded day or boarding places. Boarders come from more than 40 countries, and only 20 per cent of the student body are day pupils. ‘I wanted my daughter to experience that warm, civilised environment conducive to wellbeing and self- confidence,’ says the mother of a thriving third-generation CLC girl.

Brecon, Powys LD3 8AF

Website christcollegebrecon.com Head Alex Wallace, since 2026; previously deputy head academic of Leighton Park School Pupils 381. Day: 135 boys, 111 girls; boarding: 73 boys, 62 girls Ages 4–18 Term Fees Day: £9,453; boarding: £18,222 Bursaries 19.8% of pupils, up to 100% AlumniThe first Welsh woman to climb Mount Everest Tori James, pioneer in law Lord Atkin, garden designers David and Harry Rich

It is only natural that this ancient institution in the Brecon Beacons National Park – some of the most beautiful scenery in the country – considers what goes on outdoors to be as vital as what is learnt in the classroom. Its academic year begins with an all-age Adventure Day, with activities including climbing and abseiling. And team games are vigorously encouraged, with everyone taking part in competitive sports for at least one term, and most pupils competing in multiple sports across all three terms. But kindness and a friendly disposition are also highly valued here. The school has a warm feeling, according to a recent visitor; a sense that its children ‘grow up a little slower’; and that everyone is welcome. With a 64–36 per cent mix of day pupils to boarders, students come from across the UK, Europe and beyond, and currently include teens from Hong Kong and Japan; and there’s no shortage of ways to keep them occupied. For example, there are no formal classes at weekends; instead their ‘second-curricular Saturdays’ are filled with such wholesome and hardy group outings as mountain biking and hiking, and indoor clubs include societies dedicated to model railway, debating and robotics. What’s more, Welsh language can be studied as a subject at Christ College. Incoming head Alex Wallace, who played rugby at semi-professional level, seems an excellent fit for a school which runs three distinct pathways to create a lifelong love of sport and physical activity.

Iwerne Minster, Dorset DT11 8LL

Website clayesmore.com Head Rob Lane, since 2026; previously senior deputy head of Oswestry School Pupils 412. Day: 123 boys, 115 girls; boarding: 95 boys, 79 girls Ages 4–18 Term Fees Day: £11,890; boarding: £16,503 Bursaries Variable % of pupils, on application Alumni England cricketer Lewis McManus, artist Tony Hart, curator Gus Casely-Hayford

Inclusive excellence is the name of the game at Clayesmore, with every child empowered to succeed in their own way, in and outside the classroom. When it comes to activities, there is certainly a rich palette to choose from, with some sounding very high-brow indeed. Never mind the Model United Nations and the Shadow Monetary Committee; in the politics society, students energetically debate complex themes such as the wisdom of using nuclear power and the role of the BBC. That is when they’re not preparing for the limelight: the drama department put on the hairdressing horror Sweeney Todd, with the children also taking roles behind the scenes in set design, props and stage management. Or making music: from carol services to the Concerto Festival, the calendar is peppered with events. (Hampers and Champers, an outdoor concert on the school lawn, sounds particularly appealing.) Or winning prizes and titles, especially in cricket, under the encouraging eye of director of sport Dan Conway. The establishment is split down the middle between day pupils and boarders (many from military families). All of them can join trips that roam far and wide: whether on World Challenge expeditions to Nepal, ski trips to Italy, language jaunts to Spain or visits to Clayesmore’s sister school in Tanzania. Back home, the Art and DT departments have now been combined and housed in a bright, purpose-built hub – complete with a dedicated Sixth-form space, where older students can explore and experiment – while a new nursery endeavours to inspire creativity and collaboration from the youngest age; and the opening of a pre-prep means Clayesmore now offers a seamless, all-through experience.

College Road, Clifton, Bristol BS8 3JH

Website cliftoncollege.com Head Dr Chris Stevens, since 2026; previously head of Bradfield College Pupils 1,208. Day: 459 boys, 347 girls; boarding: 223 boys, 179 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £13,170; boarding: £20,725 Bursaries 10% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Implementor of the Belisha Beacon Leslie Hore-Belisha, comedian John Cleese, Olympic hockey gold medallist Lily Owsley

At Clifton College, situated in a leafy quarter of Bristol just a few hundred yards from Isambard Kingdom Brunel’s famous Suspension Bridge, Saturday morning schools begin with a singing session called ‘Congers’ – short for congregational practice – which is approached by pupils with gusto. It’s an upbeat way to mark the end of a packed school week, any day of which might see pupils design a car to race at Goodwood Motor Circuit, sail on the Chew Valley Lake or follow in the footsteps of Old Cliftonian Simon Russell Beale to tread the boards of the school’s Redgrave Theatre, named after fellow alum Sir Michael. Outgoing head Dr Tim Greene leaves behind a school committed to getting the best from its boys, while keeping an eye on the future through mandatory work experience for Year 12s (often at companies with whom the school has links) and by encouraging pupils to train in public speaking. ‘You simply cannot underestimate the importance of being able to present yourself well,’ he says. The school is involved in outreach with the Cabot Learning Foundation to expand learning opportunities for young Bristolians. Filling Dr Greene’s shoes comes Dr Stevens, who was head at Bradfield College for 10 years; and he inherits an accomplished academic institution, which last year saw an admirable two-thirds of its 150 leavers move on to Russell Group destinations, while five students won places at Oxbridge.

West Lavington, Devizes, Wiltshire SN10 4HE

Website dauntseys.org Head John Davies, since 2024; previously senior deputy head and vice principal of Kingswood School Pupils 840. Day: 260 boys, 260 girls; boarding: 160 boys, 160 girls Ages 11–18 Term fees Day: £9,920; boarding: £16,400 Bursaries Variable % of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Thomas the Tank Engine creator The Rev Wilbert Awdry, former president of the Maldives Mohamed Nasheed, Hong Kong pop star Eason Chan

‘The pupils at Dauntsey’s are down-to-earth, grounded and well-rounded,’ proclaims one parent, adding: ‘They are kind, polite, engaging and comfortable adapting to different environments.’ And perhaps this attitude stems from Dauntsey’s opening-out of its offering to the wider community, with teachers running academic sessions and coaching sports at nearby primaries, and local children visiting the vast campus for such activities as den building and campfire making. But then, adventure is a big part of life here – which should come as no surprise when its students have sailed across the Atlantic numerous times in the school’s very own boat – and the full-time head of adventure education promotes such achievements, encouraging pupils to develop new skills and embark on daring exploits. Still, energy is also burnt off in their social initiatives; pupils gathered at the top of a field to form a human rainbow, symbolising the school’s commitment to joy, unity and inclusion; and there’s a huge range of sports, from the niche (say, historic European martial arts) to such traditional games as hockey. Rugby, meanwhile, is particularly strong, with the girls’ team becoming an increasingly formidable strength. However, it seems parents don’t come only to cheer on their offspring: ‘The match teas here are unrivalled. I can’t think of a single school where they are better,’ reports one well-travelled parent.

Shelburne Road, Cheltenham, Gloucestershire GL51 6HE

Website deanclose.org.uk Head Ali Harber, since 2026; previously deputy head (pastoral)at Winchester College Pupils 928. Day: 347 boys, 276 girls; boarding: 152 boys, 153 girls Ages 2–19 Term Fees Day: £12,150; boarding: £17,950 Bursaries 22% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Surgeon and politician Lord Ribeiro, actor Hugh Quarshie, England rugby player Tom Pearson

Dean Close prides itself on offering the full boarding experience; blending that with academic and sporting ambition, artistic prowess and genuine warmth; and underpinning it all with a Christian tradition that manifests itself in chapel gatherings, house events and Saturday evening socials. And it has form. New head Ali Harber – described by parents as ‘very supportive’ and ‘very available’ – has been busy in the archives, where he has discovered beautifully preserved 19th-century exercise books. These, he says, reveal just how long intellectual curiosity has been part of the community’s DNA; and that same curiosity is now being channeled towards the future, thanks to such innovative initiatives as a recent dedicated AI week, when different departments explored the opportunities and ethical considerations of artificial intelligence within their disciplines. Pupils here excel at sport. (Eleven former students play rugby in the Premiership or French Championship, and two recent old girls have become professional cricketers.) Drama is a collaborative affair with ample opportunities to do set design, lighting and costumes. And artists can display their work at the dedicated Bon Bernard Gallery. Dean Close is an all-round, non-pretentious school rooted in its values. ‘We’re comfortable that our children will have all the benefits of private education,’ says one parent. ‘And when they come out at 18, we are going to have grounded, rounded, down-to-earth young adults who will have a strong work ethic and a good level of self-worth.’

Bath Road, Marlborough, Wiltshire SN8 1PA

Website marlboroughcollege.org Head Louise Moelwyn-Hughes, since 2018; previously head of St Edmund’s School, Canterbury Pupils 1,005 boarding: 561 boys, 444 girls Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £17,076; boarding: £21,345 Bursaries 15% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Textile designer William Morris, poet Sir John Betjeman, The Princess of Wales

Centered around a 4,000-year-old Neolithic mound (said to be the burial place of the wizard Merlin), Marlborough describes its campus as a ‘world in itself’. But it’s a world that’s set to change with the opening of College House, its first-ever co-ed day house, with entry in Years 9 and 12; and here, its 100 day pupils will be kept as busy as their overnight peers. A typical weekend sees some 50 teams competing in sports that range from clay-pigeon shooting to girls football. (Not forgetting rackets, in which one girl recently won her sixth Queen’s Club title, to become the most decorated Marlburian Queen’s champion in the tournament’s 157-year history.) Elsewhere, the fully refurbished Mount House Gallery inspires students by exhibiting work by professional artists; the drama department produces everything from all-singing, all-dancing whole-school productions to pupil-written plays; and music scholars are provided with a particularly impressive level of support, including visits from specialist consultants and an annual opportunity to collaborate with Southbank Sinfonia, the school’s ‘professional orchestra in partnership’. Societies range from climbing and chess to the whimsically named Romance of Learning club. A years-long overhaul of the science department has just completed, resulting in a university-style facility that contains an incredible array of auditoriums, labs, inter-active collaboration pods and an exhibition space. Further afield, history of art trips to Naples and Rome are supplemented by jaunts to Lapland and Peru’s Inca Trail.

Butleigh Road, Street, Somerset BA16 0YD

Website millfieldschool.com Head Gavin Horgan, since 2018; previously head of Worksop College Pupils 1,707. Day: 391 boys, 292 girls; boarding: 591 boys, 433 girls Ages 2–18 Term Fees Day: £13,155; boarding: £20,205 Bursaries Variable % of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Para-astronaut John McFall, Olympic rower Helen Glover, Formula 1 world champion Lando Norris

A thriving international institution – its second-biggest feeder school is in Nairobi – Millfield feels a bit like an American college transported to the middle of the Somerset Levels. There are more than 1,000 staff, and the sheer size is one of its many strengths: everyone can find their groove in a culture where busy-ness is the name of the game. The athletics track and swimming pool exude near-Olympic energy, and a number of current and former pupils competed in the Commonwealth Games this summer. Rowers, rugby players, footballers and more amass cabinetfuls of trophies. There’s an on-site equestrian centre (and did we mention the tailored student-athlete programmes that marry sport, academics and pastoral care?). Creativity is nurtured here, too: the artistically inclined make sets for plays and exhibit in the Atkinson Gallery, which annually showcases work by pre-prep and prep pupils right the way through to Sixth-form specialists. The Millfield Activity Programme steers students to try something new every Friday afternoon, be that scuba-diving theory or film making. Elsewhere, ‘green power’ sees them design and build an electric car to race against teams from across the country. Amid all this activity, there’s always space for maintaining mental health. Head Gavin Horgan has dinner with groups of Lower Sixth pupils throughout each year, to ensure he meets every one of them; there are restrictions on phone use; and a wellbeing programme that encourages students to build resilience.

Milton Abbas, Blandford Forum, Dorset DT11 0BZ

Website miltonabbey.co.uk Head James Watson, since 2023; previously senior deputy head Pupils 218. Day: 25 boys, 14 girls; boarding: 102 boys, 77 girls Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £12,032; boarding: £22,352 Bursaries Variable % of pupils, up to 95% Alumni Sky News royal events correspondent Major General Alastair Bruce, documentary maker Anthony Geffen, dyslexic child academic prodigy Alexander Faludy

Located on a site whose first church was built in 934 AD, Milton Abbey is spine-tinglingly, breathtakingly beautiful. The current school was founded some 70 years ago, with 24 boys and a singular mission to provide an alternative to traditional private education; so a focus on wellbeing, with a personalised and holistic approach to teaching, has always been at its heart. ‘One of its greatest strengths is its wonderfully different learning culture, which truly embraces every pupil as an individual,’ says one parent, who also loves the emphasis on fostering camaraderie between different year groups: ‘My daughter is in the 3rd Form and has genuine friendships with pupils in the Sixth Form, which speaks volumes about the inclusive, respectful and family-like community.’ Art and creative subjects thrive, and students explore interests from photography and fashion to stop-motion animation, while the New Barn Theatre is a valued space for self-expression and confidence-building. Extra-curricular clubs – many started by students – display a wide range of interests that include dodgeball, knitting, couch to 5K and and an RHS award-winning horticultural society, while made-from-scratch meals are eaten in the atmospheric Abbots’ Hall. (Afternoon teas are the province of a pastry chef, no less.) The school’s size allows ‘for every student to be known, noticed and nurtured’. Those who think outside the box are celebrated, and a mix of GCSEs and BTECs provide a level of exam flexibility. Plans are in the pipeline to both extend the learning development suite (dubbed ‘LD’) and spruce up the boarding rooms, while a new equine area allows riders to train in all conditions. With 97 per cent of pupils representing the school in sports last year, it is well on its way to achieving the target of 100 per cent participation for the full year.

Church Lane, Monkton Combe, Bath BA2 7HG

Website monktoncombeschool.com Head Bradley Salisbury, since 2026; previously head of Dean Close School Pupils 640. Day: 221 boys, 173 girls; boarding: 119 boys, 127 girls Ages 2–18 Term Fees Day: £11,040; boarding: £17,985 Bursaries 11% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni TED owner and entrepreneur Chris Anderson, former head of MI6 and former master of Pembroke College, Cambridge Sir Richard Dearlove, former England netball player Ama Agbeze

Under Monkton Combe’s Think Differently regime – and much to parents’ approval – inquiry and soft skills are threaded throughout the curriculum. ‘It follows what it feels is best for the children, rather than tradition, [and] we found its holistic approach to education very appealing,’ says one, who also praises the school’s ‘encouragement to embrace failure and try again’.If the recently revamped sports facili-ties provide a perfect setting for pupils to learn core skills, try, fail and ultimately succeed, partnerships with such local organisations as Bath Rugby open up perspectives beyond the school gates. Likewise, the drama department likes to push boundaries, as evidenced by one recent performance in which pupils performed from AI prompts. The annual house music competition is a cherished event in the calendar. Artistic types can while away the hours in the photography suite and print workshop; and clubs span mechanical engineering, crochet and creative writing. Otherwise, the refurbished boarding houses feel cosy and warm; and investment will next be funnelled into growing the Culinary School. Responding to a surge in interest, a former staff room will become a purpose-built teaching kitchen, complete with eight workstations.

Curzon Street, Calne, Wiltshire SN11 0DF

Website stmaryscalne.org Head Anne Wakefield, since 2024; previously deputy head of Benenden School Pupils 340 girls: 68 day, 272 boarding Ages 2–18 Term Fees Day: £15,102; boarding: £20,346 Bursaries 17% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni War artist Arabella Dorman, journalist and broadcaster Roya Nikkhah, actress Belinda Stewart-Wilson

Parents are drawn to St Mary’s by its ‘excellent academic reputation, kind and supportive ethos and happy alumnae’. With a horizontal boarding structure separating girls into their age groups – rather than a vertical house system – tight friendships are formed here. Not least because the boarding provision itself is brilliantly flexible: the on-site co-ed prep has introduced boarding for Years 5 and 6, while Years 7 and 8 have a new flexi-scheme, whereby they can sleep over for up to three nights a week. In another development, the art rooms have been refurbished, providing even more inspiration to an already thriving department. (An artist in residence is welcomed every year, and a London exhibition takes place every three.) Still, some things never change. The annual fashion show, for example, which raises funds to address global period poverty; and Charity Week, which unites students, staff and parents. In that vein, the school says it will be making a concerted effort this year to boost giving for bursaries, through such events as cabaret nights. Lucky recipients – results are strong and, in 2025, 62 per cent of A-levels were graded A*–A, with leavers heading to such universities as Durham, Exeter and Edinburgh. But as the motto says, Calne Girls Can. The tennis academy has 20 courts and gives access to year-round coaching, while lacrosse and riding – the latter from nearby stables – are ever-popular. As for ‘wonderful’ head Anne Wakefield, one pupil says: ‘She is so approachable and kind, and encourages us to pursue our individual strengths and talents.’

Abbey Road, Sherborne, Dorset DT9 3LF

Website sherborneboys.group Head Simon Heard, since 2025, previously deputy master of Haileybury Pupils 560 boys: 30 day, 530 boarding Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £12,175; boarding: £19,350 Bursaries 18% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Newsreader Tom Bradby, Coldplay singer-songwriter Chris Martin, actor Hugh Bonneville

Musical enterprise hasn’t stopped with a certain chart-topping alumnus: having already released two singles, a student band called Gauge the Room is racking up streams, and the symphony orchestra performs at such venues as London’s Cadogan Hall. Music is a big deal at Sherborne: 320 lessons are delivered every week and the lunchtime recitals on the town’s High Street are popular with locals. Core sports here are rugby, football, hockey, cricket and tennis, but less conventional pursuits such as polo and shooting are popular, too. As for financial assistance, around 100 boys are supported by means-tested bursaries; and a partnership with The Royal National Children’s Springboard Foundation gives transformational 110 per cent awards to those identified as having the promise to thrive here. The ‘Separate, Yet Together’ relationship with Sherborne Girls means there’s plenty of collaborating and socialising; and their latest joint ventureis the Sixth Form of the Future programme.
