Celebrating the Duke of Kent at 91: How a boy thrust into the spotlight amid family tragedy became a trusted confidant of his cousin, Queen Elizabeth II

It will be a poignant day for Prince Edward, Duke of Kent, as he marks his 91st birthday one year after the death of his wife and just days after his private secretary passed away

By Stephanie Bridger-Linning · October 9, 2026

Prince Edward, the Duke of Kent, turns 91 on 9th October. No doubt it will be a day steeped in poignant reflection, as the birthday comes just weeks after he joined the royal family to mark the anniversary of the death of his beloved wife, Katharine, Duchess of Kent. The Prince is also grieving the death of his private secretary, Nicholas Turnbull, who died in September.

Yet despite his personal losses and ageing years, the Duke of Kent remains a stalwart of the Royal Family. Still the holder of many patronages, he can be found supporting the arts at his beloved Wigmore Hall, sitting in the royal box at Wimbledon, or celebrating the Scouts in Scotland in the middle of a heatwave. This work ethic is typical of the royal, who embodies the same sense of tireless duty as his late cousin, Queen Elizabeth II, with whom he enjoyed a lifelong friendship. ‘I always felt I wanted to support her,’ he once said. ‘That’s by far the most important thing in life.’

Perhaps taking inspiration from his late cousin, the Duke of Kent has continued to work into his tenth decade of life. Indeed, he has carried out dozens of engagements this year alone. This unwavering devotion to the monarchy has led to the Duke being affectionately known as ‘Steady Eddie’ – a trusted confidant to generations of royals who maintains a position at the very heart of The Firm.

Born in 1935, the Duke of Kent is the eldest child of the late Prince George, Duke of Kent, and Princess Marina of Greece and Denmark, making him a grandchild of King George V and Mary of Teck. His mother was also a first cousin of Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh. Edward was named in honour of his uncle, the future King Edward VIII. Among the guests at his Buckingham Palace christening was the then nine-year-old Princess Elizabeth, as well as the King and Queen, who served as godparents. His sister, Princess Alexandra, The Hon. Lady Ogilvy, was born the following year. The Duke's younger brother, Prince Michael of Kent, followed.

However, tragedy struck the family just weeks after the arrival of the youngest Kent son when Prince George was killed in action while serving in the RAF during the Second World War. The 39-year-old royal died alongside 13 other personnel after their aircraft crashed shortly after take-off from the Scottish Highlands in a non-operational mission bound for Iceland. The exact circumstances of the death were not made public and remain a mystery to this day.

It meant Prince Edward succeeded his father as the Duke of Kent at just six years old. In keeping with royal tradition, the young Duke of Kent was educated at Ludgrove (also attended by Prince William and Prince Harry), then Eton and later Le Rosey in Switzerland. The Duke then entered the Royal Military Academy at Sandhurst, where his aptitude for foreign languages was recognised with a prize.

Aged just 16, the Duke of Kent stepped up and represented his family line when he walked behind the coffin of his uncle, King George VI, as it processed to Westminster Abbey. A year later, at the 1953 coronation of Queen Elizabeth II, he made a solemn vow to be his cousin's ‘liege man of life and limb’ – and continues to live by these words today.

The Duke of Kent reflected on the enormity of the occasion in his autobiography, A Royal Life, and confessed that he had been anxious about forgetting his lines. ‘It was quite daunting,’ he wrote, adding: ‘The whole service was pretty long and it was certainly very impressive.’

The Duke of Kent served in the military for 21 years, retiring in 1976. It was thanks to the Armed Forces that he met the woman who would become his wife, Katharine Worsley – the only daughter of Sir William Worsley, 4th Baronet, and his wife Joyce Morgan Brunner. The Duke of Kent spent time stationed with his regiment at Catterick Garrison, near the Worsley family seat of Hovington Hall. They announced their engagement in March 1961, with the royal wedding itself taking place that June.

When the couple married, they broke with centuries of royal tradition. Rather than opting for Westminster Abbey, St Paul's Cathedral, the Chapel Royal at St James's Palace or St George's Chapel at Windsor, Katharine insisted that they tie the knot in her home county of Yorkshire, at the historic York Minster, which had not been used as a royal wedding venue for 600 years.

The bride's choice of dress was decidedly 1960s in style. Designed by John Cavanagh, on the recommendation of her future mother-in-law, Princess Marina, it featured a round neck with fitted sleeves, as well as a full skirt with a 15-foot train. There were some concerns that the dress was too awkward and heavy to manoeuvre, with the bride reportedly practising her curtsey multiple times. She borrowed a diamond and pearl tiara from her mother-in-law's collection, opting for a diadem that had belonged to her husband's grandmother, Queen Mary. The same tiara was later loaned to Katharine's daughter, Lady Helen Taylor, on her wedding day in 1992.

Guests at the wedding included the late Queen, as well as the Queen Mother, the Duke of Edinburgh, King Charles III (then Prince Charles) and the Princess Royal, who served as a flower girl. There were also royals from Denmark, Greece, the Netherlands and Norway in attendance. It was at this wedding that the future King Juan Carlos of Spain would meet his royal bride, Princess Sophia of Greece and Denmark.

The Duke and Duchess of Kent went on to welcome three children, Lord Nicholas Windsor, Lady Helen Taylor and George, Earl of St Andrews, and 10 grandchildren, including Lady Amelia Windsor, Lady Marina Windsor and Cassius Taylor.

The Duke of Kent is involved with over 140 different charities, organisations and professional bodies. These ‘cover a wide range of issues, from commemorating the war dead, to fostering the development of British technology and industry,’ according to the Royal Family website. He is arguably best known as the President of the All England Lawn Tennis and Croquet Club, a position he held for more than five decades from 1969. In the role, the Duke of Kent was on hand to present the winners' trophies to the champions. He was succeeded by the Princess of Wales in 2021.

The Duke of Kent represented his cousin Queen Elizabeth II on numerous overseas engagements and remained a trusted member of her inner circle until her death. Indeed, the esteemed position in which he was held was made plain for the world in 2022 when he joined Her Majesty at Buckingham Palace – her first balcony appearance since Prince Philip's death the previous year. When the world mourned the death of a beloved monarch in 2023, Prince Edward was grieving the loss of his beloved cousin.

After marrying the Duke of Kent, the Duchess of Kent became a working member of the Royal Family. Her experience as a primary school music teacher reflected a wider lifelong love of music, and much of her work centred around this. However, she stepped back from royal duties in 2002 and rarely made public appearances in her later years. Katharine did not attend the Coronation of King Charles III or the state funeral of Queen Elizabeth II, and was last seen photographed in public when she and her husband watched a performance by three pipers from the Royal Scots Dragoon Guards (Carabiniers and Greys) to mark the Duke's 89th birthday in October 2024. Buckingham Palace announced her death at the age of 92 on 4th September 2025.

The Royal Family turned out in force to pay their respects and support the Duke of Kent at the funeral, with King Charles III and the Prince and Princess of Wales among those in attendance. Katharine's coffin was taken by hearse from Kensington Palace to Westminster Cathedral on Monday, 15th September, where the Rite of Reception and Vespers took place, on the eve of the Requiem Mass. In a sign of the respect the Duchess of Kent earned from the Royal Family, her coffin was transported in the Royal Hearse, designed by Queen Elizabeth II, and draped in the Royal Standard.

The anniversary of the Duchess's death – and the death of the Duke's private secretary – will no doubt weigh heavily on the heart of the Duke of Kent as he marks his birthday today. But if there's one thing that the couple stood for throughout their royal careers, it was the importance of diligence and duty – values that the 91-year-old continues to embody.
