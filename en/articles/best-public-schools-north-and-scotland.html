Edit Schools Guide 2027: The best public schools in the North and Scotland

The 2027 Edit Schools Guide reveals the best public schools in the North and Scotland

By Edit · September 29, 2026

Carrington Road, Edinburgh EH4 1QX

Website fettes.com Head Richard Girvan, since 2026, previously principal & CEO of the Stephen Perse Foundation Pupils 682. Day: 137 boys, 127 girls; boarding: 221 boys, 197 girls Ages 5–18 Term Fees Day: £15,900; boarding: £18,850 Bursaries 8% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Former prime minister Sir Tony Blair, athlete Jake Wightman, author Elizabeth Macneal

You won’t hear any grumbles about stodgy beige food at Fettes. Two years in a row, the executive chef has won Chef of the Year in the Scottish School Food Awards; his clientele devours avocado on sourdough for breakfast, and pork belly and bao buns for lunch. They burn off all the energy in such activities as the Scottish Islands Peaks Race or by running around the incredible grounds. (These are maintained by George and Kevin McNab, recipients last year of this guide’s Unsung Heroes Award.) Bagpiping is a big deal here, and the band’s venues have included Edinburgh Castle, the palace roof of Holyrood House and the Tartan Day Parade in New York. Academic results are equally impressive: the IB points average is 38; 41 per cent of the latest A-levels were graded A*–A; and eight leavers took up Oxbridge places. Next is the Allardice Centre, which will centralise medical and pastoral care, and a Sixth-form programme entitled ‘This will be your world’, to provide global perspectives. New head Richard Girvan inherits a school wherethe staff ‘work tirelessly to ensure students can thrive’, say parents.

Giggleswick, Settle, North Yorkshire BD24 0DE

Website giggleswick.org.uk Head Sam Hart, since 2022; previously housemaster at Winchester College Pupils 345. Day: 103 boys, 90 girls; boarding: 81 boys, 71 girls Ages 4–18 Term Fees Day: £10,160; boarding: £15,710 Bursaries 6.7% of pupils, up to 90% Alumni TV presenter Richard Whiteley, actor Anthony Daniels, golfer Gary Wolstenholme

After merging three boarding houses into two and trimming a few treats – there’s still bread and fruit at mid-morning ‘Stidge’, but no more cake – ‘Giggs’ has promised parents that the VAT imposed on private education will never be passed on to them; yet it still managed to open a new Sixth Form study centre. Kudos to head Sam Hart, who has overseen grade rises at every exam level since he arrived four years ago. What is more, he has launched a programme called ‘CASE’ (short for Creative, Active, Service and Enrichment), to equip children with all the non-academic skills they will need in adulthood, which encompasses everything from astronomy to Welsh for beginners. Hart says the gorgeous wild location is one of the key drivers of school life; so naturally, he’s keen on the biennial, 36-mile ‘Gigg-to-Gigg’ trek round the nearby Three Peaks, which sees pupils set off at 5am and return as late as 9pm. While he aims to ‘instil values and resilience’ in his charges, he’s proud of his improve-ments to pastoral care as well.

Glenalmond, Perthshire PH1 3RY

Website glenalmondcollege.co.uk Head Timothy Bayley, since 2026; previously executive principal at Doha British School Pupils 299. Day: 56 boys, 55 girls; boarding: 95 boys, 93 girls Ages 12–18 Term Fees Day: £11,610; boarding: £19,030 Bursaries 32% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Actor Robbie Coltrane, golfer Carly Booth, former royal private secretary Baron Geidt

Parents love Glenalmond’s magical location for being reminiscent of Hogwarts. ‘However, that’s only the cover,’ says one. ‘Inside the book, there are many more treasures.’ Namely, 300 acres of Perthshire which foster an outdoorsy ethos. If these hardy students aren’t training for the Scottish Islands Peaks Race, you might find them scrambling up cliffs, paddling kayaks or using the recently completed mountain-bike trail. Rugby is thriving under the former Scotland international WP Nel and the school has launched a partnership with Chelsea FC. In the creative sphere, the actors brought the house down with a thundering interpretation of We Will Rock You, while the Gala Concert brought together soloists, ensembles and choirs for a musical programme that showcased both classical and contemporary repertoires. With a growing number of pupils opting to study art GCSE, one 2025 leaver was recognised as a top performer in the Cambridge exam board’s Art and Design: Fine Art A-level. New developments include a strengthening of the pastoral provision with the appointment of a dedicated wellbeing lead, and some sprucing-up of the Sixth Form Centre and boarding houses – which is all good preparation for the wider world, in which 15 per cent of leavers won places to St Andrew’s, and 45 per cent overall to Russell Group universities. ‘At Glenalmond, standards and hard work are the mantras; and fairness and community spirit are prevalent,’ remarks one parent. ‘But there is always an underlying sense of mischief and fun.’

Elgin, Moray IV30 5RF

Website gordonstoun.org.uk Head Simon Cane-Hardy, since 2023; previously deputy head of Prior Park College Pupils 486. Day: 79 boys, 89 girls; boarding: 175 boys, 143 girls Ages 4–18 Term Fees Day: £16,365; boarding: £23,000 Bursaries 35% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni HM King Charles III, composer of Flower of Scotland Roy Williamson, actress Oona Chaplin

Nestled in the foothills of the Cairngorms, Gordonstoun instils a sense of responsibility and resilience in pupils, challenging them to discover who they are and what they’re capable of. The school’s connection with nature and its emphasis on practical skills fosters confidence; and whether kayaking, sailing, skiing, surfing, golfing or climbing, pupils are encouraged to push their boundaries. It’s little wonder that the student team came second in the gruelling Strathpuffer 17-hour mountain-bike race, despite facing professional-level competition. (The recent opening of the Gordonstoun Adventure Campus has provided even more opportunities to get the adrenaline pumping.) Elsewhere, in 2025, Gordonstoun took delivery of 17 new Steinway pianos – thus achieving prestigious All-Steinway status – and welcomed leading conductor, composer and organist Matthew Owens as its artistic director. In the pedagogic field, both a Deer Management Diploma and a BTEC Level 3 in medical sciences are being introduced.

Wrexham Road, Chester, CH4 7QL

Website kingschester.co.uk Head Luke Rogers, since 2026; previously deputy head academic of Bedford School Pupils 1,170 day: 702 boys, 468 girls Ages 4–18 Term Fees £6,826 Bursaries 5% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Financial expert Martin Lewis, GB rower Harry Brightmore, broadcaster Jonathan Samuels

Incoming head Luke Rogers is committed to honouring his school’s rich heritage while extending its impact beyond the gates and encouraging innovation. Its Chester Schools Together scheme already offers cultural, sporting and educational opportunities to all secondary pupils across the city, alongside an Oxbridge support programme. In-school developments have included the appointment of a dedicated head of futures, to prepare pupils for university and the world of work, and of a new director of rowing who will ramp up what is already a very strong suit (as are hockey and cricket). Elsewhere, the introduction of a piano to the gallery in the middle of the senior school has resulted in numerous impromptu sing-alongs – a great way to warm the vocal cords for such productions as this year’s West Side Story, with props made by the art students. Further afield, trips have taken in such destinations as Sri Lanka and Tanzania, and such is the popularity of the Dungeons and Dragons club that it now meets four times a week.

Linkfield Road, Musselburgh, East Lothian EH21 7RE

Website loretto.com Head Pete Richardson, since 2024; previously Vicegerent of Loretto Pupils 450. Day: 152 boys, 104 girls; boarding: 109 boys, 85 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £11,567; boarding: £16,967 Bursaries Variable % of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni 1960s’ Formula 1 world champion Jim Clark, journalist Andrew Marr, historian and academic Clare Jackson

Legendary 19th-century Loretto head Hely Hutchinson Almond believed that unimpeded access to fresh air was vital for development. (He consequently banished ‘restrictive’ ties from the uniform in a ruling that prevails to this day.) And certainly, the students benefit from exposure to the great outdoors: led by head of rugby Andrew Binikos, or Bini as he’s affectionately known, students are regularly selected for regional and national teams; and its golfers are just a hop away from some of the finest courses in the world – as well as their very own academy. Described by parentsas ‘absolutely top class’, the latterhas now partnered with Edinburgh university to deliver a new Gold Performance programme that will ensure its members can continue to flourish both athletically and academically. However, it’s not just the sporting excellence that appeals to parents. They’re also attracted by Loretto’s ‘small, family community’ and its ‘culture of kindness and inclusivity’. The medical centre provides access to a counselling service; pastoral care is strengthened by a thriving house system; and a Life Skills initiative provides the tool kit to deal with the day-to-day pressures facing modern teens. With boarders from 28 different nations, the school offers a busy roster of weekend activities, ranging from jaunts to secret beaches and pizza-making sessions to trips into Edinburgh. Overseeing it all is head Pete Richardson who, says a parent, is ‘interested in every individual and passionate about Loretto’s legacy’.

Old Hall Lane, Manchester M13 0XT

Website mgs.org Head Dr Martin Boulton, since 2013; previously under master of Westminster School Pupils 1,635 day boys Ages 7–18 Term Fees £6,603 Bursaries 14% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Actor Sir Ben Kingsley, cricket journalist Michael Atherton, author and TV presenter Dr Rangan Chatterjee

Manchester Grammar boys are guided by the school motto sapere aude (‘Dare to be Wise’), a phrase coined by the Roman poet Horace and borrowed by the philosopher Immanuel Kant. It almost goes without saying that their results are excellent: last year 32 pupils scored Oxbridge offers and 41 received offers from Russell Group universities to read medicine or dentistry. Because the aim here is to educate the brightest young minds in the North West of England, regardless of circumstances, an incredible 200 full-fee bursaries are currently in place. The school’s resulting diversity is one of its huge strengths and distinctive characteristics, leading for example to Friday’s assemblies run by the Sixth Form offering five options across different faiths (or non-religious) with the boys going to whichever one interests them, regardless of their beliefs. Work is under way on a new Sixth Form Centre, while Years 10 and 11 must now take a course on the use of AI as a research tool. In sport, swimming and water polo remain popular, while table tennis grows in strength with several pupils reaching national finals. As befits an outward-facing institution, musicians perform at such notable venues as Manchester Cathedral; there are regular rambles, camping expeditions and trips to the Owl’s Nest (a hut given to the school bya previous head many decades ago). And in the summer term, the much-loved tradition of Activities Week means that teaching stops and all the students follow their own chosen pursuits.

Colinton Road, Edinburgh EH13 0PU

Website merchiston.co.uk Head Huw Jones, since 2025; previously deputy head of Warminster School Pupils 294. Day: 123 boys, 24 girls; boarding: 147 boys Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £8,640; boarding: £17,748 Bursaries 25% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Scotland rugby player Jamie Dobie, former governor of the Bank of Scotland Sir Peter Burt, tennis professional Jacob Fearnley

Merchiston Castle might be small, but it reckons it’s pretty mighty. Drawing strength from its diminutive size, it makes great efforts to get to know its pupils as individuals and to build tight-knit bonds between parents, staff and students; and equipped with a detailed understanding of every child’s strengths and weaknesses, its teachers can design personalised programmes to offer targeted support. As for its setting, complete with an original castle, the leafy 100- acre grounds conceal Merchiston’s proximity to Edinburgh while also providing ample opportunities for sport. The tennis academy provides an established pathway for elite players. (Established British pro Jacob Fearnley is an alum.) The rugby 1st XV has been in the final of the Scottish Schools Cup for the last nine years, winning it seven times. And the school’s golfers are delighted with their new whizz-bang centre, packed with fancy technology and simulators. But more cerebral pursuits are far from neglected. The STEM extracurricular programme is extremely popular; big drama productions are often held in collaboration with sister school St George’s; the music department is growing; and following the appointment of an artist in residence, the art department is thriving. Another plus for parents is the tier of flexible day and boarding options. The school aims to provide education in parallel with enjoyment. Crucially, one student tells us, there are plenty of opportunities for fun. ‘The events most looked forward to are the various balls and ceilidhs through the year,’ he says. ‘Everyone gets involved and dresses up in kilts or trews and bow ties. It’s always a great time.’

Garscube Terrace, Murrayfield, Edinburgh EH12 6BG

Website stge.org.uk Head Carol Chandler-Thompson, since 2022; previously head at Blackheath High School, London Pupils 507. Day: 10 boys, 471 girls; boarding: 26 girls Ages 2–18 Term Fees Day: £8,180; boarding: £16,774 Bursaries 5% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Philosopher and science writer Cordelia Fine, All3Media CEO Jane Turton, golf broadcaster Iona Stephen

At St George’s, the aim is to give girls the freedom to choose and the courage to achieve on their own terms. And more than 130 years after its founders campaigned for girls to have access to a full, liberal education, that spirit still runs through the establishment. Pupils are known ‘by name and by nature’, and the combination of intimate class sizes, a flexible curriculum and personalised pathways gives the teaching a notably tailored feel. There is real academic ambition here, and it’s paired with a distinctly forward-looking vision. (St George’s is the only Microsoft Showcase School in Scotland, and its work with industry, universities and the Scottish government is helping to create more opportunities for girls in technology.) But beyond the classroom, horizons are broad, too: pupils might ski in Les Menuires, debate Middle East policy at St Andrew’s, see art in New York City or camp out in the Cairngorms; and outreach is woven neatly into school life. Fearless Fridays open the doors to local girls for free workshops in such topics as animation, coding and craft, while the £500,000 or so spent yearly on means-tested bursaries broadens access. At St George’s, inclusion feels lived rather than labelled, with international days bringing a riot of colour and the curriculum reflecting the wide range of heritages which are represented here. Add in a smart new food and nutrition space, and this is a school displaying purpose, polish and plenty of confidence.

St Andrews, Fife KY16 9QJ

Website stleonards-fife.org Head Simon Brian, since 2021; previously deputy head of Charterhouse Pupils 529. Day: 193 boys, 186 girls; boarding: 78 boys, 72 girls Ages 4–18 Term Fees Day: £7,931; boarding: £19,138 Bursaries 32% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Former Vogue editor Audrey Withers, tennis player Kitty McKane Godfree, suffragette Margaret Haig Thomas

One of only two schools in the UK to deliver all four IB programmes, St Leonards now runs from kindergarten to age 18. Ending with offers from an array of esteemed universities, it starts with brilliant outdoor play areas and a wonderful Beach School which takes full advantage of the fantastic Fife coast setting. Perhaps it’s all the fresh air that supercharges the first-rate rugby and tennis players – and keeps boarders battling it out over games of dodgeball and basketball in an indoor sports centre which has just had a glittering revamp. Still, its actors take plays to the Edinburgh Festival Fringe, while musicians perform in numerous concerts and events. It is innovative, too, as demonstrated by the launch of the ‘postgraduate’ year – the first of its kind in Scotland – which will offer students aged 17 to 19 the opportunity to consolidate their academic strengths, develop in inde- pendence and prepare for university.

Clifton, York YO30 6AB

Website stpetersyork.org.uk Head Jeremy Walker, since 2019; previously principal of King’s School Rochester Pupils 1,273. Day: 634 boys, 535 girls; boarding: 51 boys, 53 girls Ages 2–18 Term Fees Day: £10,110; boarding: £17,315 Bursaries 4% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Gunpowder plotter Guy Fawkes, cricketer Jonny Bairstow, TV presenter Kat Downes

‘As a former pupil myself, and one who benefited hugely from the type of education St Peter’s had to offer, it felt an easy decision to educate our children there,’ says one parent, who reveals that her children love the place, ‘fundamentally because of how their teachers make them feel in their day-to-day learning environment’. Its ethos rests on the four principles of challenge, community, kindness and equity; and as a consequence, more than 50 pupils are in receipt of bursaries and one is even sponsored by the racing car company Ginetta (which supports a gifted STEM student through the Sixth Form and gives them work experience). Meanwhile, there’s also a lot of outreach in the local community: masterclasses and summer schools are given to students across York, while GCSEs in Latin and astronomy are offered to those who can’t access them; and every year, the public is invited to a series of lectures, often delivered in partnership with the likes of the York Literature Festival. Some recent developments have included Saturday school starting from Year 7 rather than Year 4, the adoption of thematic curricula in some years and a mobile phone ban up to Year 11. And the sparkling new Sixth Form Centre is part of a wider campus masterplan which will honour the institution’s 1,400-year history while simultaneously innovating for the future – and ultimately transforming St Peter’s from a three- to a two-school structure. Another change that will excite parents? A new nursery, welcoming lucky children from the age of six months old.

Sedbergh, Cumbria LA10 5HG

Website sedberghschool.org Head Robin Pyper, since 2026, previously deputy rector at Dollar Academy Pupils 717. Day: 57 boys, 52 girls; boarding: 357 boys, 251 girls Ages 4–18 Term Fees Day: £13,226; boarding: £18,300 Bursaries 82% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Cricketer Harry Brook, England rugby international player Abbie Ward, England 2003 World Cup winner Will Greenwood

New head Robin Pyper takes the reins at a school with serious history. Treasures in the archives here include hymn books signed by The Beatles, Will Greenwood’s World Cup-winning rugby boots and a rugby ball that’s been sent into space. Then there are the endurance sports. The school recently produced national champions in fell and cross-country running, as well as medallists at the prestigious English Schools Athletics Championships. With 2025 seeing 100 staff and pupils complete the Great North Run, raising £50,000 for Save the Children, they certainly have something to sing about – namely, the school’s ‘Long Run’ song, which is about its famous 10-mile fell race. As they do in the Chapel Choir, which competed for the National Choir of the Year award for the past two years; and also in the drama department, whose recent production of Les Misérables was booked solid three nights in a row. But all tastes are catered for here. Green-fingered pupils manage the gardens at their boarding houses, and the BTEC in agriculture is attracting more and more entrants; both open-water swimming and mountain biking are popular pastimes; and pupils have launched an Amnesty group, an investment club and a Pride Alliance. On the academic front, 54 per cent of last year’s leavers accepted offers to Russell Group universities, and a growing number are receiving full scholarships to the USA. Next up? As the Sedbergh Schools Group launches, a merger with Terrington Hall Prep School is forthcoming.

Stonyhurst, Lancashire BB7 9PZ

Website stonyhurst.ac.uk Head William Doherty, since 2026; previously principal of Xavier College, Melbourne Pupils 683. Day: 164 boys, 126 girls; boarding: 216 boys, 177 girls Ages 3–18Term Fees Day: £10,386; boarding: £17,847 Bursaries 57% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Former New York Times CEO Mark Thompson, founders of Joseph Joseph Antony and Richard Joseph, physician Gabriel Leung

Bringing more than 30 years of experience at Australia’s leading Jesuit schools, new head William Doherty recently joined Stonyhurst, the oldest Jesuit boarding school in the world (and the only one run by the Society of Jesus in the UK to offer the IB). With its cosmopolitan outlook, it recently appointed a head of global education to oversee international partnerships. There’s a flexible range of boarding options; and with the view that teens need sleep, a drive to promote healthier habits has been manifest in workshops, one-to-one guidance and restful areas called ‘sleep stations’. Students are encouraged to connect with nature in the 1,000-acre grounds in the Ribble Valley; and rested brains clearly produce impressive results, with more than half of last year’s A-levels graded A*–A. In other developments, a number of creative subjects – such as textiles and product design – have been introduced, and these are taught in the brilliant new Design and Technology Centre.

Forgandenny, Perth PH2 9EG

Website strathallan.co.uk Head Mark Lauder, since 2017; previously head of Ashville College Pupils 486. Day: 141 boys, 134 girls; boarding: 122 boys, 89 girls Ages 5–18 Term Fees Day: £11,832; boarding: £18,089 Bursaries 18% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Invictus Games medallist JJ Chalmers, rugby player Zander Fagerson, Team GB swimmer Duncan Scott

Strathallan was founded in 1913 by a 25-year-old teacher who started with just seven boys and a determination that pupils should be known as individuals, that education is to be enjoyed, and that schools should uncover hidden talent. This ethos prevails: ‘There are many teaching staff whom we consider to be exceptional,’ one parent says. The school has launched a new initiative to provide financial help for families connected with the armed forces – it’s been busy broadening its athletic development programme, too – while the music department delivers more than 6,300 lessons a year and the Big Sing is a much-loved annual event that has enjoyed viral online popularity. Sport is inclusive – every single pupil up to the age of 15 plays on a school team – but it also focuses on excellence, and there are more than 60 national age-grade players, 11 full-time pro-fessionals and nine international rugby caps among the alumni. All this activity builds up an appetite; so it is lucky that, as one pupil declares, the food is ‘amazing’.

Wellington Road, Manchester M14 6BL

Website wgs.org Head Tom Bennett, since 2026; previously deputy head academic Pupils 733 day girls Ages 7–18 Term Fees £6,580 Bursaries 15% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Givenchy creative director Sarah Burton, neuroscientist Brenda Milner, Wimbledon Championships Referee Denise Parnell

Former deputy head Tom Bennett has taken the helm at Withington, and he’s inherited a school where the girls get great results: 58 per cent of recent GCSEs were graded 9, and an annual average of one in 10 leavers has won Oxbridge places (although there’s a growing trend in applying for degree apprenticeships with such companies as JP Morgan and Dyson). ‘At Withington, the atmosphere is welcoming and friendly… and there are endless opportunities for us to explore our interests,’ says one student. Sport is designed to be inclusive and enjoyed by all, which is why there is a lot of choice: alongside lacrosse and hockey, Sixth-form girls can play padel, take wellbeing walks in local parks and play ultimate frisbee. Or get involved in the theatre: one recent event – ‘Return to the Dionysia: A Celebration of Greek Theatre’ – transported audiences back to Ancient Greece and the origins of performance; and all-school productions take place every two years. Or students can play in garage bands and strut their stuff on Open Mic Fridays. After a five-year hiatus, the dance competition has returned, with 2025’s Dancing Through the Decades theme taking audiences from the 1960s to the present day. (There was even a surprise rendition of Don’t Stop Me Now by some teachers, who kept the hilarity going by joining in the lip-sync challenge.) But there’s a philanthropic streak here, too, with the well-established Marjorie Hulme Bursary Fund extending the possibility of a Withington education to a wider demographic.

The Friarage, Yarm TS15 9EJ

Website yarmschool.org Head Dr Huw Williams, since 2019; previously second master of Tonbridge School Pupils 1,154 day: 576 boys, 578 girls Ages 0–18 Term Fees £7,361 Bursaries 6.3% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Rower Katherine Copeland, Paralympian swimmer Faye Rogers, actor and singer Yazdan Qafouri

Yarm offers a vast selection of extracurriculars, from cheerleading to dance and chess; twice a week, pupils undertake such timetabled activities as creative writing, mindfulness and entrepreneurship; and the outdoor education department is always busy fostering resilience, leadership and teamwork. ‘There is a constant range of things that bring everyone together,’ says one pupil – who’s similarly enthusiastic about head Huw Williams. ‘He’s one of the nicest people you will meet,’ they continue. His charges compete at the highest sporting levels: the U14 team was crowned winners of the 2025 English Netball National Finals, while the senior girls’ quad qualified for last year’s Henley Regatta – a first in school history. There are plentiful opportunities to tread the boards (and musicals produced every other year); and there is a really long list of ensembles for musos to join. What’s more, facilities have undergone a revamp – the Sixth Form Centre has been refurbished and extended, as has the library – and a 750-seat auditorium, new dining hall and boat house are now on the drawing-board. All perfect for the serious studying that goes on here: this is one of the few British schools to offer the prestigious Arkwright Scholarships in engineering, there’s a focus on nurturing future leaders in that discipline as well as technical design, and a growing number of leavers are undertaking degree apprenticeships with such industry leaders as PwC and Morgan Stanley. On the other end of things, the new nursery is taking children from the age of six months.
