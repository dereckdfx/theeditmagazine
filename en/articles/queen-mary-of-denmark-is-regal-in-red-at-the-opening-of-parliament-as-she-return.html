Royals

Queen Mary of Denmark is regal in red at the opening of parliament as she returns from her late father's memorial service

The Danish royal joined King Frederik and Princess Benedikte for the traditional ceremony

By Isaac Bickerstaff · October 6, 2026

Queen Mary of Denmark was at her elegant best as she attended the official opening of parliament with her husband, King Frederik X, on Tuesday. Traditionally, the Folketing opens with a royal visit and a speech from the Prime Minister and the event is constitutionally obligated to take place on the first Tuesday of October, after a service at Christiansborg Palace Church.

It is a tradition to which Queen Mary has become accustomed over her royal career – 20 years as Crown Princess followed by her current two years as queen. As such, she understood precisely how to toe the line between the formality of the engagement and the dash of glamour that is so often expected from royals, donning a regal burgundy peplum ensemble that looked strikingly similar to the Roland Mouret number worn by Princess Sofia of Sweden last year. The Danish royal completed her look with a matching fascinator and sparkling stud earrings.

Pinned to her chest was a gilded Dior brooch, created by the Maison in 1964, according to royal fashion blog UFO No More. The gilded piece is made from turquoise, rhinestones, and red glass and was previously worn by Queen Mary on her April state visit to France – perhaps a diplomatic nod to the origins of the brooch.

There was another special guest at the opening of parliament on Tuesday, King Frederik’s aunt, Princess Benedikte. While she is perhaps less frequently seen than the royal couple, or her sister, Queen Margrethe, the 82-year-old Princess has been a key pillar of the Danish royal family for decades. Described by Margrethe as ‘the most royal of all’ among her generation, Benedikte is known for her down-to-earth approach to her job.

A biography by Anne Sofie Kragh, published to commemorate Benedikte’s 75th birthday, gave royal fans some insight into the personal life of a Princess who has often been reluctant to open up to the press. ‘There are enough people who talk about themselves,’ she told Kragh. Among contributions from her family, including the revelations that Benedikte is an excellent driver, a terrible cook, and enjoys a cold Tuborg beer before bed, the biography paints a picture of a Princess dedicated to public service.

‘The press has an annoying way of depicting us as if we were actors or members of the jet set,’ she said in the book. ‘Call me old-fashioned, but our role is not to act like film stars. Not that there is anything wrong with film stars, but none of us, at least in my generation, wants to act like them.’

It is an attitude that has surely been inherited by her nephew’s wife, who is far less than fussy in her royal engagements. Mary greeted Benedikte, dressed in a powder-blue jacket and pearls, with a kiss and a warm embrace at the opening of the Folketing, a testament to the bond between the two royal women across the generations.

Queen Mary and King Felipe were in high spirits at the event – no doubt a reassuring sight to royal watchers, considering the solemn week the couple have had. The couple travelled to Queen Mary’s native Tasmania for a memorial service in honour of her late father, Professor John Donaldson. The event, which was not officially photographed, was announced in the Danish Royal Court’s Agenda, the equivalent of the Court Circular.

Professor John Dalgleish Donaldson, who was born in East Lothian, Scotland, died in Hobart, Tasmania, on Saturday 11 April. His health had been in decline in recent years. Queen Mary announced the news in a moving statement alongside a black-and-white photo of her father.

‘My heart is heavy, and my thoughts are grey. My beloved father has passed away,’ the Queen wrote. ‘But I know that when the grief settles, the memories will brighten my day, and what will remain strongest is love and gratitude for everything he gave me and taught me.’

Though she has lived in Denmark ever since she started dating Frederik, then the Crown Prince, Mary has maintained a close relationship with her family and with Australia writ large. She was able to spend time with her father during her state visit to the continent at the end of March. The Danish royal family lowered the flags of Amalienborg Palace, Copenhagen, to half-mast as a mark of respect.
