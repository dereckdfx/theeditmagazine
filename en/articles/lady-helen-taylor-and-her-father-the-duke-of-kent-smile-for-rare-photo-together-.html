Lady Helen Taylor and her father, the Duke of Kent, smile for rare photo together at memorial concert honouring the Duchess of Kent

Katharine, Duchess of Kent, dedicated her life to supporting young musicians. One year after her death, the royal family celebrated her legacy in London

By Stephanie Bridger-Linning · October 7, 2026

The Duchess of Kent's family marked the anniversary of her death with a poignant charity concert in support of one of her treasured patronages. Now new photos have been released showing Lady Helen Taylor, the Duke of Kent and other royals who gathered at Sinfonia Smith Square, in Westminster, for the occasion.

One of the most touching images shows Lady Helen Taylor, 62, taking a seat next to her father, who turns 91 this week, ahead of the concert, which was organised by Future Talent, the charity co-founded by the late royal in 2004 to provide funds to young musicians from less advantaged backgrounds. In the photo, Lady Helen leans in towards her father as they smile together for the camera. On her other side is her daughter, Estella Taylor.

Lady Helen's two brothers, the Earl of St Andrews and Lord Nicholas Windsor, were also in attendance. Lord Frederick Windsor, the Duchess’s nephew, attended alongside his wife, Sophie Winkleman – the couple announced their joint patronage of the charity Wild Arts earlier this year. Lady Marina Windsor and Lady Amelia Windsor, daughters of the Earl of St Andrews, were also present.

The concert, which saw performances from over 40 musicians and alumni, was hosted by Future Talent patron Alexander Armstrong, who paid tribute to the Duchess of Kent with a heartwarming speech. ‘Katharine’s love of life, her love of music, but most particularly, her love of people in all their infinite variety,’ he said.

‘Katharine was no aloof patron of Future Talent. She loved nothing more than rolling up her sleeves and getting involved and getting her hands dirty. She loved to keep up with all of the musicians. So much so that she would frequently just ring them out of the blue, just to hear how they were getting on.’

Other speakers told stories of the Duchess’s kindness, with one performer telling the crowd how the Duchess lent her her childhood violin when she heard she was unable to afford her own, The Telegraph reports; another recounted how Katherine told him they would be ‘great friends’ after playing the piano together.

Buckingham Palace announced the death of the Duchess of Kent on 5 September 2025; she was 92. Her funeral, which saw the likes of the Prince and Princess of Wales gather at Westminster Abbey, was the first Catholic memorial service held for a royal in modern history.

Despite the grandeur that came with her royal position following her marriage to the Duke of Kent in 1961, the Duchess preferred to remain as down-to-earth as possible. She famously told the BBC to call her Katharine in an interview after she renounced her title to become a music teacher at Wansbeck Primary at age 61. Despite a life spent as the wife of a prince, Katharine enjoyed complete anonymity at the school. ‘I was just known as Mrs Kent,’ she told the Guardian in 2014, ‘I was just known as Mrs Kent. Only the head knew who I was. The parents didn’t know, and the pupils didn’t know. No one ever noticed. There was no publicity about it at all - it just seemed to work.’

This decision to renounce her royal prerogative had the blessing of Her Majesty The Queen. Katharine explained that ‘there was nothing that I felt I wanted to hide away from… It was just something that happened in my life. I was always - I wouldn’t say proud of it, but I was glad I did it. I was supported through it as well. The Queen said: “Yes, go and do it,” so I did.’

The role was incredibly rewarding for The Duchess, who dedicated herself to instilling a love of music in children from less privileged backgrounds. ‘It's a privilege,’ she told the BBC in 2004. ‘To me it's one of the most exciting jobs anyone can do.’
