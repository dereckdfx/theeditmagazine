Edit Schools Guide 2027: The best prep schools in the Midlands and East Anglia

The 2027 Edit Schools Guide reveals the best prep schools in the Midlands and East Anglia

By Edit · September 29, 2026

Nacton, Suffolk IP10 0ER

Website orwellpark.co.uk Head Alex McCullogh Pupils 167 day and boarding: 94 boys, 73 girls Ages 2–13 Term Fees Day: £9,397; boarding: £13,798

Children are never bored at Orwell Park. When they’re not immersed in lessons, competitive sports or preparing for their latest show – last year’s The Addams Family was a big hit – they’re enjoying a huge variety of activities. (From kit-car club and clay pigeon shooting to sailing and crafts, the opportunities across the board are huge.) The arts and DT are also strong here and the children are encouraged to take pride in their creations, which are regularly put on display. Benenden (whose ‘family’ it joined this year), Harrow and Oundle prove consistently popular leaver destinations; 89 per cent of last year’s departing pupils achieved scholarships; and the most anticipated night of the year is Bust Up & Revue, when the entire school delights in a black-tie Christmas dinner followed by music, singing and skits, with everyone joining in.

Ruyton XI Towns, Shrewsbury SY4 1HX

Website packwood.school Head William Goldsmith Pupils 185 day and boarding: 125 boys, 60 girls Ages 4–13 Term Fees Day: £9,969; boarding: £12,742

There can’t be many preps that issue fishing permits to their eager charges so that the children can spend their leisure hours catching trout and perch in a river running through the grounds. Lucky Packwoodians in their idyllic rural setting. There’s more than half an acre of land per pupil, and its woodlands and open spaces are also big draws for parents who prize the sense of independence their offspring gain from exploring them. To steer this kind of ship, you need confidence, calmness and steadiness; and, reports one parent, head Will Goldsmith has all three. Boarding is popular at Packwood, and almost every pupil will sleep over – either full-time or on a flexi-basis – at some point during their years here. That’s no surprise when evenings at The Warren include hot chocolate and board games.

Trumpington Road, Cambridge CB2 8AG

Website stfaiths.co.uk Head Dr Crispin Hyde-Dunn Pupils 531 day: 282 boys, 249 girls Ages 3–13 Term Fees £8,605

‘A St Faith’s education can look different to every child, whether that means a focus on academic scholarship preparation or having professional input in music or sport,’ says head Crispin Hyde-Dunn. It is all about the ‘right combination of stretch and support’, he explains; and this translates into happy children, half of whom will move on locally to The Leys, with The Perse and King’s Ely other popular choices. Spacious for a city prep, the campus is 30-plus acres, including several sports pitches and plenty of space for outside play, while the classrooms are large, light and inspiring. There is also a fan-tastic Engineering Lab where DT is taught: pertinent at St Faith’s, which was the first school in the country to introduce engineering as a subject for its age range.

Grange Road, Cambridge CB3 9AB

Website sjcs.co.uk Head Sarah Wright Pupils 378 day and boarding: 210 boys, 168 girls Ages 4–13 Term Fees Day: £8,670; boarding: £13,920

At SJCS, all the details add up to engender outstanding pupil happiness. There’s a strong inter-year buddy system; students have five-minute meditation sessions; girls can wear shorts and ties if they like; and both parents and children praise the Emotion 4 Learning wellbeing programme. (‘It calms me down,’ reports one Year 3.) That said, anyone who imagines this school might be a bit hippy-ish should think again. While it isn’t a hothouse, St John’s academic results remain as strong as its exceptional music, dance and drama departments. Head Sarah Wright – who is ‘dynamic, energetic, engaged’, says one parent – has been in situ since last September, and can often be found pitch-side or eating with the children; and her amusement at watching a group of Form 6 boys dancing to Chappell Roan’s ‘Pink Pony Club’ in the school talent show is still fondly remembered.

High Street, Brackley, Northamptonshire NN13 7AZ

Website winchester-house.org Head Antonia Lee Pupils 355 day and boarding: 189 boys, 166 girls Ages 3–14 Term Fees Day: £9,620; weekly boarding: £12,060

It’s sport that traditionally rules at Winchester House, whose 18 acres spread across suburban Brackley and are centred on an historic Tudorbethan mansion (actually rebuilt in the Victorian era, but still hugely characterful). The facilities include Astroturfs for tennis, netball and hockey, a huge seasonal rugby and cricket pitch, an outdoor swimming pool and more – plus all those offered by nearby Stowe. Nonetheless, all interests are catered for: extracurriculars range from cookery and textiles to science club, while a ‘free range’ policy has been instigated by head Antonia Lee, encouraging the children to spend time outside and explore. The theatre has been redeveloped with a professional-standard stage and LAMDA is big (100 per cent of ‘Housian’ candidates achieve merits or distinctions in acting exams); art also thrives. Life here, declares Winchester House proudly, ‘is both broad and joyfully eclectic’.

Witham on the Hill, Bourne, Lincolnshire PE10 0JJ

Website withamhall.com Head William Austen Pupils 230 day and boarding: 126 boys, 104 girls Ages 4–13 Term Fees Day: £8,780; weekly boarding: £11,880

Witham Hall has a quintessentially English pedigree. Its children used to be driven to away matches in an old double-decker London bus owned by founder William Banks. And though times have of course changed, there’s still something ageless about the place. (‘Very rural, ancient trees, big skies’ were our researcher’s first impressions.) Cricket nets installed last year are propelling the team from strength to strength, while a record 23 scholarships – fully two-thirds of the leaving year – included two to Eton, one a prestigious award for Latin and Greek. In drama, the pupils perform everything from Shakespeare to Shrek, and LAMDA is so popular that the length of an entire assembly is needed to present certificates. Music is similarly diverse, a highlight being the school’s famous marching drum ensemble, who kick off the annual fireworks night celebrations by soundtracking the Guy being marched up to the sports field to be set alight. Parents talk of ‘a genuine culture of inclusion’, and one pupil says: ‘What I love about Witham is that it’s cool to want to learn.’
