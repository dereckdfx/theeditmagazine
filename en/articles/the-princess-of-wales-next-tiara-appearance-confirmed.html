Royals

The Princess of Wales' next tiara appearance has been announced. So which piece will she wear?

If there's any royal who knows how to wear a tiara, it's the Princess of Wales. Could the future queen don one of these dazzlers during the upcoming Oman state visit?

By Isaac Bickerstaff · October 8, 2026

From heroic headteachers to unsung legends, the Edit Schools Awards celebrate the crème de la crème of British education. Now, the winners are revealed…

The Princess of Wales has had a busy season after her summer holiday. Whether she's been stopping the show at red carpet premieres, travelling to the Isle of Bute on an official visit with Prince William, or meeting with patients on moving engagements at the Royal Marsden, Catherine's calendar is busier than ever. That's not to mention dropping off her eldest son, Prince George, at Eton College, or completing the Three Peaks Challenge earlier this summer.

One of Catherine's skills as a future queen has always been her ability to deliver exquisite glamour during royal galas and state visits while maintaining the down-to-earth attitude to engagements that has made her so beloved by members of the public. The Princess has been focusing on the latter for the past month or so, but the coming weeks may well see her don her finery once more.

The Sultan of Oman is set to make his way to Britain for a state visit from 20-22 October, which means there may be a Windsor Castle banquet on the cards. And if that were to occur, tradition seems to dictate that Catherine will be among the guests of honour – and will likely wear a tiara. Which might she pick? Edit has everything you need to know about the Princess of Wales' tiara collection.

The Lover's Knot tiara

Although there are a number of beautiful tiaras to choose from, the Princess of Wales has a clear favourite: the Lovers’ Knot tiara.

The tiara has great sentimental value for her and Prince William. Princess Diana also took a shine to the Lovers’ Knot, which was created in 1914 by the House of Garrard for Queen Mary, who was inspired by a similar headpiece owned by her grandmother, Princess Augusta of Hesse.

After Queen Mary’s death in 1953, she bequeathed the Lovers’ Knot to her granddaughter, Queen Elizabeth II. After wearing the tiara throughout the 50s, the late Queen is said to have loaned it to Princess Diana as a wedding present in 1981. In a somewhat iconoclastic move, the late Princess of Wales instead chose to wear the Spencer Tiara, which had been in her family for decades. Diana did, however, soon become closely associated with the Lovers’ Knot, in spite of the fact that it was so heavy to wear that it is said to have given her headaches.

Soon after tying the knot to then-Prince Charles, Princess Diana wore the Lovers’ Knot to the opening of Parliament in 1981, before pairing it with a white dress and pearl drop earrings during a state banquet on her tour of Canada in 1983. Three years later, in 1986, the late Princess wore the piece for a dinner with the Crown Prince of Saudi Arabia. Perhaps her most iconic moment in the tiara was during a 1989 trip to Hong Kong, when she wore it with her Catherine Walker gown that became widely known as the ‘Elvis Dress’.

Following Princess Diana’s divorce from Prince Charles in 1996, she reportedly returned the tiara to Queen Elizabeth, who stored it in the Buckingham Palace vaults. And there it remained, gathering (metaphorical) dust for nearly two decades until Catherine, then Duchess of Cambridge, wore it for the first time during 2015’s annual diplomatic reception at Buckingham Palace.

Throughout the years, Kate would wear the Lover’s Knot tiara at six such diplomatic dinners, as well as for a 2017 state banquet in honour of King Felipe and Queen Letizia, a 2019 dinner for King Willem-Alexander and Queen Maxima and a 2025 dinner for President Macron. When the royals gathered to welcome US President Donald Trump to Buckingham Palace in 2019, Kate was wearing her favourite tiara, and she also chose it for her first state banquet as the Princess of Wales when the Firm hosted South African President Cyril Ramaphosa in 2022.

So, probability, at least, would suggest that Catherine would wear this most treasured of family heirlooms for any upcoming state dinner. But there are plenty of other jewels that the Princess of Wales could also select…

Queen Victoria Oriental Circlet Tiara

Welcoming the German President and First Lady to Windsor in 2025, Catherine wore the Queen Victoria Oriental Circlet Tiara, an heirloom piece created by Garrard for Queen Victoria in 1853 under the direction of her beloved husband and consort, Prince Albert. The piece was a favourite of Queen Elizabeth, the Queen Mother, but was only worn once by Queen Elizabeth II and has only been seen a handful of times in recent decades.

‘The Oriental Circlet was originally set with a collection of opals, one of Albert’s favourite gemstones, and 2,600 diamonds,’ Garrard explains. ‘Later, believing that opals were unlucky, Queen Alexandra replaced them with rubies. However, the magnificent, Indian-inspired design of lotus flowers and Mughal arches remains largely the same today as when it was first created.’

Five years after it was commissioned, the tiara was returned to Garrard for alterations. A small space was added to the back so that it was no longer a complete circle. However, Queen Victoria never had the opportunity to wear the tiara again. Shortly after it was returned to the royal collection, Prince Albert fell ill and died unexpectedly at the age of 42. Queen Victoria was heartbroken and spent the remaining 40 years of her life in mourning. She chose to wear black ensembles and replace her colourful gems – like those in the Oriental Circlet Tiara – with mourning jewellery.

The stunning tiara was inherited by Victoria and Albert's daughter-in-law, Queen Alexandra. However, she had different ideas about the stones that should take pride of place. Owing to superstitions, she chose to replace the ‘unlucky’ opals with a collection of Burmese rubies given to Queen Victoria as a gift from the ruler of Nepal in the 1870s. Garrard notes, ‘It is also noted in our Royal Ledger that while the opals were being replaced, some of the arches were also removed to make the tiara smaller. Despite this, she is believed to have only worn it once on a state visit to Germany.’

The Oriental Circlet was not seen in public again until it was passed to Queen Elizabeth, The Queen Mother in 1936. ‘Photographed wearing it on numerous occasions, it quickly became one of her favourite jewels,’ notes Garrard. ‘When the celebrated British photographer Sir Cecil Beaton captured portraits of the Queen in 1939 following her husband King George VI’s accession to the throne, in 1950 to mark her 50th birthday, and in 1953, just a few weeks before the coronation of her daughter, Queen Elizabeth II, she chose to wear the Oriental Circlet each time.’

‘After the death of King George VI in 1952, the tiara should have passed down to Queen Elizabeth II. However, realising how much her mother loved the piece, she allowed her to keep it. When the Queen Mother passed away in 2002, Queen Elizabeth II inherited the Oriental Circlet. She wore it only once during her reign, on a trip to Malta in 2005.’

Cartier Halo Tiara

At Tiffany &Co., French jewellery designer Jean Schlumberger created some of the house's most enduring silhouettes, loved by society swans including Jackie Kennedy and Bunny Mellon. Now, his artistic vision is being conserved for future generations thanks to a new partnership with Paris' Musée des Arts Décoratifs

There are other potential options for the Princess of Wales' next choice of headpiece. She could, perhaps, push the boat out and wear her wedding day tiara, better known as the Cartier Halo tiara that she wore when she and Prince William tied the knot. Back in 1936, George VI commissioned the French jewellery house to create something spectacular with the diamonds and platinum he had purchased for his wife three weeks before he became King George VI and she became Queen Elizabeth (the future Queen Mother).

Elizabeth was only pictured wearing it shortly after she received it, in the period before she became Queen Consort – after that, she chose to wear larger, grander pieces instead as her jewellery collection grew. The tiara then landed in the hands of her eldest daughter, Princess Elizabeth (the late Queen Elizabeth II), who was gifted the diamond headpiece as an 18th-birthday present in 1944. The Queen has never worn the Cartier Halo tiara publicly. Experts say this is probably due to the fact that she received the tiara at a time when Britain was at war, when it wouldn't have been appropriate to wear such a piece. After her marriage to Prince Philip in 1947, much like her mother, the late Queen had access to other tiaras - and this one never quite made the cut.

Queen Elizabeth, however, was happy to lend her tiara out. Princess Margaret, Her Majesty’s sister, was the first to borrow the Cartier Halo tiara, and it quickly became one of her go-tos when she was young. The tiara was also lent to Princess Anne, who last wore the piece in public in the 1970s.

Lotus Flower Tiara

What other tiara might Catherine wear? Assuming she doesn’t debut a new dazzler, there are two other pieces in her collection. During her first Buckingham Palace state banquet in 2015 (where the royals hosted Chinese President Xi Jinping and his wife, Madame Peng Liyuan), the then-Duchess went for the Lotus Flower tiara, a 1920s piece created for the Queen Mother. She then re-wore it seven years later for a 2022 Diplomatic Corps dinner at the palace.

Strathmore Rose Tiara

In one recent tiara moment in 2023, the Princess delved deep into the archives to welcome the President and First Lady of Korea, choosing to wear the Strathmore Rose Tiara for the first time in nearly a century.

Whether it's the return of her treasured Lover's Knot or a brand new debut in the style of the Oriental Circlet, the Princess of Wales is sure to dazzle in whichever headpiece she should choose for her next grand royal occasion. Watch this space!
