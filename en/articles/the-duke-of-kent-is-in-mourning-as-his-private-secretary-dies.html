The Duke of Kent, 90, is in mourning following the death of his trusted private secretary

Prince Edward marks his 91st birthday this week, and the occasion will be a sombre one as the Duke of Kent mourns one of his closest aides, Nicholas Turnbull

By Isaac Bickerstaff · October 8, 2026

A year after the death of his beloved wife, the Duke of Kent is in mourning once again. Prince Edward, who celebrates his 91st birthday this week, will be grieving the death of his Private Secretary, Nicholas Piers Turnbull, who died on 16 September at the age of 63. Just two days earlier, King Charles had appointed him a Lieutenant of the Royal Victorian Order – an honour awarded for distinguished personal service to the royal family.

Turnbull joined the Duke of Kent’s household in 2019, according to royal blog Gert’s Royals, joining a list of predecessors that includes Lieutenant-Commander Sir Richard Buckley, Nicolas Clark Adamson, and Nicolas Marden. As Private Secretary, Turnbull would have been responsible for organising the Duke’s engagements, responding to his correspondence and managing his public relations. Earlier this year, he joined the Prince in the royal box at Wimbledon.

Born in 1962 to John Earle Turnbull and Mary K Heath, Turnbull married the Hon Arabella Maroussia Philimore, daughter of the 5th Baron Phillimore, around 2006. The pair welcomed two children, Alexander Tristan, 17, and Crispin Finlay John, 14.

It has been a difficult year for the Duke of Kent, who has diligently continued to support the royal family on both official and personal engagements following the death of the Duchess of Kent in September 2025. Katharine Worsley, who was married to Prince Edward for 61 years, passed away on Thursday 4th September, with the King and Queen quickly paying tribute upon the news of her death. The Duchess became the first royal to convert to Catholicism for more than 300 years, and is set to be honoured in the first Royal funeral at Westminster Cathedral since its construction in 1903.

Funeral arrangements on Tuesday 16th September saw members of the wider royal family coming together to honour and remember the Duchess of Kent. The King and Queen and Members of the Royal Family joined the Duke of Kent and members of the Duchess’s family, including their daughter, Lady Helen Taylor, for a Requiem Mass.

In September, the Kent clan reunited for a memorial in honour of the late Duchess. Held at Sinfonia Smith Square in Westminster, the poignant charity concert was put on in support of one of her most treasured patronages, Future Talent, which she co-founded in 2004 to provide funds for young musicians from less advantaged backgrounds.

The Duke of Kent was in attendance at the event, posing for a photograph next to Lady Helen and her daughter, Estella Taylor. Other invitees included the Duke’s two sons, the Earl of St Andrews and Lord Nicholas Windsor, as well as his nephew, Lord Frederick Windsor, who attended with his wife, Sophie Winkleman. Lady Marina Windsor and Lady Amelia Windsor, daughters of the Earl of St Andrews, were also present.

The concert, which saw performances from over 40 musicians and alumni, was hosted by Future Talent patron Alexander Armstrong, who paid tribute to the Duchess of Kent with a heartwarming speech. ‘Katharine’s love of life, her love of music, but most particularly, her love of people in all their infinite variety,’ he said.

‘Katharine was no aloof patron of Future Talent. She loved nothing more than rolling up her sleeves and getting involved and getting her hands dirty. She loved to keep up with all of the musicians. So much so that she would frequently just ring them out of the blue, just to hear how they were getting on.’

Now, just days after paying tribute to his wife on the anniversary of her death, the Duke of Kent is in mourning once again for one of his closest personal aides. With his birthday coming up, no doubt the diligent royal will be surrounded and supported by his family in this trying time.
