Royal stalwarts the Duke and Duchess of Gloucester support King Charles III on major tour of Botswana

The Duke and Duchess of Gloucester have jetted out for a milestone visit marking Botswana's Diamond Jubilee

By Stephanie Bridger-Linning · October 6, 2026

The Duke and Duchess of Gloucester, whose work for the royal family often flies under the radar, have embarked upon a major tour of Botswana.

The Duke and Duchess' four-day tour kicked off on Monday 5th October, with the couple touching down in Gabotone, Botswana, where they will celebrate Botswana's Diamond Jubilee and 60 years of UK-Botswana diplomatic relations.

Showcasing her typically polished style, the Duchess opted for a timeless pinstripe navy suit, which she layered over a simple pale blue top. However the royal, 80, always knows how to add a splash of personality and livened up the ensemble with a stack of beaded necklaces and a distinctive brooch pinned to her lapel.

The Duchess and her husband, Prince Richard, the Duke of Gloucester, are among the only royals to have a joint cypher, formed in the shape of a crown above an intertwined R & B, their initials. It appears as though the Duchess has had the cypher made into a sparkling brooch, which was on full display as she touched down in Botswana. The other royal pair to opt for a joint cypher? Prince Harry and Meghan Markle, who revealed their symbol, made up of an H and an M, after they tied the knot and became the Duke and Duchess of Sussex in 2018.

This tour of Botswana is a major moment in the royal spotlight for the Duke and Duchess of Gloucester. The pair are acting as representatives of King Charles III and will play a part in celebrating the 60th anniversary of the nation’s independence. As part of their trip, they will pay a courtesy call to President Advocate Duma Gideon Boko in Gaborone, lay a wreath at the national War Memorial, and visit significant sites, including the Botswana National Museum.

News of the tour was announced last month by the Botswana government. In a caption posted alongside a portrait of Their Royal Highnesses, the post read: ‘The visit by The Duke and Duchess of Gloucester is a testament of the excellent bilateral relations that Botswana and the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland continue to enjoy, which predate the establishment of diplomatic relations in 1966.’

For many royal watchers, it will be a delight to see the Firm recognising the couple’s decades of service to the royal family. Their tireless dedication to duty often goes unsung, despite Birgitte and Prince Richard becoming key pillars of support for the House of Windsor, especially when King Charles and the Princess of Wales stepped back following their cancer diagnoses.

It has been an exceptionally busy season for the Duchess of Gloucester, who has joined her husband at Trooping the Colour, Garter Day, and the Royal Procession at Ascot this month. Indeed, the Duchess spent her birthday at Ascot, surrounded by her rarely seen royal descendants. Her son, the Earl of Ulster, brought his two children (Xan, Lord Culloden, and Lady Cosima Windsor). Her daughter, Lady Rose Gilman, brought her daughter, Lyla. The Duchess's final daughter, Lady Davina Windsor, brought her daughter, Senna, to celebrate Birgitte's birthday, too.

The diligent Duchess’ last major international trip was a year ago, when she travelled to Bermuda in her capacity as Colonel-in-Chief of the Royal Bermuda Regiment. This April, she enjoyed a homecoming trip to Denmark, her home country, to support the St Alban’s Anglican Church, where she has served as Patron since 2025. The Church’s previous patron was Elizabeth II, whom Birgitte no doubt had in mind as she carried out her duties in Copenhagen.

For now, all eyes will be on Botswana as these royal family stalwarts quietly carry out their duties in the low-key manner for which they are known.
