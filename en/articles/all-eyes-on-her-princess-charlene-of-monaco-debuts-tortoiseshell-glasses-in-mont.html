All eyes on her! Princess Charlene of Monaco debuts tortoiseshell glasses in Monte-Carlo

Princess Charlene donned a supremely elegant pair of specs at the Monte-Carlo Bay on Wednesday

By Isaac Bickerstaff · October 8, 2026

From heroic headteachers to unsung legends, the Edit Schools Awards celebrate the crème de la crème of British education. Now, the winners are revealed…

Princess Charlene of Monaco debuted a new look on Wednesday, as she made a rare appearance with her brother to announce a historic achievement for the Principality’s rugby team.

The ever-stylish wife of Prince Albert II attended the presentation of the 2026-27 season at the Monte-Carlo Bay and made a rare public address to announce that the Monaco rugby team had qualified for the European Games, which will see 7,500 athletes from 50 countries gather in Istanbul in June. Princess Charlene was in high spirits – and rightly so. This milestone achievement marks the first time that Monaco has qualified for a team sport at the Games.

In her praise of sport, the Princess said: ‘It gives a small country a voice that carries very far and shows the next generation what it is possible to achieve when passion meets patience.’

Her Serene Highness, who serves as the President of the Monégasque Rugby Federation, wore a supremely elegant chiffon blazer and trousers from Max Mara for the announcement. Her simple beauty look included a rosy lip, and her characteristic blonde bob was swept back behind one ear. But it was her tortoise-shell glasses that truly drew the attention of royal watchers.

‘Words can’t describe how stunning and elegant the Princess looks in this perfectly fitted attire,’ wrote one fan: ‘Gorgeous hair and attractive glasses lend more flair to her look.’ Another gushed: ‘Well, doesn’t she look fabulous. Her hair I love down like this, her glasses frame her beautiful blue eyes.’

Princess Charlene has impeccable taste in glasses and is particularly fond of cat-eye sunglasses. From Christian Dior and Fendi to Chanel, Dolce & Gabbana and Louis Vuitton, the Princess’s eye for designer labels is impressive.

The Monégasque royal is occasionally seen attending official engagements with her brother, who moved to Monaco to ‘make sure his sister was looked after’ back in 2013, shortly after Charlene tied the knot with Prince Albert in 2011. Their parents, Michael Wittstock and Lynette Humberstone, are said to have moved nearby in 2023. MailOnline has reported that the couple travelled more than 8000 miles from their home in the suburbs of Johannesburg to live permanently in the Monaco area. They reportedly moved into a property in La Turbie, a French village next door to the tax haven principality where their daughter lives.

The move came around the time that Princess Charlene returned to Monaco following a prolonged absence due to health concerns. ‘It has made the world of difference,’ said a palace source in Monte Carlo at the time. ‘The Wittstocks have moved into a house that’s just a few minutes’ drive away from Charlene, and they meet all the time. They make her feel safe and secure, and remind her of the far more carefree life she enjoyed in South Africa before becoming a Monaco princess.’

Princess Charlene has long been considered one of the most stylish of European royals, but her outings in the previous few weeks have been particularly glowing. She was glittering in a golden Elie Saab gown on 2 October, when she joined her husband to welcome the Italian President and First Lady for a gala banquet on their state visit to Monaco. Earlier that morning, for the official welcome to the Princely Palace, she opted for sharp tailoring, wearing a Christian Dior white bar jacket and matching flared trousers with a pair of Louis Vuitton pumps.

At Tiffany &Co., French jewellery designer Jean Schlumberger created some of the house's most enduring silhouettes, loved by society swans including Jackie Kennedy and Bunny Mellon. Now, his artistic vision is being conserved for future generations thanks to a new partnership with Paris' Musée des Arts Décoratifs

Princess Charlene also dazzled in Dior for an audience with Pope Leo XIV in September, combining a display of modern diplomatic dressing with an archaic royal dress code. The elegant Monégasque was in Metz with her husband, Prince Albert II, where she joined the likes of President and First Lady Macron and Grand Duke Guillaume and Grand Duchess Stéphanie of Luxembourg for Mass at the Cathedral of St Etienne before leading a meeting at a conference called ‘At the Roots of Europe: For Peace and Unity’.

Indeed, it was the French couple who welcomed the Grimaldis to Metz earlier that day. Princess Charlene, who has long earned a reputation as the royal most committed to luxury style, dressed diplomatically in Dior, opting for an embroidered jacket and matching trousers from the French designer. Her Serene Highness paired the look with a Lady D-Joy clutch bag from the brand – a piece that takes its inspiration from Princess Diana.

But there was more to Princess Charlene’s outfit than sartorial diplomacy and a nod to one of the most iconic royals of all time. The keen-eyed will have noticed that, among the gathering of Europe’s power players in Metz, Princess Charlene was one of the only women to wear white. That is because the former Olympian, as a consort of Prince Albert of Monaco, possesses Privelege du Blanc: the right to wear white in the presence of Pope Leo.

Only queens, princesses, and consorts from Catholic countries can claim Privelege du Blanc, which technically only applies while meeting the Pope in the Vatican. Traditionally, however, women who do not possess the privilege wear black even outside the Holy See, and royals who can wear white tend to wear it wherever they may be meeting the Pope. Princess Charlene was the first Monégasque princess to make use of the rule when she donned an all-white look to meet with Pope Benedict XVI in 2013.

Then, as now, she is a royal who is able to combine traditional regal glamour with modern, fashion-forward style. All eyes on Princess Charlene – glasses or not.
