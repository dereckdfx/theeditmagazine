Princess Alexandra, the Hon Lady Ogilvy, 89, sparks health fears after missing tribute to her late sister-in-law, the Duchess of Kent

Princess Alexandra, the 89-year-old sister-in-law of the Duchess of Kent, had been expected to attend the service on Sunday

By Stephanie Bridger-Linning · October 1, 2026

Princess Alexandra, the Hon Lady Ogilvy, was notably absent when her family gathered to celebrate the life of her late sister-in-law, the Duchess of Kent.

Princess Alexandra, whose brother is the Duke of Kent, was listed among the attendees on the Court Circular, the official record detailing the royal family's engagements. It read: ‘Princess Alexandra this afternoon attended a performance by students and alumni of Future Talent celebrating the legacy of the Charity’s Co-Founder, the late Duchess of Kent, at Sinfonia Smith Square, London SW1.’

However, the Princess did not attend the concert, with a source telling the Daily Mail's Richard Eden that she ‘unfortunately had to pull out at the last moment’.

The Duke of Kent, led tributes at the memorial at Sinfonia Smith Square. The stoic cousin of Queen Elizabeth II has continued to champion musical causes following his wife’s death in September last year and was supported on Sunday by his brother, Prince Michael of Kent, 84.

All three of the Duke and Duchess of Kent's children also attended the concert, which was organised by Future Talent, the charity co-founded by the late royal in 2004 to provide funds to young musicians from less advantaged backgrounds. The Earl of St Andrews and Lord Nicholas Windsor joined the audience, as did Lady Helen Taylor and her daughter, Estella Taylor. Lord Frederick Windsor, the Duchess’s nephew, attended alongside his wife, Sophie Winkleman – the couple announced their joint patronage of the charity Wild Arts earlier this year.

It wasn't necessarily a surprise that Princess Alexandra missed the occasion. She stays largely out of the spotlight and has done for some time. Her last public outing came six months ago when she joined all working members of the royal family for an event marking what would have been Queen Elizabeth II's 100th birthday.

The King and the Queen stood at the centre of the photograph, flanked by Princess Anne, Prince William, and the Princess of Wales. To their left stood Prince Edward and the Duchess of Edinburgh, while the Duke and Duchess of Gloucester stood on their right.

The Duke of Kent sat on a chair in front of the Gloucesters. Princess Alexandra sat on a chair in front of the Duchess of Edinburgh. It is a rare official outing for these two royals, both of whom have largely stepped back from royal duties as they enter their later years.

The only daughter of Prince George, Duke of Kent and Princess Marina of Greece and Denmark, Princess Alexandra was sixth in line to the throne when she was born on Christmas Day in 1936, and is closely related to the most senior royals: as her mother was a first cousin of Prince Philip, she is both a second cousin and first cousin once removed of the King.

It is, perhaps, little surprise that someone with such a hard-and-fast bond with The Firm should have been called upon to be a bridesmaid to Queen Elizabeth on her wedding day, and become a staunch friend of the late monarch. Indeed, the Queen was so fond of Princess Alexandra, that she held a special birthday reception for her at Buckingham Palace, one month before she turned 80.

Although the Queen offered to style him as an earl upon marriage, Angus Ogilvy declined, meaning that the couple’s children carry no titles. He was knighted in 1988, when Princess Alexandra assumed the title of the Hon. Lady Ogilvy, and together, they had two children: James in 1964, and Marina in 1966. Angus died in 2004, but the Princess still lives in Thatched House Lodge, the Grade II-listed home in Richmond Park she has occupied since 1963, and is known to be very close with her grandchildren – society stalwarts Flora Vesterberg, Alexander Ogilvy and Zenouska Mowatt.

Throughout her life, Princess Alexandra has carried out an extensive programme of engagements in support of the Queen. Although often spotted at film premieres studded with celebrities and fabulous parties, she has always been dedicated to duty – and to this day, she continues to be an official, working royal. In June 2025, she accompanied King Charles to a special church service at the Queen’s Chapel to mark its 400th anniversary celebration, and she joined Queen Camilla at a service of thanksgiving for a mutual friend in December. Just a few weeks later, she and the rest of The Firm descended upon Buckingham Palace for the King’s annual Christmas lunch, and waved merrily to photographers as she arrived by car. She also regularly steps out to support her own, immediate family: in August 2025, she proudly attended the graduation ceremony of Alexander Ogilvy at Sandhurst.
