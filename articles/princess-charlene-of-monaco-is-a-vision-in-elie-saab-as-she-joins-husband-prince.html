Royals

Princess Charlene of Monaco is a vision in Elie Saab as she joins husband Prince Albert to host a state banquet

The glamorous Monégasque royal wore the Princess of Wales-approved designer to a sparkling night at the opera

Por Isaac Bickerstaff · 5 de octubre de 2026

As Rivals returns, Cotswold fever hits a new high – and with Hollywood blockbusters behind her and Broadway ahead, Hayley Atwell is ruffling feathers in Rutshire as the former Mrs Rupert Campbell-Black

Glamour abounded in Monte Carlo when Prince Albert and Princess Charlene of Monaco welcomed the Italian President and First Lady for a state visit. (How could it not, with a fashion capital joining forces with the world’s most glamorous principality?)

The royal couple hosted President Sergio Mattarella and the First Lady, his daughter, Laura Mattarella, for a glorious banquet in the Throne Room of the Princely Palace on 2 October, after a glittering evening at the opera. Princess Charlene, long famed for her ultra-luxurious approach to royal dressing, wore a suitably regal gown for the occasion, donning an embroidered silk georgette Elie Saab number, which she paired with her Graff diamond earrings, previously seen in 2023 for the Monaco Women Forum.

The style showcase began earlier that morning, when Princess Charlene and Prince Albert accompanied their two children, Prince Jacques and Princess Gabriella (who wore a sweet pink Bonpoint jacket and Stuart Weitzman Mary Janes), to welcome the President and First Lady to the Princely Palace ahead of a private audience and the exchange of orders. Princess Charlene opted for sharp tailoring for the introduction, wearing a Christian Dior white bar jacket and matching flared trousers with a pair of Louis Vuitton pumps.

The Dior jacket is eminently popular with royal women: a piece that has truly toured the great dynasties of Europe. Queen Mary of Denmark was spotted in the jacket for the state visit to France in 2025 and the Te Deum celebrating King Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden in April this year. Princess Astrid of Belgium has worn it for an economic mission to Norway in 2024, while Queen Mette-Marit of Norway wore it throughout 2022 and 2024, including for official photos with her husband, King Haakon, and a lunch in honour of NATO’s Secretary-General. Her mother-in-law, Queen Sonja of Norway, is a big fan of the Dior jacket, not only donning it for the House’s Haute Couture SS24 show, but also for a number of art exhibitions in 2025 and 2026.

Princess Charlene also dazzled in Dior for an audience with Pope Leo XIV in September, combining a dazzling display of modern diplomatic dressing with an archaic royal dress code. The elegant Monégasque was in Metz with her husband, Prince Albert II, where she joined the likes of President and First Lady Macron and Grand Duke Guillaume and Grand Duchess Stéphanie of Luxembourg for Mass at the Cathedral of St Etienne before leading a meeting at a conference called ‘At the Roots of Europe: For Peace and Unity’.

Indeed, it was the French couple who welcomed the Grimaldis to Metz earlier that day. Princess Charlene, who has long earned a reputation as the royal most committed to luxury style, dressed diplomatically in Dior, opting for an embroidered jacket and matching trousers from the French designer. Her Serene Highness paired the look with a Lady D-Joy clutch bag from the brand – a piece that takes its inspiration from Princess Diana.

But there was more to Princess Charlene’s outfit than sartorial diplomacy and a nod to one of the most iconic royals of all time. The keen-eyed will have noticed that, among the gathering of Europe’s power players in Metz, Princess Charlene was one of the only women to wear white. That is because the former Olympian, as a consort of Prince Albert of Monaco, possesses Privelege du Blanc: the right to wear white in the presence of Pope Leo.

Only queens, princesses, and consorts from Catholic countries can claim Privelege du Blanc, which technically only applies while meeting the Pope in the Vatican. Traditionally, however, women who do not possess the privilege wear black even outside the Holy See, and royals who can wear white tend to wear it wherever they may be meeting the Pope. Princess Charlene was the first Monégasque princess to make use of the rule when she donned an all-white look to meet with Pope Benedict XVI in 2013.

Rulers granted the right are a rare few, but the meeting in Metz saw two gather together, as Grand Duchess Stéphanie is also a holder of Privelege du Blanc. In addition to the Grand Duchess and Princess Charlene, the other royal women include: Queen Letizia of Spain, Queen Mathilde of Belgium, and Princess Marina of Napals. Two former queens, Paola of Belgium and Sofia of Spain, are permitted to wear white, and it is likely that Stéphanie’s predecessor, Grand Duchess Maria Teresa, also bears the privilege. The first queen to make use of the exception was reportedly Victoria Eugenia of Spain, during a private conversation with Pope Pius XI in 1923.

A grand history, manifested in an über-chic style masterclass from Princess Charlene.
