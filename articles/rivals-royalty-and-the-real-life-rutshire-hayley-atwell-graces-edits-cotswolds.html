Rivals, royalty and the real-life Rutshire! Hayley Atwell graces Edit ’s Cotswolds special as she returns as Rupert Campbell-Black's icy ex-wife

As Rivals returns, Cotswold fever hits a new high – and with Hollywood blockbusters behind her and Broadway ahead, Hayley Atwell is ruffling feathers in Rutshire as the former Mrs Rupert Campbell-Black

Por Annabel Sampson · 1 de octubre de 2026

‘She’d be like, “Oh, you’re my Helen, hello,”’ says Hayley Atwell, imitating the late Dame Jilly Cooper in a gloriously heightened Queen’s English accent. ‘I think there was a sense in the books that she wasn’t that keen on Helen,’ she says diplomatically of Helen Gordon, Rupert Campbell Black’s terribly pretty – but terribly prissy – icy American first wife, who Atwell plays in the second season of Rivals. ‘She would talk about me in the third person when I was there: “Isn’t she lovely?” And I’d be like, “Yes, I’m here, hello,”’ she says, laughing, sunk in a burgundy sofa in the library of Batty Langley’s – a marvellously eccentric hotel in Spitalfields, a short hop from her East End home. ‘She was just divine. When she passed away, it was so unbelievably sad, yet there’s something bittersweet thinking she was having the best time [on set].’

It was Atwell’s friendship with literary scene megastar Felicity Blunt, one of Rivals’ executive producers and Cooper’s agent, that helped her secure the role. ‘Will you come and do a cameo?’ Blunt had asked. ‘Only if I can have a big hat – that’s my entrance – and if I can give it a bit of an air of Joan Collins,’ Atwell shot back. The deal was done. Her friendship with Blunt goes back more than a decade, forged a couple of years after Atwell first appeared on Edit’s cover, dubbed ‘the Brideshead Babe’ thanks to her playing Lady Julia Flyte in 2008’s Brideshead Revisited. That part and more leading roles in Mansfield Park and The Duchess saw a young Atwell billed as ‘the queen of period drama’. Today, with a CV spanning the Avengers franchise to the Mission: Impossible films, via Black Mirror and Heartstopper, Atwell, 44, has more than earned her stripes as a bankable, endlessly versatile star. ‘I’ve stood the test of time,’ she says with a laugh, dressed in a crisp grey T-shirt and high-waisted trousers, her dark bob immaculate and her golden jewellery catching the light.

Damn right. The actress touched down in London at 6am, fresh from doing press in New York ahead of her Broadway debut in Jamie Lloyd’s Much Ado About Nothing, the smash hit that set new box-office records when it opened in the West End last year. It will play at the Winter Garden Theatre, right beside Times Square, and Atwell received a video of the theatre painted pink for the first time in its history – in keeping with the set’s theme – as she was boarding the plane home. ‘I got goosebumps. I thought, “If only my younger self could see this and think it’s with Tom [Hiddleston], who I met at a drama school audition 20 years ago; it’s with Jamie [Lloyd], who we’ve both worked with before, and at this particular theatre.” It felt like a real honour.’

After avidly watching the first season of Rivals, Atwell arrived to make her debut as a fully formed fan of the show. ‘I stepped on set going, “Oh my God, it’s Rupert Campbell-Black! Oh my God, it’s Taggie!” I was so nervous, and Alex Hassell [who plays Rupert] came up to me and just went, “I’m a bit embarrassed, I have to let new people that come on set know… I’m wearing a bum,” Atwell whispers conspiratorially. ‘And he pulled up his suit jacket and I was like, “Oh, damn.” There were certain scenes, because he’s an Olympian, which called for… a little bit more presentation on that side. By the end of it, he’d been doing so many squats that he didn’t need the bum any more. It was a graduation. And I thought, “I’m so in love with these people, I’m going to have a great time.”’

It was a near-calamitous fall that brought her and Blunt together all those years ago. Atwell and Stanley Tucci (Felicity’s then boyfriend, now husband) were filming Captain America: The First Avenger together in London. ‘It was this idyllic summer where the America cast had come over and they just wanted to go out,’ she says. Tucci had invited her, Chris Evans and Dominic Cooper to Ronnie Scott’s; his friend Emily Blunt and her sisters were going to be with them, Tucci told Atwell. ‘The friendship really began that night because we were walking to a bar after Ronnie Scott’s and I slipped on the cobbled streets. I went flying.’ She landed ‘in some bins’ with a lamppost centimetres from her head. ‘It was a very committed fall, I could never recreate it.’ As soon as the girls had worked out that Atwell was alive and hadn’t hurt herself, there was a pause before they burst into hysterical laughter. ‘And I was like, “Oh my God, I think I’m going to know these women for a very long time.”’

The global lust for the Cotswolds, which Rutshire, Rivals’ fictional setting, has helped feed, is something Atwell understands. ‘I’m half-American and every time I bring American relatives or friends over, the Cotswolds is their dream idyll of what the English countryside looks like.’ She has fallen for it, too, recently visiting for Felicity’s birthday. (‘All the Rivals actors were there too. We thought, “Are there cameras on us? Are we in a scene right now?”’). She also rented a little cottage there with her husband, the music producer Ned Wolfgang Kelly, and their daughter, born in 2024. Such was the excitement at a local coffee shop one Sunday morning, the waitress hollered: ‘Are you Hayley Atwell? What are you doing here!?’

After a frenetic year of international travel for work, Atwell and Kelly committed to investigating rural England over the summer. ‘We went to Blenheim Palace, Beaverbrook, the New Forest…’ she reels off. Throughout her career, she has filmed at stately jewels from Castle Howard to Chatsworth. As a paying punter, though, she says, ‘I’ll be walking around going, “Have I filmed here? Oh, I think I filmed here.” And it’s so hard because when shooting I’m always thinking about it from the point of view of the character. So I often forget that actually outside this scene I’ve shot, this house is this house. It’s like, “This is just my living room.”’

Since growing up in west London, helping at a stall in Portobello Market on Saturdays, Atwell has migrated eastwards, partly because she adores ‘the incredible cast of artistic characters’. ‘I would see Jeanette Winterson walking around; Tracey Emin’s studio was here. And then going down Elder, Fournier and Wilkes [Streets] and that road where they filmed a part of The King’s Speech…’ One of her favourite spots is Facing Heaven in London Fields. ‘It’s Haitian-Sichuan fusion with this chequerboard floor and a disco ball.’

Atwell also adores The Mandrake in Fitzrovia. ‘We just walked in and went, “Oh my God, we want our house to be like this.” So we called Tala [Fulstok, the interior designer] and she was like, “OK, I’m in.”’ Atwell is mad about games: she has completed more than 300 escape rooms and she and Kelly have a chef/bartender mother-and-son duo who come to the house and host all their dinner parties – including an upcoming murder-mystery special.

Grand titans of stage and screen have co-starred with Atwell, perhaps none more colossal than Tom Cruise. How was that first impression? ‘It’s almost similar to the Royal Family or a politician who knows their power in the room. The ability to disarm, to make you feel comfortable, because they carry so much status,’ Atwell explains. ‘Meryl Streep has this, too – she just has this ability to make you feel very seen.’ Atwell’s Mission: Impossible screen test was made easier by the fact that she’d met Cruise in a social setting. ‘I think Emily [Blunt] was doing Edge of Tomorrow with him and it was Felicity and Stanley’s engagement party in London, so then there became a bit more of a social aspect to it.’

Immersion in Cruise levels of fitness was par for the course for the Mission: Impossible films. ‘I mean, you can imagine being in Tom Cruise’s bubble for five years. He gets the best stunt team, the best chefs. It’s hyperbaric chambers and infrared saunas… I’ve never done anything like that. I was always kind of quite naturally strong and dense as a person. So if you pushed me, I’d be very solid – it’s why I was prop in rugby. I was little, but I was like, “Can’t move me,”’ Atwell beams.

Andrew Parker Bowles and Robin Smith-Ryland – two alleged real-life inspirations for Rupert C-B – may not have made Atwell’s acquaintance, but she has met the King. ‘Now, if this ends up being in print in Edit, I’m sure lots of people will be like, “Yeah, he says that to everyone,”’ Atwell laughs. ‘But I think I must have just done Howards End and I was doing Measure for Measure at the Donmar Warehouse, and he shook my hand at a BFI reception and said, “Oh yes. Well, you’re a genius.” I thought, “The future King’s called me a genius. I wonder if he’s actually seen it? I’m going to take that.”’ And so she should. Atwell is a genius for our times, and a lot of fun with it. Unrivalled, you might say.
