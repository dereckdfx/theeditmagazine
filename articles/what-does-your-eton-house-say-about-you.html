What does your Eton House say about you? An insider's guide to the world's most famous school

Por Tatler

Everything you need to know about Eton: whether you want to master Fives or bag the best Private Side, Tatler has the insider tips on the world's most famous school

Prince George is set to enrol at Eton College, joining a storied list of alumni at what is perhaps the nation’s most famous and most prestigious school. As a first-year student, the future king is not only following in the footsteps of his father, Prince William , and his uncle, Prince Harry , but also the likes of George Orwell, Tom Hiddleston, and 20 Prime Ministers.

But, considering Eton’s level of fame on the international stage, the day-to-day life of a student at the school is something of an insider’s secret. Fortunately, Tatler is on hand to give an insight into what life might look like for Prince George in this newest chapter of his royal career.

Heading to Oxford? Whether you're a religious rower, a union hack, or a Right Hon, as results day rolls around, here's a totally objective, comprehensive guide to the college you're about to call home

When it comes to life at Eton, one choice reigns supreme: your House . The School is a federal republic. Just as in the USA, individual states matter: e pluribus unum . There are 25 Houses, each taking roughly 55 students per year (apart from the oldest and original, the College itself, which accepts 75 boys on King’s Scholarships). But how does the House system work, and what does your House say about you?

Prince George is following in the footsteps of Prince William and Prince Harry by studying at Eton

The main School apparatus has very little influence within the individual Houses. It is all about the House Master (occasionally, historically, a woman but mostly men these days). The House Master sets the tone, the culture, the rules. They are selected from a spread of sportsmen, Army Officers, published authors, vicars, classical musicians, ornithologists, playwrights and more.

He will see the boys at meal times; he will watch his boys in concerts and on the playing fields; he will receive regular updates from other masters about his boys’ progress in all aspects of their School life. Three times a week, for relatively non-denominational ‘Prayers’, the House will gather together in the evening, and the House Master will pass out House bulletins, maybe read a prayer, or offer his own views on life, the universe and everything.

Results day has the next gen in a furore. If you're heading to Cambridge, it's time to get au fait with what your college of choice says about you

The House is known by the House Master, not by the building. Any current Etonian, when asked ‘which House are you in?’, will reply with the initials of his House Master. Any Old Etonian is known by their year of leaving School, and by their House Master’s initials, not by the building’s name. When they meet for dinners at White’s or Boodles, their old House Master is their honoured guest. And when the House Master dies, those same boys, now men, will flock from far and wide for the service, often back at School, in respect and tribute to the man who guided them years earlier.
