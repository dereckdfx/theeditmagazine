Royals

When eight kings, 11 queens and a teenage Princess Anne gathered for the wedding of King Constantine II and Queen Anne-Marie of Greece

When the Greek king and the Danish princess tied the knot in 1964, it was an eminently royal affair, with the future King Charles lifting a crown over the happy couple's heads

Por Isaac Bickerstaff · 18 de septiembre de 2026

When King Constantine of Greece married Princess Anne-Marie of Denmark, the celebrations spanned Europe – which is no surprise, considering the wedding brought together two royal families, each with dynasties spanning the continent's most noble lineages.

After a dinner at Fredensborg Palace, which marked an official farewell for the Danish princess from her parents, King Frederik IX and Queen Ingrid of Sweden, and her siblings, Princess Benedikte and the future Queen Margrethe, the bride and groom made their way to Greece to kick off their wedding in earnest.

The big day took place on 18 September 1964 at the Metropolitan Cathedral of the Annunciation in Athens, presided over by the Primate of Greece, Archbishop Chrysostomos. A major part of the ceremony involved guests holding crowns over the head of the happy couple – a glamorous affair at the best of times, but even more so when the guest list included eight kings, eleven queens, and a dazzling array of princes, princesses, dukes, and duchesses.

Prince Charles, then Prince of Wales, was one of the royals to hold a crown over the heads of the newlyweds. So too did Crown Prince Harald of Norway and Crown Prince Carl Gustaf of Sweden – all three would go on to reign as monarchs in their respective nations. The groom’s mother, Queen Frederica of Greece, took part in the ceremony, as did Prince Michael of Greece, Prince Michael of Kent, Crown Prince Alexander of Yugoslavia, Prince Karl of Hesse, Prince Ingolf of Denmark and Count Michael Bernadotte.

Nor did the regal participation in this most blue-blooded of weddings stop there. Princess Anne-Marie’s bridesmaids were a truly royal crowd: her sister, Princess Irene of Greece; her childhood friend, Princess Tatiana Radziwiłł; Princess Clarissa of Hesse; Crown Princess Margareta of Romania; Princess Christina of Sweden; and none other than Princess Anne of the United Kingdom, who, then aged 14, had not yet been granted the title of Princess Royal.

Princess Anne-Marie – now Queen of Greece – most certainly dressed the part. In contrast to the lavish displays at the Cathedral, she opted for a simple wedding gown from the Danish designer Jørgen Bender. She paid a moving tribute to her heritage, wearing an Irish lace veil that once belonged to her grandmother, Princess Margaret of Connaught, and the Khedive of Egypt tiara, which has been worn by every single one of Queen Ingrid’s female descendants. The tiara began its royal story when Margaret of Connaught, a granddaughter of Queen Victoria, tied the knot with Prince Gustaf Adolf of Sweden in 1905 and received the dazzling headpiece as a gift from the Khedive of Egypt.

Anne-Marie’s family ties to the British royals illustrate just how involved the House of Windsor has been with the Greek and Danish royal families throughout the years. Prince Philip, a first cousin once removed of Constantine, was an honoured guest in the congregation, as was his uncle, Lord Mountbatten. There was also British representation from Princess Alexandra of Kent and Angus Ogilvy and Princess Marina, Duchess of Kent.

The bride and groom’s parents all attended the illustrious event, as did Princess Anne-Marie’s grandfather, King Gustaf VI Adolf of Sweden, and Constantine’s grandmother, Princess Victoria Louise, Duchess of Brunswick. Queen Juliana and Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands were seated in the pews, as were King Olav V of Norway, King Baudouin and Queen Fabiola of Belgium, King Bhumibol Adulyadej and Queen Sirikit of Thailand, King Hussein and Princess Muna of Jordan, King Umberto II and Queen Marie José of Italy, Tsar Simeon II and Tsaritsa Margarita of Bulgaria, Queen Anne of Romania, Queen Helen of Romania, Queen Farida of Egypt, the Duchess of Aosta, Prince Franz Joseph II and Princess Gina of Liechtenstein, Grand Duke Jean and Grand Duchess Joséphine Charlotte of Luxembourg, Prince Juan Carlos and Princess Sofia of Spain, Prince Michael of Greece, the Count and Countess of Barcelona and the Duke and Duchess of Braganza.

Rounding out the royals in attendance were the Prince of Prussia and Grand Duchess Kira Kirillovna, the Duke and Duchess of Aosta, Princess Margarita of Yugoslavia, the Prince of Naples, Princess Maria Gabriella, Princess Maria Beatrice of Savoy, Infanta Pilar, Prince George William and Princess Sophie of Greece and Hanover, Princess Margarita of Hohenlohe-Langenburg, Hereditary Princess Caroline-Mathilde of Denmark, Princess Viggo of Denmark, Prince George Valdemar and Princess Anne of Denmark, the Margrave of Baden, Count Flemming, and Countess Ruth of Rosenberg.

The wedding ceremony ended with a carriage procession back to the Royal Palace, where the 80 guests were hosted for a celebratory breakfast. Not long after, the new King and Queen of Greece left to kick off their honeymoon on the island of Corfu. They would be the final royals to rule over Greece, as King Constantine and Queen Anne-Marie were officially deposed in 1973, following the abolition of the monarchy by a military junta.

King Constantine II died on 10 December 2023 at the age of 82. Queen Anne-Marie recently celebrated her 80th birthday, enjoying warm tributes from her family, including her son, Crown Prince Pavlos, her daughter-in-law, Crown Princess Marie-Chantal, and their grandchildren.
