From St Moritz to Singureni: inside Romania’s new polo scene

Polo in Romania is hitting its stride with Argentine ponies, a glamorous country estate and the reopening of Bucharest’s grandest hotel

Por Tamzin Reynolds · 24 de septiembre de 2026

Thwack! A bamboo mallet meets the ball and an explosion of hooves sends dust flying as the players thunder towards goal in pursuit. On the sidelines, over the sound of popping champagne corks and pulsing DJ beats, booms ‘the voice of polo’ Jan-Erik Franck. Splicing his breakneck commentary with tongue-in-cheek asides, he keeps the crowd – a VIP tent thick with Romanian society and international polo followers – hooked on the action.

It has all the swagger of an event that has been running for decades. It has not. The Singureni Manor Polo Cup was launched in 2024: Romania's first international tournament. By its second edition, in 2025, it had assembled a seriously pedigreed field, including Argentine polo royalty Alejandro Novillo Astrada and Nacho Figueras, Prince Harry’s long-time pal. From America came Nic Roldán, the sport’s ‘most potent combination of athletic ability and good looks’, according to The New York Times; and player-patron Melissa Ganzi, who made history as the first woman to win the St Moritz Snow Polo World Cup in 2019.

These are names tied to the world’s top teams and most prestigious tournaments, from the Queen’s Cup at Guards to the Argentine Open. It’s quite a roll call for a place that, unlike, say, Cowdray or Sotogrande, hasn’t spent decades developing its polo culture.

Much of the credit goes to Reto Gaudenzi, the ‘godfather’ of World Polo, whose events have taken the sport from the snow of St Moritz to the beaches of Miami and Dubai. By helping to establish the tournament at Singureni, he’s slingshotted Romania into polo’s most rarefied circles.

Not that equestrian glamour was lacking here: a vast country estate an hour outside Bucharest, Singureni began life as a hunting lodge and family retreat before turning its attention to Arabian horses, bred for bloodline and beauty, and later evolving into a luxury hotel. Today, guests can practically sleep alongside the prize purebreds, in rooms overlooking the stables.

Polo is the latest passion – Singureni is now home to Romania’s first and only polo club, with its own school, home team and a new clubhouse, launched this summer with an Argentine asado cooked by three-Michelin-starred Mauro Colagreco. It means Romania is starting to produce polo players rather than just host them, and it signals a new confidence in the country’s emerging luxury scene that says: ‘We can play that game too.’

That confidence is underscored in Bucharest, where the Belle Époque masterpiece once revered as the Grand Hotel du Boulevard has reopened under Corinthia Hotels after 10 long years of painstaking restoration. Built in 1873, it once stood at the heart of a capital glamorous enough to be nicknamed ‘Little Paris’, before the wrecking ball of World War II and communism altered the city almost beyond recognition. Grand boulevards and ornate mansions still jostle with slabs of communist concrete and scarred grey buildings, but the city’s old splendour is beginning to reassert itself. The clearest evidence stands at the corner of Calea Victoriei and Regina Elisabeta Boulevard, where the Grand Hotel – now the Corinthia Bucharest – resumes its role as one of the city’s smartest addresses.

This was the first building in the city to have electric lights, running water and lifts, and it became a hub for dignitaries, diplomats and the highest tiers of society. A menu from a dinner held in honour of Queen Victoria’s birthday, discovered during the renovation, shares the walls with surreal Romanian royal family portraits by British artist Simon Casson. Originally, the hotel boasted 80 rooms, but today, when exclusivity reads as glamour, the space has been reconfigured into just 30 suites, all with Santa Maria Novella scented lotions in the bathrooms and fresh flowers from local florist Iris, who has a boutique bursting with blooms in the lobby. The old bones of the building – its fine columns, pillars, soffits and beams – remain gracefully in place; so too the ballroom, still in regal gold and blue, but freshly fitted out as a restaurant, with Murano chandeliers overhead and a champagne trolley on hand for refills.

The spa, with its 111 Skin facials and Thai massages, is pure new-Bucharest luxury, as is Sass' Restaurant & Lounge – a slice of Monaco nightlife transplanted to the Romanian capital – which sponsors the Singureni Manor Polo Cup and hosts the glitzy pre-tournament party. In fact, the Corinthia has designed an entire weekend stay around the tournament: breakfast in the city, a chauffeured drive out to the estate, VIP access to the polo, then back for cocktails at the hotel’s Heritage Bar. And this year, the UK joins the action for the first time, represented by the Red Star team, with society stalwarts Lady Amelia Spencer Mallett and Lady Eliza Spencer set to join the VIP crowd backing Britain from the sidelines.

Romania may not yet readily spring to mind for a polo weekend, but for anyone who’s done the Cowdray Gold Cup and the Cartier Queen’s Cup at Guards on repeat, there is the obvious appeal of being there first – first to a new tournament, first among a growing social scene, and first to check into a grand hotel before everyone else catches on.
