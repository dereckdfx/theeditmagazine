Style

The peaks (and pit stops) of racing glamour: Edit joins the Louis Vuitton Classic Run as it returns for a four-day tour from Venice to Monza

600km of style and chic: the French Maison makes Italy its home as it breaks a 14-year hiatus and hits the road via the winding roads of the Dolomites. Style editor Chandler Tregaskes went along for the ride…

Por Chandler Tregaskes · 8 de septiembre de 2026

From the Palazzos of Venice, to the peaks of the Dolomites, and over to the pits of the Monza Circuit; this weekend, the Louis Vuitton Classic Run returned to the road for the first time in 14 years. Touring 22 vintage automobiles, 44 drivers and navigators, and a dizzyingly dazzling crowd of red-hot, classic racecar-yearning gents and glamazons, the Maison put on a spectacle of prestige and style for a 600km race across the sun-drenched vistas of Italy. They say that ‘Victory travels in Louis Vuitton’, well these guests come with baggage (the trunks-full of refined opulence kind) and they’re revving the gears up to one-hundred-and-downright-gorgeous.

Town House or Country House? To Bekynton or not to Bekynton? When Prince George enrols at Eton College this week, he will need to know the tricks of the trade when it comes to life among the Tugs, Beaks, and Oppidans

It’s a scene that demands its very own scenography as the rides zoom by; a competition that favours precision and performance over pedal-to-the-metal speeds. First raced back in 1993 when a fleet of historic vehicles took to the Malaysian jungle for its inaugural iteration, Louis Vuitton – now also the title sponsor for the F1 – selected a rather arresting route filled with Italian beauty spots to break its hiatus and returned with a petrol-fumed extravaganza of elegance.

Beginning in Venice, the line-up of masterful motors set off from the lavish late-Baroque era Villa Pisani – first built for the former doge of Venice, Alvise Pisani, in the early 18th century. Along with the Venetian Heritage Foundation, the house has been supporting its refurbishment, installing a new lighting system in its Sala di Bacco music room. So, who else but Vuitton to shut down the ‘Queen of Venetian Villas’ and fill the grounds with a cohort of early-mid century motors?

That evening, competitors, their counterparts, and a smattering of the Maison’s uber-chic VICs were joined by the likes of Italian model beauty and actress, Giulia Maenza, and esteemed Italian actor, Maurizio Lombardi, for a black tie dinner in the Renaissance era-muralled halls of Palazzo Ducale, another historic site of which the Maison has a longstanding relationship – this time supporting in its artwork-upkeep – and where the ultimate prize the drivers would be competing for was unveiled in duly glitzy fashion…

The trophy – a Sabine Marcelis-designed wave-like structure from the Italian heritage ‘art glass’ emporium, Maison Venini – was, naturally, encased in a Louis Vuitton trunk, specially crafted at the former home of the house’s namesake founder where its custom luggage creations are still made to this day by the house’s immensely skilled artisans.

The next day, the group rode down to Possagno, with a quick pit stop at the Museo Gypsotheca Antonia Canova, where nearby residents were sure to swing by to marvel at the machines, stopping to take selfies with the Maserati Ghibli Spyder [1968], the Ferrari 500 TR Spyder Scaglietti [1956], and the Lagonda LG45 Rapide [1937], among other remarkable rides.

Then it was up to the mountains, where the roads began to wind – quite fittingly – like hot linguine. Rather wildly, hotrod-craving spectators were spotted scarily close to the edges of the route. About midway up the Unesco World Heritage-awarded Dolomites, the drivers and the LV in-crowd stopped for a picnic in the chocolate-box-esque surrounds of a traditional Cortina d’Ampezzo chalet. Guests were treated to fine fare al fresco, set to the soothing sounds of a sonic celebration from a mountain-singing choral group – all dressed in head-to-toe Vuitton, of course.

After an intimate evening of dining in the heights of the hills, the following day saw the Classic Run continue in its journey of esteemed glamour, the peaks of the mountains slowly descending into the lush plains and lakes of Lombardy. This day’s ‘pit-stop’ (too glamorous to not put in scare quotes) found itself at the beating heart of another transcendent transport titan: the historic Riva shipyard, where VICs took turns speeding around Lake Garda like Sophia Loren as they waited for the arrival of the fleet to pull up by the water.

Later, they hot-wheeled it to the final destination and finish line, Monza’s Villa Reale. A neoclassical palace first commissioned by Empress Maria Theresa of Austria in the late 18th century. One by one, the procession of valiant vehicles arrived to the sound of rip-roaring applause and a scene of LV boiler suit-clad flag-wavers toasting the end of the race as they pulled up around the dancing fountain of the estate’s imposing entrance with impressive precision following four days of racing. It was cause for much celebration and guests enjoyed a dreamy ‘dinatoire’ in the gardens as they caught up on the day’s events.

But the fun - and the favoloso - didn’t stop there. Coinciding perfectly the Italian Grand Prix weekend, the cars took to the road again, this time on the racetrack of the Monza’s ‘Temple of Speed’ to open the esteemed event. Alas, while the likes of Leclerc, Norris and Russell had only begun this leg of their race tour, the evening’s itinerary beheld the Louis Vuitton Classic Run’s crowning moment, with an awards ceremony and winner reveal at Milan’s Castello Sforzesco.

It was the 1939 Aston Martin C-type driver, Gianandrea Redaelli, who took home the top prize and its coveted Louis Vuitton trunk as the race’s ‘Overall Champion’ during a gala dinner hosted in the vaults of the historic castle. Carlo Gussalli Beretta and Melissa Satta, driving a 1965 Ferrari 275 GTB Competizione received additional honours as ‘Regularity Champions’; Katarina Kyvalova and Marta Carello, driving a 1931 Bentley 8 Litre Vanden Plas Two-Seater Drophead won the ‘Elegance in Motion’ award; and David and Virginia Sydorick, driving a Ferrari 250GT Zagato, received a ‘Legacy and Long-Time Commitment to Louis Vuitton Classic Run’ prize.

Designated driver status fully over for the evening, attendees made their way up to stately grounds of the Castello for an afterparty fit for speed - or perhaps more appropriately - ‘prestige’ kings and queens. Flashing racing grid patterns and Damier prints lighting up the walls of the 15th citadel, booming Italo Disco hits filling the thick summer evening air; it was the ultimate finale for a festival of artful luxury and esteemed heritage only Louis Vuitton could conjure. Always follow la route de LV…
