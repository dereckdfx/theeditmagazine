Inside Deauville: Europe's under-the-radar film festival loved by A-listers

‘Tis the season of the film festival. Edit ’s Hannah Kitty Brown discovers why cinema’s hidden heavyweight, the Deauville American Film Festival, is beloved by Hollywood’s finest

Por Hannah Kitty Brown · 22 de septiembre de 2026

London, New York, LA… Normandy? The answer is yes, at least in September. Each year, the great and gilded of Hollywood gather for the Deauville American Film Festival – and there is nowhere chicer to stay than Hotel Barrière Le Normandy.

As Charles Spencer makes headlines with Swan Song, his bombshell book about his sister, Princess Diana, Edit revisits the Earl's chequered romantic history

It is a place where sandy Chanel pumps trot from the beach to the bar, cobbled streets are lined with vintage cars, and there’s always an intoxicating mix of espresso and designer perfume lingering in the seaside air, giving everything an effortlessly romantic and distinctly French charm. But there is serious star-power too. Clint Eastwood, Elizabeth Taylor and Orson Welles have all journeyed to this postcard-perfect corner of France for the annual film festival, their appearances commemorated with beach cabins adorned with their names – Deauville’s answer to the Hollywood Walk of Fame, if you will.

Everybody wants a room at Hotel Barrière Le Normandy – the flagship of Groupe Barrière, the hospitality group founded by Lucien Barrière which has supported the festival since its inception. With 269 luxurious rooms and suites boasting a uniquely Norman character and cosy atmosphere, the bedrooms have been designed with the building's history in mind. Renowned interior designers Alexandre Danan and Nathalie Ryan succeed in creating a space that preserves glamorous memories of the past whilst honouring contemporary sensibilities. Aside from the hotel's obvious treasures, like the green half-timbered façade and distinctive roof, or La Belle Epoque's huge custom-made mosaic; the site is full of surprises. On one wall hangs an unsigned red chalk portrait of a man, thought to date from the construction of the hotel, which is now an integral part of Le Normandy's decor. King Edward VIII's 30th birthday and Claude Lelouch's 1966 film Un homme et une femme were both played out against the backdrop of Le Normandy's timeless interiors. The hotel even housed Coco Chanel’s first ever hat shop… what could possibly be more glamorous?

As I pulled up to the hotel, paparazzi peered expectantly towards my car, hoping to catch a glimpse of Ethan Hawke, who, thir­ty-five years after his first appear­ance at the fes­ti­val, was back in Deauville to accept the Deauville Talent Award. (I spotted him enjoying the unlikely combination of a Caesar salad and cappuccino in the foyer of Hotel Le Royal). Unfortunately for them, it was me, fresh from a sublime stay at the eternally opulent Fouquet's Paris, a sister hotel of Le Normandy, and I was quickly whisked off by the concierge, whose suave uniform looked plucked straight from a Jean-Luc Godard flick.

The bedroom, overlooking the hotel's impressive padel and tennis courts and the glittering La Manche, was a bijou confection wrapped in the classic toile de jouy wallpaper. Having done my utmost to look French for the evening’s festivities – blunt bob, plenty of black, and a generous helping of smudgy lipstick – I headed down the hotel’s piano bar, Le Bar du Normandy, for a cocktail.

The romance novelist – and Princess Diana’s step-grandmother – was the third best-selling author of all time, behind Shakespeare and Agatha Christie. Edit’s Isolde Walters visits her astonishingly well preserved country estate

An intoxicating celebratory atmosphere, with music, bouchées, camera flashes, and ball gowns…everybody was abuzz before walking the festival's legendary red carpet. Inside The Centre International de Deauville, which was opened in September 1992 by actor and director Clint Eastwood, Ethan Hawke delivered a touching speech, proclaiming ‘nothing has meant more’ to him than receiving the Deauville Talent Award from French screen legend Juliette Binoche.

Following the ceremony, the night unfolded at the afterparty, held at Casino Barrière Deauville, where, after a delicious three-course meal delivered by Barrière chefs, there were colourful cocktails on tap and debonair dancing in abundance. With attendees sneaking out to the casino's balcony to enjoy moonlit ocean views, and a cigarette or two.

A celebration of cinematic art backdropped by a fairytale town from another world, Deauville’s eclectic offerings delight even the starriest of stars. To quote Patti Paige, ‘If you're fond of sand dunes and salty air, quaint little villages here and there’...you’re sure to fall in love with Deauville. Add the Côte Fleurie to your to-do list – half of Hollywood already has.
