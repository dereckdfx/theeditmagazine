Happy birthday to the Duke of Kent! King Charles leads tributes on the royal's 91st birthday

It has been a difficult year for the Duke of Kent, who was a loyal servant to his cousin Queen Elizabeth II

Por Isaac Bickerstaff · 9 de octubre de 2026

King Charles led the royal family in wishing a happy 91st birthday to the Duke of Kent on Friday. Buckingham Palace celebrated the milestone with a stoic photo of the diligent Prince Edward, adorned in his many military medals, standing with a walking stick, his stately signet ring on display. ‘Happy birthday to the Duke of Kent!’ read the message below.

This show of support from the royal family comes as the Duke shoulders the burden of a difficult year. In September, he marked one year since the death of his beloved wife, Katharine, Duchess of Kent, who passed away at the age of 92 after 64 years of marriage.

The Duchess of Kent married the Duke of Kent and dutifully served the crown – before becoming a school teacher. Hugo Vickers went inside palace circles to paint the definitive portrait of an extraordinary royal, who died one year ago today

Just this week, there was more loss for the Duke of Kent following the sad death of his private secretary, Nicholas Turnbull. One of the Duke’s closest aides, Turnbull joined the royal household in 2019, according to royal blog Gert’s Royals, joining a list of predecessors that includes Lieutenant-Commander Sir Richard Buckley, Nicolas Clark Adamson, and Nicolas Marden.

As Private Secretary, Turnbull would have been responsible for organising the Duke’s engagements, responding to his correspondence and managing his public relations. Earlier this year, he joined the Prince in the royal box at Wimbledon.

It means that this birthday celebration, like the Duke’s 90th birthday last year, will be a sombre day. When Prince Edward marked his ninth decade in 2025, it came just weeks after the death of the Duchess of Kent. Despite the shadow of grief, the Duke was supported and celebrated by the royal family.

The King had announced that he planned to mark the occasion on 9 October 2025, with a touching social media post in honour of the oldest working member of the royal family. ‘Wishing The Duke of Kent a very happy birthday - 90 today!’ read the caption, which also declared that the King planned to host a birthday celebration for the Duke at Windsor Castle.

The royal family also posted a previously unseen photograph of the Duke, believed to have been taken behind the scenes at King Charles' Coronation in 2023.

Royal watchers had hoped that pomp and circumstance might be on the cards, but the Court Circular revealed by 30th October that the birthday party for the Duke of Kent had come and gone with a quiet dignity that is perhaps fitting for the dutiful royal.

He’s the grandson of Grace Kelly, the eldest son of Prince Albert II of Monaco, and for 20 years he has kept his life private. But what happened when Alexandre Grimaldi went public?

‘The King and Queen this evening gave a Reception at Windsor Castle in honour of The Duke of Kent’s Ninetieth Birthday, revealed the Court Circular, before disclosing which members of the family gathered to celebrate the beloved cousin of Queen Elizabeth II. On the guest list? The Duke’s siblings – Prince Michael of Kent and Princess Alexandra, the Hon. Lady Ogilvy – as well as the Duke of Edinburgh, the Princess Royal, and the Duke of Gloucester.

The Duke’s daughter, Lady Helen Taylor, has been a vital source of support throughout the year, accompanying her father to the Duchess of Kent’s memorial service last month. Last year, Lady Helen revealed that her father is ‘slowing down’ as he depends increasingly on walking aids and wheelchairs during his (impressively many) royal engagements.

One of only 11 working royals, the Duke is involved with 140 different organisations, and is a familiar face on the balcony of Buckingham Palace for Trooping the Colour. When Lady Helen Taylor was asked whether her father might soon consider retiring, however, the 61-year-old cautioned that he may soon look to wind down his royal engagements.

‘I don’t want him to. I mean, he sort of is naturally, because his body won't let him go at the speed that he would like to,’ she told True Royalty TV, ‘but I don't want him to retire because what will he do?’

Fortunately, the Duke of Kent is very much still engaged with his royal role. Whether he is supporting the arts at Wigmore Hall or working with the King’s Scouts Awards, the 91-year-old likes to keep busy. He is also a regular in the royal box at Wimbledon, where he served as president for many years.

It is this diligent approach to duty, even in the face of grief, that King Charles and the royal family will no doubt be celebrating today, as they honour decades of service to the crown from the Duke of Kent.
