Royals

The Princess of Wales dazzles in rarely seen amethyst necklace as she joins Prince William and Tom Cruise at star-studded premiere in London

Por Stephanie Bridger-Linning

The Princess of Wales dazzled in an amethyst necklace for the premiere of Digger in London

It was a star-studded evening for some of the biggest names in Hollywood but all eyes were on the Princess of Wales as she joined Prince William for a charity film screening on Tuesday night.

Catherine, 44, was resplendent in a flowing purple gown by Jenny Packham as she made her grand entrance in Leicester Square for the Royal Film Performance 2026 world premiere of Digger , the hotly anticipated film directed by Alejandro González Iñárritu and starring Tom Cruise. The event was held in support of the Film and TV Charity, which supports people working in the industry with everything from mental wellbeing to financial help.

Charlie Miller was charmed by Tiffany & Co.'s unveiling of the Fall expression of Blue Book 2026 – where the real theatre was among the guests

For the occasion, the Princess turned to Packham, one of her most trusted designers who has dressed Her Royal Highness for high-profile evenings including the Royal Variety Performance, the BAFTA awards and a string of state banquets. The sweeping silhouette featured voluminous sleeves, a nipped-in waist and a plunging, deep-V neckline that perfectly framed the rare piece that dazzled on her décolletage.

The Princess of Wales chose a romantic heart pendant for the star-studded evening in London

For adorning the Princess of Wales' neck was a show-stopping amethyst necklace that was given to the Queen Mother as a wedding present in 1923 and is believed to have only been seen twice in public in the century since. The glamorous sautoir features a dramatic heart-shaped amethyst, suspended on three rows of pearls, interspersed with oval amethysts with a diamond surround. The rich purple colour of the stone perfectly complimented the regal hue of Her Royal Highness' gown.

When the Greek king and the Danish princess tied the knot in 1964, eight kings and eleven queens gathered to celebrate – and King Charles lifted a crown over the happy couple's heads

The heirloom necklace was reportedly originally owned by Queen Alexandra, wife of Edward VII and mother of George V. In 1923, it was presented to Lady Elizabeth Bowes-Lyon (the future Queen Elizabeth, Queen Mother) on her wedding to the Duke of York (later King George VI). While some jewels from this era have remained in frequent rotation in the wardrobes of generations of royal women, the striking amethyst necklace lay largely forgotten for almost a century.
