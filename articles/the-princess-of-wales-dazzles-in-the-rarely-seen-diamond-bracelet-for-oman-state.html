The Princess of Wales dazzles in a rarely seen diamond bracelet for Oman state banquet

The Prince and Princess of Wales arrived in style for the white tie dinner at Windsor Castle

Por Stephanie Bridger-Linning · 20 de octubre de 2026

The Princess of Wales looked every inch the future queen as she arrived for the Oman state banquet alongside her husband, Prince William.

The Prince and Princess of Wales were among the senior royals who arrived at Windsor Castle for the white tie dinner, hosted by King Charles III and Queen Camilla in honour of the Sultan of Oman. Sultan Haitham bin Tarik al Said landed at Stansted Airport on Monday evening ahead of the official start of the state visit on Tuesday. The three-day itinerary includes a meeting with the Prime Minister and charitable outings – but the state banquet is the glittering centrepiece.

All eyes were on the Princess of Wales, who brought a splash of sunshine to an overcast October evening in a canary yellow gown by Elie Saab. The caped creation marks a sartorial gear shift for the Princess, who typically favours go-to British designers like Jenny Packham and Alexander McQueen for such occasions.

However there was a sense of familiarity when it came to Her Royal Highness' look: the gown was topped with the Lover's Knot Tiara, which is Catherine's undisputed favourite. She adorned her ears with the Greville Chandelier Earrings, which previously belonged to Queen Elizabeth II and were given to the late monarch on her wedding to Prince Philip.

But it was the Princess of Wales' bracelet that really caught the eye: glittering on her right wrist was the Queen Mary Choker Bracelet, an extraordinary piece reserved for only the most glittering of evenings.

As the name suggests, the piece was originally designed for Mary of Teck, wife of King George V. Composed of diamond bars in an art deco design, the piece was created in the 1920s, around the time when it was notably worn by Queen Mary in a portrait. Although it was worn as a choker on that occasion, it is not known whether it was originally designed as a bracelet or a choker – although it has certainly become more popular as a bracelet in recent years.

After the death of Queen Mary in 1953, the choker was not seen again for more than 20 years, when it reappeared on the wrist of Queen Elizabeth, the Queen Mother, in portraits marking her 75th birthday, notes the Royal Watcher. After her death in 2002, the bracelet was inherited by Queen Elizabeth II.

However, it is in the last decade that it has found a new royal fan: the Princess of Wales, who first wore the piece as a bracelet for the Chinese state banquet in 2015. The then Duchess of Cambridge dazzled in the piece, wearing it with the Lotus Flower Tiara and an elegant red gown. It has been worn on a handful of formal occasions in the years since, including a charity gala in 2016, on a state visit to Paris in 2017 and the Diplomatic Corps reception at Buckingham Palace in 2018. Most recently, it adorned Her Royal Highness' wrist at the Royal Variety Performance in November 2025.

The Sultan of Oman was met in London by Prince William on Tuesday morning before the pair travelled together to Windsor, where the majority of the royal engagements will take place. The Sultanate of Oman owns a number of London residences, including Nuffield Lodge, 35 Upper Brook Street, and the embassy at 167 Queen’s Gate.

The Sultan made his way to the Royal Dais on Dachet Road in Windsor, where he was officially welcomed by King Charles and Queen Camilla as the sound of a Royal Salute was fired from the Home Park and the Tower of London. The trio then joined the Prince and Princess of Wales for a carriage procession to Windsor Castle – as has become tradition in recent state visits.

State visits are traditionally hosted at Buckingham Palace, but it remains under renovation.

Upon arrival in the Quadrangle of the Castle, the royals were greeted with a Royal Salute by a Guard of Honour while the regimental band played the Omani and British National Anthems. The Sultan and King Charles inspected the Guard before all five royals viewed a military parade. This will be followed by a lunch in the State Dining Room, with other members of the royal family, and a special exhibition of Oman-related items in the Green Drawing Room.

The highpoint of the state visit, however, is the state banquet. King Charles III and Queen Camilla were supported by the Prince and Princess of Wales, Princess Anne and Sir Timothy Laurence and the Duke and Duchess of Gloucester, as they welcomed the Sultan and some 150 esteemed guests to St George's Hall, Windsor Castle.

There was a selection of dazzling tiaras on display, with many of the royal women returning to their favourite pieces. Princess Anne sparkled in Princess Andrew's Meander Tiara, inherited from her father, Prince Philip, and worn by her daughter, Zara Tindall on her wedding day in 2011. Queen Camilla, elegant in amethysts, had her crowning moment in the Girls of Great Britain and Ireland Tiara, the most famous diadem in Queen Elizabeth II's collection. The Duchess of Gloucester, meanwhile, stepped out in the Gloucester Honeysuckle tiara.
