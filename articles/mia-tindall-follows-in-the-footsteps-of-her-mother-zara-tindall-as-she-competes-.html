Royals

Mia Tindall follows in the footsteps of her mother, Zara Tindall, as she competes in the Blenheim Horse Trials

The 12-year-old granddaughter of the Princess Royal proved that a talent for all things equestrian truly runs in the family

Por Isaac Bickerstaff · 21 de septiembre de 2026

There was a Tindall in the saddle at the Blenheim Horse Trials over the weekend – but not the one you might expect. Mia Tindall, 12, followed in the footsteps of her mother, Zara Tindall, and her grandmother, Princess Anne, when she took the reins to compete at the esteemed equestrian competition on 20 September.

With proud mother Zara watching on from the sidelines, Mia rode her horse, Bunny Two Twirls, at the Heythrop Pony Club Blenheim Palace Show Jumping competition. The young Tindall already appeared to be a seasoned competitor, wearing a smart traditional riding uniform and boots with her blonde hair tied up beneath her helmet.

Zara Tindall revealed back in 2019 that both her daughters had a passion for horses. ‘They both ride. Lena is in a little basket on the saddle, purely a passenger. But we just bought Mia a new pony called Magic,’ she told Australian publication Now to Love.

And young Mia’s love of equestrianism goes beyond simply competing. While Zara Tindall has also been busy competing at the Horse Trials, her daughter was spotted grooming her mother’s horse, Classicals Euro Star, between events. The Olympic silver medalist also coached her daughter through some jumps, no doubt offering up the years of competition practice which saw her claim a stunning victory at the British Open Championships in August.

Princess Anne’s daughter, whose last solo major championship victory came 20 years ago at the World Equestrian Games in Aachen, topped the podium after a stunning show on the cross-country course with her horse, Class Affair. The victory – Zara’s first at the British Open – did not come easily, though. Her performance in the dressage test had landed her outside the top 10, and Class Affair, she admitted, is a challenging ride at the best of times.

‘He’s super talented and very capable, but he doesn’t always do the thing you want him to do,’ she told Horse & Hound, describing the chestnut gelding as ‘one of the hardest horses I’ve ever ridden’.

‘He can go against you,’ the Olympic silver medalist added. ‘You ask him to go faster, and he’s like, “No, I don’t want to go faster,” and then, when you want to slow down for a fence, he goes, “No, sorry,” and sticks his head in the air.’

Speaking of her victorious horse, Zara concluded: ‘He’s a funny soul, but I love him.’

One onlooker told Hello magazine that, while the royal was not joined by her family at Hartpury, she was in high spirits throughout the event. ‘Zara was in a crazy mood, she seemed super happy, smiling as she jumped over the jumps, even going 'Whee', she was smiling and beaming, kissing the horse,’ the eyewitness said. ‘She's taken this horse, who is on the downward trend, and taken him to glory in the British Open championships.’

Mia Tindall has been joining her parents more and more on the British racing circuit. In April, she wore a very sharp blue pinstripe suit to join Zara and Mike Tindall for Ladies’ Day at the Grand National, which came after she kicked off the new year with a trip to Cheltenham alongside cousins Savannah and Isla Phillips.

The Tindalls possess a unique position within the wider family, with the Firm often turning to the clan to bring some levity to the more serious situations. As one source told The Times: ‘The Tindalls are very much the glue of their generation of royals and bring the fun to a family gathering, no matter how serious the occasion.’

Accordingly, Mia Tindall has long been a favourite of royal watchers for her bubbly personality and close bond with the Wales children. Chatting with Princess Charlotte over Christmas walks in Sandringham and giving Prince Louis a run for his money with her animated facial expressions at the Platinum Pageant in 2022, the young Tindall is sure to bring a bundle of energy to any royal event.

Born in 2014, Mia is the granddaughter of the Princess Royal and a great-granddaughter of the late Queen Elizabeth II, making her 19th in the line of succession. However, she does not hold a formal royal rank. When Mia’s mother, Zara Tindall, was born in 1981, her parents, Princess Anne and Captain Mark Phillips, declined to give her a title.

In June 2018, Mia became a big sister when Mike and Zara Tindall welcomed Lena Elizabeth Tindall. Three years later, in March 2021, the Tindall family grew once again when little Lucas Philip was born.

Energetic and with bundles of joy, Mia has proven that she’s always ready for a photo opportunity. From being carried across a rugby pitch in the arms of her father Mike, to leaning out of a window to watch Trooping the Colour next to her cousins, Prince George, Prince Charlotte and Prince Louis, life is never quiet when Mia is around. But could her next step be a riding career that shines as bright as her royal mother and grandmother?
