Why is the world so obsessed with high-society divorces?

As Sienna Miller and Dominic West prepare to go to war in Sky's explosive new divorce drama War , why can't we look away when the rich and powerful call in the lawyers?

Por Emma Rose · 25 de septiembre de 2026

Divorce is rarely pretty. But what happens when there are fortunes, reputations and quite possibly an entire public image at stake? Throw in an international film star, a tech entrepreneur and two of London's most formidable law firms, and suddenly a private heartbreak becomes something altogether more compelling.

Enter War, Sky's alluring new legal drama starring Sienna Miller and Dominic West. Miller plays international film star Carla Duval opposite West's British tech entrepreneur Morgan Henderson, whose marriage has reached a spectacularly acrimonious end. As the couple prepare to battle it out, two prestigious rival London law firms are pitted against one another, armed with secrets and a determination to win at almost any cost.

It has all the ingredients of deliciously addictive television: sex, scandal, betrayal and a superfluity of money. But beneath the glamorous London offices and eye-watering legal bills lies a more interesting question: why are we quite so fascinated by watching other people's marriages fall apart?

Television has long understood that divorce makes excellent drama. In The Split, Abi Morgan took viewers inside London's high-end divorce circuit, following lawyers entrusted with the affairs of the rich and famous. Then came Ryan Murphy's gloriously excessive All's Fair, in which Kim Kardashian, Naomi Watts and Sarah Paulson inhabit a world of powerhouse divorce attorneys and extraordinarily wealthy clients.

It seems we simply cannot resist getting involved in other people’s business, particularly when those people inhabit worlds that normally exist behind closed doors. Perhaps that is because a high-profile divorce offers a rare glimpse into a life usually kept meticulously out of view. We see the lavish wedding and the adoring glances; however, divorce dismantles that image. Suddenly, the very details kept firmly behind closed doors become newspaper fodder – headlines, memes – and readily accessible.

Real life has provided no shortage of examples. The separation and eventual divorce of the then Prince Charles and Diana, Princess of Wales became a global spectacle, as intimate details of one of the world's most famous marriages played out before an enthralled public. Then there was Sir Paul McCarney and Heather Mills in 2008 and Earl Spencer and Karen Gordon in 2024 – all represented by the ‘steel magnolia’, Baroness Shackleton, Britain’s most prominent divorce lawyer.

Then there is the question of money. Few break-ups demonstrated the extraordinary financial stakes of modern marriage quite like Jeff Bezos and MacKenzie Scott's 2019 divorce. When billions sit alongside broken hearts, divorce becomes not simply a question of who keeps the house, but one capable of reshaping entire fortunes.

And perhaps that is precisely where War finds its allure. There is something uniquely compelling about watching people who appear to have everything discover that wealth cannot necessarily insulate them from heartbreak. In fact, success can make the collapse all the more complicated. When a marriage encompasses businesses, properties, careers, public reputations and extraordinary sums of money, separating two lives becomes a negotiation of power as much as emotion.

For Carla and Morgan, divorce is therefore not simply the ending of a love story. It is a battle over who controls the narrative once that story falls apart. And, crucially, War understands that the lawyers can be every bit as fascinating as the couple themselves. Behind every society separation are the people tasked with keeping secrets and deciding just how far they are prepared to go in the interests of their client.

War promises to encapsulate not only London life and celebrity, but the uncomfortable collision between love and status. Because however private divorce may begin, history has shown that when the people involved are rich and powerful, the rest of us have an unfortunate habit of pulling up a chair. After all, nothing draws a crowd quite like watching a seemingly perfect marriage go to war…
