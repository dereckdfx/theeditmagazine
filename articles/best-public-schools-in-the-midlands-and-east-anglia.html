Edit Schools Guide 2027: The best public schools in the Midlands and East Anglia

The 2027 Edit Schools Guide reveals the best public schools in the Midlands and East Anglia

Por Edit · 29 de septiembre de 2026

Culford, Bury St Edmunds, Suffolk IP28 6TX

Website culford.co.uk Head Claire Bentley, since 2023; previously head of prep and pre-prepPupils 826. Day: 347 boys, 310 girls; boarding: 113 boys, 92 girls Ages 1–18 Term Fees Day: £10,750; boarding: £17,040 Bursaries 10% of pupils, up to 25% Alumni Snoop founder Dame Jayne-Anne Gadhia, deputy private secretary to the King Theo Rycroft, rugby player Tommy Freeman

In early 2022, Culford’s admissions team received an email from a 14-year-old Ukrainian boy, who had played the clarinet with several Kharkiv orchestras. He explained how his plans for the future had been cut short by Russia’s invasion, so Culford offered him a bursary. After he’d escaped the conflict, he threw himself into everything the school had to offer – from musical ensembles to maths clubs – and last year he won a place at Oxford. Such is the power of the all-round education here. There’s a dynamic roster of activities, with the sports offering particularly noteworthy: the golf academy has an outdoor driving range and short-game practice area; the Year 9/10 boys’ tennis team was crowned 2024 National Champions at the LTA Finals; and a partnership withTottenham Hotspur will provide access to football coaches and to Spurs’ training centre in Enfield. Up next? A revamp of the tennis facilities, two new padel courts and a comprehensive horse racingprogramme that aims to combine classroom-based learning with extensive practical training to provide a pathway into the equine world. Responding to parental requests, the school has rejigged its senior timetable so that students can pursue their interests every day of the week, be that pickleball, drone-flying, Esports or falconry. They still find time for putting on the Ritz. Of Culford’s regular ‘formals’, one teen says: ‘They’re such a great time for the school to come together and have some fun. And in the most elegant of attire!’

Ellesmere, Shropshire SY12 9AB

Website ellesmere.com Head Jonathan Shaw, since 2025; previously head at King’s ElyPupils 530. Day: 229 boys, 133 girls; boarding: 102 boys, 66 girls Ages 7–18 Term Fees Day: £7,740; boarding: £16,368 Bursaries 25% of pupils, on application Alumni The Duke of Westminster, former World Rugby chairman Bill Beaumont, Net World Sports founder Alex Lovén

At Ellesmere College, what stands out is not just the scale of opportunity, but how accessible it all feels. ‘Everyone knows everyone. There is no chance [you] could be overlooked,’ says one parent. Founded in 1879 and set among Shropshire’s rolling hills, the school faces outwards, too, through a sociology A-level option and days dedicated to PSHE. Meanwhile, the college’s Life: Ready approach is evident in a balanced regime whereby pupils combine study with sport, the arts and leadership – and with pastoral care, through a Sixth Form-led scheme that trains pupils to recognise when peers may be struggling and direct them to support. But sport is also central to Ellesmere: it has academies in rugby, cricket, golf and swimming, as well as a high-performance hockey programme – three alumni competed in the last two Olympic Games – and offers scholarships to talented athletes that give them access to high-level coaching and tailored academic provision. Still, the arts are firmly embedded here. With three consecutive Artsmark Platinum awards won by the drama department, the seniors tour their annual play to Tabor Academy in Massachusetts; and a new Sixth-form art space, funded by the illustrator Charlie Adlard, has lately been added. Elsewhere, the campus itself becomes part of the learning process: Lower School pupils use the Grow Dome and wildlife garden to plant and maintain habitats and to investigate sustainability first-hand. ‘There’s a lot of choice,’ says one pupil, ‘and loads of space and fresh air.’

Felsted, Essex CM6 3LL

Website felsted.org Head Lara Pechard, since 2026; previously head of St Margaret’s School, BushyPupils 928. Day: 227 boys, 203 girls; boarding: 252 boys, 246 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £12,558; boarding: £19,225 Bursaries 28% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Former surgeon gynaecologist to the Royal Family Sir Marcus Setchell, England rugby player Max Malins, playwright Alecky Blythe

The privileges theoretically afforded to heads of school here include growing beards, smoking pipes and exercising goats on the cricket pitch. The establishment was founded in 1564 (and its original building, converted from a guildhall, is still used by the prep’s music department). Beside a charming village, and surrounded by a 90-acre rural campus, Felsted is undoubtedly rooted in history. However, it firmly embraces modernity, as evidenced by the recently completed performing arts studio and the computer science centre staffed by specialist technicians. And though its musicians are regularly selected for the Essex Youth and National Youth Orchestras – also performing in a huge number of choirs and ensembles for all ages – a student reports that one of the most anticipated annual events is the House Shout singing contest, when ‘competition is fierce and it’s amazing to see every-one come out of their shells’. One third of Sixth-formers take the IB, two thirds A-levels; and popular university choices include Exeter, Bristol and Leeds, with 90 per cent heading to their first choice. But Felsted’s mission to ‘make a positive difference’ also extends to sharing teaching expertise and facilities with local primaries – and offering full Sixth-form bursaries to suitable applicants from the Royal Docks Academy in east London. The latter is near the Felsted Mission, founded in 1902 and funded by the public school, which supports multiple local community projects.

Cromer Road, Holt, Norfolk NR25 6EA

Website greshams.com Head Douglas Robb, since 2014; previously head of Oswestry School Pupils 537. Day: 107 boys, 107 girls; boarding: 168 boys, 155 girls Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £12,255; boarding: £17,560 Bursaries 15% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Industrial designer Sir James Dyson, actress Olivia Colman, England rugby players Ben and Tom Youngs

Most Gresham’s families hail from East Anglia, perhaps drawn by the 200-acre grounds a stone’s throw from the coast; perhaps by the charismatic head Douglas Robb, described by his charges as ‘great’, ‘friendly’ and ‘giving with his time’. Students certainly seem happy here; charming, well-mannered and willing to throw themselves into a whole range of activities and pursuits. (Winning five national titles, the rifle team is hitting the bullseye, with one shooter crowned Grand Aggregate Champion 2025.) In the classroom, IB students can now take film studies – allowing them to explore cinema as an art form – and performing arts has been added to the BTEC offering. Regarding which, both the IB and BTEC results are extremely impressive: two pupils in last year’s cohort attained the maximum 45 points, placing them in the top 0.2 per cent globally, while the school also recorded its best ever BTEC showing. Elsewhere, physical types are nurtured by Ben Youngs, England’s most capped rugby player and head of performance sport, and the U16 rugby team has been on a particularly ferocious streak, losing just three games out of 34 in the past few seasons. An All-Steinway school, Gresham’s not only has a piano academy but offers music technology and songwriting masterclasses, while recent leavers have gained offers and recognition from some of the most prestigious conservatoires in the country. And in other developments, the new Michael Goff Lecture Theatre provides a fantastic facility for pupils, and the prep is preparing to move to its purpose-built home on an expansive 86-acre site funded by alumnus Sir James Dyson.

Edgbaston Park Road, Birmingham B15 2UA

Website kes.org.uk Head Kirsty von Malaisé, since 2024; previously principal of King Edward VI High School for Girls Pupils 815 day boys Ages 11–18 Term Fees £7,695 Bursaries 15% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Author and philologist JRR Tolkien, former Mayor of the West Midlands Andy Street, thriller writer Lee Child

The winds of change are blowing through KES. Kirsty von Malaisé, who’s been head since September 2024, has abolished the IB – the final cohort will take the exams this summer – and reintroduced an A-level-only syllabus. And while take-up in such subjects as Greek, Latin and German is currently a little low, she has come up with a savvy solution that will likely prove popular: joining up with the neighbouring girls’ school for mixed lessons in these disciplines,as well as for music, drama, foreign exchanges and some trips. For all the syllabus changes, though, KES will doubtless remain an academic powerhouse. Last year, 43 per cent of A-level grades were A*, and an astounding 71 per cent of GCSEs were graded 9. The school typically sends about 10 per cent of its leavers to Oxbridge every year, and a handful to American universities. (Last year’s head boy secured a place at Harvard.) Otherwise, 22 pupils went on to read medicine and dentistry. However, it’s far from all work and no play. There are more than 100 after-hours societies here – from debating to ‘animal club’ – and the school will soon be introducing cookery to the curriculum from Year 7 onwards. There are also huge amounts of sport on offer, with teams ranked from A to E so that everyone can take part. And according to von Malaisé, who is involved in music coaching herself, KES boasts an ‘ambitious and phenomenal’ music programme. The countdown to its 475th anniversary next year is on; and the school looks set to go from strength to strength.

Banbury Road, Warwick, CV34 6YE

Website kingshighwarwick.co.uk Head Dr Stephen Burley, since 2021; previously deputy head (academic) Pupils 816 girls. 808 day; 8 boarding. Ages 3–18Term Fees Day: £7,655; boarding: £18,773 Bursaries 7% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Actresses Sophie Turner and Gemma Whelan, soprano Catherine Bott

Head Dr Stephen Burley describes his fiefdom as a ‘community of changemakers’; and it is indeed a forward-thinking place, intent on preparing its pupils for the age of artificial intelligence. Take the Sixth Form’s recent curriculum extension through Science for Change, a new ‘future-ready course’ that incorporates innovation, entrepreneurship and an AI component. On top of that, Burley is ‘enormously proud’ of his recently launched Super Saturday programme, an inclusive sports initiative that enables a range of activities for girls who don’t have competitive fixtures that day. This project also offers places to students from local state schools – including those in KHS’s newly established multi-academy trust – while long-standing links with Warwick School [for boys] mean that King’s High girls receive some of the benefits of a co-ed set-up. Clearly, it’s an effective formula: 58 per cent of GCSEs were graded 9–8, 44 per cent of A-levels were A*–A, and 88 percent of leavers went on to their first-choice universities – including top drama and music institutions, Oxbridge and McGill in Canada. (One student was even accepted to a course on horology.) But equally valued are the prestigious degree apprenticeships won with everyone from Dyson to Deloitte; and last year saw a record number take up places at Jaguar Land Rover, Unilever and HSBC. New arrivals here include a second school dog, Moose. And there are plans to establish international schools in the near future, as well as to install a fresh 3G Astroturf for hockey and open a cricket academy.

Trumpington Road, Cambridge CB2 7AD

Website theleys.net Head Dr Clare Ives, since 2025, previously senior deputy head of Sevenoaks Pupils 564. Day: 124 boys, 86 girls; boarding: 203 boys, 151 girls Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £11,835; boarding: £18,276 Bursaries 10% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni The King of Tonga, former reporter and MP Martin Bell, tennis player Jamie Murray

Dr Clare Ives ‘is really supportive and kind and makes really active efforts to engage with and get to know the pupils’, says one, endorsing the head’s commitment to the individual needs of every child.Set in the heart of Cambridge, The Leys makes full use of its surroundings through its CamEx programme – whereby students sing Evensong in college chapels, attend university lectures and receive mentorship from postgraduates – in an arrangement few can replicate. Hardly surprising that last year saw a 61 per cent A*–A rate at A-level and the school’s best results on record at GCSE. (Facilities continue to improve, too, and now include a design lab with 3D scanners and resin printers.) Outside the classroom, there are more than 100 clubs, from debating and drama to gardening and volunteering. And sport is a central pillar of life, with its scholars benefiting from tailored programmes that have recently propelled some to international caps in cricket and rugby. Art is on a par, with students regularly exhibiting at Michaelhouse, a public city-centre gallery. Academic provision is complemented thoughtfully by a learning support department which offers both digital wellness apps and a weekly open-door clinic. As one parent puts it, ‘The pastoral care here is the glue that holds everything else together.’

College Road, Malvern, Worcestershire WR14 3DF

Website malverncollege.org.uk Head Keith Metcalfe, since 2019; previously deputy head of Harrow Pupils 782. Day: 143 boys; 124 girls; boarding: 275 boys; 240 girls Ages 0–18 Term Fees Day: £13,920; boarding: £20,505 Bursaries 12% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Author CS Lewis, chief medical officer for England Sir Chris Whitty, 2023 Earthshot Prize-winner Talal Hasan

Ask the students what their favourite aspect of Malvern is, and its ‘super-curriculum’ gets multiple votes. Interested in architecture? There’s a society. Medicine? There’s a society. Engineering? Physics? Languages? There’s a society. As one pupil says, ‘You are given every opportunity. The school doesn’t limit you in the least.’ And head Keith Metcalfe proudly admits the environment is ‘unashamedly academic’. However, he adds that Malvern is definitely not a hot house. Instead, the focus is on how the children will go on to present themselves to the world, so there’s plenty of character-forming afoot. Alongside the academics, clubs and trips – including a Nordic expedition for outward-bound pursuits, the Azores for geography and science, and Canada for skiing – sport is a defining strength; and rugby is a priority. The school is proud of its elite professional and academy pathways. Still, there’s also a deep commitment to the arts, underpinned by a recent £3.1 million investment that has transformed the music building into a modern All-Steinway School, complete with a recording studio, specialised teaching spaces and performance areas. Director of music James Ramm has introduced Live Lounge sessions to The Grub café, to help his charges build confidence.

Weston Rhyn, Shropshire SY11 3EW

Website moretonhall.org Head Michael Brewer, since 2023; previously head of Thetford Grammar School Pupils 332. Day: 64 boys; 178 girls; boarding: 90 girls. Ages 0–18 Term Fees Day: £10,594; boarding: £15,834 Bursaries Variable % of pupils, on application Alumni Opera and classical music composer Thea Musgrave, editor-in-chief of The Economist Zanny Minton Beddoes, judicial commissioner Dame Linda Dobbs

A recent £1 million investment at Moreton Hall has resulted in some very impressive enhancements, including a fabulous 25-metre indoor pool and upgrades to the school’s pitches and fitness facilities. But then, sport is big here, with lacrosse especially popular. (The first team ranked third in the U19s England Lacrosse Challenge Cup in 2025.) The arts are taken equally seriously at Moreton. The drama department was shortlisted for the Music & Drama Education Awards 2026 and achieved 100 per cent distinctions in LAMDA; its annual shows are much anticipated; and The Holroyd Community Theatre – a multi-functional space shared with several local arts groups – is an incredibly slick venue in which to perform. ‘Teachers are so passionate about drama, musicals are one of the best times of the year,’ says a current pupil. ‘There’s nothing like performing a show you love with friends.’ Located in 100 acres of Shropshire parkland and founded more than a century ago, Moreton Hall is a small school with no plans to grow; and the upshot is that every student seems to be known as an individual, academically, pastorally and socially. Day girls, flexi- and full boarders mostly come from within an hour’s radius, although a quarter of the student body is international. And since being so rural means a lot of time is spent on the school campus, enrichment activities and the co-curricular timetable are extensive and extremely popular.

**Chelmsford, Essex CM3 3HS **

Website newhallschool.co.uk Head Katherine Jeffrey, since 2001; previously deputy head of the Marist School Pupils 1,368. Day: 506 boys, 511 girls; boarding 179 boys, 172 girls Ages 1–18 Term Fees Day: £9,998; boarding: £15,948 Bursaries Variable % of pupils, up to 100% Alumni CNN chief international anchor Christiane Amanpour, Saracens rugby player Dom Morris, designer Dame Anya Hindmarch

Originally acquired by Henry VIII in 1517, New Hall is approached by an avenue of trees which majestically opens on to the site of one of the oldest Catholic schools in the country. Less historic, but probably just as important to parents, is the recent opening of Beaulieu Park railway station located at the end of this drive, providing links to London Liverpool Street in under 40 minutes. It’s well worth the journey. The first Catholic independent school in the UK to be formally accredited by the Royal College of Organists, it has just launched a new choral society that brings together local residents, parents, staff and students. A new exchange programme with a sister school in Belgium is thriving. In response to the popularity of football among both boys and girls, a cricket pitch has been repurposed. The Sixth Form curriculum has been expanded to include a BTEC in performing arts, and the classics department now offers classical civilisation alongside Greek and Latin. A refurbishment of the Sixth-form boys’ boarding house has delivered en-suite bedrooms modelled on the top UK universities, and new soundproof workspaces in their study centre provide spaces for focused learning. Pupils describe their school as ‘a place of familiarity and stability’, where they are ‘able to excel in [their] personal and emotional growth’. And in giving back: a group of Rwandan nuns recently arrived to live at New Hall; the school had been raising funds to support their community in Africa.

The Close, Norwich NR1 4DD

Website norwich-school.org.uk Head Steffan Griffiths, since 2011; previously deputy head of Magdalen College School Pupils 1,111 day: 629 boys, 482 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees £8,406 Bursaries 6% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Admiral Lord Nelson, Olympic athlete Emma Pooley, England rugby player Freddie Steward

It really is hard to imagine a more picturesque setting than Norwich’s Cathedral Close, where cobbled passageways and old church properties are jumbled together with the school’s buildings. ‘Being so near the cathedral, a historic landmark on our city skyline, is really inspiring,’ says one student. And life here is interwoven with the local community; through partnerships and collaborations with a wide range of charities and schools as well as, for example, through playing and singing with the acclaimed Norwich Baroque ensemble. That’s not the limit of their talents: a bespoke studio has promoted a surge of interest in dance, and both the internal choreography competition and Senior Showcase allow all pupils to perform in a professional theatre setting and be judged by industry veterans. Nor is that the limit of developments: recently completed building works have resulted in an impressive extension containing specialist facilities for such subjects as science, design and engineering; and a new senior school facility has just been opened. (Complete with everything from a second-hand uniform shop to innovative teaching spaces, this space hosts conferences, career events and parents’ evenings.) Alongside tra-ditional GCSEs, students can take electives in such topics as space science, finance and filmmaking, which are not tested by examination but by oracy, presentation and so on. Those who want to take a quick breather from all the dynamism can head to the Jubilee Wellbeing Garden, with its outdoor classroom, and tranquil wildflower area.

Chapel Close, Oakham, Rutland LE15 6DT

Website oakham.rutland.sch.uk Head Lucy Pearson, since 2026; previously head of Cheadle Hulme College Pupils 812. Day: 217 boys, 225 girls; boarding: 205 boys, 165 girls Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £11,508; boarding: £19,482 Bursaries 5% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Actor Matthew Macfadyen, former England cricketer Stuart Broad, actress Katie Hall

Old Oakhamian Lucy Pearson has returned to take the helm here, the first woman to lead the school in its 400-year history. And the former head girl and England cricketer – whose roles have spanned the FA and Wellington College – says she is committed to inclusion. (After all, bursary support opened the door to her own education.) So it’s fitting that, in partnership with the Rutland Learning Trust, Oakham has such an impressive outreach programme, providing 13 local primaries with minibuses for excursions, support from teachers and sports training sessions. In the works is a major extension to the sports centre, which will further enhance an already-strong provision that has led to formidable rugby, hockey, cricket and netball teams alongside enthusiasts for less mainstream offerings such as fives and sailing. In the pipeline is a sensitive transformation of the original 1584 school building, into a performance venue, which will further strengthen a dramatic output that ranges from roaring musicals to dance showcases. The art department’s crown jewel is the Wheelhouse Gallery, an exhibition space where pupils curate their own shows; and musicians have the opportunity to play annually in 50 concerts, from intimate recitals to showpiece galas. In the classroom, the seniors can choose between A-levels, BTEC and IB – about a third go for the latter – and nearly half their recent results were graded A*–A or equivalent. Halfthe intake are day pupils, but they are all given two free nights of boarding every term.

The Great Hall, New Street, Oundle PE8 4GH

Website oundleschool.org.uk Head Dominic Oliver, since 2025; previously head of Lancing College Pupils 1,160. Day: 170 boys, 150 girls; boarding: 352 boys, 488 girls Ages 4–18 Term Fees Day: £13,300; boarding: £20,385 Bursaries 18% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni ConservationistSir Peter Scott, biologist Richard Dawkins, rugby player Tom Curry

‘Those who know, know; those who don’t, probably haven’t heard of it,’ confides one parent. One of Oundle’s great strengths is that it produces youngsters who are repeatedly described as grounded and hard-working – and definitely not entitled or arrogant. And as one student says, ‘I love that it focuses on creating well-rounded individuals above all else. You can find hundreds of schools that are grade factories, but you’d be hard pressed to find one that provided opportunities to grow, pursue one’s interests and develop character on the scale that’s found here.’ With their buildings dotted round the school’s beautiful, honey-hued, eponymous town, pupils – the girls wearing their distinctive culottes – can be spotted buzzing between an incredible array of activities. Radio Oscar, their in-house station, brings on budding presenters and DJs. The Stahl Theatre is open to the public, and so puts on school plays as well as professional performances from touring companies. The art school comprises four dedicated studios and, situated close to the sublime chapel, the Yarrow Gallery hangs exhibitions that are sources of great inspiration. Popular clubs range from fashion textiles to drone racing and chemistry cosmetics, while downtime is spent catching up with friends in such nearby cafés as The Coffee Tavern and Beans. New head Dominic Oliver has made a great impression, and his tenure will be underpinned by his emphasis on balancing intellectual ambition with kindness. He believes that ‘schools are people-places’.

Hills Road, Cambridge, CB2 8QF

Website perse.co.uk Head Edward Elliott, since 2008; previously deputy head Pupils 1,774 day: 922 boys, 852 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees £9,482 Bursaries 6% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Actor Colin McFarlane, founder of the Royal Shakespeare Company Sir Peter Hall, GB hockey player Tess Howard

The Perse has opened a refurbished science laboratory, which is quite fitting when you consider that Old Persean John Clarke received a 2025 Nobel Prize for his work in super-computing. (Fun fact: having educated three winners, the school has more Nobel laureates to its name than some countries.) Pupils certainly love the ‘wholly unique feeling that everyone around you is genuinely invested and interested in what they can learn’. These clever children have not only reached the finals of international debating competitions but won national mathematics contests and Schools’Challenges (the junior equivalents of University Challenge). A fresh sports centre has resulted in an increased uptake of basketball and badminton, while a brand-new performing arts school provides a range of classes covering singing, drama and musical theatre.

Repton, Derbyshire DE65 6FH

Website repton.org.uk Head Mark Semmence, since 2019; previously head of Mount Kelly Pupils 1,007. Day: 289 boys, 268 girls; boarding: 261 boys, 189 girls Ages 3–18 Term Fees Day: £14,370; boarding: £19,375 Bursaries 33% of pupils, on application Alumni Legendary athlete Harold Abrahams, author Roald Dahl, actor George Rainsford

With some 10,000 pupils around the world wearing the uniform of Repton, its ‘family’ of schools spans multiple countries and continents – and provides brilliant opportunities for building global connections. An example of this is the Digital Priory, situated next to the original 12th-century church building at the heart of campus: its interactive learning space allows pupils to link with their international colleagues and work collaboratively on projects. Further additions includetwo artificial turf football pitches, padel and tennis courts and some updated music technology equipment. Singers perform in such memorable locations as Derby Cathedral; the drama department conceives a dynamic roster of plays every year; and one particularly appreciated spectacle is the quadrennial ‘Pedants’, at which the entire teaching staff perform skits, dances and poetry recitals for the students. Repton is also well-known for sport, and for good reason. The list of its achievements could fill a whole review, and the amount of silverware it has won could filla warehouse of cabinets. Those who join the department’s High Performance programme are allocated a mentor who will go beyond honing their physical prowess and provide them with emotional and psychological succour. All this excellence is overseen by the ‘app-roachable’ and ‘supportive’ head Mark Semmence. ‘He epitomises what Repton strives to produce,’ says one parent. ‘He has the gravitas you’d expect in the school’s head but is full of character. He has poise and swagger in equal measure.’

Ipswich, Suffolk IP9 2RX

Website royalhospitalschool.org Head Steve Dixon, since 2026; previously Second MasterPupils 556. Day: 148 boys, 127 girls; boarding: 152 boys, 129 girls Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £8,734; boarding: £17,033 Bursaries 60% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Sailor Hannah Stodel, England cricketer Reece Topley, actress Kate Somerset Holmes

The Royal Hospital School was founded in 1712 to educate the children of Royal Navy seamen who were injured or killed at sea. Much has changed over the last three centuries, but nautical traditions still persist: it’s one of only two UK schools whose pupils wear Royal Naval uniforms, and the marching band is integral to life here. ‘These aspects are actually wonderful and teach the children so much,’ says one parent. ‘The pupils are incredibly proud when they wear their uniforms, and it clearly fosters a strong sense of belonging and responsibility.’ Given their rich heritage, it’s perhaps unsurprising that the students are accomplished sailors, holding national titles and competing around the world. Still, on dry land, there are 96 acres of playing fields to navigate and a seriously impressive indoor climbing wall. Academics are soaring, with 2025 seeing RHS’s best-ever GCSE results; and about 80 per cent of leavers head to university, with most of the remainder enrolling in app-renticeships, the armed services and the police. A programme of capital investment has delivered renovations to the Clubhouse and Sailing Hub and effected a transformation of the school theatre, now the largest of its kind in the region. In an appealing move to balance well-being with ambition, the timetable has been rejigged to introduce Super Saturdays, an innovative programme of workshops that span critical thinking, CCF, financial education and outdoor skills. Those who like the sound of RHS will be delighted by the new nursery.

Rugby, Warwickshire CV22 5EH

Website rugbyschool.co.uk Head Gareth Parker-Jones, since 2020; previously deputy head academic Pupils 877. Day: 122 boys, 125 girls; boarding: 350 boys, 290 girls Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £13,070; boarding: £20,620 Bursaries 41% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Author Lewis Carroll, writer Dolly Alderton, Deliciously Ella founder Ella Mills

Rugby can be proud of its literary associations – alumni include the likes of Salman Rushdie, Anthony Horowitz and Lewis Carroll – and creative writing is popular among the pupils, who spend time flexing their brains in some 200 clubs. Many volunteer in local primaries to help with languages and reading (and even teach the children how to play brass instruments) while an extensive bursary provision supports 150 students. The sports programme sees both genders training together for football, while a double triumph at Lord’s last year saw the girls’ and boys’ cricket teams crowned U18 National Cup champions. A new indoor golf simulator is part of a broader schedule of improvements that will include an extension to the library, a spruce-up of the theatre with new seats, lighting and technical facilities fitted, and an upgrade to the boarding houses. What lucky pupils these are.

Shrewsbury, Shropshire SY3 7BA

Website shrewsbury.org.uk Head Leo Winkley, since 2018; previously head of St Peter’s School, York Pupils 848. Day: 134 boys, 89 girls; boarding: 388 boys, 237 girls Ages 13–18 Term Fees Day: £14,051; boarding: £20,141 Bursaries 45% of pupils, up to 100% Alumni Naturalist Charles Darwin, mountaineer Andrew ‘Sandy’ Irvine, actor Sir Michael Palin

Some of Shrewsbury’s football rules made it into the FA’s original Laws of the Game (1863). It’s also the home of cross-country running: when the then-head rejected pupils’ requests to start fox hunting, they agreed on a plan to traverse hill and dale by foot, no foxes involved. It’s little wonder sport is exceptionally strong: the school has a partnership with Shrewsbury Town FC, with one pupil securing a professional contract with the club; and the girls’ 1st XI hockey team were crowned Midlands County champions. Alongside a new indoor golf facility, padel courts are on the way. Academically, the mathematicians have excelled in local competitions; the biologists were shortlisted for a prize from Imperial College; and its leavers secured six Oxbridge places as well as a Harvard offer. Describing itself as ‘serious fun’, Shrewsbury takes plays to the Edinburgh Fringe, has a rich musical offering and an array of international trips.\

Uppingham, Rutland, LE15 9QE

Website uppingham.co.uk Head Dr Richard Maloney, since 2016; previously head of Bede’s School Pupils 820. Day: 66 boys, 58 girls; boarding: 389 boys, 304 girls Ages 11–18 Term Fees Day: £11,814; boarding: £20,172 Bursaries 11% of pupils, up to 100%+ Alumni Actor and writer Stephen Fry, musician Harry Judd, chef and restaurateur Rick Stein

In 1865, Uppingham was one of the first English public schools to make music prominent in its curriculum. This spirit of euphonious adventure, along with an emphasis on the importance of a musical education, remains very much at its heart: there’s communal hymn singing in Chapel five mornings a week, and more than 70 musical performances are given every year. Over in the theatre department, director Matt Bulmer brings his wealth of professional experience to put on such stand-out productions as Chicago and Romeo and Juliet; and art, photography and other creative subjects are taught in the award-winning Leonardo Centre, whose cutting-edge tech encompasses 3D printing and CAD design. Enrichment activities span everything from Scottish reeling to beekeeping, and pastoral care is forefronted in an annual Wellbeing Week. As for sports, the staffing complement – replete with top-level coaches, physios, doctors and data analysts – rivals that of any professional club, while the teams regularly win fixtures and finals. Launched in 2024, the day house is proving popular, while the new lower school is going from strength to strength. Operating as a self-contained establishment, its Year 7 and 8 pupils follow their own timetable and are based in a recently renovated house with its own dining area and games room, dedicated study spaces and garden. Expansion is also afoot further afield, with the opening of Uppingham’s first international school in Asia, on an incredible site in Vietnam.
