Royals

Queen Rania of Jordan wishes her lookalike daughters a happy birthday in a series of sweet social media posts

The ever-glamorous Jordanian royal has just returned from a powerful visit to the UN General Assembly in New York

Por Isaac Bickerstaff · 28 de septiembre de 2026

Queen Rania of Jordan has been busy representing her country at the United Nations General Assembly, but the self-proclaimed ‘mum and wife with a really cool day job’ still found time to wish her two beloved daughters a happy birthday in a series of sweet social media posts.

On 26 September, her second daughter, Princess Salma, turned 26. The royal shared a new photo with Princess Salma, with a number of royal watchers commenting on just how similar the pair looked. Queen Rania wore a geometric print dress from Eman Al Ahmed in the snapshot, while her daughter donned a simple white shirt dress from Alexis.

‘Happy birthday to someone I could never quite sum up in a caption!’ Rania wrote alongside the photo. ‘Salma, I love your mind, your heart, and who you are.’

The mother-daughter duo certainly have plenty in common. When Princess Salma stepped into the royal spotlight as she joined her father and her brother, Prince Hashem, on an official visit to China in August, she channelled her mother’s grace and elegance – borrowing one of Rania’s glamorous gowns for the occasion.

Salma, who became the Jordanian Armed Forces’ first female jet pilot at just 19 years old in 2020, joined her father and her brother for a conference in China, donning the Bottega Veneta A-line belted shirt dress first seen on Rania back in 2018, when the Jordanian Queen wore it during a state visit to the United States. Princess Salma paired the look with a pair of black leather pumps from Jennifer Chamandi, looking every bit the elegant young royal.

It is not the only way that the Princess has followed in her mother’s footsteps. In 2018, she graduated from the International Amman Academy in 2018, the same school where Rania studied before she married into the Royal Family. It is notable that Princess Salma chose to attend an open-to-all private school in Jordan, as many of her fellow royals prefer to undergo private tuition.

It was just another of the Princess’s achievements, which have seen her become a jewel in the crown of the Jordanian monarchy. In 2018, she took part in Sandhurst’s short commissioning course programme, before she was commissioned into the Jordanian Armed Forces in 2018. In 2023, she graduated from the University of Southern California with a BA in Archaeology. Her older sister – and near birthday twin – Princess Iman studied Strategic Design and Management at Parsons School of Design in New York City.

Indeed, the day after Princess Salma's birthday, it was time for Queen Rania to celebrate Iman – the first daughter she welcomed with King Abdullah. Princess Iman celebrated her 30th birthday on 27 September, and her mother marked the occasion with an über-glamorous photograph of the pair. ‘Happy birthday, my dearest Iman,’ the Queen wrote, before making a sweet reference to Iman’s children – Amina and her twin sisters, Rania and Talia, who were born in August 2026: ‘Mom to three beautiful little girls, but forever my precious little girl.’

Queen Rania no doubt did her daughters proud on her own outing to New York last week. The Jordanian queen joined the Irish Taoiseach and representatives from Save the Children and the State of Palestine at the High-Level Dialogue on Palestinian Children in Military Detention – an event co-sponsored by the Jordanian, Irish, Palestinian, Spanish and South African governments. Queen Rania called for immediate international action to protect children subjected to military detention in Palestine, in a powerful speech addressed to the crowd.

‘I just don’t understand. What is so terrifying about Palestinian children?’ Queen Rania asked. ‘Before they have even come of age, Palestinians are cast as threats: demographic threats, security threats, presumed guilty of future crimes they haven’t committed. Their innocence stripped away, they are dehumanised so thoroughly that even a kid with roller skates, a soccer ball, or a kite can be seen as something menacing. And a child can be branded a terrorist – as if they were never a child at all.’

Queen Rania, more than many royals, has imbued her royal role with advocacy on the world stage. Five years into her reign, she told the BBC that she sees the position as ‘an opportunity to bring about change.’

She added: ‘For me the title of a Queen means a certain responsibility. For me it’s a job; it means having to be aware of what is going on in my country, look at the people of Jordan and see how to improve their lives. It is not something that I carry; it does not change my personal life, it does not change who I am as a person, and I see it as a job title.’

A mum and wife with a really cool day job, indeed.
