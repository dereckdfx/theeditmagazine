Meet the Victoria Beckham of British country style: Jade Holland Cooper is taking British fashion by storm

She began with a stall at Badminton. Now Jade Holland Cooper has a flourishing fashion empire and £10 million Cotswolds HQ. Among her fans? The Princess of Wales, who stepped out in a Holland Cooper blouse this week

Por Chandler Tregaskes · 26 de agosto de 2026

There’s an air of Stanley Tucci declaring, ‘Gird your loins!’ when one of Jade Holland Cooper’s many black-tweed-and-jewellery-clad minions announces: ‘She’s en route!’ The sparkling new £10 million office – which doubles, triples and quadruples as a boutique, custom atelier and eatery – descends into a scene of sheer camp panic. Jade drives her slick, midnight-black Ferrari over the gravel driveway to her reserved ‘JHC’ parking spot and a sea of assistants – all in silk blouses and gold-buttoned blazers – scurry to make sure everything in sight is perfect for her arrival. ‘We have a dress code, which is “smart”,’ the designer says. ‘In a world where people aren’t doing that, or they’re working from home, I’m doing something different. I want to empower people through clothes, and I believe my team should be doing the same thing. Otherwise, we’re not delivering what we’re preaching.’

Ahead of today’s London Fashion Week show, the V&A opens the doors to a new showcase focusing on Burberry’s most famed silhouette, the trench coat

It’s how the Suffolk-born founder of her namesake label and Cotswolds atelier garnered herself a reputation as ‘the queen of Cheltenham’, famously transforming race-day fashion with her elegant, tailored designs. Factor in her towering pile, enviable clique and the picture-perfect family to match, and she’s really the Victoria Beckham of British country style.

This 70,000-square-foot HQ is a gilded palace of tartan trench coats, pussy-bow blouses and coveted tri-button-shoulder knitwear. Does she feel like regional royalty, alongside her husband, the astronomically wealthy Superdry co-founder Julian Dunkerton? ‘Definitely not. I just beaver away and if anyone recognises [me], I just think, “God, how odd,”’ she laughs. ‘There’s very little time to feel like anything other than hardcore working, focusing on the next big thing. And I would say my husband’s exactly the same.’

All that hard work has paid off. Look beyond London and as soon as you hit anything ‘rural’, you’ll find girls donning HC-logo jeans, jodhpurs and those well-known jackets with their logo-emblazoned gold buttons like a badge of honour. It’s become a uniform at the Cheltenham races, where, in a few short years, Jade has changed the face of the event, replacing those ’90s furs and sometimes-stuffy coat dresses with her sleek tartans and twinsets. Holland Cooper (the label) has been the official luxury partner for the past three years, and Jade herself is the unofficial people’s princess of the weekend. Does it ever get old, walking through those gates each year and seeing the swarms of racegoers dressed in her tweeds? ‘Gosh no!’ she says. ‘Cheltenham is a great indicator, like a litmus of how much we’ve grown, because I can physically see it. I can physically feel more people recognising me and the brand every year. It’s always amazing to see people wearing it.’

From the comfort of his bed, Edward Bluemel talks Shakespeare, snogs, and skin-tight suits with Ben Jureidini

Having hit upon the perfect balance between haughty chicness and horsey realness, Holland Cooper turned over £60 million last year; ‘hopefully 70’ this year, she says. Not bad for a woman who started back in 2008 with a stall at Badminton Horse Trials, a line of tweed miniskirts and ra-ra skirts, and a dream. ‘I am an absolute junkie for trading and selling. That commercial adrenaline rush? There’s just nothing like it,’ she says. Could she ever have imagined how far the brand would go? ‘I’ve always just thought, “It is going to be possible. It is going to happen, if you are dogged and determined enough.” Once I set a goal, I am going to achieve it. It’s just who I am. I want to get up to £100 million now, that is going to happen. I want it to happen before I’m 40.’ She is 39.

Perhaps it was both mother nature and mother nurture that led her to preside over one of the most successful country-cool British brands of the past two decades. Her farmer father, Oliver, convinced her couturier mother, Miranda, to swap the New York hustle and bustle for the rural calm of Suffolk. She obliged and Great Bricett Hall Farm is where Jade spent her childhood. ‘I wanted to be a farmer. I wanted to take over the farm. All I really wanted to do was to be with the horses and chickens,’ she says.

Jade’s earliest memories of fashion came from the buzzing energy of Miranda’s at-home atelier, a studio in the grounds. ‘It was couture. It was very elaborate runway pieces for the stars, and amazing appliqué work. I would open the door and see her at work, and it was like a hive of industry and colour and feathers and lamé. She was very extravagant and still is. Probably subliminally I was absorbing that.’

But there was a moment when Zara Tindall, Jodie Kidd and the Princess of Wales might never have had swathes of vivid HC tartan to don – because Jade initially embarked upon an agricultural degree. Thank goodness the arithmetic put her off. ‘I was thinking, “This is very unexciting.” So I decided to bite the bullet and leave.’

What does a hot, connected, bright young thing with deep pockets and a penchant for culture need now? In this city, you’re no one without a membership card to a young patrons’ scheme in your wallet

Her fashion story began there. ‘I’m so thankful for going on that journey and not necessarily the traditional route of London College of Fashion or Central Saint Martins, because I don’t think I would ever have gone down the route that I did. Look, it’s a tough industry. It’s very crowded and it’s very difficult. But going to Cheltenham, going to Badminton, going to these events where there were hundreds of thousands of people going, “I really can’t find anything to wear", I knew I was onto something.’

Now Holland Cooper offers womenswear, menswear, equestrian performancewear, lifestyle products and accessories. This year, she’s debuting a line of gardening items (‘trugs, aprons, gardening pouches, kneeling pads, gloves…’ she reels off the list) and she plans to expand the menswear division ‘quite dramatically. That’s going up to about 300 options.’

Remarkably, fashion is only half of the story. Jade also co-owns the nearby No.131 hotel with Julian, as well as Dunkertons Park, home to a cider brewery, boulangerie and a selection of local food traders (everything from pizza, tacos and burgers to dim sum, tandoori and crêpes), beside the new HQ.

The Marchioness of Bute has announced she is closing her eponymous fashion label, Serena Bute London. The clothes – loved by Poppy Delevingne, Rita Ora and so many more – never looked more glamorous than when they were modelled by Serena and her daughter, Lady Lola, at the family seat of Mount Stuart, the staggering four-storey Victorian Gothic fantasy on the Hebridean Isle of Bute

Jade and Julian first met at No.131 around nine years ago. ‘We are very similar as people,’ she says. ‘I think anyone that’s worked with both of us would say we’re like a carbon copy of each other. We’re both workaholics. We both have to own what we’re doing. And we know that. So, it’s divide and conquer. We should never both try and do the same thing – it’s hopeless.’

They now live at the early 19th-century Georgian mansion Dowdeswell Court. It’s reportedly worth £50 million, with eight bedrooms and multiple living rooms, and sits in 250 acres of land. But over the material luxury, it’s the couple’s commonalities that Jade is most moved by. ‘Jokes aside, it is amazing to be married to someone that you can share with,’ she says. Being a business owner ‘is quite a lonely place and quite isolating sometimes. It can be very daunting,’ she adds. ‘I don’t have a business partner, nor does he, so if you couldn’t go home and share any of that with someone, it’s very difficult for people to understand what it means – what being in a growth business means, and the pressures. We’re constantly talking about work, in multiple different guises, whether it’s fashion or hotels or food or whatever else we’re trying to build all at the same time.’

Power couple? Power parents, too. She and Julian have two children together, aged five and three. ‘There’s no bigger privilege than being a mum,’ says Jade, with a clear sense of balance. ‘Building something like this is a series of trade-offs, and the sooner you accept that, the sooner you can come to terms with it. I think the way I compartmentalise it is: when I’m at work, I’m at work, and when I’m with them, I’m completely with them,’ she explains. ‘They come and see me at the office, they’re involved. And for me, that’s really important. I want them to grow up like I did, watching their parents work really hard [and understanding that’s why] we have nice things. That’s incredibly important.’

Mogul, mother – and the monarch of a very chic crew made up of some of society’s brightest lights. The Marchioness of Bath? ‘So glamorous. I mean, the chicest woman I know, always.’ Sabrina Elba? ‘Just the warmest, kindest person I think I’ve ever met. I’ve known her from day one and she hasn’t changed at all.’ Lady Kitty Lewis (née Spencer)? ‘Gentle, kind, beautiful. So serene.’ In fact, Jade was a guest at Kitty’s ultra-glamorous multi-day wedding in Rome in 2021. And they’ve all in return attended the designer’s well-renowned soirées at home in Cheltenham. ‘We don’t entertain that much, but we’ve had a couple of really great parties,’ she says. ‘We do love a party. When you’re in the business of trying to make other people feel good, parties just come under that same remit. It’s no different really from making a good dress or a good pair of jeans, or creating a beautiful hotel room or putting together a lovely menu.’ Her 37th birthday celebration in 2023 was a Studio 54-themed extravaganza where she led her pack in a dancefloor-ready vintage cut-out design that her mother created some decades prior. She paired it with lashings of gold bling and posed for pictures beside an ice-sculpted reimagining of the Manhattan skyline.

She’s got the looks, the lifestyle and, now, the lavish new digs. Really, the HQ is the material manifestation of everything she epitomises. Desks for some 120 workers, a photography studio and a working custom atelier flanked by towering olive trees, tasteful objects and portraits of Jade herself. The brand has reach: some of her most recognisable campaigns and original design sketches have resulted in a cult-like following on social media – 664,000 Instagram followers at the time of writing. ‘Over the past two to three years particularly, my social media has become more and more integral to who we are and intrinsically linked with the business,’ she says. ‘How do I feel about it [social media]? Terrified for my children. I feel like as a society, we have to do something drastic about it. But as I sit here today, I’ll be endlessly doing stories to promote my business and if someone took it away tomorrow, it would be a real problem,’ she admits.

So, what’s next? What about… Holland Cooper caviar? ‘Maybe. I do love caviar.’ Holland Cooper champagne? ‘We’ve just done a beautiful sparkling wine. It sold out immediately.’ A Holland Cooper private jet? ‘It could be on the list. We’ve done a horsebox, we’ve done a car,’ she laughs. ‘What’s so lovely is you really can Holland Cooperise anything. And that is generally what I’m doing.’

Quite right. Jade Holland Cooper’s empire is not slowing down any time soon. Try to catch her in the fast lane: she’ll be the one in red-hot tweed tailoring behind the wheel of that heart-racingly sexy Ferrari.
