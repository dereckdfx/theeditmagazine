Royals

Queen Mary of Denmark dons her rarely seen military regalia for a glamorous palace reception

From the casual to the ultra-formal, Queen Mary of Denmark has long mastered every aspect of royal dressing

Por Isaac Bickerstaff · 28 de septiembre de 2026

Queen Mary of Denmark was in full regal regalia on Saturday, as she donned her military honours to celebrate the traditional Badge of Honour Dinner at Christian VII’s Palace in Amalienborg.

The Danish queen is usually known for her elegant daywear, bringing a modern twist to royal dressing, but this weekend’s dinner saw her wear the Danish Armed Forces’ formal dress uniform for women, which she first stepped out in back in 2019. The outfit was completed with some of Mary’s favourite jewellery – her Bruun Rasmussen earrings and Francesca bracelet – but it was surely the medals adorning her lapel that drew the most attention.

Queen Mary is the proud owner of 14 glittering medals, amassed through her participation in a number of milestone royal moments throughout her career. Ahead of her wedding to then-Crown Prince Frederik in 2004, she was named a Dame of the Royal Family Decoration of Queen Margrethe II, and went on to amass a number of decorations to mark her mother-in-law’s jubilees and birthdays, as well as the birthdays of her late father-in-law, Prince Henrik, and the couple’s anniversaries.

She has also been awarded the Home Guard Medal of Merit, the 350th Anniversary Medal of the Royal Danish Life Guards, the Reverse Officers’ Association in Denmark’s Badge of Honour, and Greenland’s Nersornaat Medal for Meritorious Service in Gold. Her husband awarded her the Dame of the Royal Family Decoration of King Frederik X upon his accession to the throne in 2024.

Both King Frederik and Queen Mary were in high spirits for the Palace reception, which saw them hand out the Badge of Honour to members of the Army who have registered 25 years of service. The medal has been awarded every year since King Christian X’s birthday on 26 September 1945.

Few royals have mastered both the ultra-formal and the dressed-down looks that come with royal engagements quite like Queen Mary. On the day she donned her full military regalia, the mother-of-four had spent the morning celebrating the 10th anniversary of her ‘Antibulli’ campaign, which she set up with the Mary Foundation to help young people find solace in sport.

Taking to the Østerbrohuset, she joined sports stars to champion the cause, which has been so close to her heart throughout her royal career. Dressed in an ‘Antibulli’ t-shirt, Queen Mary got stuck in with some sports, joining a crowd of high-spirited children waving Danish flags and practising a spot of handball.

Queen Mary has become a passionate anti-bullying advocate, using her royal platform to promote education and reform. In 2023, she delivered a speech at the World Anti-Bullying Convention in North Carolina, calling upon researchers and practitioners to celebrate the ‘uniqueness of every individual’ and our collective ‘desire to belong’.

‘Regardless of who we are or where we find ourselves in the world, we all yearn for a sense of belonging. We crave deep connections with others, as this need is innate within all of us. We all need someone who values us and someone we can value in return,’ she told the Convention. ‘This is precisely why it is so heartbreaking for a child to experience bullying. When a child is bullied, they are stripped of that vital sense of belonging and connection.’
