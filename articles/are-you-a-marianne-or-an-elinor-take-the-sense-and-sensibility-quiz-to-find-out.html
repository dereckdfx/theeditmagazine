Are you a Marianne or an Elinor? Take the ultimate Sense and Sensibility quiz to find out

In honour of Edit cover star Caitríona Balfe's upcoming role as Mrs Dashwood, Jane Austen super-fan Isolde Walters asks: which sister are you most like?

Por Isolde Walters · 25 de agosto de 2026

In Sense and Sensibility, her first published novel, Jane Austen gave us two enduring types of women. Elinor is the ‘sense’ of the title: composed, rational, tactful, with self control in abundance. She is restraint personified. But her sister, Marianne, the ‘sensibility’ of the novel, has a different approach to life. Ruled by her emotions, with a stubborn refusal to conceal her feelings, she is impetuous, impulsive and deeply romantic.

From bidding farewell to time-travel smash Outlander to playing a ‘manifesting’ Mrs Dashwood in Sense and Sensibility, actress Caitríona Balfe is a busy woman. So much so that Dora Davies-Evitt had to hunt her down on holiday at an Irish castle…

The Dashwood sisters have inspired filmmakers throughout the years. The 1995 version memorably paired Emma Thompson as the cool-headed Elinor, with a pre-Titanic Kate Winslet as Marianne to great and lasting effect. Now, three decades on, the perennially popular story is getting a 2026 update, with Daisy Edgar-Jones as Elinor and Esmé Creed-Miles as Marianne – and Edit cover star Caitríona Balfe as their mother, Mrs Dashwood.

Balfe addressed which sister she was most like in the cover interview. ‘I think I used to be a Marianne and now I’m more of an Elinor,’ she said. It’s your turn to learn if you are an Elinor, a Marianne, or a chaotic combination of the two. Take the quiz to discover which Dashwood sister you are…

You’re a fan of unbridled emotions with an almost pathological commitment to honesty. In love with love, you believe romantic communion with another soul is the highest calling in life. Interests include poetry, painting, playing the piano, and going on long life-threatening walks when in the throes of heartbreak. There’s much to praise in your approach: you wear your heart proudly - and bravely - on your sleeve and won’t let social expectations get in the way of your mission to experience life to the fullest. Other people find you charismatic and you’re never short of admirers. But heed caution: sometimes it is best to take a leaf out of your sensible sister’s book and practise restraint. Turn down the love songs every now and again and cultivate an interest in other people. Keep the open heart but practise your poker face.

Unfairly maligned as ‘the boring sister’, you are composed, practical, thoughtful, with tact in abundance. Cool as a cucumber, you are exactly the sort of person one would want around in an emergency. Nobody has a bad word to say about you and you collect friends wherever you go. Your good opinion is highly sought after and you are wise counsel. You’re a sociable type and everyone knows their secrets are more than safe with you. But, when it comes to matters of the heart, it may be time to let down the walls a little bit. There is such a thing as being too restrained, you know. That poker face may be a little too impressive; the man of your dreams might not actually realise the depth of your feeling. And you do have depth of feeling - people may not see it in your face, but you feel things as keenly as your sister, you just don’t let on. It’s time to let those emotions show - every now and again.

And if your answers were all over the place. You are, in fact, a normal person

The genius of Sense and Sensibility is that neither sister’s approach is wholly the ‘wrong’ one. Yes, Austen is probably more critical of Marianne’s emotional hyperbole but we learn there are dangers to Elinor’s cool restraint too. The ideal is clearly a mix of the two: and according to this quiz, that’s you! Some days you are Elinor, some days you are Marianne. Sometimes you zip the lip, other times you let those feelings show. Congratulations! You have achieved the Austenian balance of sense and sensibility.
