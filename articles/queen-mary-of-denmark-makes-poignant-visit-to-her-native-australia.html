Royals

Queen Mary of Denmark makes poignant visit to her native Australia for memorial service honouring her late father

Professor John Dalgleish Donaldson died in Australia on 11 April

Por Isaac Bickerstaff · 1 de octubre de 2026

Amid all the royal glamour that has come with the start of their Autumn season, King Frederik and Queen Mary of Denmark found time to pay their respects to the Queen’s late father, John Donaldson, in Australia.

The couple travelled to Queen Mary’s native Tasmania for the memorial service, which was not officially photographed, but was announced in the Danish Royal Court’s Agenda, the equivalent of the Court Circular.

Professor John Dalgleish Donaldson, who was born in East Lothian, Scotland, died in Hobart, Tasmania, on Saturday 11 April. His health had been in decline in recent years. Queen Mary announced the news in a moving statement alongside a black-and-white photo of her father.

‘My heart is heavy, and my thoughts are grey. My beloved father has passed away,’ the Queen wrote. ‘But I know that when the grief settles, the memories will brighten my day, and what will remain strongest is love and gratitude for everything he gave me and taught me.’

Though she has lived in Denmark ever since she started dating Frederik, then the Crown Prince, Mary has maintained a close relationship with her family and with Australia writ large. She was able to spend time with her father during her state visit to the continent at the end of March. The Danish royal family lowered the flags of Amalienborg Palace, Copenhagen, to half-mast as a mark of respect.

Professor John Dalgleish Donaldson was born on 5 September 1941 to Captain Peter Donaldson and his wife, Mary Dalgleish. Captain Donaldson faced a crisis at sea in 1962 when a vessel under his command was lost near Ninth Island, near the north-east coast of Tasmania. Captain Donaldson and his crew were later rescued.

In a twist of fate, the following year John Donaldson would marry Henrietta Clark and just three months later emigrate to Tasmania. It was a family affair: Captain and Mrs Donaldson also relocated, as did their other son and daughter. It was on Tasmania that John and his family built a life for themselves. He and Henrietta welcomed four children, the youngest of whom, Mary Elizabeth Donaldson, would become Queen Mary of Denmark.

A graduate of the University of Edinburgh, John Donaldson studied for his PhD in Mathematics under the direction of mathematician Professor David Elliott at the University of Tasmania. On completing his doctorate in 1967, Donaldson remained at the university and became a lecturer in applied mathematics. He also acted as visiting professor at universities in Houston, Montreal and Oxford. In a move that ties his career to his daughter's path as a royal, he was employed by the University of Copenhagen from 2006.

Professor Donaldson was catapulted onto the international stage when his youngest daughter, Mary Elizabeth Donaldson, wed the then Crown Prince Frederik of Denmark in May 2004. Dressed in a kilt, Donaldson was radiant with pride as he walked his daughter down the aisle at Copenhagen Cathedral, with the eyes of the world watching. Donaldson and his second wife, Susan, were given the honour of joining Queen Margrethe II of Denmark, her husband Prince Henrik and the newly married couple on the balcony of Christian VII's Palace.

Just three years after the wedding, the family was dealt a blow when Mary's mother, Henrietta, died. John Donaldson later married Susan Horwood in September 2001.

By virtue of her position, Queen Mary has spent decades living on the other side of the world from her family, although she has always made time for holidays – and official visits.

Their first trip was in 2005, when Mary returned home as a Danish royal. The couple joined the then Governor and First Lady at Government House in Canberra, where they watched kangaroos and chatted with the head gardener. John Donaldson has also made the return journey to Denmark on a number of occasions, including for the christening of Prince Christian of Denmark in 2005.

Queen Mary has returned home to Australia twice since her husband acceded to the throne, following the abdication of her mother-in-law, Queen Margrethe, in 2024. Both visits have been in a private capacity, with Mary flying over to visit her Australian family, including her father and her sisters, Jane Stephens and Patricia Bailey. Her most recent official trip, in March, allowed her some precious time with her family, making memories that will no doubt have been front of mind when she and King Frederik flew back for the memorial service.
