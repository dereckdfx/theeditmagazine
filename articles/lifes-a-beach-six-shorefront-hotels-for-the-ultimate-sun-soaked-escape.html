Life's a beach: six shorefront hotels for the ultimate sun-soaked escape

Swim, snorkel, sunbathe, sup, spa, stargaze… Chase the sun to enjoy the best of beachfront living

Por Edit · 7 de agosto de 2026

The Beach House Antiparos, Greece

There are many redeeming qualities to this barefoot, laid-back hangout. With one of the best beach clubs on Antiparos, its recent redesign – masterminded by Athanasia Comninos of The Rooster fame – has brought in a shot of rattan, as seen in the divine Bamboo Room, and a revamp of the bedrooms (now free of conspicuous design details, bar Smeg fridges filled with artisanal fig-leaf sodas). This is the very dream of a summer Greek-island idyll, with whitewashed walls, duck-egg-blue shutters and forests of bougainvillea. The hotel is perfectly situated on the east coast, sheltered from the Meltemi winds. You can bask in bliss, watermelon juice to hand, admiring the smattering of rocks that look as if Apollo himself had dropped them in the bay. The dining is chic and simple, and elevated by an outpost of the hip Athenian restaurant Cookoovaya. But forget any bassnote beats. Instead, lunches are fuelled by the sound of octopus sizzling over an open fire and by the chink of ice cubes melting in wines from Santorini. Dinners are early so nights can be spent lounging on the beach, watching shooting stars and the moonrise. And after a miraculous healing session with the island’s real-deal healer Lefteris (combining osteopathy, massage and reflexology), you’ll fall into a sound sleep.

Doubles from £350, including breakfast (beachhouseantiparos.com).

Minos Beach Art Hotel Crete, Greece

The vibe is encapsulated in the very name. The specialness of this delightful hotel in Mirabello Bay comes from the simplicity of beachfront life and the quirky artworks hidden among the paths of its maze-like gardens. Styled like a village of sugar-cube bungalows and villas – some with private pools and sun terraces, all decorated in an earthy palette with vast walk-in showers – the place is infused both with warm hospitality and the spirit of mythology. A ‘Minotaur’ sculpture stands sentry by the entrance and the amphitheatrical Adeste Kafenion restaurant sets the stage for open-sky feasts of meze and raki. This corner of Crete is loved for its mirror-clear waters and the seafront is the soul of the hotel. While you might want to punctuate your stay with a massage at the Ananea Wellness spa or a wine tasting at La Cave, days pass languorously on sunbeds amid the chatter of cicadas as you relax, only rising to slip into the refreshing sea between sips of herby Cretan lemonade and snacks of tangy salads and juicy shrimp skewers. Four furtherrestaurants and three bars serve local specialities in a variety of settings. Fine dining by the sea? Cocktails by a bonfire? The gloriously addictive mantra is sleep, swim, unwind, repeat.

Doubles from £220, including breakfast (minosbeach.com).

Four Seasons Resort Maui At Wailea Hawaii, USA

We won’t pretend it’s easy to get here – but the longer the journey, the greater the adventure. And as soon as you arrive, you’ll feel the uniquely Four Seasons Wailean hospitality that was forever immortalised in Series 1 of The White Lotus. So enjoy the island’s tempo and gracious service on golden sands, with attendants who will set your chaise longue and umbrella wherever you like; snorkel through turquoise waters, where you may find a turtle or two for company; or enjoy a massage in an oceanside hale. Further fantasies can be indulged at the pool, which offers panoramic views of Lanai and the West Maui Mountains from its infinity edge (best enjoyed with a pina colada). And at sunset, torch lighting sets the mood for a spectacle infused with the resort’s legendary spirit of aloha; say, hula shows and fire-knife dancers soundtracked by live music. At dinner, you can feast on the Toro rice bowl or other Japanese cuisine at KOMO; but should the impossible happen, and you tire of sashimi and seafood, amble over to Ferraro’s open-air restaurant for pastas, pizzas and crudo under the stars. Then savour a nightcap in the Lobby Lounge, creating another Maui memory that will last a lifetime, before the fun starts again the next day.

Doubles from £815 (fourseasons.com).

Milaidhoo Baa Atoll, Maldives

At Milaidhoo you can truly appreciate how those traders sailing along the ancient Spice Route would have felt when they first landed on islands in the Baa Atoll 5,000 years ago. It’s perhaps best encapsulated when dining at the Ba’theli Lounge restaurant, which celebrates the culinary legacy of those maritime merchants. The setting – on the decks of Ba’theli cargo boats stationed in the shallows – is beyond romantic, with sharks milling around the turquoise water and the Milky Way glittering above. This is one of five eateries on the island that provide a host of options, such as wood-fired pizzas at Azure or tuna burgers at the Shoreline Grill. Breakfast is another highlight, as you wander past chefs picking herbs from the kitchen garden before enjoying smoothies and scrambled eggs at the water’s edge. After breakfast, most residents retreat to their private terraces and pools, or go snorkelling along Milaidhoo’s exceptional coral reef (the dive centre takes guests to Hanifaru Bay to see its manta rays and whale sharks), while at the Serenity Spa, the sound of waves forms the backdrop for aerial yoga.

Turquoise Holidays (turquoiseholidays.co.uk) offers seven nights in a Water Villa from £5,599, half board, including flights and transfers.

Cheval Blanc St-Barth Saint Barthélemy, France

With serious savoir faire, the Cheval Blanc sits pretty on a picture-perfect north-west bay; and while it discreetly cocoons guests from the more vibrant island activities, it’s a mere 10-minute drive to Gustavia, where the well-heeled moor their yachts to shop, play and party. The only challenge you face here is leaving the maison. Everything you see, touch and hear within this glorious tropical compound is pure heaven: from the ultra-luxe interiors by Jacques Granges, famed for his work with Karl Lagerfeld and Princess Caroline of Monaco, to the level of thought you’ll find in your room at every twist and turn. (Think silver-embossed bookmarks that suddenly appear at the page you just turned down and candle-lit baths, strewn with flowers, on your return from dinner.) But aesthetics aside, it’s all about the beach – and the food. Guests can choose between La Case restaurant or the more relaxed La Cabane, where you can experience the joys of eating with your feet in the sand. At the former, chef Yann Vinsot gives tropical twists to European flavours, while La Cabane puts a local spin on such classics as shrimp croquettes with avocado lobster sauce, or fish and chips with coconut dip and chilli lemon.

Inspiring Travel (inspiringtravel.co.uk) offers seven nights from £10,225, including breakfast, flights and transfers.

Four Seasons Resort The Nam Hai Hoi An, Vietnam

This resort feels as close as you’ll get to Nirvana: walking along the sand at daybreak, chasing the waves in the warm water and gathering tiny shells along the tide line. Gazing out to a statue of the Buddhist deity Quan Yin across the bay inspires a Zen-state of happiness. Built along the contours of a former fishing village, the resort’s villas have ocean views, but most importantly, they have the exquisite Four Seasons mattresses that induce deep slumber – a peace further enhanced by the book left by your bed: A Love Letter to the Earth by the Vietnamese monk Thích Nhat Hanh. It was this sage’s teachings that inspired the spa and its programme, and the sound-healing crystal bowls – all tuned to a frequency of 432 hertz in harmony with nature – are unparalleled. The grounds are home to 4,500-plus coconut palms whose fruit husks are used to mature sherries at the Sol & Sao bar. At the omakase feast created by chef Alex Moranda in the Japanese restaurant Nayuu, wakame martinis and sake highballs may precede a sizzling wagyu ishiyaki and divine cuts of toro and tuna sashimi flown in from the Toyosu Fish Market in Tokyo.

Original Travel (originaltravel.co.uk) offers seven nights from £4,500, including breakfast, flights and transfers.
