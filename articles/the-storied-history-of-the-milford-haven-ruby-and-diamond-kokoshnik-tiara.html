A tumultuous marriage, a rumoured Vanderbilt affair and a British royal wedding: inside the storied history of the Milford Haven Ruby and Diamond Kokoshnik Tiara

Commissioned by a Russian Grand Duke doomed to live in exile, the tiara travelled with its owners to Britain, where it was gifted to a royal bride

Por Emma Samuel · 5 de octubre de 2026

The history of the Milford Haven Ruby and Diamond Kokoshnik Tiara is as glittering as it is tumultuous. Commissioned by an exiled Russian duke, passed down through generations then brought to Britain where it found its way onto the coiffured heads of some of the nation's most glamorous aristocratic brides.

The tiara was commissioned by Grand Duke Mikhail Mikhailovich, a grandson of Tsar Nicholas I of Russia, for his wife, Countess Sophie von Merenberg; a dazzling gift to mark the start of a marriage that caused irrecoverable damage to the grand Russian dynasty.

The young royal, 22, has inherited her grandfather Prince Philip's love for the sport

The troubles started when Grand Duke Mikhail announced his intentions to find a bride. He unsuccessfully pursued Princess Mary of Teck (later wife of King George V) and proposed to Princess Irene of Hesse and by Rhine, only to be rejected. Another engagement to Princess Louise, daughter of King Edward VII followed, but this was also broken off. After another doomed romance – to Countess Catherine Nikolaevna Ignatieva, opposed by his mother – Grand Duke Mikhail fell in love with Countess Sophie von Merenberg, his third cousin once removed.

Sophie was the daughter of Natalia Pushkina, the morganatic wife of Prince Nikolaus Wilhelm of Nassau, and daughter of famed Russian poet Alexander Pushkin. But this storied lineage was not enough to satisfy Grand Duke Mikhail's family. Not only was the union morganatic, the fact that he and Sophie were third cousins also made it illegal.

The couple decided to proceed with their marriage regardless. The cost? Grand Duke Mikhail was stripped of his military honours and his position at the Imperial Court – and was ordered to stay away from Russia for life.

The couple were married in Sanremo, Italy, in 1891. That same year, the Grand Duke commissioned for his bride the tiara that is today known as the Milford Haven Ruby and Diamond Kokoshnik Tiara. There are an abundance of motifs represented in this diamond and ruby tiara, including trefoils, scrolls, fleur-de-lis, crescent moons and stars, all set in gold and topped by a connecting row of ruby and diamond beads making the open setting appear kokoshnik in style. But the significance of the central octagram represents more than just decoration. It features an eight-sided star with a central heart-shaped cabochon ruby, which is designed to mimic the Russian Imperial Order of Saint Catherine, the highest dynastic honour for women. It could be seen as a protest to the exile from his homeland.

Sophie inherited her position as Colonel-in-Chief of the Corps of the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers from her beloved father-in-law, Prince Philip

Cast out from Russia, Mikhail and Sophie were given the titles Count and Countess of Torby by her uncle, Adolphe, Grand Duke of Luxembourg. The couple spent much of their time in England and became entwined with the British monarchy when their daughter, Countess Nadejda Mikhailovna de Torby, married Prince George of Battenberg, son of Louis Alexander Mountbatten, 1st Marquess of Milford Haven. George was the great-grandson of Queen Victoria and uncle to Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh. Nadejda was bequeathed the diamond and ruby tiara as a wedding gift and wore it at the coronation of King George VI in 1936.

George and Nadejda, who became the Marquess and Marchioness of Milford Haven, remained married until his death in 1938. Rumours swirled that Nadejda was in a secret romantic relationship with American socialite Gloria Morgan Vanderbilt.

The Milford Haven Ruby and Diamond Kokoshnik Tiara remained in the vault until it was worn by Penelope Thompson on her wedding to Lord Ivar Mountbatten in 1994. A decade later, the tiara was sold by Sarah, the first wife of George Mountbatten, 4th Marquess of Milford Haven. In a full-circle moment, the treasure was bought by a Slavic businessman.
