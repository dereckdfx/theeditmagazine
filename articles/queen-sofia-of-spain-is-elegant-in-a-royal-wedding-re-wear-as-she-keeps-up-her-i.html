Royals

Queen Sofia of Spain is elegant in a royal wedding re-wear as she keeps up her impressive workload despite a year of grieving

The 87-year-old royal has been mourning the loss of her sister, her close friend, and her fellow monarch

Por Isaac Bickerstaff · 22 de septiembre de 2026

Queen Sofia of Spain is turning 88 in a few weeks, but the hardworking royal is showing no signs of slowing down. It’s been a difficult year for the wife of King Juan Carlos, who is grieving the deaths of her sister, Princess Irene; her lifelong companion, Princess Tatiana Radziwill; and her friend, King Harald of Norway. All the more impressive, then, that Queen Sofia is keeping up her impressive schedule of official engagements.

This Monday, she was at the Virgen de los Reyes Centre in Seville, where she presided over the opening of the International Congress on Neurodegenerative Diseases. Queen Sofia serves as Honorary President of the organisation and lent her support in a tomato-red blazer and a pair of white trousers, completing the look with her characteristically glamorous necklaces, bracelets, and brooch. The diligent royal listened to speeches from medical experts at the launch of the ‘Extraordinary Brains’ campaign and met with researchers about their work.

Up next on Queen Sofia’s busy schedule was a visit to the Royal Alcázar of Seville, where she spent the evening at a concert highlighting the use of flamenco as a therapy for those living with neurodegenerative diseases. Beaming in the front row, Sofia pulled an ensemble from her royal archives for the concert, rewearing an Alejandro de Miguel outfit from two years ago, when she joined the Spanish royal family at the pre-wedding celebrations for her niece, Princess Theodora of Greece.

And the engagements don’t stop there. On Thursday, Queen Sofia will fly to New Orleans, where she will take part in the third ‘America & Spain 250 Symposium’, exploring ‘Spain and the American Revolution along the Mississippi River’ – a joint endeavour between the Queen Sofia Spanish Institute and the Ramón Areces Foundation. Nor is this the first time that the royal has flown stateside this year: in March, just weeks after the death of her sister Irene, Queen Sofia was in Miami, representing her eponymous institute on the 250th anniversary of the signing of the Declaration of Independence.

Having spent the first months of the year in mourning, the royal added more and more colour to her wardrobe, culminating in an evening of grandiose glamour at King Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden’s 80th birthday gala in Stockholm. Queen Sofia cut an elegant figure in a long-sleeved duck-egg blue gown with just a touch of sparkle as she arrived at the Royal Palace for the evening extravaganza, where she joined the likes of King Philippe and Queen Mathilde of Belgium, Grand Duke Guillaume and Grand Duchess Stephanie of Luxembourg and King Frederik and Queen Mary of Denmark.

Queen Sofia of Spain dazzled in a diamond and pearl creation from the Spanish royal collection: the Mellerio Shell Tiara, which is also a favourite of her daughter-in-law, Queen Letizia of Spain.

The pearl and diamond piece traces its history back to Queen Isabella II of Spain, the last Spanish queen to rule in her own right, who commissioned the piece in 1867 as a wedding present to her daughter, Infanta Isabella, who married Prince Gaetan, Count of Girgenti, the following year.

The tiara takes its name from the jeweller, Mellerio dits Meller, a French jewellery house founded in 1613. The Mellerios were responsible for a number of other pieces owned by Europe's royal houses, including the Netherlands' Mellerio Ruby Tiara. This particular design features a series of diamond fans, each set with an individual pearl, which are set alongside each other to create the shape of a shell. Others have compared the shape to a cresting wave (which was also the inspiration for Princess Charlene of Monaco's wedding tiara).

The Mellerio Shell Tiara was passed down through the family and later worn by Queen Victoria Eugenie of Spain, Queen Victoria's granddaughter who survived dynastic disease, political exile and an assassination attempt. Queen Ena, as she was better known, loaned the tiara to her daughter-in-law, the Countess of Barcelona, according to royal jewellery authority The Royal Watcher. It came into the possession of Queen Sofia of Spain, then Princess Sophia of Greece and Denmark, on her wedding to Juan Carlos in 1962. The bride-to-be made her debut in the tiara at her wedding ball, and it has become a favourite choice for formal events. The tiara has also been worn by her daughters, Infanta Christina of Spain and Infanta Elena, Duchess of Lugo.

It is a reminder that, in a year Queen Sofia is surely looking to put behind her, there were still moments to celebrate, reunite with old friends, and dazzle as only a royal can.
