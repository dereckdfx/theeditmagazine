Pop stars, poodles and plenty of Mancunian pride: what went down when the BRITs arrived in Manchester?

The former 19th century Cottonopolis gets a healthy dose of cool thanks to a rip-roaring bash in the heart of the red brick city….

Por Chandler Tregaskes · 2 de marzo de 2026

Manchester might just be the coolest place to be right now… Ever since the former Chanel creative director Virginie Viard bought the famed French Maison over to the trendy Northern Quarter of the city to present her 2024 Metiers D’Art collection with Charlotte Casiraghi, Kristen Stewart and Tilda Swinton, the 19th century ‘Cottonopolis’ has become a coveted alternative to the hustle and bustle of the capital; swapping the sometimes monotonous grey of winding London roads with the red brick streets of the Warehouse City. This weekend, there was another reason for a Northern rendezvous: the first BRIT Awards hosted outside of The Big Smoke since inception back in 1977. And it was a party.

Perhaps it should come as no surprise. The stars of the lovingly-dubbed ‘Madchester’ music movement include legendary acts like the Happy Mondays, the Stone Roses and New Order. Then there is the birth of the beat-laden acid house scene at the fabled Hacienda club in the 1990s. This weekend, the streets were lined with a palpable buzz of excitement ahead of the event just waiting to celebrate all of the music world-glory that hails from this area, with crossing lights reshaped into BRITs logos, and even the busy Deansgate station renamed ‘Olivia Deansgate’ for one weekend only.
