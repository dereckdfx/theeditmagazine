Royals

The Duchess of Edinburgh is a vision in Suzannah London florals as she supports one of her most treasured charities

Sophie got straight back to work after her six-day tour of Zambia

Por Isaac Bickerstaff · 24 de septiembre de 2026

The Duchess of Edinburgh took a dive through the royal fashion archives on 23 September, as she got straight back to work following her tour of Zambia with a reception celebrating the 80th anniversary of the Royal Mencap Society.

Sophie, who inherited patronage of the Society following the death of former Royal Patron, the Queen Mother, in 2002, wore one of her favourite outfits for the occasion. A vision in florals, the Duchess re-wore the Suzannah London ‘grandad’ dress, previously seen when she supported King Charles at the King’s Award for Enterprise ceremony in Windsor Castle last year. Sophie completed her look on Wednesday with her Strathberry mosaic nano bag and her trusted espadrilles from Penelope Chilvers.

The Duchess of Edinburgh was in high spirits as she joined patients, families and caregivers at the event. She was delighted to receive a chocolate lollipop in the shape of her face from The Traitors finalist Alexander Dragonetti, and she enjoyed a warm reunion with Strictly Come Dancing star Ellie Goldstein, who performed for the Duchess at St James’ Palace earlier this year.

In a conversation with guest of honour Geoffrey Leer, 80, the Duchess of Edinburgh spoke of why she is so proud to support the charity. ‘What makes Mencap special is meeting lovely people like you. Things have changed a lot during your lifetime for people with a learning disability, which is good. They have a voice and opinions, and that’s being listened to,’ Sophie said.

‘The next challenge is to have more opportunities for people in workplaces. People with a learning disability are life enhancers. A lot more people are taking interest in giving people with a learning disability opportunities, but there’s a long way to travel.’

Sophie’s partnership with Mencap has been one of the defining causes of her royal career. When the charity was announced as the official partner of the London Marathon in 2020, the Duchess joined their team runner, Tomas Cardiollo-Zallo, for the first 1.5 miles of his Marathon race to help raise awareness for the 1.5 million people living with a learning disability in Great Britain.

In 2022, she presented the first-ever Commonwealth Games medal for wheelchair basketball in her role as patron of British Wheelchair Basketball. Her husband, Prince Edward, has served as patron of the British Paralympic Association for over two decades, and the pair flew to Italy earlier this summer to meet with athletes at the 2026 Paralympic Winter Games.

More widely, the Duchess of Edinburgh has long been a champion and advocate for those living with disabilities. This July, Sophie reached out to Chloe, a 17-year-old girl with cerebral palsy, to wish her luck on a cycling charity ride from Land’s End to John O’Groats to raise money for PALS and Treloar’s, two charities that support children with disabilities. In a break from the traditional forms of royal communications, the Duchess sent Chloe a video message, in which she sent her ‘warmest wishes’ and reflected on her own experience cycling 450 miles from Holyroodhouse in Edinburgh to Buckingham Palace in 2016.

‘This is an enormous feat of endurance, and having done my own cycle challenge 10 years ago, I am so proud of you,’ she said. ‘It takes a lot of effort. I’ll be thinking of you as you set off on the 26th July, and I look forward to you telling me all about it when we meet again. Take care, and good luck.’

In the caption to the video, Chloe’s mother shared just how important the Duchess’s message had been for the family: ‘A wonderful message from Her Royal Highness the Duchess of Edinburgh. We are so proud to have received this. Chloe and I wrote to her about the ride, and she wowed us with a personal message.’

Continuing her busy schedule on Wednesday evening, the Duchess of Edinburgh wore a Roksanda Luna dress to attend a reception in honour of the Women in Business Group of the Duke of Edinburgh Award Scheme. The Group was founded by Sophie herself in 2003 to help young women develop skills in the workplace. A characteristically jam-packed afternoon for the Duchess of Edinburgh, but one that allowed the ever-glamorous royal to support some of the causes closest to her heart.
