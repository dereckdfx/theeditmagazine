Meet Henry Pollock, rugby's golden boy with charm, ambition and invitations to spare, as he graces the cover of Edit 's latest issue

Good looks, great tackles: rugby star Henry Pollock is sport and society's latest pin-up. In the December issue, he shares top spot on Edit 's Little Black Book with Valentina Pinault. Dora Davies-Evitt meets the man of the moment

Por Dora Davies-Evitt · 23 de octubre de 2026

At 6ft 2in and built for the business of running into other similarly built men at speed, Henry Pollock is not, at first glance, a delicate proposition. He is, however, remarkably pretty: almost an English rose, with pink cheeks, full lips and clear blue eyes that catch the Northamptonshire sunlight as he settles into a box at the Saints’ DPD stadium. Below lies the pitch – the stretch of turf that doubles as Pollock’s office on match days.

There is a certain amount of pressure on Pollock at the moment. At the start of the 2024-25 season, he had barely played senior Premiership rugby, the highest level of the club game in England. By May 2025, aged 20, he had made his England debut, scoring two tries in his 32 minutes on the pitch and becoming England’s youngest-ever Six Nations try-scorer. He had also helped his club, Northampton Saints, reach the Champions Cup final and been selected for the British & Irish Lions.

He has since reportedly nearly tripled his club salary by signing a new deal with Saints. With records already broken, a sense of humour and 325,000 Instagram followers, there is a growing feeling that this particular back-row forward might just be the man to inject a little rock ’n’ roll into rugby. Little wonder he is dominating the sports pages.

Today, sitting in one of the stadium’s green fold-down seats, he wears an Arc’teryx hat – a staple of the Gen Z wardrobe – pulled over freshly plaited cornrows. David Beckham, circa 2003, springs to mind. Could Pollock be rugby’s answer to Becks? It certainly feels possible. Pollock does not merely tackle, ruck and score. He has unorthodox hairstyles, has a decent wardrobe and boasts both a burgeoning social-media presence and considerable commercial appeal. In March this year, he signed with Eddie Hearn’s Matchroom Talent Agency, marking the sports promoter’s first foray into rugby union. Hearn, whose boxing roster includes Anthony Joshua, declared that Pollock could ‘single-handedly ignite’ the sport. Big deals surely beckon. His dream fashion collaboration? ‘Burberry,’ he answers without missing a beat. He is already being recognised in the street, though Hearn has insisted that fame will not go to his client’s head. Today, Pollock seems grounded enough, describing the surreal experience with amusement. ‘They just walk past and stare at me. It’s funny.’

There are commercial shoots, a new YouTube channel and trips to fashion parties in London. His daily screen time over the summer peaked at just two hours: there’s little room for scrolling when you are this in demand. Rugby’s newest pin-up, it seems, is not content simply to be good at rugby. Yet, asked what people tend to misunderstand about him, he says, almost convincingly: ‘I’m actually a normal 21-year-old.’ His soft RP accent isn’t quite cut-glass, but betrays his boarding-school roots.

Normal? Sure. His idea of a relaxing Saturday is breakfast with the boys, followed by padel, tennis, golf, recovery and a film. On days on, he wakes at 6.45am for a session under red light, goes to the club for breakfast and meetings, does a gym session, more meetings and two or three hours of rugby before heading home to recover, then does it all again. ‘Eat, sleep, repeat,’ he says.

His life is a world away from university campuses and student nights out. Most of his contemporaries from Stowe, where he spent five years, are now scattered across Russell Group universities. He makes an effort to visit – undoubtedly elevating his friends to BNOC (big name on campus) status in the process. Where would he have studied if rugby superstardom had not intervened? ‘Edinburgh,’ he says, without hesitation. ‘Love it there.’

Pollock grew up in Northamptonshire, attending Beachborough School before joining Stowe as a sports scholar. He played plenty of sports, but rugby gradually won out. ‘I wasn’t the best,’ he admits modestly. ‘Then, as I got older, it became something I loved to do, and then I got better at it…’ Quite the understatement. Saints had been watching him. Scouted while at Stowe, he came through the club’s academy system and eventually signed a professional contract. He has just extended it in a lucrative new deal, with Hearn in his corner. In typically brazen style, the promoter has argued that Pollock ought to be earning £1 million a year at Saints; the agreed figure is reportedly around £400,000.

‘We knew that where I want to be in my career and as a player, the [Saints] was the best place for me,’ says Pollock. But, like any ambitious young sportsman with a savvy management team, he is also making hay off the pitch. Beneath his YouTube vlog ‘I’m Staying at Northampton Saints!’ – now watched more than 150,000 times – fans have been quick to applaud his decision. One comment reads: ‘Pollock signs for Saints for money the game can afford, and then subsidises his salary by doing bts [behind the scenes] work! What a way to raise the profile of the game, when it’s so desperately needed!’

It helps that the squad is young. Pollock lives in Northampton with a teammate. There is not, he admits, an enormous amount to do in the town centre. Fortunately, there are plenty of rugby players to keep him company. His best friends at the club are 25-year-old Tommy Freeman, who plays on the wing, at centre or at full-back, and the 24-year-old fly-half Fin Smith.

London provides the escape hatch. Roughly every fortnight, Pollock heads to the capital to see friends and his siblings: his sister lives in Angel, his brother in Battersea. But where exactly does a handsome, internationally recognised sports star take a date? ‘Soho House,’ he says. ‘Yeah, somewhere easy like that.’ It’s an intriguing choice, considering he isn’t actually a member. Presumably, his dates usually are.

Pollock is no rugger-bugger ‘meathead’, though, and seems a sensitive soul at heart. He plays acoustic guitar in his basement, a deliberately phone-free room, where he can disappear for a while. His repertoire includes Ed Sheeran, Justin Bieber and Oasis, but he will only serenade a date with ‘Wonderwall’ if they are ‘very special’, he says, feigning a blush as he looks out towards the stands. He can sing, too. At a recent karaoke night, he contemplated performing a Bad Bunny song in Spanish. He had learnt the lyrics but ultimately ‘didn’t quite have the balls’ to go through with it. It helps that he speaks Spanish: his family have a house in Menorca, where he has holidayed all his life and spent five weeks this summer.

Perhaps even learning a language is fuelled by his competitive streak. Pollock says he is competitive at ‘whatever I do’ and insists he rarely loses. It comes, he reckons, from growing up as the youngest of three in a wildly sporty family.

The apple certainly doesn’t fall far from the tree. His mother, Hester, is a triathlete who has competed at national level, while his older sister, Zoe, 25, is a 400m hurdler who competed for the University of Georgia and came close to qualifying for the Paris Olympics. His brother, Angus, 23, is a scratch golfer. His father, John, who works for advertising firm Link, has been a steadfast supporter, driving Henry long distances to matches and travelling internationally to watch him compete. Pollock credits him with giving him the freedom and encouragement to pursue his sporting dreams.

His ambitions extend well beyond the rugby pitch. He would like to run an ultramarathon. He would like to take on an Ironman challenge. Rugby, for all its physical demands, has clearly not exhausted his appetite for punishment… And there is a rather more serious side to this physical confidence. Like many a young professional rugby player, he is well aware of the sport’s long-term risks. Dwelling on them though, he says, would stop him committing fully to the game. His approach is pragmatic: look after his body, be honest about how it feels and trust that advances in technology will help.

That willingness to concentrate on what is immediately in front of him may explain why the prospect of the 2027 Rugby World Cup, hosted by seven cities across Australia, has not sent him into a frenzy. It is there, ‘in the back of your mind’, he says, but his priority is to perform week in, week out for Northampton and England. The aim is to peak at the World Cup. Everything he does now, he says, is about getting there.

Within the England set-up, he is still one of the younger players. He describes senior figures including Maro Itoje, Ellis Genge and Jamie George as big brothers, valuing not only their rugby experience but the fact that they are ‘amazing people’.

Pollock may still only be 21, but there is little sense of panic about what comes next for him. He knows his sporting career will be short, that the attention on him is increasing and that rugby cannot be the only thing in his life. For now, though, this is where he wants to be. What would his 15-year-old self make of his high-profile status today? ‘He’d probably say that I’m living the dream,’ he says simply, shrugging those very, very broad shoulders. For all the fanfare and enthusiasm for a scrum, there is a charming modesty about him. If Pollock is the catch of the day consider me caught.
