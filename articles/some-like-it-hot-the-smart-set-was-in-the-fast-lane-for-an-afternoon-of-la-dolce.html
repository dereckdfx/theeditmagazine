Some like it hot! The smart set was in the fast lane for an afternoon of La Dolce Vita dressing at Goodwood Revival

Beehives, Bentleys, and Back to the Future: this time last week, the Duke of Richmond welcomed A-listers and petrolheads alike to the annual celebration of all things vintage cars. Sarah Edworthy got the inside scoop from the paddock

Por Sarah Edworthy · 25 de septiembre de 2026

What event could possibly bring together Golda Rosheuvel, the imperiously stylish Queen Charlotte in Bridgerton; former Formula One world champions Jenson Button and Jacques Villeneuve; fashion legend Dame Zandra Rhodes in full ‘pinkissimo’ bobbed splendour; and Harlem jazz bandleader Dandy Wellington? Add in a dawn flypast of World War II fighter planes, a swarm of 300 vintage Vespas in candy colours and acres of fabulously tailored tweed. The answer is, of course, the Goodwood Revival Meeting presented by Dunlop on the Duke of Richmond’s estate in West Sussex.

I say ‘of course’ because what began 28 years ago as a festival of historic motorsport celebrated in authentic period dress has evolved into a society fixture of full-throttle glamour. This year, the theme was La Dolce Vita: the bunting came out to mark 100 years of Maserati’s racing heritage, 60 years since Ford GT’s victory at Le Mans, and 80 years of the aforementioned Italian scooter.

Entering this timewarp was like walking on to a sprawling, live film set (last weekend’s attendees rubbed tailored shoulders with the likes of Hugh Bonneville, David Harewood and Hannah Waddingham). There’s the buzz and whirr of aeroplanes and helicopters overhead, the drone of cars tearing down the Lavant Straight and the unmistakable whiff of combusting race fuel in the air. Everything presents a perfect performative cameo, from the Duke’s VIP guests being chauffeured to the Old Control Tower in stately classic Bentleys to race marshals blowing their whistles in white cotton boiler suits that replicate the styling from 1948-1966, the motor circuit’s original active era.

The Best Dressed Competition presented by Ancestry has grown to have as much prominence as the races contested around the 2.4-mile circuit that underpin the three-day event. In the Revival Style village, Betty’s Salon, a sassily reimagined 1950s beauty parlour, was open for all to have their hair pinned into 1940s Victory Rolls, 1950s barrel curls or 1960s beehives. Long queues formed at the Ancestry Portrait Studio where one of Edit’s own Bystander photographers Dan Stevens captured visitors posing to flaunt their sartorial flair.

‘We set up a white background with vintage lights as props and I tell people to imagine they’re actors being photographed during a break in filming Some Like It Hot at the back of a Hollywood studio,’ he said. ‘The same people come back year after year. Clearly a lot of thought goes into their outfits. The Meeting has benefitted from the current philosophy of eschewing fast fashion and valuing beautifully made vintage pieces.’

The paddocks are always the beating heart of the three-day fancy-dress parade. Here, the line-up of entrants – lovingly preserved Maseratis, Ferraris, Jaguars, Aston Martins and 1960s Cooper-Climaxes – are polished and preened by mechanics. In such a fashion-conscious environment, one can’t but see each car’s progress to the race assembly area (cue a chorus of throaty revs) before their pre-race lap as a mirror of the dynamic in the Revival Style Village where models lined up to parade in La Doll-ce Vita fashion show.

With a tunnel under the track linking the two hubs of action, it was perfectly possible in the space of 30 minutes to marvel at a 1938 Lagonda V12 Le Mans (looking like Chitty Chitty Bang Bang from the front but with a socking great 4.5-litre V12 engine at the rear) and take your seat for a talk about Givenchy’s on- and off-screen collaboration with Audrey Hepburn. Style, line, silhouette, period-defining accessories: these were the buzzwords.

For the record, the St Mary’s Trophy – a thrilling two-part race featuring Jaguar MK2s, Ford-Lotus Cortinas and Alfa Romeo Giulia GTAs – attracted the starriest line-up of drivers including David Brabham, Jenson Button, Karun Chandhok, António Félix da Costa, Dario Franchitti, Jimmie Johnson, André Lotterer, Andy Priaulx and Gordon Shedden. And the Barry Sheene Memorial event saw riders run across the track to jump on their waiting, high-revving vintage bikes in a Le Mans-style start.

The cutest spectacle on the track once raced on by such greats as Juan Manuel Fangio, Jim Clark, Stirling Moss, John Surtees and Bruce McLaren had to be the young drivers, aged four to 11, who pedalled their Austin J40s in the Settrington Cup presented by Ralph Lauren. Back to the future indeed!
