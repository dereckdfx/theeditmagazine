Six hotels that make even hectic family holidays feel seriously chic

My family and other animals: the mini-VIP delighting fantasies shot through with grown-up refinement

Por Edit · 29 de julio de 2026

Hotel Santa Caterina Amalfi, Italy

The intention of this hotel's owners, the fourth-generation Gambardellas, is that this authentico Amalfi jet-set playground feels like your home. And it’s a place where you see the Italian joy of caring for la famiglia in all its glory – partly because the staff, despite their white-jacketed finesse, are just utterly charming as you bumble from your room to the pool or beach, via the terrace for a cocktail or ice-cream moment. The interiors are furnished with squishy sofas, billowing white sheer curtains and shelves that groan with with an eccentric mix of holiday novels. So why not pick up a book at whim and find a spot to unwind in the romantically overgrown gardens? Meanwhile, there are a few culinary experiences that can rival dining under the sun-baked bamboo ceiling of the beachside restaurant Al Mare. Every night it feels like a soulful Dean Martin song has come to life – even before you’ve tasted the divine lemon spaghetti. Doubles from £388, including breakfast (hotelsantacaterina.it).

Giraffe Manor Nairobi, Kenya

Passing through the gates, you enter a world of storybook wonder. The hotel itself – a picturesque, creeping-fig-clad building designed in the 1930s to resemble a Scottish hunting lodge – is idyllic. But its situation on 12 acres of private grounds is truly the stuff of dreams for children and grown-ups alike. Here, giraffes and warthogs potter nonchalantly through the garden; and when you wake after a night in your four-poster – all very Out of Africa – you’re likely to find Kelly or Jock, two of the friendlier giraffes, poking their heads through your window to say hello. As for the staff, no request is too outlandish; no detail goes overlooked; with the kitchen happily taking off-menu requests for whatever children want, be that pasta pomodoro or a scrumptious burger and fries. And though you feel like you’re staying in a private country home, you’re afforded all the luxury of a five-star hotel, including a gym, an excellent spa and a lap pool with sweeping views over the neighbouring forest. Best of all is daily afternoon tea on the front lawn: a feast of freshly baked cakes, scones and finger sandwiches, all served on fabulously chintzy fine china. Double from £1,052, full board (thesafaricollection.com).

Island Shangri-La Hong Kong, China

An entire floor devoted to families? Such obvious genius: children find instant playmates, while parents trade tips and talk itineraries over tea. A lift whizzes up 45 floors, opening to reveal a toy train racing around the ceiling. The 21 suites are identified by animal motifs, so even a three-year-old can find their own quarters; and inside, beds are tucked into airships, boats and Cinderella carriages, with slides, canopies and child-sized doors. Meanwhile, UV torches reveal hidden pictures of sea creatures or woodland animals on the walls, and windows frame giddy views of Hong Kong's cityscape and The Peak. (Little princesses love the Fairy Garden Suite, complete with a magic wand to control the lights and a chic second bedroom for parents). Elsewhere, the Hangout is part-salon, part-playroom, with healthy snacks and ready-to-heat penne marinara to hand, as well as an ice-cream cart. And a utility room – stocked with bottle warmers, car seats and prams – relieves the burden of travelling with infant paraphernalia. So clever. The challenge comes when it’s time to check out. Family rooms from £464 (Shangri-la.com).

Eagles Resort Halkidiki, Greece

At Eagles Resort on the Halkidiki peninsula life slows to the rhythm of the sea and cicada, while Mount Athos shimmers on the horizon. The family-run hideaway, which comprises the Eagles Palace Hotel and, across a jasmine-draped footbridge, Eagles Villas, blends authentic away-from-it-al hospitality with relaxed luxury – and for parents, that starts with ushering their offspring (from four months old to age 11) to the Explorers Kids Club. Inside, children flit from cooking to paining to play; outside, they garden, try water sports and visit the resort farm; and the exceptional staff regularly send WhatsApp updates, so parents can relax over languid lunches at Lofos restaurant, gazing at views across the bay, or drift between the Elemis spa and the private beach. Childcare extends to nighttime, too, with the stargazers programme enabling adults to enjoy a cocktail and child-free evening. Scott Dunn (scottdunn.com) offers seven nights from £4,850 in a Junior Suite for one adult with one child, including breakfast, flights, transfers and a space in the Scott Dunn Explorers Kids Club.

The Peligoni Club Zakynthos, Greece

Dispelling the myth that taking teenagers on holiday never quite works, the Peligoni has turned it into an artform. The 70-plus luxury villas, located within 15 minutes’ drive from the club, give families the freedom to divide time between their own space and the club itself; but the latter is where the magic happens. It has the welcome fuss-free feel of barefoot practicality, however it’s bang on-point when it comes to style details: from chic glassware to the Land Rover fleet that shuttles you to and from the villa. Happy youngsters lie at the heart of this place’s phenomenal success. An endless array of age-specific nautical activities, along with tennis, football and even dance workshops, keep things rolling by day, while by night a fun mix of entertainment options are expertly curated so that parents can party alongside their offspring. Beach barbecues and courtyard dinners – think tapas and smashing plates, followed by dancing – all bring the generations together. A week’s stay from £810; membership starts from £375 per adult; £250 per child (peligoni.com).

Sani Asterias, Halkidiki, Greece

Fresh from a recent makeover, Sani Asterias lies in an enviable spot between a powder-soft stretch of white sand and the sun-drenched marina. Suites are spacious and oh-so-tastefully designed. There are private-pool options too, adding an extra layer of seclusion for those seeking a little more privacy. For families, the scale of activities available is impressive. The Rafa Nadal Tennis Centre is a major draw, but Sani also boasts football academies, archery, BMX tracks, zip lines, climbing walls, yoga classes and boating excursions to ensure there is something for every age and energy level. There is even a Mermaid Academy for any budding Ariels… Separately, the Kids Club arranges an enviable range of activities: treasure hunts, cooking sessions and a packed programme of adventures. How to spend the child-free time? One option is the spa. Set within peaceful gardens, it offers facials using Anne Semonin products alongside a complete menu of body treatments. Dining is another standout. With dozens of restaurants across the resort, there is something to satisfy gourmands and fussy-eaters alike. Water – the recently reopened restaurant by two-Michelin-starred chef Bruno Oger – is a must-try. Evenings bring yet more choices and entertainment. Live music spills across the marina, DJs spin the hottest beats whilst you sip banging martinis, and the resort's outdoor theatre hosts nightly family-friendly performances. A little planning ahead is definitely worthwhile, especially during peak season. Popular restaurants and activities fill quickly, so downloading the Sani app and making reservations before arrival is highly recommended. www.sani-resort.com/our-hotels/sani-asterias
