Tramp Health: the next chapter for this one-time bastion of legendary hedonism? Wellness

The sybaritic Mayfair nightspot now has a sumptuous wellness club on Grosvenor Street. Astrid Joss is first on the guestlist

Por Astrid Joss · 5 de junio de 2026

It was in December 1969 that Johnny Gold opened his exclusive, members-only nightclub on Jermyn Street, in the ultrasmart district of St James’s. The name? Tramp. Built on reputation and discretion, it was dreamt up as an alternative to the city’s then popular supper clubs. Photography was banned, as were gossip writers, in order to protect its 300 celebrity founding members. What Gold and his fellow founders Bill Ofner and Oscar Lerman had hoped would be popular for a few years at best turned into a phenomenon so much bigger than anyone imagined.

Tramp established itself as the playground of the rich and famous, hosting everyone over the decades from Frank Sinatra to Kate Moss. It was here that Michael Caine met his wife, Shakira; that Roger Moore helped mop the floor after a flood; and that socialite Tara Palmer-Tomkinson attended her 21st birthday party wearing nothing but a bikini and fur coat.

When The Who’s drummer, Keith Moon, was barred for destroying a chandelier, Gold reduced the ban to 48 hours after a desperate Moon sent his chauffeur to the club with £500 in cash, phoning Gold in tears, since there was nowhere else in town to party.

Today, while other clubs open and close on repeat, Tramp continues its starry ascent under the new(ish) mastership of suave Italian-about-town Luca Maggiora. Maggiora’s modern vision for the brand is nothing short of genius – to such an extent that he’s now opening Tramp Health.

Health? Wellness? For Tramp, that bastion of legendary hedonism? Well, yes.

‘What’s ironic is I never liked nightclubs,’ says Maggiora, who decided to take a sabbatical from banking in 2007 when a friend opened up Maddox Club in Mayfair and asked him to help. ‘Bankers and nightclubs back then were a match made in heaven. The lack of social media as we know it meant you really had to know someone to get in, making night clubs almost like members’ clubs themselves. I found myself getting calls from everyone.’

The die was cast, with Maggiora quickly realising that he excelled at dealing with operations, leadership, people and detail – crucial ingredients that saw him become a master operator and club owner on the London luxury hospitality scene, going on to open an impressive 15 nightclubs, restaurants and lounges, largely in Mayfair, over the course of 17 years, with successes including The Cuckoo Club, Luxx and Bardo St James’s.

In the run-up to 2020, Maggiora sensed a cultural shift in the capital that directly impacted his industry. ‘Three things happened. London went from a 12- to a six-month city – as soon as private schools were on holiday, people would disappear. Secondly, restaurants and clubs previously owned and run by families and single operators like myself were being bought out [by big corporations] whose level of wealth you couldn’t compete with. Finally, the way people were having fun changed. People stopped spending on alcohol. The £2,000 tequila bottles that would arrive at tables with sparklers and had served as every nightclub’s business model for years – that was coming to an end. These had once been places to show off, and now people could do this on social media. Clubs suddenly weren’t cool any more.’ For Maggiora, a self-professed ‘sociologist of going out’, clubbing as we knew it was over.

The pandemic provided the way ahead. Selling all his properties except for Bardo (which he still owns), Maggiora stepped back to inhale his sense of the future. What he exhaled was a vision that took on board everything he had learnt over the past two decades, combined with an understanding of the direction in which London was moving. ‘I always knew that real life was daytime,’ he says, acknowledging the serious shift into wellness that shows no sign of tailing off. As he reflected on his own powerful network, the penny dropped. He wanted to build a legendary health club.

In order to move forward, he decided he needed to own a heritage brand name. ‘If I opened a club and called it “Luca”, who would come?’ he says, laughing. ‘I looked at major fashion brands in distress and saw how the likes of Remo Ruffini had resurrected Moncler into the success it is today.’

For Maggiora, the solution lay in Tramp, last bought in 1998. He messaged the owner, Scottish businessman Kevin Doyle, weekly for two years. (‘I didn’t even know if I had the right email!’)

When Doyle did finally reply, Maggiora had just landed in Tuscany. ‘I never left the airport,’ he says. ‘I was on the next flight back to London and met the owner at 10am the following morning. He gave me 30 minutes, with five conditions and 30 days to decide.’ A deal was struck. Maggiora received the keys in October 2023 and closed the 54-year-old club the very same day. What he did next nobody saw coming.

‘The vision I had was clear from day one – I was going to take the business from night to day, as I knew the full human experience is connected by unforgettable nights and mindfulness and clarity by day. I was going to link the two.’ And so Maggiora conceived Tramp Health – which, as he points out, he knew people would find odd at first, until they understood his vision in full. ‘I was wrong to think people no longer wanted to have fun – they just wanted to have fun with the right crowd,’ he says.

And so he vowed to bring back the golden era of clubs like Tramp and Annabel’s – he just ‘needed the right group to do it with’. It wasn’t something that could be rushed. ‘This was a four-year project that I couldn’t mess up, but I realised the one thing I knew I could do as an outstanding operator was to build a club nobody could replicate, by creating a community and a true ecosystem in the best part of London.’ This was only achievable by meeting every member individually. He says over and over again: ‘Don’t make it about money.’

Tramp remained shut for a year while Maggiora cancelled every membership and set about meeting his future clique one by one. The reopening in September 2024, following a £12 million refurbishment, was a huge success, with 87 percent of its members renewing after the first year. What’s more, its 300 founder members truly represent London’s modern day elite. The capital’s OG private members’ club was back.

The final piece of the puzzle fell into place in 2025, on securing 16,000 square feet of real estate at 3 Upper Grosvenor Street, a corner of the Qatari-owned Grosvenor Square building (formerly the American Embassy, now home also to The Chancery Rosewood hotel). Tramp Health had lift-off. With a pre-existing membership base eager to join part two of the journey, a five-month race was on to build (in time for the promised May opening) a super-slick private wellness and longevity community worthy of both their high standards and Maggiora’s: ‘I know every single member, so I asked them all what they think it should look like, feel like and even cost. As I told them, “I’m building it for you and I don’t do anything without you.” No matter who you are, members want to be seen and heard and to believe that their feedback genuinely matters. And no matter how rich or poor you are, nobody wants to feel like a number.’

At the heart of the club are its nine pillars of wellness – nutrition, prevention, recovery, data and diagnostics, mindfulness, mental wellbeing, beauty, strength and community – along with ‘spaces and experiences’. Tramp Health is a place where science, culture and community coincide at the very highest levels. ‘Ultimately,’ says Maggiora, ‘people want to make friends and to network. My job is to provide fun and connect them, but with a focus on supporting how you live, move and feel.’

Spaces for community blend in with movement and restoration areas. At the club’s entrance is a chic, sun-drenched, south-facing terraced café designed in the house colours of green and pink, complete with cashmere throws and flower-adorned tables. You sense a spirit of belonging in style before you’ve even stepped in.

This is only the beginning – the interior setting is a thing of beauty, inspired by nature, with earthy, restorative tones and textures in artful contrast to a 1970s glamour that draws on the Jermyn Street club’s origins. Oval stone baths, surrounded by plants, are dotted here and there; hand-painted floral designs climb the walls of the treatment rooms; and Italian-wood flooring runs throughout, teamed with clever lighting – all coming together to celebrate the art of living. Alongside the wellness café is a vast gym, HIIT studios and a spa, where members can sauna, steam and cold plunge for recovery after a workout or personal training session. There are quiet alcoves to recharge in; a further mindfulness studio offers meditation, breath work and sound therapy; and there’s a studio for reformer pilates and strength and conditioning, too.

Classes and programmes range from yin yoga, reiki sessions and advanced meditation practice to gong baths, energy alignment and full-moon gatherings. Among the membership options on offer are unlimited gym and spa access, pilates, fitness, mindfulness and hot yoga, along with 10 complimentary guest passes a year.

And what’s a serious health joint without its experts? Maggiora has assembled an impressive team. The medical director, Dr Mark Mikhail, is a leading expert in preventative and regenerative medicine. Specialising in identifying early markers of imbalance, creating data-driven health strategies and offering advanced health diagnostics, he is all about preventative rather than reactive measures.

Meanwhile, nutritionist and author Eve Kalinik, who specialises in gut health, hormone imbalance and metabolic optimisation, oversees members’ nutritional needs while steering the wellness café. Fancy topping up your Botox after a mindfulness session with Stephanie Reynolds? Skin specialist Libi Roos is on hand for beauty and aesthetic treatments. To keep up with all the options, members are supported by Tramp Health Concierge, an app that manages appointments and personal details.

What does it take to impress Maggiora enough to be granted the golden pass? ‘I meet on average 30 new people a week looking for membership, to decide whether I like them or not. I’m looking for people from all walks of life, in fashion, art, lifestyle and beyond. They can’t all be finance bros. In my club, the make-up artists have as much say as the finance billionaires, and if you are cool and have something interesting to say, I’ll give you a platform.

‘For me, it’s never about money and I don’t have a dress code. But it is about age, as people need to be able to mix – thirties, forties are ideal, and a bit beyond. There is, of course, room on either side if I think you are cool.’

So how does he define that? Scusa, but that’s for him to know and you to find out.
