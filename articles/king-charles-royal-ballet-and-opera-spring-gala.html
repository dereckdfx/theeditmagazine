Royals

The Crown come to life! King Charles was the guest of honour at a historic spring gala for the Royal Ballet and Opera

His Majesty was joined by Sir Ian McKellen, Pretty Yende, Marianela Nuñez, and Sir Lloyd and Lady Dorfman as the new Main Stage curtain was revealed at the Royal Opera House

Por Ben Jureidini · 15 de mayo de 2026

The audience at the Royal Opera House might have felt as though they’d been whisked away to a scene from Bridgerton or The Crown on Thursday evening, as King Charles was heralded as the guest of honour at the Royal Ballet and Opera’s Spring Gala.

Amidst the splendour, history was made as the RBO revealed its new Main Stage curtains, adorned with the King’s personal cipher, reflecting the enduring relationship between the royal family and the world of British art.

The King watched on from the box, sitting alongside Sir Lloyd Dorfman – a philanthropist who serves as Chairman of the Royal Opera House and previously worked closely with His Majesty on the Prince’s Trust charity – and his wife, Lady Dorfman. The crowd erupted with applause as the curtain descended, bringing about a new era for the Royal Opera House and its Royal Patron.

Think of Royal Ballet principal William Bracewell, think of the starry-eyed romantic lead. But not this time – in Mayerling, he’ll be playing Kenneth Macmillan’s compulsively dark Crown Prince Rudolf, and making him all his own

The starry invitees were serenaded by a gala performance and a speech from Sir Ian McKellen before King Charles took to the stage with Royal Ballet Principal dancer Marianela Nuñez and soprano Pretty Yende, who previously performed at the King’s Coronation in 2023. It marked the first glimpse of the newly designed curtains, which will make their first showing at a repertoire performance when the Royal Ballet puts on Kenneth MacMillan’s Mayerling on 16 May.

His Majesty is known for his dedication to British artisan crafts, a cause that he consistently promotes through his work with the King’s Trust. As such, curtain production specialists worked with the Royal School of Needlework to bring to life a new staple of the Royal Opera House, one that audiences will see each and every evening.

Following her debut in Handel’s controversial drama at the Royal Opera House this week, Pretty Yende welcomed Edit into her dressing room

‘It was a privilege to welcome His Majesty The King this evening, bringing together artists from both companies in a shared celebration,’ said Alex Beard, Chief Executive of the Royal Ballet and Opera.

‘The unveiling of the new stage curtains marks an important moment for the organisation, reflecting our continued investment in the future of our stage and in the remarkable craft that underpins every performance.’

This may have been real life, rather than an episode of sumptuous period drama, but when it comes to the Royal Ballet and Opera, all eyes are on the new season.
