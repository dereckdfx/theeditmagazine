Phoebe Dynevor proves the ‘Princess Bob’ is the ultimate It-girl haircut

As Bridgerton star Phoebe Dynevor becomes the latest It-girl to trim her tresses, Edit reveals why the ‘Princess Bob’ is the style to know

By Harriet Kean · November 17, 2025

Edit looks at the glamorous world of Nicola Peltz’s family in an article first published in the July 2020 issue

Nothing says ‘I’m fine’ more than a post-break up haircut. Think Gwyneth Paltrow in Sliding Doors; Carrie Bradshaw in Sex and The City and now Princess Olympia: Greek royal Princess Olympia, the daughter of Pavlos, Crown Prince of Greece. Once a glossy haired Rapunzel, Olympia is marking her post-Perry Pearson era with a new look: her razor sharp, peroxide bob: now forever called by Edit ‘The Princess Bob.’

Joan Collins and Isabella Rossellini are set to star in The Bitter End, an upcoming biopic following the ‘satanic’ influence of Suzanne Blum on Wallis Simpson. Yet, it was the Duchess's personal secretary, Johanna Schutz, who saw the tragic events firsthand from Villa Windsor

Blunt, sheer and immediate, the Princess Bob is an accessory in its own right. An accessory that I happen to need. So I call up my man at Hershesons: Neale Rodger, a neon-yellow-haired stylist who is known as ‘The Bob King.’ (Just ask Izzy Richmond, Lennon Gallagher’s model girlfriend). ‘You’ve got proper posh hair,’ Neale says while fluffing my ends at the sleek Hershesons’ salon in Fitzrovia. Perhaps my journey from commoner to royal might not be that difficult?

Instagram content

We scroll through Princess Olympia’s Instagram feed for inspiration: there are photos of her hot girl summer at Glastonbury, in Ibiza, and in Noto, Sicily – her edgy, elegant bob perennially stealing the show. Neale muses: ‘Olympia can get away with having just one length because she has quite fine hair which always tends to sit quite flat.’ As my hair is much thicker, my Princess Bob will need more layers to remove some ‘weight.’ Otherwise, I would end up with a ‘big wedgy thatched roof style Bob,’ says Neale. The horror!

But first: the colour. I am whisked over to the back of the salon, where India Warrender is lazily flicking through a magazine. How fitting, I think: this West London It girl runs in a similar clique to my Greek muse. While Luke Hersheson gets to work on India’s long, curly hair, I have a short consultation with Shannon: a lovely Australian with a pixie cut who calls me ‘girlfriend’. I explain that I do not want my hair ‘to fry’ – citing my past experiences with bleach – but I still want a blonde lift à la Olympia. We decide on a ¾ head golden glow: a ‘touch of bleach’: with blonde foils, one golden foil and Olaplex (a clever product which strengthens hair post-bleach).

Lady Kitty Spencer and her twin sisters, Lady Amelia and Lady Eliza, eclipse all others with their blonde elegance and can-do attitude – they are bougie bombshells with an irresistible back story

After my hair is twisted into neat little silver foils, I am sent to the elegant wash basins for a double shampoo and a massage (the chairs at Hershesons have this delightful feature). Finally, I’m ready for the chop. Neale gets to work, explaining how this style is called a ‘Box Bob’: a structured short haircut that stops at the chin and has an overall ‘box’ shape that frames the face. We decide on jaw length – much shorter than Olympia’s – but we want my Princess Bob to be even more striking. Even more editorial.

Neale cuts with absolute precision - and soon enough I have a neat, sharp bob. After a messy blow dry, he gets out the thinning scissors. Like an edgy Edward Scissor Hands, he chops roughly at the ends to create ‘invisible layers’. ‘The whole point of invisible layers is that you remove weight and create maximum texture without seeing that you’ve done it,’ Neale says.

Queen Charlotte may be a popular character in the Bridgerton universe. But Charlotte of Mecklenburg-Strelitz, the real Queen Charlotte, was a captivating historical figure

‘Yes, girlfriend!’, Shannon says, as she swans past, referencing my hair’s new golden tones. ‘It’s so sleek!’ Clearly the Olaplex worked its magic. Neale is equally as pleased: ‘I love that we pushed the length to the absolute maximum,’ he says. ‘It gives the haircut so much more energy and attitude.’

With hair so sharp it could cut glass, I leave the salon feeling ever-so-chic. What would Princess Olympia do now? With no black-tie balls to attend, I hail a Black cab on Berners Street. The destination? 5 Hertford Street…
