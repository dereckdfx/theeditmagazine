Royals

Queen Rania of Jordan is polished in a classic suit as she joins fellow royals in New York for the United Nations General Assembly

By Isaac Bickerstaff

Queen Rania of Jordan demonstrated her keen ability for statesmanship at the UN General Assembly in New York on 22 September

Think you know cashmere? Think again. China's favoured manufacturer of the softly stylish knitwear is pairing centuries-old heritage with cutting-edge innovation…

Queen Rania of Jordan delivered an impassioned speech at the General Assembly of the United Nations in New York City on Tuesday. As an Eminent Advocate for UNICEF and Honorary Chairperson for the United Nations Girls ’ Education Initiative, the wife of His Majesty King Abdullah has long made a name for herself on the diplomatic stage.

The Jordanian queen joined the Irish Taoiseach and representatives from Save the Children and the State of Palestine at the High-Level Dialogue on Palestinian Children in Military Detention – an event co-sponsored by the Jordanian, Irish, Palestinian, Spanish and South African governments. Queen Rania called for immediate international action to protect children subjected to military detention in Palestine, in a powerful speech addressed to the crowd.

‘Children’s accounts follow a grim, familiar pattern: Ripped from their homes in the middle of the night, not knowing where they are being taken or why,’ said the Queen , who is of Palestinian descent. ‘Brought alone, handcuffed and blindfolded, to interrogation sites; hauled before military courts; pressured to sign documents they don’t understand. Assaulted and threatened with rape… kept underfed, isolated, and afraid.’

‘I just don’t understand. What is so terrifying about Palestinian children? Before they have even come of age, Palestinians are cast as threats: demographic threats, security threats, presumed guilty of future crimes they haven’t committed. Their innocence stripped away, they are dehumanised so thoroughly that even a kid with roller skates, a soccer ball, or a kite can be seen as something menacing. And a child can be branded a terrorist – as if they were never a child at all.’

Queen Rania, more than many royals , has imbued her royal role with advocacy on the world stage. Five years into her reign, she told the BBC that she sees the position as ‘an opportunity to bring about change.’

She added: ‘For me the title of a Queen means a certain responsibility. For me it’s a job; it means having to be aware of what is going on in my country, look at the people of Jordan and see how to improve their lives. It is not something that I carry; it does not change my personal life, it does not change who I am as a person, and I see it as a job title.’
