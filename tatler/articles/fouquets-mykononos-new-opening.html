Fouquet's arrives in Mykonos – but can Greece do it better?

Edit heads to the ancient Cyclades to witness a debut of modern decadence on the Greek island

By Chandler Tregaskes · July 20, 2026

It turns out that Greek yoghurt tastes better… in Greece. And specifically on the island of Mykonos. Even more so at the freshly-opened new Fouquet’s which finds itself in a rather glorious spot on the Southern coast. You can find it served with tahini and sesame for breakfast in the new hotel’s Éole restaurant, sat outside, looking over an infinity pool and out into the bay, where a mountainous vista backdrops a scene filled with superyachts and uber-exclusive beach clubs. It’s pretty dreamy stuff…

This new bolthole - boasting 60 rooms, three sizeable private villas, multiple pools, a cutting-edge spa and enough art to fill the Tate - is the latest from the Barriere Collection, whose rendezvous’ for respite span five sumptuous sites from gay Paree to St Barths, via Courchevel, New York and now Mykonos. Unlike other Fouquet’s locations - like the historic Paris address which has been injecting silver screen-worthy romance into the City of Love since the end of the 19th century - this maiden Mykonos voyage is entirely, and thoroughly, modern, while still paying homage to the natural charms of the regions. Think state-of-the-art architecture next to centuries-old olive trees; runway fashion and modish fare beside the breathtaking views of the Aegean Cyclades; and - rather fabulously - a basketball court housed inside an actual cave for those interested in a Batman-like spot of shooting hoops.

At the core of the property there’s seemingly four main nebulae: Art, high fashion, luxury dining and lavish reclining. The art oscillates between ultra modern, Andy Warhol-esque prints that command attention in vivid, splashy hues, to Salvador Dali surrealism, as seen in the elongated-leg elephant sculptures that you’ll spot around the property that add lashings of avant-garde to the otherwise ultra-refined appeal.

Walking along the approach to the lobby, you’ll spot a captivating wired metal sculpture of a pair of clasping hands, some 8ft tool. Across the way - via stepping stone - you can enter the in-hotel boutique. The rails are pretty impressive, proposing you wrap yourself in Bottega Veneta, Loewe and Casa Blanca for your stay (this Style Editor agrees that these coveted labels would indeed make a great uniform when on the island). And, if suitably impressed by the canvases dotted about, you can take your own home with you at Fouquet’s very own gallery.

Dining is nothing short of delectable. The aforementioned Éole eatery will supply you with all your mezze, mains and more needs, but conveniently posted up at the corner of the location, is the Roka beach club, where hotel guests can book a sun bed beneath a parasol sunken into the rocks of the Paraga beach bay, and enjoy near endless plates of sashimi, ancient Japanese Robatayaki and fish so fresh you won’t be surprised you’re literally on the ocean front - all soundtracked by its own DJ spinning impenetrable Mykonos beats.

If that’s not enough for partygoers and gourmands alike, nearby must-gos include the Scorpios and Nammos beach clubs, which are accessible via a short water taxi - chic! - and are where you’ll find a coterie of Grecian goddesses wrapped in crochet gowns and rara-skirted bikini sets all clamouring for that perfect Instagram selfie as the Azure-tinged sky softly turns into a heady hue of juicy peach.

Rooms back at Fouquet's Mykonos are capacious, considered and tastefully curated with a French-glam-meets-Mediterranean-heart edge. Soft stone floors and earthy-toned upholstery feeds into that au naturelle coziness, and many suites boast their own private pools which back straight out on the bay, giving you a feeling that you’re in the ocean-blue while perfectly temperate-regulated and just a few steps from your cloud-soft bed. (Those without their own pool can make use of the many around the hotel, or climb down from the rocks and straight into the waves if they so wish).

Undeniably, there is a certain element of James Bond-cool that breezes through its walls; like somewhere he might take his leading lady for champagne, caviar and that martini - shaken not stirred. It must be all that luxury built into the centuries-old rock coves, filled to the historic rafters with next-gen tech (see the Technogym fitness suite, cryo-chambers, star-lit cave pools and LED masks in the stunning spa) that feels suitably opulent and gadget-y.

Mission relax in resplendence? Accomplished, Agent 00-Heaven. Return to the sun lounger for some more Greek yoghurt and an Ouzo cocktail - you deserve it.

Fouquet's Mykonos is available to book now via hotelsbarriere.com
