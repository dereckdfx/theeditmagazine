Having a non-stop summer? It's time to retreat to one of these gloriously grounding sanctuaries

Bid goodbye to yacht parties and island hopping and book a room in one of these retreats where silence is golden and quiet glamour prevails

By Edit · August 7, 2026

Château Saint-Martin & Spa Cóte d’Azur, France

This hotel is the quintessence of quiet, refined glamour. You sweep along a cypress-studded drive to be greeted by… the ruins of a 12th-century Knights Templar castle. Then you’re struck by the most romantic scent-scape, supplied by the château’s rows of lavender and tango roses, as well as the jasmine arching down its walls. Inside, the dizzying repertoire of stone-flagged rooms is equally exquisite – the wood-panelled bar is hung with Aubusson tapestries – and beyond there’s a terrace made for sipping ice-cold kir royales. Everything is innately satisfying, especially the bedrooms in gentle Riviera hues, which have soft rugs, gentle lighting and luxurious bathrooms. The most peaceful pool is buried deep in gardens where, even if the hotel is full, you feel like you have the place to yourself. Dusk is when the restaurant comes alive, serving delectable tasting menus that subtly exaggerate the most distinctive flavours of Provence. Try the fig-leaf ice cream.

Doubles from £599 (oetkerhotels.com)

The Torridon Wester Ross, UK

Instagram content

For off-grid glamour, look no further than the UK’s most northerly five-star hotel, a red brick, turreted beauty buried deep in the Highlands. The interiors here charm you with their jewel tones and abundance of wood (not to mention some fabulous and colourful cornicing). And nowhere is this more evident than in the drawing room, library and two restaurants. In 1887, chef Danny Young does miraculous things with scallops, turbot and venison; in the less formal and more lively Bo & Muc brasserie, such local dishes as Scottish mozzarella and slow-cooked Beinn Eighe venison curry are served with verve. For a nightcap? Head for the cosy bar-lounge, a whisky wonderland stocked with 365 classic and rare malts, as well as its Arctutus gin distilled on the premises. Should you yearn for even more space and soul-stirring seclusion, you can hole up in the two-bedroomed Boathouse and admire the loch from your terrace, Adventures are always encouraged, and guests can explore the three great mountain ranges to the north – each one boasting two Munros – yomping from Liathach to Beinn Eighe and Beinn Alligin.

Doubles from £462, including breakfast (thetorridon.com).

Son Ermità Menorca, Spain

Reached by a slow, bumpy ride through northern Menorca’s rust-red hills and wild olive groves, Son Ermità feels less like a hotel and more like a discovery. The journey along rocky tracks edged with stone walls and cacti ends at a restored 18th-century finca that enchantingly blurs the line between old and new. With just 11 rooms and two sunset-facing infinity pools suspended over a wooded valley, it’s a places defined by silence, stillness and light. Created by Vestige Collection, the family firm behind Spain’s most exquisite restorations, the hotel exemplifies its understated ethos – sustainability through craftsmanship, luxury through restraint – and every element, from its antique furniture and locally hewn stone to the hand-applied lime plaster, honours the local heritage. By day, the pools sparkle in the sun and the pathways alive with butterflies. By night, birdsong echoes across the hills and the stars appear impossibly close. You feel drawn to the Brisa restaurant, glowing softly beside the terrace at dusk, which itself draws from the island’s soil and sea. For those who equate luxury with privacy, authenticity and time in nature, Son Ermità is this rugged island’s purest expression of it: a retreat where design, skill, history and landscape exist in reverent harmony.

Doubles from £541, including breakfast (vestige collection.com).

Aman Rosa Alpina San Cassiano, Italy

The old Rosa Alpina has been reborn as an Aman resort, its façade embellished with Japanese-style strips of wood; but the Pizzinini family, which has owned the hotel since 1939, still runs this remodelled property dominating the pretty skiing village of San Cassiano. They do it in a style that strikes you as soon as you walk into its double-height Il Salotto, hung with supersized tapestries. The 51 rooms follow a palette of muted colours, Dolomite stone, pale wood and huge floor-to-ceiling windows to show off the vistas. None of which could be further in the looks or scale from the traditional Alpine bolthole. This place appeals to a different kind of international customer, as is evident from the dining opportunities: aside from Italian offerings, a Japanese Shabu-Shabu (hot pot) restaurant called Akari has recently opened. Still, each room has a fireplace, a hidden television and a deep bath. In winter, there’s a ski lounge, ski butler and shuttle service to the nearby lifts; and everything from remote heli-skiing to private dining in an alpine hut can be arranged. There’s also a megaspa across two levels, with a yoga room, seven treatment suites whose emphasis is on Alpine ouls, a heated outdoor infinity pool and two more indoor. And did we mention the Finnish sauna, the steam room and jacuzzi? This is Aman at its peak.

Doubles from £1,552, including breakfast (aman.com).

A Mandria Di Murtoli Corsica, France

The long-awaited new opening from the family behind Corsica’s star hotel of Domaine de Murtoli has exceeded all expectations. A Mandria has a wonderfully romantic design of descending terraces and little stone cottages, some with their own pools; and after a fabulous restoration of its long-derelict old stables and farmhouses, chandeliers now hang from their ancient wooden beams. This haven works wonders. There’s not a great deal to do beyond splash in your villa plunge – or hang out by the hotel’s pretty pool – and take in those views of olive groves. But being perfectionist as well as laid-back, it’s much like the Domaine de Murtoli – a short ride away in a 4X4 – where you can hang out at one of the world’s standout beach restaurants. La Table de La Plage is a riot of. driftwood shacks overlooking a swathe of golden sand. As for the cuisine, northing compares to its vongole, salt-baked seabass and grilled steaks – enhanced by a delicious glass of chilled Fleur de Murtoli, the family’s bio-dynamic wine. Its vines were planted next to mystical dolmens, which are said to give off a special energy. It’s an elixir you’ll want in excess.

Doubles from £237 (amanariadimurtoli.com).

Singita Lebombo Kruger National Park, South Africa

Perched high above the N'wanetsi River in Singita’s 33,000-acre private concession, Lebombo is a place where nature moves freely. The lodge’s 15 air-conditioned glass suites, inspired by eagles’ nests built into the cliffside, hover above the riverbank. Animals wander unhindered through the unfenced property, padding along the raised wooden walkways that weave through the bush. You can watch the sunrise over a coffee at the Long Bar before heading out on a game drive, crossing one of Kruger’s most wildlife-rich districts as lions doze on the open plains and leopards slip between the shadows. Here, staff combine warmth and discretion, anticipating every need. The kitchen focuses on locally sourced ingredients, prepared in conjunction with the Singita Community Culinary School and served throughout the day. (It’s also one of the region’s most important wine collectors, with a meticulously curated cellar.) Despite the service, Singita’s conservation mission anchors everything here: its Lowveld Trust funds anti-poaching patrols, rhino dehorning and other community-driven projects that protect endangered species while creating opportunities for those living beside the park. The circle of life.

Doubles from £2,282, full board (singita.com)

La Posta Tuscany, Italy

La Posta stands out as a place of deep tranquility and relaxation: in the middle of the Val d'Orcia, under the watch of an ancient castle on the hill opposite. While this backdrop is already enough to melt any tension away, the real star is the natural thermal spring that feeds the property. Bubbling up in the piazza of Bagno Vignoni, its mineral-rich waters rush down to the hotel, where they cascade into the spa and outdoor swimming baths at a pleasant temperature of 38º; and the pool and its leafy environs are so relaxing that you could easily spend the day there. But the rest of the hotel works its magic, too; as a family enterprise, La Posta boasts old-school hospitality that places guest comfort above anything else. You end up feeling welcome wherever you go, whether selecting records from the owner’s impressive vinyl collection in Bar Verde or making new friends over a game of chess in the sitting room. At La Rocca restaurant, expect a delicious mix of modern and classic dishes, from exquisitely prepared raw scampi to delicate pecorino ravioli, all paired with wine from the nearby vineyards.

Doubles from £259, including breakfast (lapostahotel.it).

Casa La Siesta, Cádiz, Spain

It’s muy fácil to imagine how a stay at a hotel named Casa La Siesta might unfold, and this rural idyll certainly lives up to its name. This a place to rest and recharge, where life slows to a soporific pace the moment you pass through the cast iron gates into lavender and jasmine-scented gardens. The only mild threat to complete peace and quiet comes courtesy of Mother Nature: a veritable symphony of birdsong and roosters provides an early morning alarm call; the bells of wandering cattle and soft bleats of goats gently interrupt an afternoon slumber; a dive-bombing swift might punctuate a dip in the saltwater pool. Visit in spring and the fields are swathed in wildflowers, in summer the hills shimmer, scorched and hazy beneath the Andalucian sun.The beautifully restored cortijo is reached by a farm track through the Cádiz countryside. Six generous rooms feature terracotta tiled floors, cool bare plaster walls, wooden beams and carefully curated antiques, some have rolltop baths placed just so for a view over the gardens. It's all akin to a stay with friends, were you lucky enough to have friends who excel in Spanish hospitality and cookery. Seasonal, nourishing dishes showcase local and home grown produce; perfect gazpacho, grilled meat or fish and inventive salads, it’s all simple yet elegant. Daily set lunch and dinner menus are chalked on a blackboard propped in the salon removing the burden of choice. Save your decision making for the honesty bar - will it be a chilled local rosé or a glass of Manzanilla ? It would be easy to spend a whole day on the balcony watching the light shift across the hills and listening out for larks (for bird nerds the Merlin app is perhaps the only justifiable concession to tech). Or to simply drift from breakfast in the garden to an afternoon by the pool, lazy games of chess in the gorgeous courtyard where lunch and dinner are served. As the day ends, return to the balcony to watch bats take over from swifts in the deep blue sky, to gaze at the stars, as undisturbed as you by the modern world and to revel in the quiet.

From 425 euros, including breakfast, www.casalasiesta.com
