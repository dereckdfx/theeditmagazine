The history of the Princess of Wales' rarely seen amethyst necklace

Despite sitting in the royal vaults for generations, the necklace worn by Catherine to the Digger premiere has only been seen twice in a century

By Isaac Bickerstaff · September 23, 2026

The Princess of Wales stepped out in a rarely seen piece of royal history on Tuesday evening, as she joined Prince William for a star-studded night at the movies.

Catherine, 44, was a regal vision in a purple Jenny Packham gown at the premiere of Digger in Leicester Square, joining the film’s star, Tom Cruise, to support the Film and TV Charity, which supports people working in the industry with everything from mental wellbeing to financial help. It is a testament to the future queen’s star power that, despite her entourage including Hollywood legends, it was all eyes on her as she made her grand entrance.

For the occasion, the Princess turned to Packham, one of her most trusted designers who has dressed Her Royal Highness for high-profile evenings including the Royal Variety Performance, the BAFTA awards and a string of state banquets. So impressive was her elegant ensemble that some royal watchers dubbed the look ‘dynastic dressing’.

The star of the show, however, was the amethyst necklace adorning the Princess of Wales’ décolletage. The glamorous sautoir – which features a dramatic heart-shaped amethyst, suspended on three rows of pearls, interspersed with oval amethysts with a diamond surround – has been in the royal vaults for generations, but has very rarely graced the public eye.

Originally, the show-stopping jewels were owned by Queen Alexandra, the wife of King Edward VII and the mother of George V. In 1923, it was presented to Lady Elizabeth Bowes-Lyon (the future Queen Elizabeth, Queen Mother) on her wedding to the Duke of York (later King George VI). Along with many of the Queen Mother's wedding gifts, it was put on public display at St James' Palace, alongside a note from Queen Alexandra, addressed to her ‘dear future granddaughter’. As queen, Elizabeth was not photographed in the necklace, but reportedly wore it during a period of courtly mourning for Queen Alexandra.

While some jewels from this era have remained in frequent rotation in the wardrobes of generations of royal women, the striking amethyst necklace lay largely forgotten for almost a century – indeed, it is believed to have only been seen twice in public in the past 100 years.

Before the Princess of Wales, the most recent royal to step out in the necklace was Queen Camilla, then the Duchess of Cornwall, who wore it for an equally grand red carpet moment: the London premiere of Skyfall. Like Catherine, Camilla chose the necklace as a masterly, monochromatic finishing touch to an all-purple ensemble, pairing the pearl-and-amethyst confection with a sumptuous velvet gown.

There are a number of other amethysts in the royal collection – including the ‘Kent Amethysts’, a stunning, diamond-bordered suite most recently worn by Queen Camilla on the state visit to the US. However, the Princess of Wales chose to forgo co-ordinating earrings for the Digger premiere, and instead opted for heirloom earrings that belonged to Princess Diana – two iconic jewels with their own storied royal history.

Each earring features a round, diamond stud from which is suspended an additional round diamond, a diamond-set bell cap and a large, pearl drop, making them a truly statement piece. Princess Diana was given the earrings by Collingwood, a jewellery firm often favoured by the Spencers, which lent gems to the young Diana while she was engaged to the then Prince Charles.

When Diana posed for a portrait session with Lord Snowdon, Princess Margaret's photographer husband, during her engagement, Collingwood lent her an elaborate diamond necklace and matching earrings. Reportedly, the jewellers wanted to gift the two pieces to Diana as a wedding gift, but the Palace cautioned that the set would be considered too extravagant. Instead, they gave her a pair of pearl and diamond earrings.

Diana made her debut in the pearls at a dinner held in honour of King Khalid of Saudi Arabia at Claridge's in June 1981, several weeks before her wedding. They quickly became a mainstay in her jewellery collection and remained a favourite throughout her marriage. On grand state occasions, the Princess normally paired them with other pearl pieces, such as the Queen Mary Lover's Knot Tiara.

The current Princess of Wales is just as fond of the Collingwood earrings as her late mother-in-law was. She has been spotted wearing them on a number of occasions, including to the annual Festival of Remembrance in 2024, for a service of thanksgiving to commemorate VE Day last year, and to welcome President Macron of France and his wife, Brigitte, for their official state visit to Britain in July.

The decision to wear a pair of earrings so closely associated with Diana has been read by some as a loaded gesture given the current tensions between King Charles III and his former brother-in-law, Earl Spencer, whose bombshell memoir has levelled shocking claims at His Majesty.

Nonetheless, the future king and queen were the picture of dignity and professionalism as they brought a touch of royal glamour to Hollywood, no doubt keen to stress the strength and stability that come with centuries of royal history.
