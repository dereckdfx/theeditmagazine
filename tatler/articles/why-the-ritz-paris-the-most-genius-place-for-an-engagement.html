Why the Ritz, Paris is the most genius place for an engagement

By Delilah Khomo

Ben-Sylvester Strautmann proposed to Princess Alexandra of Hanover at The Ritz, Paris. But first to check-in was Tatler travel editor Delilah Khomo

Ben-Sylvester Strautmann executed the perfect proposal to Princess Alexandra of Hanover , not only the with the choice of ring – a vivid yellow cushion cut diamond – but, most importantly, the engagement location. Who could possibly say no in the Ritz, Paris ?

The Sheffield scion left the heir to the throne ‘yearning’, and after royal regulations prevented the pair from marrying, she named her sons William and Harry

There are many zones of enchantment at this Place Vendôme jewel. Take the subterranean pool, studded with columns and a ceiling painted like a Jean-Honoré Fragonard sky, which features in the couple's ecstatic Instagram announcement post. In bedrooms, there are bottles of champagne on ice, and chocolates and peach-coloured roses await among marble fireplaces. This is a place to have room service on repeat.

But a particularly luxuriant and romantic mood is at play in its bathrooms. Marcel Proust loved the hotel so much that he was at the opening back in 1898. (There’s a suite and salon named after him.) And when you flick the gold-swan taps of the huge marble tubs, it’s like plunging into the author’s dreamscape. You can imagine him pouring in a generous amount of the Ritz’s bubble bath, which has a strong scent of magnolias – they majestically grow in the Grand Jardin here – and madeleines. If you’re seeking temps perdus by means of the latter, don’t miss Le Comptoir, the destination patisserie downstairs, where star chef François Perret specialises in these evocative little cakes. They are best eaten – still warm – in the bath, which you should take mid-morn, mid-afternoon and midnight to add to the sense that you’re dwelling in a place that is effectively a palace of excess suspended in the Belle Epoque. Oh, those colours of champagne, and the blues and creams in graduating tones, which give the walls their powdery quality. But that rich attention to detail is everywhere, with an abundance of marble and chandeliers (Ritz purists will argue that the pre-renovation gold was far more chic and subtle), not to mention the signature blush-pink that you see in the choice of roses, or the peachy shade of the towels and bath robes, the slippers and silky cotton sheets. They all combine to give off a certain glow – for which we have Cesar Ritz’s wife to thank, as she believed those hues were the most flattering on a woman.

Princess Alexandra of Hanover, what could possibly outshine her 4.80 carat yellow diamond? The gold swan bath taps of the Ritz, Paris

The whole point of being here is to stay in the hotel, which just exudes 'celebration'. So, should you be so inclined, they could have slinked downstairs to the Ritz Bar, where a zodiac-inspired metal grate theatrically rises every night to signal that cocktail hour has begun. The celestial mood continues with heavenly martinis; but whatever you drink, make sure to try one of the jasmine martinis, which taste as if the Sun King himself had mixed them, using various distillations and essences of these tiny white, waxy flowers. Better still, order one on room service. And savour it in the bath. And as for Princess Alexandra of Hanover, what could possibly outshine her 4.80 carat yellow diamond? The gold swan bath taps of the Ritz, Paris, naturellement!
