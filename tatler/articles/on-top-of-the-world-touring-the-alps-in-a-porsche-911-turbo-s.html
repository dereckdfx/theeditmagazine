On top of the world: touring the Alps in a Porsche 911 Turbo S

Motoring enthusiast Xavier Bastin explores the Alps' winding mountain passes and verdant rolling scenery from the driver's seat of a Porsche 911 Turbo S

By Xavier Bastin · August 28, 2026

I have nearly arrived at the Alpina in Gstaad, the first stop of my Swiss road trip. I have been in the driver’s seat for almost five hours – but the 701 horsepower of the 911 Turbo S, along with the typically tight Porsche handling and supreme ride comfort of this elite long-distance grand touring sports car, mean I am still feeling fresh.

I follow the signs for the hotel leading up increasingly narrow – yet always manicured – roads of the town that the international jet set have called their second home since the 60s. A discreet tunnel entrance suddenly appears at the end of an otherwise unassuming residential row of pristine chalets. Other than a smart sign signalling that this is the entrance to the Alpina Gstaad, nothing hints at what lies beyond.

I drive into its mouth, past the soothing sounds of an underground waterfall. A ray of sunlight from a lightwell dances off an imposing golden statue, as I make my way up the ramp where the valet is waiting. Long-time Gstaad enthusiast Sir Roger Moore would have felt right at home in this private underground lair.

I hand my keys to the valet, and the theme continues: the lobby is grand and meticulously laid out to feel spacious yet intimate. I am taken to my room – a suite that balances the traditional stone and wood of a Swiss chalet with modern homely elements, like a steel and glass fireplace that sparks to life at the touch of a button.

The suite brings an instant feeling of relaxation. But it is the view that takes my breath away. The hotel is positioned at the upper end of Gstaad, so it authoritatively overlooks the valley. I open the suite’s balcony doors to let the cool, alpine air wash over me, cleansing me of the 43C inferno heat of central France I experienced on my way down.

My time here is spent luxuriating in the hotel grounds where there is no shortage of private areas to hang out in, like quiet nooks next to sculptures on the lawn, or a peaceful table on the terrace. I make a short trip to town for a stroll along the famous Gstaad Promenade before heading back to a massage in the Alpina’s Six Senses Spa – all the knots I have worked up driving here dissolve into nothingness under a haze of essential oils.

The hotel offers an eclectic mix of restaurants, from the Japanese Megu to the relaxed Alpina Lounge & Terrace. But my favourite is the Italian-inspired Monti, where the veal Milanese is a definite highlight.

All too soon, my time at the Alpina has drawn to a close. The valet brings the Turbo S round, ready for the 200-mile stretch of glorious Alpine driving ahead.

The first leg of my journey takes me along the gentle, winding roads of the Saanenland valleys. The route is calm, and this is where the grand touring capability of Porsche’s flagship 911 Turbo S cabrio is obvious. Despite possessing ferocious amounts of power, the car is completely at ease cruising along at a relaxed pace – it will do exactly what you want it to, and the adaptive suspension, supple leather seats and surgically precise handling almost make me forget that I am driving the most powerful production 911 model Porsche has ever made. Indeed, it feels positively soothing to drive along with the roof down, with the invigorating mountain air swooping through the cockpit.

But like a sort of automotive Henry Jekyll, there is – and always has been – a dark Edward Hyde lurking within. And to make him come alive, I simply flick the driving mode selector on the steering wheel from ‘normal’, past ‘sport’ to the somewhat intimidating ‘sport plus’. Instantly, the exhaust note changes. What was previously a civilised rumble turns into a menacing growl. The car immediately lowers and the suspensions stiffens. A lip under the front bumper extends, and a wing at the rear deploys.

The car now looks, sounds and feels like a completely different animal. The imperfections in the road are no longer hidden because of the sudden tightness in the chassis. An eagerness, an urgency to get going is dramatically felt. And when I command it to, it goes. It really goes.

Acceleration is brutal (the Turbo S can do 0-60 in 2.5 seconds – hypercar territory), and the journey to high speeds is full of theatrics. The car’s electrically driven twin turbos spool up with virtually no delay, and the signature high-pitched whine over the roar of the engine sounds like something out of Mad Max.

And though it’s very tempting to pretend that only the most talented drivers can wrestle with this amount of power and keep the car compliant on the drivers’ paradise of the Susten pass, the reality is that Porsche’s stability management system makes the Turbo S very easy to drive quickly. The four-wheel drive system will keep the car completely stable around even the tightest hairpins at virtually any speed. And by the time I reach the fabled Julierpass – another of Switzerland’s crown jewel driving roads – I am in the zone, carving through hairpin after hairpin. Soon enough, I am entering the outskirts of the Swiss town perhaps most beloved by the glitterati, St Moritz.

I drive through the town, with the 911 burbling along streets lined with luxury boutiques, art galleries and charming cafés, and reach the hotel. It is the legendary Badrutt’s Palace, an icon of Swiss luxury to those in the know.

The entrance is discreet, but emits an aura of old-school hospitality, particularly of the golden age of continental hotels. Smartly uniformed doormen and porters stand outside, and the picture is completed by the presence of a classic Rolls-Royce Phantom (one I later find out has British Royal family provenance).

As I pull up, the doorman immediately helps me out of the low-slung Turbo S, and I am ushered inside. I am introduced to Gaia, my butler, who shows me to my room where my other butler, Stefane, is waiting. I am staying in the newest part of the hotel, the Serlas wing. It sits just across the street from the original hotel, and is connected by a secret underground tunnel system that joins up the whole hotel complex – affording guests total privacy once checked in.

My suite is the epitome of good taste. The smartest Italian furniture, soft fabrics and chic objets are meticulously laid out to create the most inviting spaces within the enormous open plan sitting area, bedroom and dressing room. It is entirely possible to spend the entire stay within the palace grounds without running out of things to do. Think expansive gardens, tennis and padel courts, swimming pools, spas and a state of the art fitness and reformer pilates suite to name just a few attractions.

But I am here to indulge. I start my day with an elaborate breakfast – an almost infinite spread of the most delicious pastries, fresh fruit, alpine meats and cheeses, along with a delicious a la carte menu – in the grand dining room overlooking the emerald waters of Lake St Moritz, all under the gentle glissando of the resident harpist. I feel ready for a day of hard relaxation ahead.

I explore the streets of St Moritz before the palace’s 1968 Phantom whisks me away to a ski lift that takes me up the mountain, for a short, refreshing Alpine walk to the palace’s Paradiso mountain club. I enjoy a mountain cheese and charcuterie platter, followed by a beef tartare. I choose the light option because I would like to try and show some restraint, I don’t want to overdo it… (although I am quaffing what feels like an infinite stream of rosé Champagne, but it would be rude not to in St Moritz).

Later that day, I enjoyed an aperitif in the palace’s drawing room. With high ceilings and grand windows that frame the imposing cliff face of the Pitz Rosatsch across the lake, I contemplate what to eat tonight. The palace has 11 restaurants to choose from – and top of my list is La Coupole Matsuhisa, the Japanese restaurant headed up by Nobuyuki Matsuhisa of Nobu fame. The miso black cod remains a crowd-pleaser.

My time at Badrutt’s Palace is one I will not soon forget. And though the luxury was second to none, what really sets it apart is the flawless attention to detail and care exhibited by everyone who works here. I felt truly looked after and ready to return home in the Turbo S – via Germany, where the Autobahn with no speed limits awaited…
