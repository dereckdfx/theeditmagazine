Olympians, Wimbledon icons, aristos and euro-royals: Who was in the trenches at Burberry’s London Fashion Week show last night?

By Chandler Tregaskes

Love-all in the midst? Jack Draper and Jodie Turner-Smith were spotted looking uber-cool on the front row of the Burberry London Fashion Week show last night

It’s officially Burberry season. In the British heritage luxury house ’s 170th year, the Royal Warranted weatherproofer-turned-arbiter-of-English-chic has scored some big points in the style stakes as of late. Yesterday, the V&A opened the doors to its own mini-exhibition focusing on the house’s most indelible silhouette - the trench coat - two years ahead of the South Ken institution’s reopening of the Fashion Gallery, which will be dubbed the ‘Burberry Gallery’ in 2028. The house also reported a 5% increase in revenue in the first quarter of 2026. And that’s not to mention the It- girls , ingenues and Hollywood icons returning to the house once again to claim that Burberry - and its home nation of Great Britain - is finally cool again. If you ever needed the proof, the Princess of Wales proudly donned a reportedly brand-new chocolate-hued trench up in Scotland just last week.

Ahead of today’s London Fashion Week show, the V&A opens the doors to a new showcase focusing on Burberry’s most famed silhouette, the trench coat

So, as the Nova-checked atelier closed SS27’s London Fashion Week schedule last night at the Chelsea College of Arts, what will become the Burb’s must-haves as we look towards the season ahead? Chief Creative Officer Daniel Lee called for a ‘Return to the basis of things. To something instinctive. To something felt. Something true.’ Keep calm and carry on, some might say…

Models line up for the finale of the Burberry SS27 show at Chelsea College of Arts

Citing notions of ‘intentional imperfection’ and ‘the idea of owning a piece, living in it,’ Lee enlisted the help of British textile designer, Celia Birtwell, to bring her hand-drawn motifs to the heart of the collection. Paying tribute to the swirly-floral charms of the Swingin’ Sixties and the hippy-fied hedonism of the Seventies, the 61-look-strong line proved playful, powerful and positive. While earlier in the schedule, McQueen returned to the capital with ominous elegance wrapped in bondage straps and metallic-horned hairdos, Lee’s latest Burberry mood was fresh, light and airy - the sort of stuff you might actually want to wear for a springtime jaunt and matcha break around Victoria Park.

But who was there to bear witness? The woman of the hour, Birtwell, took pride of place among the front row, of course. And she was joined by a gathering of British greats, glamazons - and even gourmands, in the form of River Café matriarch, Lady Rogers.

Of the aristo crowd, the Marchioness of Bath looked sleek in a black leather cape, while Guinness-wife and gothic grande dame. Meanwhile, Princess Maria-Olympia of Greece joined the dark debutante dress code, slipping into a pleated heavy-leather skirt and tee, her blonde cheveaux shining brighter than ever.

Then came the sports stars. Tennis titan Jack Draper was spotted cosying up to thespian beauty , Jodie Turner-Smith, and sprinting Olympian Dina Asher-Smith opted for a silken ensemble with a glossy shawl neck blouson and gem-encrusted wide-leg trousers.

There was even a battle of the It-couples. Romeo Beckham and Kim Turnbull did their usual Gen-Z-approved sports-luxe cool, while Millennial muses Pixie Geldof and George Barnett opted for more grown-up glam in coordinating navy jackets.
