Is this the Princess of Wales' new favourite necklace? How a simple gold chain became Catherine's royal staple

Catherine stepped out for a surprise engagement on Thursday afternoon, wearing a day-to-day jewel that has swiftly become a go-to from her royal collection

By Isaac Bickerstaff · September 24, 2026

The Princess of Wales swapped Hollywood glamour for a new day-to-day jewellery favourite on Thursday, as she embarked on a surprise engagement on Thursday.

Fresh from the red carpet glamour of Tuesday night’s Digger premiere, Catherine, 44, made her way to Southend-on-Sea to spend the day with addiction support charity The Forward Trust, where she serves as Patron. To mark Addiction Awareness Week, the future queen visited Forward Growing, a new community initiative that helps service users engage with the natural world with a helping hand from Garon Park.

The Princess of Wales wore a smart blazer from Max Mara for the engagement and paired the garment with Joseph Coleman trousers, a Me+Em belt and loafers from Boden. Catherine also opted for a piece that is quickly becoming a staple in her royal wardrobe: Laura Lombardi’s ‘Portrait’ necklace. The gold chain has been in the Princess’s collection since 2022, when she debuted it during her state visit to Boston with Prince William. She also wore the chain during a meeting with textile manufacturers in Leeds in 2023 and an outing at the V&A East Storehouse in 2025.

But in recent weeks, the Princess of Wales has worn the necklace twice in quick succession. She was spotted wearing the statement gold chain when she joined the Prince of Wales to drop off their oldest son, Prince George, for his first day at Eton on 8 September, a milestone moment for the Wales family.

Catherine has served as Patron of The Forward Trust since 2021 and has spoken movingly about her desire to end the stigma around addiction. As a part of the Trust’s Taking Action of Addiction campaign last year, she said: ‘By talking about it in the open, together we can bring addiction and the harm it causes out of the shadows. We can reframe this issue with kindness and understanding, and we can help individuals and families coping with addiction know they are not alone.’

Her outing to support the charity this Thursday also saw her wear another favourite piece of jewellery: her Cartier ‘Trinity’ earrings, which many royal watchers have taken to see as a tribute to Princess Diana, who was known to love the collection.

The late Princess of Wales – who wore a ‘Trinity’ ring on her little finger – popularised the range in Britain, making it especially special that her daughter-in-law has chosen to make it something of her own trademark. In 2012, Catherine wore Cartier's long and chunky ‘Trinity’ necklace for a visit to the National Portrait Gallery, which she donned again during a trip to New York in 2014. She has the matching, glittering earrings, too, which she wore with a royal blue dress for a special banquet at Kensington Palace to mark SportsAid’s 40th year in 2016.

Thursday saw a return to these day-to-day staples for the Princess of Wales, who stepped out in dynastic glamour on Tuesday to join Prince William for the premiere of Digger at Leicester Square. Catherine was resplendent in a flowing purple gown by Jenny Packham as she made her grand entrance at the Royal Film Premiere 2026, showcasing the hotly anticipated film directed by Alejandro González Iñárritu and starring Tom Cruise. The event was held in support of the Film and TV Charity, which supports people working in the industry with everything from mental wellbeing to financial help.

For the occasion, the Princess turned to Packham, one of her most trusted designers who has dressed Her Royal Highness for high-profile evenings including the Royal Variety Performance, the BAFTA awards and a string of state banquets. The sweeping silhouette featured voluminous sleeves, a nipped-in waist and a plunging, deep-V neckline that perfectly framed the rare piece that dazzled on her décolletage.

Adorning the Princess of Wales' neck was a show-stopping amethyst necklace that was given to the Queen Mother as a wedding present in 1923 and is believed to have only been seen twice in public in the century since.

It was a moment of high royal glamour and one that served to demonstrate how adept the Princess of Wales has become at switching between the sartorial registers required of a role that sees her attending premieres on the arms of A-listers one day and getting her hands dirty on a gardening engagement the next.
