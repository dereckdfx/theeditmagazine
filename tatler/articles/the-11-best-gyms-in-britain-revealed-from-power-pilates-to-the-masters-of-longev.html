Are these the best gyms in Britain?

Work up a sweat at the smartest gyms in the land, as crowned by beauty director Astrid Joss in the Edit Gym Awards 2026

By Edit · January 14, 2026

London

For the pilates queens of London, there’s no regime more stylish and strenuous. With light wood interiors and vast skylights, Nobu Pilates feels more like a condo from an Architectural Digest video than a fitness destination. The go-getting women who frequent the space are, of course, just as well put together as the studio: chic Alo Yoga and Adanolo workout sets, balletcore wraps, silk hairbands and Moncler puffer jackets. (Word to the wise: step up your pilates socks game.) Inside the spacious studio are 10 Reformer machines, each supplemented with boxes, towers and pedals to allow for a breadth of exercises, as well as a big wall of mirrors for self-correcting your technique. Book in with Harriet, whose cries of ‘beautiful!’ and ‘so strong!’ are the most invigorating way to start your day. Or drop in for an evening session and let the day’s tension melt away. (For out-of-towners, Nobu Hotel’s plush suites are the perfect place to bed down, with minimalist Japanese-influenced design.) Meanwhile, the generously spaced 140-square-metre gym is well-equipped with a wide range of machines, from ellipticals to bikes. The changing rooms are stocked with fragrant Grown Alchemist products, Dyson hairdryers and GHD straighteners, so there’s no need to sacrifice looking fabulous to feel it. And the pretty Marylebone location, mere moments from Selfridges, is perfectly situated for meeting up with the girls afterwards and hitting up Boxcar for brunch. Don’t leave without picking up a cold-pressed juice from the studio’s fridge, stacked full of Daily Dose bottles, or one of Nobu Pilates’ coveted baseball caps, so everyone will know your workout routine is as chic as they come.

Classes cost £37 each; no joining/membership fee applicable (nobuhotels.com).

Longevity is the name of the game at Jumeirah Carlton Tower’s Peak Fitness Club and Spa – in more ways than one. Some clients have been making their way to the hotel gym in Knightsbridge for more than 30 years, with many now in their eighties and nineties. Diana, Princess of Wales was a fan, and it’s a home away from home for many Chelsea locals. The camaraderie is such that you can find gym-goers gathering in the spa to sip champagne and watch the Battersea fireworks on Bonfire Night (the view from Carlton Tower is the best in London). Full-height windows offer scenic vistas stretching from Belgrave Square to Hyde Park as you stop by for a barre burn class or spot of kick-boxing, and the pool is the largest hotel pool in London to be bathed in natural light. The gym machines are fully automated, with computers to keep track of your progress, allowing you to specify the type of training you want to achieve. Viscous fluid resistance for added power? Check. No resistance to reduce joint impact? You bet. After that, head to the spa area for a Cadogan Green Detox juice and a kale salad that actually tastes good. You’ll be joined by mother-daughter duos, Chelsea CEOs and maybe even a nonagenarian master of the leg press.

Membership from £550 per month or £6,000 per annum, plus £2,500 joining fee (jumeirah.com).

Mayfair is rocking again thanks to The Chancery Rosewood. The former American embassy is now a Jenga stack of marble, gilt and fine wood, the eagle on its rooftop holding court over Grosvenor Square. The hotel’s underground Asaya spa offers one of the most exclusive wellness memberships in town, capped at 50 members – an elite group of royals, CEOs and captains of industry (hotel guests are also permitted). The space is kitted out with Technogym’s new Artis Luxury line. There’s a Reformer pilates machine, free weights, a barre, and Sky Sports playing on a gigantic TV. Most insightful is the Technogym Checkup – a scanner that reveals everything from body composition to mobility and strength. Once the sweating is done, there is the spa to enjoy. London’s finest pool – a rare 25 metres – is bordered by daybeds that you’d be forgiven for falling asleep on. There is also a sauna, steam room, ice bath, plunge pool and heated beds, the whole place a calm symphony of warm, golden-cream and marble tones. A visit is only complete with a stay at one of the House suites on the upper floors – think astonishingly spacious living and dining areas, full-height windows, a gargantuan bed you’ll be metaphorically chained to, and a bathtub that overlooks prime London real estate.

Membership POA (rosewoodhotels.com).

It’s a tale as old as time: gym at seven; desk at nine. Any finance bro worth their salt knows the best way to beat the grind is to unwind with a chest press or 12 before work, and where better for City slickers to get their fitness fix than the gym at the Four Seasons Hotel London at Tower Bridge? First-class Onyx equipment, behemoth shower cubicles in jet-black tile, and a changing room that looks like a spa in itself – the gym combines efficiency with class in a way that any young professional knows is sure to get you noticed by the powers that be. The whole place is awash with EC3, the signature scent crafted by Aromaria, and you can purchase shirts from Love Brand & Co and Vilebrequin board shorts, should you wish to vary those navy quarter-zips. The pièce de résistance, though, is what every hyper-focused financier secretly wants: a warm hug, an eye mask and a bit of pampering. Enter: the SAVA Sound Pod. The hotel was the first in Europe to offer this unique take on meditation, which has been the big name in wellness Stateside for a while now. (A familiar sight, then, for the American regulars at the hotel, who are no doubt nipping down from the Royal Garden Suite, with its private terrace, or the Grand Heritage Suite and its 16-foot ceilings, for a spot of relaxation.) You lie down in the warm embrace of the Sound Pod with a weighted blanket, listen to a soundtrack masterminded by Hans Zimmer, and let the pod vibrate you to some much-needed R&R. The perfect wind-down.

Membership £250 per month or £2,700 per annum, plus £500 joining fee (fourseasons.com).

One of the most impressive and historic buildings in the whole of London is The OWO, the former Old War Office. The only privately owned property on Whitehall, it has been transformed by its owners, the Hinduja family, into a palace of luxury for political, entrepreneurial and regal hotshots. Beneath the hive of activity above ground, there is a four-storey spa, gym and indoor pool. While the spa is run by Guerlain, the active wellness offering comes courtesy of Pillar Wellbeing. The sleek gym space is accented with wood and arched mirrors, and there’s plenty of room to breathe. Rather than being crammed full of gizmos, it has an elegant selection of machines and weights in matte black as well as a boxing bag to beat out the stress. Classes take place in studios overlooking the glittering 20-metre swimming pool. For a completely bespoke experience, there are one-to-one sessions for members, residents and hotel guests. Workout completed, there’s only one place to go: downstairs to the pool area, where you can nibble on a fruit platter, sip champagne and marvel at the lofty ceilings and all that marble. Swim or unwind with a relaxing rotation of plunge pool, sauna, steam room and vitality showers. After primping in the changing rooms, make your way to the Spy Bar (006 and 007’s offices knocked together to form a bar) for a vodka martini, then head on to Mauro Colagreco’s Michelin-starred restaurant.

Membership from £6,500 per annum, plus £2,000 joining fee (raffles.com).

Here on the doorstep of Hyde Park is old-school luxury lent five-star finesse in gleaming mahogany, marble and gold. The rooms are excessively sumptuous, as Madonna, Dame Joan Collins and Bianca Jagger can all attest, but the magnet for the Royal Borough’s elite (spot the men about town discussing their latest sports cars) is the exclusive health club for high-class self-improvement. The trainers are top-notch: Irfan previously worked with GB boxer Amir Khan; Lakei designs ultra-personalised workouts to build strength, stamina and tone; and Lemi’s Reformer pilates classes are legendary. Boxing and ‘dance burn’ classes are also firmly in demand, and the gym is stocked with every conceivable piece of Technogym equipment, plus a Boditrax machine for the data-obsessed. Need a recharge? Join a sound healing session, recover in one of the saunas or the hydrotherapy pool, or refuel in the silk-wallpapered restaurant with a superfood shake… or a glass of Moët – you’ve earned it.

Membership £600 per month or £6,600 per annum, plus £2,000 joining fee (https://www.lanesboroughclubandspa.com/).

Outside London

Roman gods watch on as you shock, scan, squat and sub-zero your way to wellness at The Elite Gym at Grantley Hall. But while the interiors are inspired by the original 18th-century manor’s Palladian façade (alcoves adorned with statues of the Three Graces and Greco-Roman pillars line the pool), a visit will put you at the cutting-edge of exercise science. Stand like a statue yourself on a 3D body scanner for an insightful look into your own physique. Combine with a turn on the Tanita bio-impedance machine analysing your visceral fat, muscle-mass-per-body-part and bone density, and you’re rewarded with a degree of self-knowledge hitherto untold (the philosophers would approve). Personal trainers can then identify risk levels, track progress and set realistic health goals. Membership is truly exclusive, maxing out at around 30 people tootling down from the nearby affluent areas of Harrogate and York, while hotel guests find themselves using the gym to get a taste of what it means to know thyself. Another revelation? Electro Muscle Stimulation (EMS). Hook up to an electric impulse machine to activate more muscle fibres and vastly improve the efficiency of your exercise, perfect for building strength in record time. Cool off in the Instagram-worthy snow room or the outdoor ice baths, or brave a three-minute blast at -85°C in the cryotherapy chamber. You can treat yourself to a glass of kombucha or Dom Pérignon afterwards in the Three Graces Spa, then skip off to one of Grantley Hall’s suites, adorned with Roman busts and oil paintings by local artists that cleverly conceal flat-screen TVs. Damson gin in hand, look out at the fog rising over the River Skell and offer up a toast to Apollo, Roman god of healing – or, indeed, to Bacchus, god of wine, depending on your style.

Membership £3,500 per annum; no joining fee applicable (grantleyhall.co.uk).

On the edge of Windsor Great Park, Surrey’s elite (TV stars and sporting celebrities among them) gather here to competitively perform, play and mingle. Every inch of the 272-square-metre Wellness Suite & Gym has been carefully considered, from the Himalayan salt room promoting respiratory health to the Japanese Ashiyu foot ritual bath and a heated tepidarium bench (super for soothing aching muscles). The bespoke gym rig and Technogym Artis equipment get pulses racing, and a 4D body scanner helps you set goals and track results. High-energy classes – HIIT, spinning, boxing and more – are balanced by yoga and mental workouts through meditation. Outdoors, tennis, padel, assault bikes, rowing machines and a 20-metre sled track are all stationed within the parkland, because nothing beats a workout with a view. Cool down afterwards with a dip in the indoor pool or some cryotherapy, then retreat to the main hotel for some well-earned R&R: high tea in the Orchid Room or sushi at the Library Club. Those staying the night will find true peace and quiet in the well-appointed, elegant rooms. Bonus points for booking the Royal Suite, which has a private lift to the Wellness Suite.

Membership from £340 per month, plus £580 joining fee; £20 per class for hotel guests; spa access from £85 for non-hotel guests (fairmont-windsorpark.com).

Rewriting the perception of rural glamour, this magnificent Oxfordshire manor mixes country grandeur with city slickness. It attracts a jet-set crowd (many fly in especially), as well as status seeking Londoners and affluent locals in search of bucolic entertainment. At the swish Clubhouse, gym-goers showcase their toned physiques in glossy activewear, breaking a sweat in style. Perched at the top is the hi-spec, hi-tech 250-square metre workout area, where everything from the PTs to the machines and even the water cups is cutting edge. Only the best equipment will do: Peloton, Wattbike and TRX. An InBody machine provides muscle-to-fat analysis and Technogym’s Biostrength equipment, powered by aerospace-grade tech and AI to track individual body movements, adapts resistance in real time to deliver better strength and toning results (up to 30 per cent) compared to classic training with equipment or free weights. Social connection is on offer, too, with group classes ranging from power yoga to Boxfit to Gas Tank (a cardio blast); it continues on the highly popular padel courts. The more adventurous can try a round of shooting, archery or even axe-throwing. A visit to the exemplary Roman-style spa recharges batteries, particularly when followed by a protein shake at the plant-filled Potting Shed cafe. The alternative sport of people watching can be indulged at the manor’s bar and four restaurants, while the ultra opulent rooms offer sanctuary. Play, eat, sleep, repeat.

Membership £3,750 per annum, plus £1,000 joining fee; under 33s £1,900, plus £500 joining fee (estellemanor.com).

Delivering wellness and luxury in exemplary fashion, Gleneagles remains ahead of the game. Set in the breathtaking, rolling hills of Perthshire, known as ‘the glorious playground’ and famous for its championship golf course, this iconic Highlands establishment has hosted movie stars and royalty over the years, and more recently, Justin Bieber. Aside from the varied outdoor pursuits (fishing, falconry, gundog handling, shooting, cycling, riding, off-road driving), the wellness offering is outstanding. The gym sits in a spacious, light-filled studio with state-of-the-art equipment, including weight racks and rowers. Classes are varied, from TRX suspension training to spinning and metalift (weightlifting) sessions. Dedicated personal trainers are on hand to tailor a workout specifically for you, but for something more calming, book the sunrise yoga class. Last year saw the addition of the Gleneagles Sporting Club, which features two indoor tennis courts and three floodlit indoor padel courts. Head coach Richard Watkins will put you through your padel paces, and you might even spot Greg Rusedski on the court (the former British tennis No 1 is a Gleneagles devotee). The clubhouse interiors, designed by AIME studio, are cosy, comfortable and welcoming – a perfect spot for relaxing and reading the Sunday papers. There’s also an on site boutique if you feel tempted to stock up on chic activewear. Post-workout, head to one of the two indoor swimming pools, take a dip in the outdoor thermal pool or head to the spa for a blissful massage. A stay at Gleneagles wouldn’t be complete without an evening cocktail in the American Bar, followed by a delicious dinner in the world-famous Strathearn restaurant. Effortlessly ticking all the boxes, this is the perfect one-stop R&R wellness stop.

Membership £1,260 per annum for Gleneagles Sporting Club; no joining fee applicable (gleneagles.com).

The prospect of hitting the treadmill and gazing upon an inspiring city view may be tantalising, but let us suggest a rural – just as alluring – alternative. Welcome to the Herb House, Lime Wood’s spa and fitness offering nestled in the heart of the New Forest, where wild ponies roam through ancient woodland. Taking direct inspiration from its immediate surroundings, Lime Wood is dedicated to natural wellbeing, aiming for each guest to re-root at every touchpoint throughout their stay. That might be through Angela Hartnett’s hearty Italian cuisine or the array of bikes beckoning you to pedal through the glorious forest. The house, originally a 13th-century hunting lodge, may be framed by supercars but Lime Wood’s focus is on laid-back country-house style. Rooms are cosy and luxurious, with an effort to bring the outside in through colourful and textured fabrics. The Herb House sits at the epicentre, with the gym on the top floor. Gym classes come with forest views through floor-to-ceiling windows. Technogym equipment is carefully curated to meet the needs of every gym-goer – whatever their fitness level – with an emphasis on user-friendly weight machines. Classes include forest runs, as well as yoga and pilates at the Hideout, a clear-sided tent with scenic views. Instructors undergo ongoing training to stay at the top of their game, be it studying anatomy with Dr Andreo Spina, or breathwork with renowned expert Patrick McKeown. With some 100 classes a week, you’re spoilt for choice.

Membership from £330 per month or £3,600 per annum, plus £390 joining fee (limewoodhotel.co.uk).
