What does your Eton House say about you? An insider's guide to the world's most famous school

Town House or Country House? To Bekynton or not to Bekynton? When Prince George enrols at Eton College this week, he will need to know the tricks of the trade when it comes to life among the Tugs, Beaks, and Oppidans

By Edit · September 7, 2026

Prince George is set to enrol at Eton College, joining a storied list of alumni at what is perhaps the nation’s most famous and most prestigious school. As a first-year student, the future king is not only following in the footsteps of his father, Prince William, and his uncle, Prince Harry, but also the likes of George Orwell, Tom Hiddleston, and 20 Prime Ministers.

But, considering Eton’s level of fame on the international stage, the day-to-day life of a student at the school is something of an insider’s secret. Fortunately, Edit is on hand to give an insight into what life might look like for Prince George in this newest chapter of his royal career.

Heading to Oxford? Whether you're a religious rower, a union hack, or a Right Hon, as results day rolls around, here's a totally objective, comprehensive guide to the college you're about to call home

When it comes to life at Eton, one choice reigns supreme: your House. The School is a federal republic. Just as in the USA, individual states matter: e pluribus unum. There are 25 Houses, each taking roughly 55 students per year (apart from the oldest and original, the College itself, which accepts 75 boys on King’s Scholarships). But how does the House system work, and what does your House say about you?

The main School apparatus has very little influence within the individual Houses. It is all about the House Master (occasionally, historically, a woman but mostly men these days). The House Master sets the tone, the culture, the rules. They are selected from a spread of sportsmen, Army Officers, published authors, vicars, classical musicians, ornithologists, playwrights and more.

He will see the boys at meal times; he will watch his boys in concerts and on the playing fields; he will receive regular updates from other masters about his boys’ progress in all aspects of their School life. Three times a week, for relatively non-denominational ‘Prayers’, the House will gather together in the evening, and the House Master will pass out House bulletins, maybe read a prayer, or offer his own views on life, the universe and everything.

Results day has the next gen in a furore. If you're heading to Cambridge, it's time to get au fait with what your college of choice says about you

The House is known by the House Master, not by the building. Any current Etonian, when asked ‘which House are you in?’, will reply with the initials of his House Master. Any Old Etonian is known by their year of leaving School, and by their House Master’s initials, not by the building’s name. When they meet for dinners at White’s or Boodles, their old House Master is their honoured guest. And when the House Master dies, those same boys, now men, will flock from far and wide for the service, often back at School, in respect and tribute to the man who guided them years earlier.

So, how to choose a House – or, more precisely, how to choose a House Master? There is no Harry Potter Sorting Hat, but there is the Admissions Office. The Prince and Princess of Wales will have selected a few House Masters to meet out of the entire list of 25, purely off the back of some vague personal profiles, and George will have gone with them to each House for afternoon tea and a tour. Those chosen few will have politely ignored George’s parents and focused all their energy and attention on George.

Then there is a discreet dance. The House Masters pool their feedback centrally to Admissions. The Prince and Princess do likewise. If they are sensible, they will also have asked their son what he thought so his views are heard.

Then Admissions do their thing, and the decision is taken. All parties are informed, and your destiny is fixed for the next five years. Boys never move House at Eton. And what a destiny it is. There is no such thing as a good or bad House, per se, but there are most certainly factors to take into account when enrolling. (A turnover of more than two House Masters in a decade might be a sign that something is not quite right…)

Would you, for example, prefer a Town House or a Country House? Some Houses are in the middle of the school, by the busy road and traffic lights. They feel urban, townie, even. Some others are set back on the edge, looking out over playing fields and woodland. These feel more rural, more quiet.

It used to be that all Houses ate in-House, with an in-House chef and kitchen. Nowadays, only half have this set-up. In the 1970s, the School established a central dining hall named after Bishop Thomas Bekynton, who served as the Secretary to King Henry VI, the School’s Founder in 1440, and who turned the King’s vision for the School into a reality. Half the Houses now dine in Bekynton. Some might prefer the flexibility, speed and freedom from House Master oversight that Bekynton offers, while others may be more partial to the community of in-House dining. There is no tangible difference in food quality.

Some Houses have more of a reputation than others – and come with some important advice for any would-be entrants before they step through the front door. Here are the key players that the Prince and Princess of Wales might have been considering.

The original: 70 poor scholars funded by Henry VI back in 1440. Here, boys will live and work in the medieval buildings of the main School, with somewhat medieval facilities. Home to Boris Johnson, Harold Macmillan and Kwasi Kwarteng, among others. Boys have the post-nominal ‘KS’ for Kings Scholars because they have been awarded scholarships from the gruelling Kings Scholarship examination.

Top tip: Boys wear a gown, and they get uppity about ‘their’ wall, where the eponymous ‘Wall Game’ is played. They refuse to let the Oppidans (i.e. the rest of the School living in the boarding houses) practice on it before the annual Wall Game of Collegers versus Oppidans on St Andrew’s Day. This is the only House that is not known by the initials of its House Master, who is known simply as Master-in-College.

A Georgian rabbit warren with a strong lineage of sporting and thespian prowess, with the incumbent House Master continuing this trend. Ideally located for all parts of the School, and also a Bekynton house.

Top tip: Where else but Eton would a humble boys’ boarding house have an extraordinary and beautiful Gormley sculpture on its outside wall. Look up and enjoy.

The oldest boarding house, tucked away invisibly down the High Street with views of Windsor Castle from the rooms for senior boys at the top of the House. One for explorers: home to Joseph Banks, botanist and founder of Kew Gardens; and Captain Oates of South Pole legend.

Top tip: Had a wobbly time with House Master churn pre-Covid, but the incumbent has since steadied the ship masterfully – not surprising, because he has a strong rowing heritage.

Where the smart money is going for Prince George, primarily because it was where his father and uncle boarded, so it already has the necessary security infrastructure. William and Harry were looked after in Manor House by the great Dr Andrew Gailey CVO, the distinguished historian, who was a vital pillar of stability for the young Princes after the tragic death of their mother. The current incumbent is cut from the same cloth: a distinguished historian and pillar of stability.

Top tip: The senior boys in the House will be quietly disappointed if George does end up here, because it means the coveted best room in the building for the last 25 years (with its own en-suite bathroom, sitting room and kitchenette) will be repurposed away from the students and back to the young Prince’s protection officer.

An Edwardian masterpiece with the best Private Side in the School and overlooking the historic Field, where ‘football as played on the Field at Eton’ became one of the three founding codes of Association Football (along with Harrow Football and Winchester Football). Named after Sir Henry Wotton, the Tudor polymath and diplomat, who was a friend of John Donne, a witness to the fire that raised the Globe theatre, an Ambassador to Venice, and Provost of Eton.

Top tip: The current incumbent is a bona fide record-holding cross-channel swimmer who takes his boys for early morning training dips in the Thames in all weathers. No wonder they are happy, industrious and resilient boys.

A muscular late-Victorian powerhouse on Keate’s Lane, generally beloved by its boys for its big rooms and high ceilings and proximity to Bekynton over the road. Home to its own 20th-century muscular powerhouse, the Olympic rowing titan Sir Matthew Pinsent.

Top tip: It had the best House colours in the School, pink and blue quarters, but they relocated to Wotton in the 1990s when the House Master of the time exercised his ancient right to move House to Wotton for his family and the bigger Private Side (the last House Master move allowed by the School).

Perhaps the most bucolic House in the School with stunning views over the flood meadows bordering the Thames in the distance, and Windsor across the river. Situated near Lower Chapel at the furthest point of the School.

Top tip: Boys in this House appreciate the connection between location and time-keeping very quickly. Anywhere south-east is easy; anywhere on the west side of the School needs an extra five minutes at least to be on time.
