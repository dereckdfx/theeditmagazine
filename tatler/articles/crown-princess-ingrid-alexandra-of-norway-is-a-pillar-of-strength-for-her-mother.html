Royals

Crown Princess Ingrid Alexandra of Norway is a pillar of strength for her mother, Queen Mette-Marit, at the royal funeral of her great-aunt, Princess Astrid

The future queen of Norway, 22, was a pillar of support for the royal family at their second funeral in so many months

By Isaac Bickerstaff · September 24, 2026

Crown Princess Ingrid Alexandra of Norway carried herself with all the grace and elegance of a future queen at the funeral of her great-aunt, Princess Astrid, on Wednesday afternoon. The heir to the throne, 22, stoically supported both her mother, Queen Mette-Marit, and her grandmother, Queen Sonja, at the solemn ceremony in Oslo, which took place less than a month after the death of King Harald V.

Ingrid Alexandra was photographed holding hands with Queen Sonja, 89, as the pair arrived at Ris Church, where the Norwegian royal family gathered to pay their respects to the late Princess Astrid, who died on 11 September, just days after the death of her brother, Harald. The Crown Princess was also seen consoling her mother, whose recent lung transplant surgery had left her less able to carry out official royal duties.

In a moving gesture, Ingrid Alexandra wore the same diamond heart necklace that she wore to King Harald’s funeral. The jewel has a poignant connection to Queen Sonja, who is said to have been gifted it by her husband in 1973 after the couple welcomed their son Haakon, now the King of Norway, in 1973. Queen Sonja then gave the necklace to Ingrid Alexandra to mark the Princess’s christening in 2004.

The new King paid tribute to his ‘aunt Astrid’ in a moving speech to the congregation. ‘In her work, Princess Astrid was always particularly concerned with care and inclusion for the most vulnerable in society,’ he said. ‘She was very loyal and always carried out her duties with commitment, presence and an infectious spirit. Aunt Astrid was an important support for King Harald and the rest of us in the family. We learned a lot from her, and she was not afraid to speak up. Her passing is a great loss for us. We will miss her.’

The Norwegian government was represented by Finance Minister Jens Stoltenberg, who delivered a moving tribute to the late Princess, who was, at the time of her death, one of the oldest working royals in the world. Stoltenberg spoke of the time that Astrid spent as Norway’s first lady following the death of her mother, Crown Princess Martha, in 1954.

‘Princess Astrid served four kings,’ he said. ‘Her grandfather King Haakon, her father, King Olav, her brother King Harald, and – in her last days – her nephew King Haakon. Hardly anyone has lived more with – and more up to – the motto of our kings: "Everything for Norway."’

Stoltenberg also noted how Princess Astrid married for love at a time when such a decision was frowned upon in royal circles. ‘Entering into marriage is a serious and important decision; that's how it is for all of us, but it's a little extra complicated as a royal,’ he told the congregation.

‘Princess Astrid wanted to marry Johan Martin Ferner. He was divorced, and it became a contentious and public matter. The princess stood up for the one she loved, and love prevailed in the end. The two were married in 1961. This is how she led other royals in choosing a spouse.’

Indeed, Princess Astrid’s refusal to let archaic royal tradition dictate her love life no doubt inspired her brother, King Harald, who married a commoner, and even her niece, Princess Martha Louise, who stepped back from royal life altogether in order to marry self-proclaimed shaman Durek Verrett – both the Princess and Verrett were in the congregation at the funeral, alongside Martha Louise’s daughters, Maud, Leah, and Emma Behn.

While the ceremony was not a state funeral, a number of foreign royals who knew Princess Astrid personally were in attendance to pay their respects, including Crown Princess Victoria of Sweden. The British royal family did not make the journey, but Princess Anne sent a wreath, with a handwritten message reading: ‘With love and happy memories.’

Born in 1932, Princess Astrid was one of three children born to Princess Märtha of Sweden and Prince Alexander of Denmark, who later became King Olav V of Norway. Among her maternal relations were King George VI and Queen Mary. Queen Elizabeth, the Queen Mother, was among her godparents.

In 1940, the entire Norwegian royal family fled Oslo as a result of the German invasion. It was deemed safer for the royal family to separate, so King Haakon (Astrid's grandfather) along with Crown Prince Olav (her father) remained in Oslo; meanwhile, the Crown Princess made her way to Sweden with the three children. En route, they encountered problems at the border station, in spite of the fact that Princess Märtha was Swedish-born. In any case, before long it was decided that Sweden was not the safest place for the family.

On 17 August, the Crown Princess and the children set sail for the US aboard the American Legion. Princess Astrid, Prince Harald and their sister, Princess Ragnhild, are said to have enjoyed their time in the US. They stayed for a time at the home of Frederic Shaefer, a wealthy Pittsburgh socialite and enjoyed the beaches of Cape Cod during the summer. One memorable moment Harald recalled was standing on the portico of the White House in 1945 as Franklin D. Roosevelt was sworn in as President. The Norwegian royals also spent time at Laura Delano’s house in Rhinebeck, New York.

Princess Astrid later spent two years studying economics and political history at the University of Oxford, and cemented her ties with Britain when she attended the coronation of Queen Elizabeth II in 1953, a moment she once called ‘one of the greatest memories of my life.’
