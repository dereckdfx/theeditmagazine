Milano just got a major makeover: Prada’s Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II flagship reopens with eight floors filled with fashion, art and even a secret passageway

Heading to the Italian fashion capital? As Milan Fashion Week gets underway, the ‘Prada Galleria Project’ is unveiled in shimmering splendour

By Chandler Tregaskes · September 22, 2026

Mayfair’s had its fair share of openings, reopenings and reincarnations in 2026. Hermés unveiled its decade-in-the-making megaplex of six Grade II-listed townhouses that lit up Bond Street; RH debuted its latest ‘gallery’ concept store on Burlington Gardens, befit with gilded dining rooms and opulent gold-leaf portrait-strewn staircases; and just last week, heritage tailors Gieves & Hawkes opened the doors to its No.1 Savile Row revamp ahead of London Fashion Week with the British Imperial Military Band on hand to toast its arrival, proving once again the luxury sector has been steadily finding its way back to a glamorous, bricks-and-mortar experience. And to think the naysayers had said physical shopping had met its fated end just a few years ago…

Now, as Milan Fashion Week inaugurates its Spring-Summer ‘27 season, Milano is getting its latest major makeover. The recipient? Prada. The rendezvous? Its historic Galleria Vittorio Emmanuele II flagship. The result? An eight-floor, all-encompassing Prada world one-stop-shop filled with womenswear, menswear, accessories, beauty, luggage, art, luxury hospitality; and even a secret passageway.

It comes with a whole lot of history, too. The structure itself is in fact Prada’s oldest and most storied boutique. Originally starting life over a century ago in 1913 as Fratelli Prada (named so after founding brothers Mario and Martino), then as a leather goods shop in the heart of the recently-opened, Duomo-adjacent, marble-floored mall in the heart of Milano, the ‘Prada Galleria Project’, as the house has dubbed it, pays homage to both its roots and its future. ‘The past is never past but an accumulation of ideas bound by intuition, collaboration and intellectual exchange,’ so read a statement from the house.

Now, Miuccia Prada – Mario’s granddaughter and Executive Director of the Prada Group; as well as Co-Creative director of Prada, and Creative Director of Miu Miu – is blending heritage with innovation; a feat perfectly assigned to the house that perfectly and consistently honours evolution in luxury. ‘The complex coalesces more than a century of family and company history with the foundational principle that Prada is not a fixed identity but a continuous exploration, sustained by dialogue with art, architecture, cinema, culture and art,’ the statement continued.

The art, of course, is central. Miuccia Prada’s own contemporary art institution, Fondazione Prada, is mere miles - or should we say kilometres - away in the Vigentino outskirts of the Italian fashion capital. The cultural centre gets its own ‘osbervetario’ on-site, as well as a new permanent ‘Pradasphere’ exhibition that can now be found in the Galleria too, tracing back the house’s heritage through iconic archive pieces and objet. Meanwhile, the beloved Marchesi 1824 - one of Milan’s oldest pasticcerias acquired by the Group in 2014 - takes pride of place, providing a space to refuel if any keen clientele quite literally shop ‘till they drop.

Ahead of today’s London Fashion Week show, the V&A opens the doors to a new showcase focusing on Burberry’s most famed silhouette, the trench coat

Interiors inside provide a decidedly glossy backdrop to the house’s eminent collections (think mahogany-hued, glass curio cabinets filled with Bonnies, Karins, and Cleos; coveted accessories in velvet boxes upon checkerboard marble floors), but perhaps its most entrancing new feature is the ‘Secret Passage’ which transports guests from the womenswear to the menswear via an underground tunnel, lined with haute artifacts any fashion fiend would champ at the bit for.

Think you know cashmere? Think again. China's favoured manufacturer of the softly stylish knitwear is pairing centuries-old heritage with cutting-edge innovation…

With Vogue World arriving in the city for its 2026 iteration, there’s never been a better moment to fly into Linate. But make sure your first port of call is Prada Galleria - and skip dinner, Marchesi 1824 have you carb-covered.
