E1: Is this powerboat racing series the world's most glamorous new sport?

Champagne, celebrity owners and electric powerboats: could E1 be even more glamorous than Formula 1?

By Orla Sheridan · July 23, 2026

It’s 4pm on a Thursday in Lake Como and the usually serene backdrop to Villa d’Este’s lunchtime service is being periodically interrupted by the sound of futuristic looking powerboats smacking the water.

Undeterred by the leopard print ‘RaceBird’ circling bobbing buoys, white blazered staff continue to weave themselves between tables of women dressed in Chanel, delivering glasses of pale rosé, plates of monkfish meunière – and velvet boxes to protect Birkins too precious to make direct contact with an alfresco table or chair.

The queens of the British summer social season have been anointed. As the all-female series F1 Academy makes its debut at Silverstone on the 100th anniversary of the legendary British Grand Prix, Edit speaks to the young women who are making history in the driving seat

This is the deliciously dolce vita setting for the Italian edition of E1, the thrilling, all-electric international powerboat racing series now in its third season. For newbie petrol heads (like me) who were introduced to racing through Netflix’s wildly popular Drive to Survive, it is most easily understood as Formula 1 on water – and not to be confused with its sister sports, Formula E (electric race cars) and Extreme E (off-road racing).

The weekend follows the same structure as F1: free practice, then qualifying, then race day. There are sleek vehicles, high-octane action, glamorous locations – and even more glamorous guests (everyone is supermodel gorgeous). The Ocean Club – the E1 answer to the Paddock Club – offers fine dining, drinks and the best view in the house. The men are all in linen shirts and Loro Piana shoes and the women look as if they’re auditioning to be a driver’s high fashion ‘paddock Princess’. Alexandra Leclerc, eat your heart out.

Though the pilots (one male, one female driver for each of the nine teams) haven’t yet reached the household name status of Lewis Hamilton, Charles Lelerc, Lando Norris and co., there is still plenty of star power. In fact, one thing you soon learn about E1: the celebrity names are relentless. Only its the owners that are the main attraction. Ex-Chelsea footballer Didier Drogba, NBA billionaire LeBron James, international cricketer Virat Kohli and tennis legend Rafael Nadal all own teams, as do Hollywood heavyweight Will Smith and former NFL quarterback Tom Brady. This star-studded group make up one of the most exclusive and competitive WhatsApp groups in the sporting world, or so I'm told.

So is there a series villain, as Christian Horner is to Drive to Survive?

‘I wouldn’t say we have any real bad guys,’ says co-founder Alejandro Agag puffing on a cigar on Villa d'Este's private veranda ahead of the final race. But that doesn't mean there's not a team to beat: Team Brady are the reigning champions and have a target on their back. As for his dream celebrity owner? Taylor Swift. Isn’t everyones?

Ferdinand Habsburg, heir to the House of Habsburg-Lorraine, has his eyes set on the road ahead – and shows no signs of slowing down

If Agag, a Spanish entrepreneur and former politician, brought international connections to E1 then Rodi Basso, a former Formula 1 engineer and McLaren business manager, brought the technical knowledge. The pair announced the series at the Monaco Yacht Club in 2022, and the first boat was unveiled with the help of original Edit girl Cara Delevingne later that year.

It has gone from strength to strength since. In 2026, there are eight races scheduled across four continents – including the Lake Como Grand Prix – with the season concluding in November in the Bahamas.

Although the races themselves were exhilarating, not everyone seemed especially interested. At one point, as I attempted to watch the action, I was politely moved aside for someone who had paid a premium for that exact view. She moved into the space and then promptly turned her back to the lake and resumed chatting with her friends, entirely oblivious to the boats hurtling past behind her.

As F1 Academy's Alba Larsen, Nina Gademan and Rachel Roberston grace the cover of Edit, its managing director – and former racing star – Susie Wolff explains why the future is female for Formula 1

In truth, E1 works almost as well as a backdrop for networking as it does as a sporting spectacle. And I can't criticise. When the opportunity arose to watch the final race from the water aboard one of Villa d'Este's immaculate lacquered boats, I gladly climbed aboard. What followed was less race coverage and more a sun-drenched tour of Lake Como's greatest hits: Gianni Versace's former villa, the mansion of a tech-developer-turned-billionaire, and the historic Villa Pizzo where John Legend married Chrissy Teigen in 2013. Somewhere between the champagne and an entirely unnecessary photoshoot, the race itself passed me by.

I only discovered who had won once we returned to shore. By then, Will Smith's team were dousing one another in champagne before diving fully clothed into the lake. It’s a tradition, I was informed, reserved for the victors: when you win, you swim.

Formula 1 may still claim the crown as the world's most glamorous motorsport, but E1 has found a rather persuasive way of challenging it. Monaco has superyachts; Lake Como has Renaissance villas, lacquered Rivas and lunches at Villa d'Este. If this is the future of racing, you can sign me up.
