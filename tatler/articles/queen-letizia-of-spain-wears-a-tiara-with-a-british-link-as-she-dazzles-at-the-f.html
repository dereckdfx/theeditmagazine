Queen Letizia of Spain wears a tiara with a British link as she dazzles at the French state banquet

In addition to some regal patriotic dressing, the Spanish queen made a nod to Britain's history of royal jewel making

By Isaac Bickerstaff · September 30, 2026

Queen Letizia of Spain represented her country with a spot of patriotic dressing as she hosted Emmanuel and Brigitte Macron at the Royal Palace of Madrid – but the true star of her ensemble was a royal heirloom tiara with a British, not Spanish, origin.

Alongside her husband, King Felipe, the Queen welcomed the French President and First Lady for a state banquet on 29 September. While Her Majesty is best known for her smart, tailored approach to regal dressing, she proved once again that she is a master of pomp and ceremony, donning a pale blue Carolina Herrera crepe gown for the occasion. A savvy choice: the brand comes under the Puig group, a Spanish business.

But it was Queen Letizia’s tiara that truly caught attention during the state banquet. The Spanish Floral Tiara, as it is known, is a stunning collection of old- and brilliant- cut diamonds, set in a frame of silver and gold. It has an equally captivating history. While it was known that Franco previously acquired the piece at Joyería Aldao and gifted it to Sofia of Greece upon her wedding to future King Juan Carlos, the precise provenance of the tiara was, for a long time, rather murky.

Eventually, the royal palace confirmed that the Spanish Floral Tiara could in fact be traced back to Britain. ‘It was commissioned by King Alfonso XII from English firm J.P. Collins in 1879 for his fiancée, Archduchess Maria Cirstina of Habsburg,’ the royal family confirmed, adding that ‘the jewel left the family at a later date, rejoining when the Dictator bought it for the Princess.’

Indeed, when Franco presented Prince Juan Carlos and Princess Sofia with the tiara in 1962, it was a sign that he approved of their union. Originally, the piece was not worn as a tiara but variously as a necklace, brooches, or a devant-de-corsage. Sofia, who would go on to be crowned queen in 1975, first wore the jewels as a necklace to her pre-wedding gala, held at the Royal Palace of Athens, and paired it with the Mellerio Shell Tiara, which was gifted by her new parents-in-law.

Sofia would go on to wear the piece as a necklace and three separate brooches throughout the ‘60s, at such events as the wedding ball of the Margrave of Baden and the Greek Monarchy’s centenary gala. It was not until the ‘70s that the jewels were converted into a tiara, a change that has never been reversed since. Queen Sofia made her debut in the tiara on her state visit to Sweden in 1979 and went on to wear it at a number of royal events throughout the early years of her reign.

Before handing the tiara on to Queen Letizia, Queen Sofia loaned the jewels to her daughters, Infanta Elena and Infanta Cristina, the latter of whom wore it to her wedding in 1997. In 2001, Queen Sofia wore the Spanish Floral Tiara to the wedding of King Haakon of Norway, then Crown Prince, in 2001.

Since marrying into the royal family in 2004, Queen Letizia has made the tiara her signature piece. As the Princess of Asturias, she wore it to a number of state visits and weddings, and, as Queen, she returned the piece to its home on a state visit to Britain in 2017.

In all likelihood, the next royal to wear the Spanish Floral Tiara will be Letizia and Felipe’s daughter, Princess Leonor. The future queen is yet to make her debut in one of the family’s heirloom tiaras, and neither she nor her sister, Infanta Sofia, joined their parents for the state visit. In his speech, however, President Macron was sure to pay tribute to the Princess of Asturias and her sister. ‘I toast His Majesty the King, Her Majesty the Queen, and Their Royal Highnesses, the Princess of Asturias and Infanta Sofía,’ the President said in his address. As for the princesses themselves, Leonor is currently beginning her first year of study at the Carlos III University of Madrid, while Sofía is busy continuing her education at Forward College, which has this term taken her to Paris.

While royal watchers await Princess Leonor’s tiara debut with bated breath, Queen Letizia is finding plenty of opportunities to dazzle in the royal family’s heirloom jewels.
